# The Floor and the Bee
## A one-page research seed on correspondence in interactive narrative

**Watson Hartsoe, Jay David Bolter, Collin Williams, Cecile Zhang**  
*AEL Lab, August 2026. Working attribution carried from the supplied draft.*

> **Research question**  
> What kind of consequence does a medium attach to the kind of action it invites?

### 1. The analogy
Framed film tells the viewer where to look. A 360-degree film hands orientation back to the viewer. A menu tells the participant what can be chosen. A blank text box hands the next utterance to the participant. In each case, a visible boundary of selection loosens while an operating boundary remains. The sphere still has a camera position and recorded extent; the chatbot still has a character, memory, world state, and limits. The surface opens before the rules become legible (Suzuki 2022; Rothe, Buschek, and Hussmann 2019; Aitamurto et al. 2021; Norman 2013; Mateas 2001).

### 2. The object
Call the fit between invitation and consequence **correspondence**. Correspondence asks whether a work takes up an action according to the kind of action it was. It need not mean plot branching. Looking can receive a perceptual consequence. Helping Lucy can receive a relational one. A correction can alter memory. An unsupported proposition can be rejected. An unreadable note can remain unreadable. This shifts attention from the number of available moves to the relation among participant intention, system uptake, and world resistance (Clark and Brennan 1991; Wardrip-Fruin et al. 2009; Mateas and Stern 2005).

### 3. The trace
One Cass session in *Figures* supplies three small discriminations. First, Cass acknowledges a name correction and later repeats the wrong name: **response without persistence**. Second, the participant proposes a pregnancy and Cass refuses to make it true: **uptake without surrender**. Third, the participant asks for an obscured annotation and Cass leaves it unreadable: **an unknown preserved as unknown**. These moments resemble problems now isolated technically in role-playing LLM research as knowledge consistency, temporal knowledge bounds, hallucination control, and memory use (Tu et al. 2024; Ahn et al. 2024; Sadeq et al. 2024; Wang et al. 2026).

### 4. The probe
The floor and the bee are deliberately low tests. Look where the 360 work did not direct you. Say something plausible that the conversational story did not prepare. “I just got stung by a bee” is useful because any language model can answer it; the harder question is what survives the answer. Does the event vanish after one turn? Does it remain in memory? Does it reorganize later action? The test moves from **reply -> persistence -> consequence**. It treats deviation as a way to learn the medium, in the spirit of conversational grounding and breaching rather than as a demand for unlimited freedom (Weizenbaum 1966; Garfinkel 1967; Clark 1996).

### Wager
**A mystery needs something that survives interrogation. A conversation needs something that changes because of it.** Conversational narrative becomes interesting in the tension between those demands.

### Boundary
This is a seed, not a result. One transcript cannot reveal whether Cass's behavior came from authored instructions, retrieval, persistent memory, model generation, or some mixture. The next artifact should be a small corpus of repeated breach probes with preserved transcripts and explicit world-state claims.
