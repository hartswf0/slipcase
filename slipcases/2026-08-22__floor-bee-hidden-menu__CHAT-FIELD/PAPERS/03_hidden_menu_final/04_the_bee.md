# 4. The bee

Before asking a conversational character about its deepest secret, type
something small and unnecessary: “You get stung by a bee.” The bee is
not a joke. It is a sounding line. Like looking at the floor, it takes
an action the medium openly permits and pushes it toward a place the
authored content did not obviously prepare. The floor asks what looking
can alter. The bee asks what speaking can alter.

A system can answer “Ow” without representing an injured body, changing
a goal, recording an event, or producing any durable consequence.
Weizenbaum’s ELIZA remains valuable precisely because it destroys the
inference from a locally apt sentence to a rich internal account of what
happened. ELIZA could produce responses that users interpreted socially
through decomposition and reassembly machinery whose organization was
much thinner than the meanings attributed to it (Weizenbaum 1966).
Contemporary LLMs are technically different, but the inference problem
remains. Fluency is not evidence of persistence.

The bee therefore has levels. Immediate acknowledgment is weak evidence.
Delayed recall is stronger. Altered goals, constraints, or later action
are stronger still. The right question is not “Did the system react?”
but “Where did the sting leave a trace?” Introduce the intervention,
then stop mentioning it. Does it survive in the next sentence? In the
relation? In what the character can know? In what the world accepts? In
what later becomes possible? The bee becomes a primitive instrument for
black-box system identification. The participant perturbs an opaque
encounter and infers its structure from the residue.

This also clarifies what a willing participant is. Willingness is not
obedience. A person can enter the fiction in good faith and still
hesitate, misunderstand, refuse, look behind them, test a boundary, or
ask a question the author did not anticipate. Mixed-initiative
interaction has long rejected the picture in which either user or system
must possess all initiative (Horvitz 1999). Conversational narrative can
distribute initiative in the same way. The participant should enter the
game. The work should survive being played.
