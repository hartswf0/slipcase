# 7. Follow the dent

We do not need a complete ontology of LLM narrative before we can study
it. We need a better unit of observation. The obvious unit, prompt plus
immediate response, is too short. Instead: make a dent and follow it. At
a reproducible point in an encounter, introduce one intervention the
story does not require. A correction. A plausible false proposition. A
refusal. A moral accusation. A frame-breaking question. A bee sting.
Then let the conversation continue without reinforcement.

Observe what changes and how long the change survives. The goal is not
to reward maximum depth. A historical character should resist fabricated
biography. A mystery should protect facts. A companion may allow
relational transformation without altering a shared external world.
*Wolves in the Walls* shows that local recognition can be valuable even
when the global plot stays in place. *Façade* warns that local variation
may later reconverge. Cass shows that acknowledgment can fail to
persist, that a proposed fact can be bounded, and that an unknown can
remain deliberately unavailable.

The decisive comparison is counterfactual: would this later state still
be different if I had not made that intervention? Matched runs can begin
separating surface variation from persistent consequence, relational
change from plot change, disclosure order from world alteration,
temporary accommodation from durable commitment, and local divergence
from global divergence. Park and colleagues’ generative-agent
architecture is useful here as a reminder that observation, reflection,
memory, and planning can be separated computationally even when they
appear as one social surface to the user (Park et al. 2023). The point
is not that every narrative system should implement those components. It
is that “the character responded” is too coarse a description of what
changed.

The method also disciplines claims about causation. A clue appearing
after a complaint is not evidence that the complaint caused the clue. A
character calling the user Maya after a reset is not evidence of
cross-session memory. A refusal of pregnancy is not evidence that the
refusal will persist twenty turns later. The exploratory Cass encounter
is valuable because it identifies where matched comparisons would be
informative. It should not be made to answer implementation questions
the transcript cannot answer.
