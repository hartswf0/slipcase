# 3. Lucy and the rule of correspondence

*Wolves in the Walls* supplies the correction that keeps this argument
from collapsing into a demand for infinite branching. Lucy does not need
to surrender the plot in order to acknowledge the participant. Pete
Billington describes the experience as reactive to viewer engagement
while preserving a continuing narrative trajectory, and Fable’s own
framing assigns the viewer a role in helping Lucy (Billington 2019;
Fable Studio 2023). A participant can therefore be recognized in
performance and relation even if the large-scale story does not wait at
a fork for a button press.

This breaks a stubborn equation: meaningful action equals branch the
plot. An action may alter relation, performance, attention, trust, or
disclosure. Lucy can look at me differently without producing a
different ending. A character can refuse me without collapsing the
fiction. A documentary can let me discover the room around Sidra while
leaving the recorded event untouched. If a term is useful, call the
requirement correspondence: the work should answer the kind of act it
invited. Correspondence is weaker than obedience and stronger than
topical relevance. It does not mean that the system does what I ask. A
refusal can correspond. A misunderstanding can correspond if it can be
repaired. A world can say no.

*Façade* makes the same point from the opposite direction. Its
natural-language surface looked radically open, but Mateas and Stern
describe an authored dramatic architecture in which enormous variety at
the sentence level was mapped into a smaller operational vocabulary of
discourse acts, social games, beats, beat goals, handlers, and
sequencing machinery (Mateas 2002; Mateas and Stern 2005). Open language
at the surface could coexist with constrained dramatic organization
beneath it. The player could perturb a local performance without
necessarily rewriting the global arc.

That possibility suggests what one zettel called an absorptive
narrative. An unexpected intervention changes the local route, the
characters adapt, and the work may later rejoin a larger progression.
This is not a defect. It is a distinct architecture of consequence. The
error is to infer global narrative agency from the fact that a system
answered an unusual prompt. An unusual response may show only that the
work is good at swallowing deviations. To know more, one has to follow
the deviation beyond the turn in which it was acknowledged.
