**The Floor, the Bee, and the Hidden Menu**

*How Open Interfaces Hide What We Can Change*

**Watson Hartsoe · Jay David Bolter · Collin Williams · Cécile Zhang**

AEL Lab \| 21 August 2026

| **ABSTRACT** A 360-degree film lets a spectator look where the filmmaker did not point. A conversational character lets a participant say what the author did not write. Both appear to remove a boundary. Neither removes constraint. The boundary moves out of sight. Drawing from Clouds Over Sidra, Wolves in the Walls, Façade, conversation analysis, recent work on role-playing language models, and an exploratory encounter with the FIGURES character Cass, we argue that open-language narrative should be judged less by how much a participant can say than by what speech can alter. We propose a small diagnostic method: introduce an unnecessary but plausible intervention, then follow its residue. The floor and the bee are two versions of the same test. |
