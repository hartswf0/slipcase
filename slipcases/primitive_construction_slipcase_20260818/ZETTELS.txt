TITLE:
SUBTRACTION DOES NOT MAKE SOIL STRUCTURAL — ROCK-CUT ARCHITECTURE BREAKS EXACTLY WHERE THE YOUTUBE BUILD BEGINS

SOURCE:
Mohammad Mangeli, Farshid Aram, Reza Abouei, Fatemeh Mehdizadeh Saradj — “A New Look at Excavation Techniques and Design of Rock-Cut Architectures” — 2022 — Introduction.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
Rock-cut architecture produces space by hollowing a naturally solid context rather than assembling conventional building materials. The authors stress that the context must be soft enough to excavate yet sufficiently coherent not to fall apart.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
The tempting description of YouTube “primitive” pools as subtractive or rock-cut architecture conceals a decisive material difference.

Rock-cut architecture inherits structural continuity from rock.

The YouTube structures described in the INPUT appear to excavate soil, clay, or earth and nevertheless produce steep walls, ceilings, stairs, ledges, furniture, tunnels, and water-retaining basins.

If the substrate is substantially less self-supporting than rock, then excavation alone cannot explain the resulting architecture.

LOCAL MOVE:
Mangeli et al. make geology part of architectural possibility rather than treating “digging” as a sufficient construction technique.

SOURCE TERMS:
rock-cut architecture
settlement context
excavation
context material
earth-shelter
cave dwelling

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The central mystery is no longer how much labor digging requires.

It is:

Why does excavated earth continue behaving like a solid architectural monolith after the void has been removed?

QUESTION:
What physical property of the substrate makes the steep excavated geometries in primitive-building videos possible?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Where does excavation end and hidden construction begin?

MECHANISM:
A cavity can remain architectural only if the remaining mass resists:

gravity
shear failure
slumping
erosion
local collapse
water softening

Rock supplies some of this resistance intrinsically.

Loose or weakly cohesive soil may require another mechanism:
compaction,
selection of unusually cohesive strata,
surface stabilization,
concealed reinforcement,
cementitious render,
temporary duration,
or geometry that is less extreme than the edited image suggests.

FORMAL SHIFT:
<SOLID GEOLOGICAL CONTEXT>
→ <EXCAVATABLE MASS>
→ [REMOVE MATERIAL WHILE PRESERVING LOAD-BEARING CONTINUITY]
→ <HABITABLE VOID>

SOURCE FORMALISM:
NONE

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

SUBTRACTIVE BUILDABILITY
=
EXCAVABILITY
∩
POST-EXCAVATION STABILITY

The first condition makes digging possible.
The second condition makes architecture possible.

TENSION:
The INPUT treats “continuous earth mass → remove material → discover rooms/walls/stairs” as the defining construction sequence.

Mangeli et al. pressure that formulation because subtraction only works architecturally when the unremoved material can remain structurally coherent.

MISSING:
soil classification
cohesion
friction angle
moisture content
density
layering
cut height
roof span
surface stabilizer
time between excavation and filming

BOUNDARY:
This source concerns rock-cut architecture in Iran.

It does not establish the composition or structural behavior of any YouTube site.

The comparison identifies a missing variable; it does not prove concealed cement or machinery.

CITATION TRAIL:
soil mechanics of unsupported vertical cuts
lateritic and clay-rich tropical soils
earthen excavation stability
cement-stabilized earth
construction geology of Cambodian primitive-video sites

TEST:
Select ten videos containing near-vertical excavated walls.

For each:
estimate unsupported height,
identify visible soil horizons,
record cracking/slumping,
inspect cuts before and after surface finishing,
and compare the observed geometry with plausible strength ranges for unstabilized soils.

If impossible geometries emerge only after finishing, the “skin” becomes a structural suspect rather than merely a cosmetic one.

TITLE:

SOURCE:
Mohammad Mangeli, Farshid Aram, Reza Abouei, Fatemeh Mehdizadeh Saradj — “A New Look at Excavation Techniques and Design of Rock-Cut Architectures” — 2022 — Methods / excavation-process reconstruction.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
Because historical documentation of Meymand’s excavation process is scarce, the researchers infer excavation techniques from surviving architectural fabric, settlement context, comparison, and local evidence. They reconstruct sequences including opening access, producing a semi-open area, refining entrances, and excavating rooms.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
A finished excavated structure can function as a record of the operations that produced it.

This changes the primitive-video research problem.

We do not need complete behind-the-scenes footage before asking how a structure was actually made.

The architecture itself may contain receipts.

finished void
to
inferred sequence of operations.

SOURCE TERMS:

architectural body

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The editing cut is not the only evidence available.

Residual geometry may betray an omitted construction stage.

QUESTION:
What construction sequences can be inferred from geometric dependencies among stairs, rooms, pools, roofs, islands, trenches, and access routes?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Can a video hide an operation while the resulting building remains unable to hide its consequences?

MECHANISM:
Certain geometric states require predecessor states.

A deep pit requires:
an access route,
a spoil-removal route,
or machinery reach.

A central island requires:
either preserving mass during excavation
or rebuilding/depositing material afterward.

An enclosed chamber requires:
a safe excavation face
before deepening.

A pool inside another pool requires:
ordered excavation and waterproofing operations.

FORMAL SHIFT:
<FINISHED ARCHITECTURAL GEOMETRY>
→ <DEPENDENCY RELATIONS>
→ [INFER PREDECESSOR OPERATIONS]
→ <MINIMUM CONSTRUCTION SEQUENCE>

SOURCE FORMALISM:
NONE

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

For architectural elements A and B:

A ≺ B

means:
A must exist before operation B can physically occur.

Construct a dependency graph for each finished structure.

If the filmed sequence violates the minimum dependency graph, then an unshown operation is required.

TENSION:
The INPUT proposes reconstructing builds as:

SITE
→ EXCAVATE
→ MASS
→ TRIM
→ FORM
→ COAT
→ SURFACE
→ FILL
→ POOL
→ OCCUPY
→ IMAGE.

That sequence is useful but too generic.

Mangeli et al. suggest recovering structure-specific excavation dependencies instead.

MISSING:
spoil piles
access ramps
temporary stairs
intermediate profiles
construction entrances
tool reach
machine reach
sequence of water introduction
whether islands are residual or deposited

BOUNDARY:

Several different operation graphs may produce the same finished form.

CITATION TRAIL:

archaeological chaîne opératoire
forensic architecture
process archaeology
rock-cut excavation methods

TEST:
Choose one highly complex “underground villa” video.

Freeze the final architecture.

Work backward.

For every element ask:
“What had to still be open, absent, accessible, dry, or unsupported immediately before this could exist?”

Generate all surviving construction sequences.

Then compare those sequences against the edit.

The unexplained intervals are candidates for hidden work.

permeability,
