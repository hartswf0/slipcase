TITLE:
THE SOIL IS PART OF THE PLAN — MATERIAL GRAIN CAN EXPLAIN FORM BEFORE STYLE DOES

SOURCE:
Mohammad Mangeli, Farshid Aram, Reza Abouei, Fatemeh Mehdizadeh Saradj — “A New Look at Excavation Techniques and Design of Rock-Cut Architectures” — 2022.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
Mangeli et al. identify settlement-context material and particle characteristics as factors affecting excavation quality, alongside excavation technique.

The primitive-video corpus is currently being coded mainly through visible architecture:

But a hidden architectural variable may precede all of them:

WHAT KIND OF GROUND WAS AVAILABLE?

LOCAL MOVE:

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
Repeated smooth curves may tell us something about site selection before they tell us something about aesthetic preference.

QUESTION:
Are creators choosing shapes after choosing sites, or choosing sites because particular soils permit the shapes their genre requires?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Has the primitive-building genre developed an undocumented empirical geology?

TENSION:
The INPUT asks why curves recur and proposes that excavation makes continuous curvature comparatively easy.

But “excavation” still generalizes across radically different grounds.

A curve easy in cohesive clay may be impossible in loose sand.

BOUNDARY:
The Iranian rock contexts in Mangeli et al. cannot be transferred directly to Cambodian soil conditions.

The source changes the variable we need to observe.

It does not supply its Cambodian value.
