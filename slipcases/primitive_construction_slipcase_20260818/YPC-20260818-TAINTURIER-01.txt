TITLE:
THE PIT IS NOT “LOCAL PRIMITIVE ARCHITECTURE” — IT INTERRUPTS A CAMBODIAN TRADITION OF BUILDING IN WOOD

SOURCE:
François Tainturier — “Building in Wood: Notes on a Vanishing Age-Old Tradition” — in Wooden Architecture of Cambodia: A Disappearing Heritage — 2006 — pp. 12–37.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
The Center for Khmer Studies volume treats building in wood as a major and longstanding Cambodian architectural tradition and studies its techniques together with social and symbolic dimensions.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
If many viral primitive-build productions are Cambodian, geographical location does not license a genealogy from Cambodian vernacular architecture to their pits, grottos, swimming pools, and underground villas.

LOCAL MOVE:
Tainturier makes timber craft historically and culturally specific rather than treating “local natural materials” as a generic vernacular category.

SOURCE TERMS:
building in wood
wooden architecture
Cambodia
techniques

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
Why does an internet genre produced in Cambodia make Cambodian timber-building knowledge nearly disappear?

QUESTION:
What architectural lineage do the excavated pool structures actually belong to?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Is “primitive” functioning by replacing a specific local building tradition with a globally legible fantasy of earth?

TENSION:
The INPUT already notices that the viral structures repeatedly substitute mass, earth, pit, cave, pool, and subterranean room for timber frame, elevated dwelling, joinery, and lightweight enclosure.

The unresolved step is genealogy.

What supplied the replacement vocabulary?

BOUNDARY:
Tainturier establishes an important Cambodian wooden-building tradition.

That does not mean every Cambodian building must descend from it, nor that excavation lacks local precedents.
