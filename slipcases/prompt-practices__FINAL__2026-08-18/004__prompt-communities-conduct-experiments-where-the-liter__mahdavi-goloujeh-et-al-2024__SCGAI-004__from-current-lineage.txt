ZETTEL

ID:
SCGAI-004

TITLE:
Prompt communities conduct experiments where the literature has not yet stabilized.

SOURCE:
Atefeh Mahdavi Goloujeh, Anne Sullivan, and Brian Magerko — “The Social Construction of Generative AI Prompts” — CHI EA ’24 — 2024 — https://doi.org/10.1145/3613905.3650947

PASSAGE:
[QUOTE] “We have a group of people and we are trying to experiment with pushing image prompts as far as they can go because there’s been no defining literature...” — P6

RESEARCH OBJECT:
The community acts as an informal experimental laboratory. Lack of settled documentation does not stop inquiry; it creates the reason for coordinated experimentation.

LOCAL MOVE:
Reclassify some prompt communities from KNOWLEDGE-SHARING GROUPS to DISTRIBUTED EXPERIMENTAL SYSTEMS.

SOURCE TERMS:
group; experiment; pushing image prompts; defining literature; references; collaborative effort

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
Prompt knowledge may originate outside formal research through coordinated folk experiments conducted directly against proprietary systems.

QUESTION:
What methodological structure do prompt communities invent when official documentation and academic literature are absent?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Can a Discord server function as an experimental institution—and how does its knowledge differ from laboratory knowledge?

MECHANISM:
Undocumented behavior → community question → distributed trials → comparison → provisional rule → circulation → further testing.

FORMAL SHIFT:
COMMUNITY AS AUDIENCE becomes COMMUNITY AS EXPERIMENTAL APPARATUS.

SOURCE FORMALISM:
The authors classify this under Collaborative Prompting and describe groups undertaking organized experiments around prompt capabilities.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
UNKNOWN MODEL PROPERTY q → {user_i test_i} → shared observations → provisional heuristic h(q) → redistributed tests.

TENSION:
Distributed experimentation can discover behavior quickly, but without controlled variables, stable model versions, records, or internal access, heuristics may mix effects with superstition.

MISSING:
Actual experimental records: hypotheses, variables, failed trials, model versions, and criteria for deciding an effect was real.

BOUNDARY:
The interview establishes that participants describe activity as experimentation; it does not establish validity or reproducibility of resulting knowledge.

CITATION TRAIL:
[[SCGAI-2024]] → P6’s community experiments → missing defining literature → compare folk experimentation with controlled prompt-engineering studies.

TEST:
Take one community-generated prompting rule and reconstruct/re-run the experiments across controlled seeds, variants, and model versions.

PLATFORM:
MidJourney / Discord
