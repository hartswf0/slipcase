TITLE:
Prompts could acquire patina: provenance can be made to look like wear on a tool.

SOURCE:
William C. Hill, James D. Hollan, Dave Wroblewski, and Tim McCandless — “Edit Wear and Read Wear” — CHI ’92 — 1992 — pp. 3–9 — https://doi.org/10.1145/142750.142751

PASSAGE:
[QUOTE] “We meant the displays of wear to foster sense-making out of otherwise unavailable data.” (p. 6)

RESEARCH OBJECT:
Hill et al. suggest a stranger provenance interface: do not put history somewhere else; put history on the object. [OUR INFERENCE] A prompt could acquire computational patina where copying, mutation, survival, and abandonment become perceptible at exact linguistic locations.

LOCAL MOVE:
Replace PROVENANCE AS METADATA with PROVENANCE AS MATERIAL PROPERTY OF THE PROMPT.

SOURCE TERMS:
wear; Edit Wear; Read Wear; authorship history; readership history; computational wear; shared artifact

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
Digital text usually looks unworn no matter how many people have used it. Prompt wear could restore a forensic quality that physical tools acquire automatically.

QUESTION:
What would users learn if a prompt visibly showed where previous users copied, altered, executed, abandoned, or preserved it?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Does the frictionless visual cleanliness of digital text conceal socially important structure that physical wear would normally reveal?

MECHANISM:
ordinary use → interaction events → accumulated activity by location → visible wear → users interpret patterns → subsequent activity changes.

FORMAL SHIFT:
PROMPT: TEXT + EXTERNAL PROVENANCE LOG becomes HISTORY-ENRICHED PROMPT whose visible state expresses previous interaction.

SOURCE FORMALISM:
Edit Wear portrays authorship history; Read Wear portrays readership history; the systems map accumulated activity onto document positions.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
W(span)={executions,copies,insertions,deletions,mutations,descendants,authors,survival}; derive POLISH, ABRASION, SCAR, FOSSIL, FORK.

TENSION:
Visible history can enable learning and credit while intensifying surveillance, territorializing phrases, and making experimentation feel observed.

MISSING:
Which wear encodings inform rather than distract, what users consent to expose, and what granularity—prompt/line/phrase/word/token/operation—is addressable.

BOUNDARY:
Hill et al. study document interaction, not prompts. Prompt Wear is [OUR INFERENCE].

TEST:
Compare ledger provenance to wear-on-text provenance for identifying stable techniques, productive mutation points, disputed contributions, and privacy discomfort.
