ZETTEL

ID:
SCGAI-006

TITLE:
Prompt sharing is described as feeding an ecosystem that feeds the prompter.

SOURCE:
Atefeh Mahdavi Goloujeh, Anne Sullivan, and Brian Magerko — “The Social Construction of Generative AI Prompts” — CHI EA ’24 — 2024 — https://doi.org/10.1145/3613905.3650947

PASSAGE:
[QUOTE] “I try to share some of my prompts every now and again because I feel like I’ve got to feed back into the ecosystem that’s providing for me.” — P4

RESEARCH OBJECT:
P4 describes prompt knowledge as a reciprocal commons: the individual receives techniques and inspiration and feels an obligation to return prompts.

LOCAL MOVE:
Replace PROMPT SHARING AS DISCLOSURE with PROMPT SHARING AS RECIPROCAL MAINTENANCE.

SOURCE TERMS:
share; feed back; ecosystem; community; contributing

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The “ecosystem” metaphor makes prompting sound like metabolism: practitioners consume collectively produced resources and return resources that maintain the system.

QUESTION:
What is replenished when someone “feeds back” a prompt—information, variation, prestige, examples, techniques, or obligation?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Can prompt communities sustain a commons when circulated resources also produce private outputs, reputation, and commercial value?

MECHANISM:
Community provides knowledge → individual gains capability → individual produces prompt/artifact → contribution returned → others gain capability → repertoire expands.

FORMAL SHIFT:
SHARING = TRANSFER FROM OWNER TO RECIPIENT becomes SHARING = REPRODUCTION OF A COMMON RESOURCE SYSTEM.

SOURCE FORMALISM:
The paper distinguishes orientations by willingness to share and reuse; collaborative and provocative orientations involve relatively high sharing.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
COMMONS_t → uptake by user i → transformation → contribution_i → COMMONS_(t+1).

TENSION:
The ecosystem metaphor implies reciprocal dependence while proprietary prompting asserts individual ownership over prompt compositions generated within the same environment.

MISSING:
Whether reciprocity is normatively enforced, personally felt, reputationally rewarded, or structurally necessary.

BOUNDARY:
P4’s statement establishes one participant’s reciprocal orientation, not a universal norm.

CITATION TRAIL:
[[SCGAI-2024]] → P4’s “feed back into the ecosystem” → commons/reciprocity literature → distinguish gift exchange, open source, reputation economies, and commons maintenance.

TEST:
Compare communities with different visibility/attribution systems and test whether receiving reusable prompt knowledge predicts later contribution.

PLATFORM:
MidJourney community
