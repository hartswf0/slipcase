TITLE:
EXIT OCCUPANCY MAKES THE PREVIOUS WORLD STATE PART OF THE NEXT SENTENCE

SOURCE:
Tim Ingold, “On Building a House,” Chapter 4 of Making: Anthropology, Archaeology, Art and Architecture (Routledge, 2013), pp. 54–56. SOURCE URL: https://doi.org/10.4324/9780203559055

ABIDING ACRES, SCENE-BRIEF.md and SCENE-AGENT-PROMPT.md, hartswf0/abiding-halfworld. SOURCE URL: https://github.com/hartswf0/abiding-halfworld/blob/main/SCENE-BRIEF.md

PASSAGE:
[QUOTE — INGOLD] “the lineaments of the structure, far from being imposed upon the material, emerge from the process of building itself.”

[PARAPHRASE — ABIDING] Every scene exports exitOccupancy computed from its ending state. The next scene in the same episode imports that state as INITIAL. Where a body was left standing is where the next scene finds it unless the screenplay moves it.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
A world can participate in specifying its own continuation by preserving state across operations.

In ABIDING, a scene is not generated only from the current screenplay text. It is generated from current text plus the inherited world produced by earlier scenes. This is a computational analogue of Ingold’s account of building in which each placed and reshaped element prepares conditions for what can follow.

The previous output becomes part of the next input.

LOCAL MOVE:
SHOTCALL RULE:

NEVER START A CONTIGUOUS SCENE FROM ABSTRACT DEFAULTS.

INITIAL := exitOccupancy(previous_scene)

Then ask:

WHO IS ALREADY HERE?
WHERE WERE THEY LEFT?
WHO FACES WHOM?
WHAT MUST MOVE BEFORE THE NEW ACTION CAN BEGIN?
WHAT DOES THE NEW SCENE INHERIT THAT ITS OWN TEXT DOES NOT SAY?

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
Continuity is not only error prevention.

Persistent state can generate staging consequences that the next textual unit never explicitly specifies.

The world therefore has memory that constrains language.

QUESTION:
How much directorial information can be generated by persistent world-state rather than explicit scene description?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Does a stateful world become a participant in authorship when its inherited conditions systematically constrain what may be said or done next?

MECHANISM:
SCENE_n:

INITIAL_n + ACTION_n
→ EXIT_n

SCENE_n+1:

EXIT_n
→ INITIAL_n+1.

Therefore:

OUTPUT_n
is part of
INPUT_n+1.

FORMAL SHIFT:
FROM:

SCENE_n = f(TEXT_n)

TO:

SCENE_n = f(TEXT_n, WORLD_STATE_n-1).

SOURCE FORMALISM:
ABIDING explicitly uses exitOccupancy, occupancyAt(), and INITIAL, with continuity handoff within episodes and intentional reset across episode boundaries.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

W_(n+1) = TRANSITION(W_n, A_n)

SHOT_(n+1) = PROJECT(W_(n+1), D_(n+1))

where D is the directorial instruction for the new scene.

TENSION:
READING A:
This is ordinary continuity management.

READING B:
Once persistence is mechanically binding, continuity becomes a generative constraint: prior action creates new conditions not stated in later scene text.

MISSING:
Examples where inherited occupancy forced an unexpected but superior shot.

Cases where the screenplay requires an explicit break from inherited world-state.

BOUNDARY:
ABIDING intentionally does not chain occupancy across episode boundaries. World memory has jurisdictional limits determined by narrative time.

TEST:
Take one long sequence on the-court.

Generate two versions:

A. exact occupancy inheritance.
B. reset all actors to plan defaults before every scene.

Compare:

invented movement
continuity contradictions
staging opportunities
number of decisions supplied by prior world-state
perceived persistence of the world.

The zettel survives if A generates consequential constraints absent from each isolated scene text.
