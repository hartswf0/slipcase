ID:
MANSION-001

TITLE:
TURING DOES NOT SAY THE MACHINE BUILDER CREATES THE SOUL; HE IMAGINES BUILDERS PROVIDING A MANSION IN WHICH SOMETHING ELSE MAY DWELL.

SOURCE:
A. M. Turing, “Computing Machinery and Intelligence,” Mind 59(236), 1950, pp. 433–460. SOURCE URL: https://doi.org/10.1093/mind/LIX.236.433

PASSAGE:
[QUOTE] In the theological-objection section Turing describes machine builders as “providing mansions for the souls that He creates,” then immediately labels the discussion “mere speculation.”

RESEARCH OBJECT:
Turing’s phrase separates construction of an inhabitable substrate from creation of the entity that might inhabit it. The machine is figured as a mansion, not as a manufactured soul. This supplies a sharper question for domestic AI: instead of asking only whether a machine is intelligent, ask what built conditions make a form of intelligence present, continuous, addressable, or capable of dwelling.

LOCAL MOVE:
Treat MANSION as an experimental variable: hold a computational process approximately constant while changing the material, institutional, and spatial environment through which it can perceive, speak, persist, and act.

SOURCE TERMS:
machine; soul; mansion; construction; theological objection; speculation

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The relevant act of building may be preparation rather than manufacture. A builder can make conditions for a form of life without possessing a complete specification of that life.

QUESTION:
What must be built before an intelligence can inhabit rather than merely execute?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Can architecture participate in the identity of an artificial agent strongly enough that moving the same model to a different house changes who or what the agent is?

MECHANISM:
SUBSTRATE + INTERFACES + PERSISTENCE + PERMISSIONS + ENVIRONMENT → CONDITIONS OF POSSIBLE INHABITATION.

FORMAL SHIFT:
FROM: BUILD MACHINE → MACHINE THINKS
TO: BUILD MANSION → SOMETHING MAY BECOME PRESENT THERE.

SOURCE FORMALISM:
NONE

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
MANSION(A) = the material, computational, social, and institutional conditions through which agent A can perceive, act, persist, and be recognized.

TENSION:
Turing is not offering a theory of architecture or endorsing theological souls. The value of the passage is structural and explicitly speculative.

MISSING:
A theory of migration between computational mansions; identity persistence; domestic AI transfer; ownership; eviction; sensorium change.

BOUNDARY:
Do not cite Turing as claiming machines literally possess souls. His wording occurs inside a hypothetical theological reply that he calls speculation.

CITATION TRAIL:
Turing 1950 → mansions → dwelling → domestic AI → distributed embodiment → Mona.

TEST:
Run one conversational intelligence across PHONE ONLY, SINGLE SPEAKER, and DISTRIBUTED HOUSE embodiments. Hold dialogue model constant and compare perceived identity, agency, privacy, memory, and authority.
