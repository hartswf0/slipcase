TITLE:
A SCREEN IS NOT A ROOM BECAUSE ITS NATIVE CONTACT IS TEMPORAL

SOURCE:
ABIDING ACRES, HALFWORLD-PROTOCOL.md and atlas/world.json, hartswf0/abiding-halfworld. SOURCE URL: https://github.com/hartswf0/abiding-halfworld/blob/main/HALFWORLD-PROTOCOL.md ; https://github.com/hartswf0/abiding-halfworld/blob/main/atlas/world.json

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE — ABIDING] The Halfworld Protocol says that screens, letters, feeds, records and visions can refuse ground-plane representation. Such objects are surfaces rather than rooms. In a surface, contact is temporal rather than spatial. The ABIDING atlas accordingly treats the-feed as a distinct staging structure for mediated cold opens.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
House Language cannot assume that all relations reduce to one geometry.

A room supports relations such as near, far, facing, crossing, occupying, and touching. A feed supports relations such as before, after, reply-to, visible-to, quoted-by, delayed-from, and scrolled-past. Displaying a feed on a rectangular screen does not make its semantic relations equivalent to bodies in a rectangular room.

The world model therefore needs typed relational spaces.

LOCAL MOVE:
SHOTCALL CLASSIFIER:

BEFORE STAGING, DECLARE:

GROUND
or
SURFACE.

IF GROUND:
use metric distance, named stations, occupancy, facing, bodily contact.

IF SURFACE:
forbid invented physical co-presence.
use order, address, reply, delay, visibility, repetition, disappearance, and frame-within-frame.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The same relation word can change semantics with the medium.

CONTACT in a room can mean physical adjacency.

CONTACT in a feed can mean one utterance arriving in relation to another without any bodily co-presence.

QUESTION:
Which recurrent media in a world require native relational grammars rather than projection into a universal ground plane?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Can one world remain coherent while different regions of it obey different spatial, temporal, and causal semantics?

SOURCE FORMALISM:
The Halfworld Protocol explicitly distinguishes surfaces from plans and uses isPlan:false for representations that should not inherit ground-plane semantics.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

RELATION(a,b | GROUND)
∈ {distance, facing, overlap, touch, path}

RELATION(a,b | SURFACE)
∈ {before, after, reply-to, visible-to, delayed-from, quoted-by}

TENSION:
READING A:
A screen still occupies physical space, so all surface relations can ultimately be rendered spatially.

READING B:
This confuses display geometry with semantic geometry. The pixels occupy space; the represented speakers need not.

MISSING:
A complete surface grammar for feed, letter, trail-camera footage, terminal output, voicemail, and bodiless household intelligence.

BOUNDARY:
Surface does not mean spaceless. It means that ground-plane bodily distance is not the primary relation organizing the represented event.
