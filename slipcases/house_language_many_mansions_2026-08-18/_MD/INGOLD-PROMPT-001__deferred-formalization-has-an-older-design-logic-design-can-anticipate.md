ID:
INGOLD-PROMPT-001

TITLE:
DEFERRED FORMALIZATION HAS AN OLDER DESIGN LOGIC: DESIGN CAN ANTICIPATE WITHOUT PRE-SPECIFYING THE FINAL FORM.

SOURCE:
Tim Ingold — Making: Anthropology, Archaeology, Art and Architecture — 2013 — Chapter 5, “The Sighted Watchmaker,” pp. 71–73.

PASSAGE:
[QUOTE]
“design is always a search for something that is unknown in advance”

[PARAPHRASE]
Ingold rejects the assumption that design must consist in specifying a finished form prior to implementation. Drawing can instead “break a trail”: the designer anticipates a future without determining it completely, leaving subsequent making to carry the process forward.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
ANTICIPATION WITHOUT COMPLETE SPECIFICATION.

LOCAL MOVE:
Ingold asks what becomes of design once DESIGN = PLAN BEFORE EXECUTION is abandoned. His answer is not to eliminate foresight; he distinguishes anticipatory orientation toward what might emerge from predictive representation of what must emerge.

SOURCE TERMS:
design; anticipation; future; open-ended; drawing; movement; gesture; search; unknown in advance; hopes and dreams

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
“The Prompt Is Not the Program” says computation can begin before specification is complete. Ingold changes the question: perhaps incompleteness is not merely TEMPORARY; successful making may require parts of the future to remain unresolved until one gets there.

QUESTION:
Which constraints are merely deferred because the user has not stated them yet, and which constraints are impossible to formulate until an artifact has actually been produced?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Does natural-language programming introduce deferred formalization, or does it make computational programming capable of a design temporality long familiar to skilled making?

MECHANISM:
A practitioner projects possibilities ahead without fixing a terminal form; action advances into that field; what becomes perceptible through action changes what can be anticipated next.

FORMAL SHIFT:

→ <PARTIALLY DETERMINED DIRECTION>

SOURCE FORMALISM:
NONE

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
The Prompt Is Not the Program: DESCRIBE → GENERATE → INSPECT → CORRECT → REGENERATE.
Ingoldian pressure: ANTICIPATE₀ → ACT₀ → PERCEIVE₁ → ANTICIPATE₁ → ACT₁ → PERCEIVE₂ → ... . CORRECTION assumes error relative to target; ANTICIPATION permits the target itself to develop.

TENSION:
Deferred formalization can preserve hidden hylomorphism if it assumes every ambiguity has a correct formal resolution waiting to be discovered. Ingold leaves open the stronger possibility that making generates new possibilities.

MISSING:
A distinction among UNSTATED CONSTRAINT, UNKNOWN CONSTRAINT, UNKNOWABLE-BEFORE-EXECUTION CONSTRAINT, EMERGENT POSSIBILITY.

BOUNDARY:
Ingold is not discussing LLMs, natural-language programming, or software specification; the connection is [OUR INFERENCE].

CITATION TRAIL:
[[THE PROMPT IS NOT THE PROGRAM]] → Ingold — Making, Chapter 5 → Juhani Pallasmaa → Lucy Suchman → Donald Schön → interactive/live programming.

TEST:
Take ten prompt-programming iterations and classify each new constraint as A known-before-generation but omitted, B discovered by seeing artifact, C created by a possibility the artifact introduced, D unresolved. If C exists, deferred formalization is not merely delayed specification.
