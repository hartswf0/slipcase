ID:
HOUSE-LANG-001

TITLE:
HEIDEGGER’S “HOUSE OF BEING” MAKES HUMANS GUARDIANS OF LANGUAGE, NOT BUILDERS THROUGH LANGUAGE.

SOURCE:
Martin Heidegger — “Letter on ‘Humanism’” — 1946; English in Pathmarks, 1998 — p. 239.

PASSAGE:
[QUOTE]
“Language is the house of being.”

RESEARCH OBJECT:
The direction of agency in the house/language metaphor.

LOCAL MOVE:
Heidegger places language between being and human dwelling: being comes to language; humans dwell in that house; thinkers and poets guard its manifestation rather than manufacture the house themselves.

SOURCE TERMS:
language; house of being; home; dwell; guardians; manifestation; accomplishment

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
“The House That Words Built” sounds Heideggerian while reversing Heidegger’s allocation of agency. The source gives something closer to BEING → LANGUAGE-AS-HOUSE → HUMAN DWELLING / GUARDIANSHIP.

QUESTION:
Would “The House That Words Built” be more interesting if treated as a deliberate inversion of Heidegger rather than an extension of him?

DEEPER QUESTION:
What historical or technical transformation would have to occur for language to move from the place in which humans dwell to an instrument that specifies, coordinates, or causes what they dwell in?

MECHANISM:
Being comes to language; thinkers and word-creators bring its manifestation to language and preserve it there.

FORMAL SHIFT:
<BEING>
→ <LANGUAGE AS HOUSE>
→ [BRING TO / PRESERVE IN LANGUAGE]
→ <HUMAN DWELLING>

SOURCE FORMALISM:
NONE

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
Heidegger: BEING → LANGUAGE → DWELLING
Provisional inversion: LANGUAGE → SPECIFICATION → BUILDING → DWELLING
Question: what makes the arrow reverse?

TENSION:
The “language becomes construction” thesis presses against Heidegger’s refusal to treat thinking simply as causal making.

MISSING:
A mechanism that moves from linguistic disclosure or preservation to physical production.

BOUNDARY:
Heidegger’s “house of being” does not establish that words physically construct houses, descriptions are executable specifications, or language has causal priority over building.

CITATION TRAIL:
Heidegger — “Building Dwelling Thinking”; Heidegger — “Language”; J. L. Austin — How to Do Things with Words; Robin Evans — “Translations from Drawing to Building”.

TEST:
Compare the operative verbs attached to language across Heidegger’s “Letter on Humanism,” “Language,” and “Building Dwelling Thinking”; code them as DISCLOSE, PRESERVE, DWELL, COMMAND, SPECIFY, CAUSE, CONSTRUCT.
