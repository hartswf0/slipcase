MANSION-003

TITLE:
THE FUNDAMENTAL SYNTAX OF THE SUBURBAN HOUSE MAY BE DEONTIC: MAY, MUST, SHALL, AND SHALL NOT.

SOURCE:
Federal Housing Administration, Underwriting Manual, revisions to February 1938. SOURCE URL: https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/title/underwriting-manual-6279/underwriting-manual-602319/fulltext

Shelley v. Kraemer, 334 U.S. 1 (1948). SOURCE URL: https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/334/1

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE] The FHA manual treated zoning and recorded restrictions as mechanisms governing building type, placement, design, use, subdivision, enforcement, and racial occupancy. Shelley v. Kraemer distinguishes private restrictive agreements from the state action involved in judicial enforcement.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
The suburb demonstrates that language can change a house without moving a wall. Deeds, covenants, zoning, codes, mortgages, and occupancy rules alter the set of futures legally or institutionally available to a parcel. The built environment is therefore partly a deontic machine.

LOCAL MOVE:
Represent each house not only by geometry but by a TRANSITION PERMISSION MAP: who may enter, remain, modify, sell, inherit, subdivide, rent, or claim.

SOURCE TERMS:
restriction; covenant; zoning; occupancy; enforcement; property; use; design; race

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
Two materially identical houses can be different operative houses because different sentences govern their possible futures.

QUESTION:
Is the deepest grammar of the suburb geometric or deontic?

DEEPER QUESTION:
What histories of exclusion disappear when computational architecture treats constraints as politically neutral “rules”?

MECHANISM:
TEXT + SCOPE + AUTHORITY + ENFORCEMENT + PERSISTENCE → ALLOWED/FORBIDDEN FUTURES.

FORMAL SHIFT:
FROM: HOUSE = PHYSICAL CONFIGURATION
TO: HOUSE = PHYSICAL CONFIGURATION + PERMITTED TRANSFORMATIONS.

SOURCE FORMALISM:
Legal and administrative instruments use explicit restrictions and enforcement mechanisms; no unified formal semantics is supplied by the sources.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
HOUSE = {states, allowed_transitions, forbidden_transitions, authorized_actors, enforcers}.

TENSION:
A software lock and a racial covenant may share a formal feature - constraints binding later actors - without moral, historical, or political equivalence. The comparison must increase scrutiny, not flatten stakes.

MISSING:
Contemporary HOA rules; zoning; building codes; insurance; mortgage conditions; accessibility law; tenant rights.

BOUNDARY:
Do not infer that text alone causes enforcement. Institutional recognition is load-bearing.

CITATION TRAIL:
FHA 1938 → restrictive covenants → Shelley → deontic architecture → permissions → AI constraints.

TEST:
For one parcel, collect governing texts and extract MAY/MUST/SHALL/PROHIBITED/REQUIRED. Map each statement to actor, enforcer, duration, and futures removed.
