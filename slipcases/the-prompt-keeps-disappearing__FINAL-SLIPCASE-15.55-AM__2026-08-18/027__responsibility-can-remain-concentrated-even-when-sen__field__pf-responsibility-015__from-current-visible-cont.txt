PF-RESPONSIBILITY-015

TITLE:
Responsibility can remain concentrated even when sentence production is distributed.

SOURCE:
Unattributed working manuscript — “Aphoristic Fragments,” fragments 23–25 — date not stated. fileciteturn0file0L306-L326

PASSAGE:
[QUOTE]
“The machine cannot absolve me.
This is easy to forget.
If the sentence is foolish, I cannot send the model to defend it.”

[QUOTE]
“Responsibility may survive where production does not.”

RESEARCH OBJECT:
Asymmetry between causal production and accountable endorsement.

LOCAL MOVE:
The manuscript separates responsibility from direct sentence production instead of trying to restore the human as sole producer.

SOURCE TERMS:
machine
absolve
sentence
defend
responsibility
production

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
Distributed production need not imply equivalently distributed accountability.

QUESTION:
Which operation transfers a generated sentence from machine output into something for which a person becomes answerable?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Is selection enough, publication enough, endorsement enough, or does responsibility depend on the institutional role from which the output is released?

MECHANISM:
A model contributes candidate material. A human actor selects, adopts, modifies, places, or publishes it within an accountable practice. Responsibility attaches through that act of uptake rather than through exclusive causal production.

FORMAL SHIFT:
<generated candidate>
→ <human uptake / endorsement / publication>
→ [enter accountable practice]
→ <responsibility attaches>

SOURCE FORMALISM:
NONE

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
PRODUCTION(x) and RESPONSIBILITY_FOR(x) are separable relations.

A possible transition:
MODEL_GENERATES(x)
→ PERSON_ENDORSES(x)
→ PERSON_BECOMES_ANSWERABLE_FOR(x)

TENSION:
Some systems operate with little meaningful human review; in such cases, assigning responsibility through “selection” may become fictive or institutionally engineered.

MISSING:
The threshold of meaningful uptake required before responsibility can fairly attach.

BOUNDARY:
The passage does not determine legal liability, moral responsibility in all cases, or whether models and organizations can bear other kinds of responsibility.

CITATION TRAIL:
Responsibility gaps; automation; editorial responsibility; publication ethics; principal-agent relations; human-in-the-loop governance.

TEST:
Compare cases with progressively weaker human uptake: authored revision, explicit selection, batch approval, automatic publication, autonomous generation. Ask where attribution of answerability changes and why.
