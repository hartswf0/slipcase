LIT-HUTCHINS-007

TITLE:
Distribution becomes explanatory only when the transformations that propagate across people and artifacts are specified.

SOURCE:
Edwin Hutchins — Cognition in the Wild — 1995 — Introduction; chapter 3 overview.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
Hutchins moves the cognitive unit of analysis beyond the individual and treats a ship-navigation team as a cognitive and computational system. His mechanism is not merely “many people contribute”: computation can be described as propagation of representational state across different media, including people and tools.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
Propagation of representational state across heterogeneous media.

LOCAL MOVE:
Hutchins supplies a mechanism for distribution rather than merely relocating cognition to a group.

SOURCE TERMS:
socially distributed cognition
unit of analysis
navigation team
computational system
propagation of representational state
media

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
Calling authorship “distributed” explains little unless we can say what moves between participants and what transformations occur at each handoff.

QUESTION:
What is the equivalent of Hutchins’s representational-state propagation in human–model writing?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Which transformations are constitutive of the eventual text rather than merely causal background?

MECHANISM:
Representational state
→ transformed in one medium/person/tool
→ propagated to another
→ transformed again
→ coordinated system-level accomplishment.

FORMAL SHIFT:
<INDIVIDUAL COGNIZER>
→ <PEOPLE + ARTIFACTS + REPRESENTATIONAL MEDIA>
→ [PROPAGATE / TRANSFORM STATE]
→ <SYSTEM-LEVEL ACCOMPLISHMENT>

SOURCE FORMALISM:
Hutchins describes navigation as computation and explicitly characterizes computation at the distributed scale as propagation of representational state across media.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
s₀
→ HUMAN(s₀)
→ PROMPT
→ MODEL(prompt)
→ OUTPUT
→ HUMAN_SELECT(output)
→ TEXT
is a candidate distributed trajectory only if the transformations themselves are analytically specified.

TENSION:
Distributed cognition is not distributed authorship. A system can possess distributed cognitive organization while credit, responsibility, ownership, and authorship remain differently allocated.

MISSING:
A theory connecting causal/representational contribution to authorship attribution.

BOUNDARY:
Hutchins provides neither an AI-authorship theory nor grounds for assigning authorship to every component of a cognitive system.

CITATION TRAIL:

TEST:
Trace one paragraph through prompts, retrieved notes, generations, selections, edits, and rearrangements. Record what representation enters and leaves every operation. Ask whether “distributed authorship” still adds explanatory value after the actual propagation graph is visible.
