ZETTEL

ID:
LIT-AUSTIN-009

TITLE:
Language did things before computers; what machines add is a new engineered regime of felicity.

SOURCE:
J. L. Austin — How to Do Things with Words — 1962 — Lectures I–III, especially Lecture II.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
Austin’s early performative analysis rejects the assumption that utterances merely report states of affairs. Some utterances participate in acts, but their success depends on conditions surrounding the utterance: an accepted procedure, appropriate persons and circumstances, proper execution, and other conditions whose violation produces infelicity.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
Felicity conditions as the apparatus of linguistic action.

LOCAL MOVE:
The causal or institutional force of words is relocated from the sentence alone to a sentence used under appropriate conditions.

SOURCE TERMS:
performative
procedure
circumstances
felicity
infelicity
act

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
“A machine in which describing has effects” may be one technical species of a much broader phenomenon: arrangements in which utterances acquire consequences because conditions around them make particular acts possible.

QUESTION:
What are the felicity conditions of a prompt?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Which prompt failures are failures of linguistic formulation, and which are infelicities of the surrounding apparatus—wrong tool, missing authority, unavailable state, absent referent, unsupported operation?

MECHANISM:
Utterance
+ recognized procedure
+ appropriate participants/circumstances
+ correct enactment
→ successful act.

FORMAL SHIFT:
<WORDS>
→ <WORDS + PROCEDURE + CIRCUMSTANCES>
→ [ENACT]
→ <SOCIAL / PRACTICAL CONSEQUENCE>

SOURCE FORMALISM:
Austin gives a taxonomy of felicity conditions and corresponding modes of infelicity; he does not provide computational syntax.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
PROMPT_EFFECT =
f(expression, interface, capabilities, permissions, state, model, tools)

TENSION:
Executing a software operation is not automatically an illocutionary act. Treating prompt execution as speech-act theory can collapse technical causation into social convention.

MISSING:
A criterion for determining when Austin’s apparatus genuinely explains a prompt interaction rather than serving as analogy.

BOUNDARY:
Austin supplies a genealogy for language-as-action, not a theory of machine execution or generative AI.

CITATION TRAIL:
Searle — Speech Acts.
Flores.
Winograd & Flores.
Institutional facts.
Human-computer command languages.

TEST:
Hold the same prompt sentence fixed while varying authorization, tool availability, selected object, interaction mode, and system capability. Classify which changes resemble Austinian felicity conditions and which require a different theory.
