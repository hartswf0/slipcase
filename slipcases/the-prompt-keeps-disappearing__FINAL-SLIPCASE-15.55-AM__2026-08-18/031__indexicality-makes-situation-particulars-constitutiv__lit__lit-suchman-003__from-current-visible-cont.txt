LIT-SUCHMAN-003

TITLE:
Indexicality makes situation particulars constitutive of intelligibility rather than supplementary context.

SOURCE:
Lucy A. Suchman — Plans and Situated Actions: The Problem of Human-Machine Communication — 1985 — p. 36.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
Suchman proposes that language stands in a generally indexical relation to the circumstances it presupposes, produces, and describes. Mutual intelligibility is achieved on particular occasions through recourse to situation particulars rather than guaranteed once and for all by a stable body of shared meanings.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
Occasion-specific achievement of intelligibility.

LOCAL MOVE:
Suchman moves meaning away from a self-sufficient symbolic message and into an interaction between language and circumstances.

SOURCE TERMS:
indexical
circumstances
situation particulars
mutual intelligibility
shared meanings

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
A richer prompt may not “contain more meaning.” It may make more situation particulars available for resolving an otherwise underdetermined act.

QUESTION:
What would it mean to treat prompt success as an achieved relation between utterance and situation rather than a property of the prompt?

DEEPER QUESTION:
When the “situation” presented to a model is itself a representation assembled by an interface, where does indexical grounding stop?

MECHANISM:
Utterance
→ interpreted relative to locally available particulars
→ interlocutors coordinate
→ intelligibility is contingently achieved.

FORMAL SHIFT:
<UTTERANCE>
→ <UTTERANCE-IN-SITUATION>
→ [RESOLVE THROUGH SITUATION PARTICULARS]
→ <LOCALLY ACHIEVED INTELLIGIBILITY>

SOURCE FORMALISM:
NONE

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
MEANING-IN-USE ≠ decode(symbols alone)

MEANING-IN-USE =
achievement(symbols, circumstances, interaction)

TENSION:
A context window can contain representations of circumstances without the model participating in those circumstances in Suchman’s sense.

MISSING:
The distinction between:
SHARED SITUATION
and
REPRESENTATION OF A SITUATION SUPPLIED TO A MODEL.

BOUNDARY:
Suchman is not proposing a theory of prompting, nor does her argument license calling model/user interaction mutually intelligible in the same sense as human interaction.

CITATION TRAIL:
Garfinkel.
Sacks, Schegloff, Jefferson.
Wittgenstein.
Clark & Wilkes-Gibbs.
Agre and Chapman.

TEST:
Hold a prompt string fixed while varying only accessible interaction history, selection state, visual scene, tool state, and prior actions. Identify which “meaning” changes are actually indexical resolutions.
