PF-FAILURE-005

TITLE:
Prompt failure can expose the hidden interpretation that successful output conceals.

SOURCE:
Unattributed working manuscript — “Aphoristic Fragments,” fragments 37–39 — date not stated. fileciteturn0file0L478-L498

PASSAGE:
[QUOTE]
“In prompting, an error can mean I have learned something about what I was trying to ask.
The failed prompt is sometimes more revealing than the successful one.
The successful prompt disappears into its result.
The failure leaves the machinery exposed.”

RESEARCH OBJECT:
Failure as an epistemic probe of an opaque language-system coupling.

LOCAL MOVE:
The manuscript changes failure from a deficit in execution into evidence about the operator’s request, the system’s interpretation, or their mismatch.

SOURCE TERMS:
error
failed prompt
successful prompt
machinery
revealing

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
Success may provide less information about the mechanism than failure.

QUESTION:
What exactly becomes observable when a prompt fails that remains invisible when it succeeds?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Could prompt research learn more from controlled breakdowns than from benchmark success rates?

MECHANISM:
Success collapses many possible interpretations into an acceptable result. Failure forces comparison between intended criterion and produced behavior, exposing otherwise latent distinctions.

FORMAL SHIFT:
<intended outcome + prompt>
→ <system interpretation>
→ [mismatch]
→ <new evidence about hidden constraints>

SOURCE FORMALISM:
NONE

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
FAILURE = observed difference between operator criterion and system output
DIAGNOSTIC VALUE = information gained about that difference

TENSION:
Failure can also be uninformative noise. The manuscript does not yet distinguish diagnostically productive failure from stochastic or irrelevant failure.

MISSING:
A typology of failure modes and a way to identify which component of the interaction produced each one.

BOUNDARY:
The passage licenses “sometimes more revealing,” not the stronger claim that failure is generally superior to success as evidence.

CITATION TRAIL:
Breakdown in phenomenology and HCI; debugging; error analysis; adversarial testing; falsification; critical incidents.

TEST:
For a fixed task, preserve successful and failed generations plus revisions. Code which latent constraints became articulable only after each failure.
