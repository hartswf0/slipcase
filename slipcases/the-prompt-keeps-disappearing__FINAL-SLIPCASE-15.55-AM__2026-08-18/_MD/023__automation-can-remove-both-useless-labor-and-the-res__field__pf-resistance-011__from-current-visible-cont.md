TITLE:
Automation can remove both useless labor and the resistance through which a problem becomes thinkable.

SOURCE:
Unattributed working manuscript — “Aphoristic Fragments,” fragments 77–86 — date not stated. fileciteturn0file0L894-L982

PASSAGE:
[QUOTE]
“The machine can save me from the labor that was merely labor.
It can also save me from the labor in which the thought would have appeared.
I cannot know in advance which kind I am avoiding.”

[QUOTE]
“Now there is always a way around.
This changes the ethics of attention.”

RESEARCH OBJECT:
Epistemically productive resistance.

LOCAL MOVE:
The manuscript abandons a simple efficiency account. Difficulty is divided into labor that obstructs thought and labor through which thought emerges.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
Friction cannot be classified as waste merely because a tool can eliminate it.

QUESTION:
Which forms of writing difficulty are incidental costs, and which function as epistemic operators?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Can a writing system preserve productive resistance without fetishizing inefficiency or suffering?

MECHANISM:
A difficult passage forces prolonged confrontation with unresolved choices. During that forced duration, distinctions, objections, or formulations can emerge. Automation can shorten the duration before those transformations occur.

FORMAL SHIFT:

→ <sustained encounter OR automated bypass>
→ [continued discrimination OR premature continuation]

SOURCE FORMALISM:
NONE

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
FRICTION has at least two classes:
F₁ = cost with little epistemic yield
F₂ = constraint that produces epistemic change

Automation cannot safely remove F₂ unless it can distinguish F₂ from F₁.

TENSION:
The manuscript itself concedes that suspicion of easy writing is “sometimes correct,” not universally correct. Difficulty can also waste attention and preserve arbitrary gatekeeping.

MISSING:
Observable criteria for distinguishing productive resistance from merely expensive labor before the work is completed.

BOUNDARY:
The passage does not license the claim that harder writing is better writing or that manual production is intellectually superior.

CITATION TRAIL:
Dewey on inquiry and problematic situations; Schön on reflection-in-action; desirable difficulties; craft studies; HCI research on friction; automation bias.

TEST:
Run matched writing tasks in which some difficult operations are automated and others remain manual. Compare not merely output quality but which new distinctions, revisions, and questions arise during production.
