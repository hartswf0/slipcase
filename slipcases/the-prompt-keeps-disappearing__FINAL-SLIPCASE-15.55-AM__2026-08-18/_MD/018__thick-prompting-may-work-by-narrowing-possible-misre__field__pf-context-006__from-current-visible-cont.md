TITLE:
Thick prompting may work by narrowing possible misreadings rather than transferring more meaning.

SOURCE:
Unattributed working manuscript — “Aphoristic Fragments,” fragments 44–47 — date not stated. fileciteturn0file0L548-L580

PASSAGE:
[QUOTE]
“Thick description interests me because it appears to add words.
Its real achievement may be the opposite.
It makes visible how much meaning was already outside the sentence.”

RESEARCH OBJECT:
Context construction as reduction of interpretive degrees of freedom.

LOCAL MOVE:
The manuscript turns “thickness” inside out. Added description is not treated primarily as additional semantic payload but as reconstruction of surroundings required to discriminate among interpretations.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
More words may matter because they remove plausible interpretations rather than because they encode the intended interpretation directly.

QUESTION:
Does a detailed prompt succeed by specifying the desired output, or by eliminating enough neighboring interpretations that the desired one becomes difficult to miss?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Could prompt “context” be measured as constraint on a space of possible interpretations rather than as token quantity?

MECHANISM:
Additional contextual material establishes distinctions among otherwise compatible readings; those distinctions narrow the system’s viable continuations.

FORMAL SHIFT:

SOURCE FORMALISM:

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
CONTEXT does not simply add CONTENT.
CONTEXT constrains POSSIBLE INTERPRETATIONS.

TENSION:
Excess context can itself introduce new ambiguities, distract the model, or overconstrain useful variation. “Thicker” is therefore not monotonically better.

MISSING:
A way to distinguish context that eliminates unwanted readings from context that merely adds textual volume.

BOUNDARY:
The passage does not claim that machine interpretation reproduces Geertzian thick description, only that the comparison opens a question about what surrounding information does.

CITATION TRAIL:
Clifford Geertz on thick description and wink/twitch; pragmatics; common ground; Bayesian constraint; specification by example.

TEST:
Hold the target constant while constructing prompt variants that add equal numbers of tokens but differ in whether they eliminate plausible rival readings. Compare effects.
