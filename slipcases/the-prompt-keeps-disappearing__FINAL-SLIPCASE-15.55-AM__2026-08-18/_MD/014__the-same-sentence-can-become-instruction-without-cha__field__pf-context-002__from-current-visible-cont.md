TITLE:
The same sentence can become instruction without changing its grammar.

SOURCE:
Unattributed working manuscript — “Aphoristic Fragments,” fragments 3–5 — date not stated. fileciteturn0file0L74-L108

PASSAGE:
[QUOTE]
“I type:
Make the house remember.
The machine does something.
Before this, the sentence might have belonged to a poem.
Now I have to wonder whether it was also an instruction.
Nothing changed in the sentence.
The world around the sentence changed.”

RESEARCH OBJECT:
Operational role without syntactic mutation.

LOCAL MOVE:
The manuscript shifts the unit of analysis from sentence form to the environment in which the sentence is used.

SOURCE TERMS:
sentence
poem
instruction
world around the sentence
door
describe
build
appear

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
An instruction may not be identifiable from linguistic form alone.

QUESTION:
If the words remain identical while their practical role changes, where should the distinction between description and instruction be located?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Should prompt research classify strings, or classify situated language-system events?

MECHANISM:
The same linguistic object enters different arrangements. Each arrangement supplies different possible responses, making different uses of the string available.

FORMAL SHIFT:
<same sentence>
→ <sentence + changed surrounding system>
→ [different response becomes available]
→ <different practical role>

SOURCE FORMALISM:
NONE

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
ROLE(sentence) ≠ intrinsic_property(sentence)
ROLE(sentence) = function(sentence, surrounding arrangement)

TENSION:
Any attempt to define prompts by imperative grammar, syntax, or lexical markers would fail on the manuscript’s example.

MISSING:
A taxonomy of the environmental variables that constitute “the world around the sentence.”

BOUNDARY:
The passage does not show that syntax never matters. It shows only that syntax cannot by itself explain the observed difference.

CITATION TRAIL:
Wittgenstein on use; indexicality and pragmatics; speech-act felicity conditions; interface studies of mode and context.

TEST:
Construct a corpus containing identical strings used as poetry, conversation, search query, image-generation prompt, executable natural-language command, and quoted text. Test whether syntax alone can distinguish their operative roles.
