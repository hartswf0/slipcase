TITLE:
Perfect provenance can document production without deciding authorship.

SOURCE:
Unattributed working manuscript — “The Unsaid” and fragments 20–25 — date not stated. fileciteturn0file0L23-L43 fileciteturn0file0L272-L326

PASSAGE:
[QUOTE]
“There is a point beyond which provenance does not settle authorship.”

[QUOTE]
“A receipt proves that a transaction occurred.
It does not prove who desired the object.”

RESEARCH OBJECT:
The inferential gap between process trace and responsibility/authorship judgment.

LOCAL MOVE:
The manuscript pressure-tests its own attraction to “prompt receipts.” Complete process documentation is separated from the normative or conceptual judgment the documentation is supposed to settle.

SOURCE TERMS:
provenance

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
More complete traces do not necessarily converge on a unique answer to “Who wrote it?”

QUESTION:
What questions can provenance answer reliably, and which questions require criteria not contained in the provenance record?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Are disclosure systems being asked to solve a classification problem that no amount of logging can solve without a prior theory of authorship?

MECHANISM:
A provenance archive records events and actors. An authorship judgment requires a rule mapping those events onto an attribution category. The trace does not itself supply that rule.

FORMAL SHIFT:
<production events>
→ <provenance record>
→ [apply attribution criterion]
→ <authorship judgment>

SOURCE FORMALISM:
NONE

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
TRACE ≠ VERDICT

VERDICT = ATTRIBUTION_RULE(TRACE)

TENSION:
A trace may still distinguish rival authorship claims once an attribution criterion exists. The manuscript’s argument is against provenance as self-interpreting evidence, not against provenance itself.

MISSING:
Explicit rival attribution rules and cases in which they assign different authorship judgments to the same trace.

BOUNDARY:
The passage does not imply that provenance is useless or that authorship judgments are arbitrary.

CITATION TRAIL:
Genetic criticism; provenance standards; scholarly attribution; legal notions of authorship and responsibility; data lineage; audit logs.

TEST:
Create one complete human-model production trace. Give the identical trace to evaluators using different operational definitions of authorship. Measure where judgments diverge despite identical evidence.
