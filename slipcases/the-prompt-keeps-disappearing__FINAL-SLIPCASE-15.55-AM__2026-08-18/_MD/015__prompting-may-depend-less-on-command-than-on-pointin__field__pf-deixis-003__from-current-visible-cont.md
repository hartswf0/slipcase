TITLE:
Prompting may depend less on command than on pointing into already-constructed state.

SOURCE:
Unattributed working manuscript — “Aphoristic Fragments,” fragments 7–9 — date not stated. fileciteturn0file0L128-L158

PASSAGE:
[QUOTE]
“Here.
That one.
Again.
Like before.
A great deal of prompting consists of words that would be almost meaningless if nothing had already happened.
The prompt leans on a world.”

RESEARCH OBJECT:
Deictic dependence on accumulated interaction state.

LOCAL MOVE:
The manuscript replaces the command as the obvious model of prompting with deixis: expressions whose function depends on an already-present situation.

SOURCE TERMS:
here
that one
again
like before
deixis
finger
world

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The informationally poorest prompt may perform substantial work when the surrounding interaction history is rich.

QUESTION:
How much of a prompt’s operative content can reside outside the prompt string?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Does prompting become more deictic as systems acquire persistent visual, conversational, spatial, and procedural state?

MECHANISM:
Previous interaction constructs a field of candidate referents and expectations. A low-information expression selects or modifies something within that field.

FORMAL SHIFT:

TENSION:
Prompt engineering often treats each prompt as a self-contained textual specification. These examples become unintelligible under that assumption.

MISSING:
A representation of conversational history, selected objects, spatial context, prior outputs, and other possible deictic anchors.

BOUNDARY:
The passage does not establish that deixis is the essence of prompting. It explicitly proposes it only as a potentially closer comparison than command.

CITATION TRAIL:
Deixis; indexicality; demonstratives; common ground; conversation analysis; graphical direct manipulation; multimodal reference resolution.

TEST:
Compare task performance after progressively deleting prior conversational and visual state while leaving “that one,” “again,” and “like before” unchanged.
