ZETTEL

ID:
PF-CRITERIA-013

TITLE:
A criterion can govern revision before the operator can state it propositionally.

SOURCE:
Unattributed working manuscript — “Aphoristic Fragments,” fragments 40–43 — date not stated. fileciteturn0file0L502-L544

PASSAGE:
[QUOTE]
“The inability to state the criterion does not mean there is no criterion.
A musician stops the rehearsal and says, ‘No.’
Everyone in the room may understand.
No metric appears.”

RESEARCH OBJECT:
Operative but unformalized evaluative criteria.

LOCAL MOVE:
The manuscript refuses the inference from “cannot state the rule” to “no rule-like discrimination exists.”

SOURCE TERMS:
criterion
musician
No
metric
taste
leave unwritten
dissertation

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
A decision procedure may be behaviorally stable enough to guide revision while remaining unavailable as an explicit specification.

QUESTION:
How can prompt research study reliable evaluative discrimination without pretending that the criterion has already been formalized?

DEEPER QUESTION:
What is lost when tacit evaluative competence is converted into an explicit rubric for the sake of reproducibility?

MECHANISM:
The operator encounters an output, discriminates acceptance from rejection, and modifies subsequent action despite lacking a complete verbal account of the discrimination.

FORMAL SHIFT:
<encountered output>
→ <tacit evaluative discrimination>
→ [accept / reject / revise]
→ <changed trajectory>

SOURCE FORMALISM:
NONE

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
EVALUATE(x) → {KEEP, REJECT, MODIFY}
while
explicit_definition(EVALUATE) = incomplete

TENSION:
Unstated criteria are difficult to audit, teach, reproduce, or distinguish from inconsistency and post hoc rationalization.

MISSING:
Evidence that the criterion is stable across cases rather than reconstructed after each judgment.

BOUNDARY:
The passage does not show that taste is ineffable in principle or that measurement necessarily destroys it.

CITATION TRAIL:
Tacit knowledge; connoisseurship; reflective practice; expert judgment; preference learning; aesthetic judgment.

TEST:
Collect a long sequence of accept/reject judgments before asking the operator to explain them. Test whether a classifier or later articulation can predict earlier decisions and identify systematic residuals.
