TITLE:
SYSTEM AND PROCESS BEFORE PRODUCT: interactive digital narrative already relocates analysis from a fixed text to the user-system process.

SOURCE:
Hartmut Koenitz - "Towards a Theoretical Framework for Interactive Digital Narrative" - ICIDS 2010 - pp. 176-185.
SOURCE URL: https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-16638-9_22

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE] Koenitz argues that applying theories of non-digital narrative to interactive narrative is problematic because digital affordances alter their assumptions. His framework foregrounds the digital system and the process of user interaction over a product-centered view inherited from legacy media.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
SYSTEM-PROCESS FOREGROUNDING

LOCAL MOVE:
Correct the genealogy of the 360/chat analogy. The important move is not that chat suddenly turns a fixed text into an interaction. IDN theory already treats narrative as a system and process. The new question is what becomes newly difficult when natural language turns the user's contribution into an open semantic address.

SOURCE TERMS:
interactive digital narrative
system

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The paper's 360 comparison works best as a narrow account of where selection is relocated, not as a general claim that older media theory sees narratives only as fixed products.

QUESTION:
What does open natural-language address add to a system-process account of interactive narrative?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Does conversational generation require a representation of multiple consequence jurisdictions because one utterance can enter several parts of the system at once?

SOURCE FORMALISM:
Koenitz proposes system and process as foregrounded analytic terms and introduces protostory, narrative design, and narrative vectors as concepts for interactive storytelling theory.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
Conversation adds a high-dimensional address channel to the user-system process. The analytic problem becomes not only which narrative vector is followed, but which internal jurisdiction is permitted to change when the utterance is interpreted.

TENSION:
The jurisdictional model may be expressible inside existing system-process theory without requiring a new umbrella concept. Negotiated consequence must therefore remain a local analytic tool unless further cases show that existing vocabulary obscures a recurrent distinction.

MISSING:
A closer reading of Koenitz's later account of narrative vectors, system, process, and instantiation against the Cass transcript.

BOUNDARY:
Koenitz does not discuss LLMs, generative conversational characters, or the Cass system.
