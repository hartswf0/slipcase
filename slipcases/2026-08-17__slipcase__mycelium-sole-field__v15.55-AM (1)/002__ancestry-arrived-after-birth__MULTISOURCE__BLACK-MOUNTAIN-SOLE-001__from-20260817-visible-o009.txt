ID:
BLACK-MOUNTAIN-SOLE-001

TITLE:
Black Mountain borrowed the SOLE name before it inherited the Black Mountain lineage.

SOURCE:
Nora Caplan-Bricker — “This North Carolina Campus Was Meant to Show Off the Future of Online Education” — The New Republic — December 23, 2013.
Sugata Mitra — How to Bring Self-Organised Learning Environments to Your Community: SOLE Toolkit — 2015.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE] Dave Dobias recalled that he and Mark Hunter decided to call their project a “SOLE” after encountering Sugata Mitra through a TED talk. He also said he discovered only after arranging the campus site that the property had once hosted Black Mountain College, describing the historical connection as “synchronicity.”

RESEARCH OBJECT:
The proposed genealogy

Black Mountain College
→ revived through Mitra's SOLE
→ Black Mountain SOLE

has its chronology backwards according to the founder account reported in 2013.

The SOLE reference entered during project conception through Mitra.

The Black Mountain College lineage entered afterward through discovery of the site's history.

This changes “historical radicalism” from an originating design input into a retrospectively available ancestry.

LOCAL MOVE:
Caplan-Bricker reconstructs the project's sequence of adoption:

Dobias and Hunter consume TED talks
→ encounter Mitra
→ choose the term SOLE
→ build the project
→ rent the Blue Ridge Assembly site
→ discover the historical Black Mountain College connection.

The article thereby supplies exactly the chronological receipt needed to discriminate deliberate genealogy from retrospective resemblance.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
“Black Mountain” looks like the deepest historical layer of Black Mountain SOLE because it is in the institution's name.

But in the reported design chronology, it may be one of the latest conceptual layers to arrive.

The apparent ancestry is produced by the finished institution rather than faithfully recording the sequence by which the institution was made.

QUESTION:
How often does an institution's eventual name manufacture a genealogy that was absent from the decisions that originally produced it?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Once an accidental resemblance becomes narratively useful, can it begin exerting real causal force on later versions of the institution even though it had no causal role in the first version?

MECHANISM:
Mitra TED talk
→ adoption of “SOLE”
→ project formation

separately:

site selection / rental
→ discovery of Black Mountain College history
→ interpretation as meaningful “synchronicity”
→ historical lineage becomes available for subsequent identity-making.

The ancestry can therefore become operative after the artifact already exists.

SOURCE FORMALISM:

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

Ordinary genealogy claim:

ancestor A
→ influences design D
→ produces institution I

Reported sequence:

Mitra M
→ influences design D
→ institution/site I
→ discovery of ancestor A
→ reinterpret(I | A)

Therefore:

A ↛ D₀

but potentially:

A → narrative(I₀)
→ D₁, D₂, ...

An ancestry can be causally false at t₀ yet become causally true at t₁ if the retrospective story begins steering later institutional decisions.

TENSION:
There are now two distinguishable questions that should not be collapsed.

GENESIS:
Did Black Mountain College cause the initial design of Black Mountain SOLE?

The 2013 founder account weighs strongly against this reading.

AFTERLIFE:
Did discovery of Black Mountain College subsequently alter Black Mountain SOLE's practices, rhetoric, recruitment, curriculum, spatial identity, or understanding of itself?

The article does not settle this.

A retrospective genealogy can still become an operative genealogy.

MISSING:
The pre-launch design archive.

Needed receipts include:

earliest project names,
planning documents,
emails between Dobias and Hunter,
fundraising decks,
501(c)(3) materials,
site-search documents,
early website snapshots,
advertisements,
ReinventEd materials,
and dated references to Black Mountain College.

Also missing is evidence showing which components of Mitra's actual SOLE protocol were knowingly imported.

The Black Mountain program described by Caplan-Bricker does not obviously reproduce Mitra's tightly structured Big Question → Investigation → Review loop.

BOUNDARY:
The New Republic article supports a chronology based on Dobias's reported recollection and the journalist's observation.

It is strong evidence against claiming that Black Mountain College's historical pedagogy originally motivated the project.

It is not enough to prove that no organizer encountered Black Mountain College earlier, nor that its history had no influence after the site was chosen.
