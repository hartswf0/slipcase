The P.E.A.C.E. Process is a five-step method that Matthew Abrams teaches in his new book Inviting Genius: How Good Leaders Use Conflict to Become Great, which comes out on August 4th. “It helps leaders move from avoiding conflict to navigating it confidently and productively”, shares Matthew Abrams with CEOWORLD magazine.

Q: Conflict is uncomfortable but also inevitable. How do you approach conflict in your new book?

Matthew Abrams: In Inviting Genius, I teach a five-step method called the P.E.A.C.E. Process, inspired by Marshall Rosenberg’s work in Nonviolent Communication. It helps leaders move from avoiding conflict to navigating it confidently and productively.

P — Pursue Alignment: Identify a shared objective both people care about.
E — Extract the Facts: Establish the observable, indisputable facts that created the tension.
A — Assess the Story & Emotions: Share the narrative you’ve created around those facts and the emotions that come with it.
C — Compassionately Spar: Explore perspectives with curiosity rather than defensiveness. The goal isn’t to “win” but to understand the full context.
E — Express the Needs & Make a Request: Identify the unmet human need beneath the conflict and make a clear, forward-looking request.
This method shifts conflict into collaboration and, ultimately, deeper relationships and greater results.

Q: What are some of the main challenges leaders face when confronting conflict?

Matthew Abrams: The biggest challenge leaders face in conflict is the perceived threat to their identity. Many leaders believe they’re supposed to be the ones who have it all figured out. That belief makes it hard to do two essential things:

Admit when they’re uncertain or have made a mistake, and
Speak up when they see things differently than others.
Our sense of who we think we should be often dictates how we show up, or more often, how we hold back. Conflict is inherently disruptive. And although it’s the sandbox for innovation, alignment, and deeper relationships, many leaders experience it as a threat to the very outcomes they’re trying to achieve.

Q: What’s next after a conflict? Do you have any post-conflict advice for leaders?

Matthew Abrams: Absolutely. The post-conflict phase is one of the greatest leadership opportunities available.

Once a conflict is resolved and we’ve shifted out of our emotional and survival brain – the amygdala and limbic system – back into our neocortex – the thinking brain – we can engage in a powerful practice: thinking about our thinking.

Q: How does your work as an EOS Implementer align with Inviting Genius?

Matthew Abrams: EOS is an exceptional operating system for businesses. It clarifies roles, focuses priorities, strengthens accountability, aligns vision, and drives execution. It brings structure and simplicity to organizational complexity.

Inviting Genius is the operating system for the humans inside that structure.
