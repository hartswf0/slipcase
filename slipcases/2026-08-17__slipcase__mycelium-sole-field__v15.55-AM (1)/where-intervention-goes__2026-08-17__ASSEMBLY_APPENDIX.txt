THESIS TO TEST, NOT ASSUME
The phrase “minimal intervention” risks measuring only visible answer-giving. In the available sources, direct instruction can recede while intervention migrates into infrastructure, timing, curricular selection, epistemic role, and the threshold of participation. The paper should test whether “self-organized learning” is better analyzed as local learner autonomy inside a deliberately maintained field of conditions rather than as the absence of pedagogical intervention.

STRONGEST EVIDENCE PATH
1. Mitra & Rana 2001: the kiosk was introduced without ordinary instruction, yet it was remotely monitored, settings were changed, shortcuts were removed, and the environment was actively maintained. This establishes a distinction between direct instruction and infrastructural intervention.
2. Mitra & Rana 2001: when discovery saturated, an external “seed” discovery was introduced to restart a spiral of exploration. This establishes temporal/novelty intervention rather than continuous teaching.
3. Mitra & Dangwal 2010: curricular agenda can remain at the planning level while learners encounter curiosity-provoking material without being told it is curriculum. This establishes a separation between learner-level autonomy and planner-level objective selection.
4. Mitra & Dangwal 2010: the mediator was instructed to encourage rather than teach, including asking children to explain things to her. This shows intervention through epistemic role and affect rather than answer supply.
5. Mitra & Rana 2001: adult nonusers did not enter the same exploratory cycle. This shows that self-organization has a threshold condition; public access is not the same as practical invitation.

STRONGEST RESISTANCE / COUNTERARGUMENT
Do not argue that self-organized learning is “fake,” secretly conventional teaching, or causally explained by infrastructure. The sources do support meaningful learner initiative and peer learning. The paper’s claim is analytic: intervention is multidimensional and distributed. Maintenance, mediation, and design are not automatically equivalent to instruction, and the available evidence does not establish which layer caused learning outcomes.

STRANGE STEPPING STONE
Mitra & Dangwal 2010 explicitly and speculatively connect their intervention to a cellular-automata “vision of the future.” Do not present the analogy as established cognitive science. Use it only to derive a sharper experimental distinction between:
- NOVELTY INJECTION: a seed that opens a new region of possibility.
- ATTRACTOR INJECTION: a future image that may orient activity toward a possible state while leaving the route unspecified.
The 2006 paper’s technical mechanism remains unresolved because its body is not locally preserved.

PAPER ARGUMENT
Develop the argument as a sequence, not a card-by-card catalogue:
A. The usual scalar picture (“more teaching / less teaching”) is analytically weak.
B. The sources reveal intervention moving to other layers.
C. These layers can be represented as an intervention vector: direct answer supply, infrastructure, objective selection, temporal perturbation, epistemic role, and entry threshold.
D. This vector creates testable research designs and clarifies where autonomy and governance coexist.
E. Self-organization is therefore not a vacuum; it is an emergent process whose conditions can be designed, maintained, and unequally entered.
