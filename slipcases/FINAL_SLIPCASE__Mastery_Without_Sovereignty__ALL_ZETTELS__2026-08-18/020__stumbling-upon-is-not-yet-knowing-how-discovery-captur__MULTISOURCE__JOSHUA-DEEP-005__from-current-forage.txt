RESEARCH OBJECT:
A GENERATIVE ACCIDENT CAN BE RECOGNIZED AS VALUABLE WITHOUT BECOMING A REPRODUCIBLE TECHNIQUE.

LOCAL MOVE:
Joshua’s unrelated comic-explosion experiment produces a character he values, yet he cannot reliably recover the aesthetic. Makri and Blandford sharpen the sequence by treating serendipity as more than chance: unexpectedness, insight, projected value, and exploitation are distinct moments. Joshua’s case exposes a further split needed for generative practice: discovery, capture, and control.

SOURCE TERMS:
“rabbit hole” · “stumble upon” · “unrelated task” · “ephemeral” · “enigmatic” · recreate · serendipity · exploit connection

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The artifact proves that a possibility existed once, but not that the practitioner possesses the operation that reaches it. A creative discovery can therefore be evidentially real and procedurally unavailable.

QUESTION:
What must be preserved at the moment of a generative surprise for the surprise to become a reusable part of practice?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Is craft constituted by the capacity to produce valuable accidents, recognize them, reproduce them, or build a lineage from them—and which of those capacities can exist without the others?

MECHANISM:
UNRELATED SEARCH → UNEXPECTED OUTPUT → RECOGNIZE CONNECTION / VALUE → ATTEMPT EXPLOITATION → EITHER CAPTURE REPRODUCIBLE CONDITIONS OR LOSE THE PHENOMENON.

FORMAL SHIFT:
DISCOVERY = TECHNIQUE → DISCOVERY ≠ CAPTURE ≠ CONTROL.

SOURCE FORMALISM:
[PARAPHRASE]
Makri and Blandford derive a process model in which serendipitous encounters combine unexpectedness and insight, potential value is projected, and actions are taken to exploit the connection.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
D=recognize valuable unexpected outcome; C=preserve sufficient generating state; R=repeat family resemblance. D can be true while C or R are false.

TENSION:
Demanding reproducibility may erase the value of singular creative events; refusing reproducibility may make it impossible to distinguish technique from lucky sampling.

MISSING:
The exact prompt, seed, model version, remix lineage, and failed reproduction attempts for Joshua’s “ephemeral” character.

BOUNDARY:
Serendipity research concerns information encounters rather than stochastic image generation. The distinction between discovery, capture, and control is our extension.

CITATION TRAIL:
[[CALLSHOT-FIELD-003]] → inability to lock in aesthetics → Joshua’s rabbit-hole character → Makri & Blandford serendipity process → split discovery from capture and control.

TEST:
For each surprising output in a live session, immediately preserve complete state. Ask the creator to reproduce a recognizable family 24 hours later from (A) memory, (B) prompt only, (C) full lineage/state. Measure which information converts discovery into reproducible craft.
