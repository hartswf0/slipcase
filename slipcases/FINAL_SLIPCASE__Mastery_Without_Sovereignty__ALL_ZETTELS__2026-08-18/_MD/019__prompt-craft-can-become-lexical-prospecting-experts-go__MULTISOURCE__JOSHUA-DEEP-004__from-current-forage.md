RESEARCH OBJECT:
PROMPT EXPERTISE CAN REQUIRE LEARNING VOCABULARIES FOR THEIR MODEL EFFECTS, NOT FOR THEIR ORDINARY COMMUNICATIVE NECESSITY.

LOCAL MOVE:
Joshua independently describes searching for terms tied to photographic domains and artist references. Chang et al. find a broader community practice of acquiring specialized domain vocabulary. The prompt engineer therefore resembles a lexical prospector: searching other practices for words with useful learned visual consequences.

SOURCE TERMS:
“specialized vocabulary” · architectural blogs · “source data” · artist references · distinctive outputs · glitches

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
A word can become valuable because it is obscure to ordinary speakers but dense with useful visual associations for the model. Expertise may increase by becoming less conversational and more archaeologically attentive to caption cultures.

QUESTION:
Which external vocabularies become disproportionately valuable in prompt practice, and what properties make them high-yield?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Are prompt communities rediscovering the latent taxonomies embedded in image-caption corpora by experimentally mining the language attached to visual production domains?

MECHANISM:
DOMAIN PRACTICE → SPECIALIZED TERM → TERM ENTERED INTO MODEL → DISTINCTIVE RESPONSE → TERM RETAINED / SHARED → NEW PROMPT LEXICON.

FORMAL SHIFT:
BETTER PROMPTING = BETTER WRITING → BETTER PROMPTING MAY INCLUDE BETTER VOCABULARY PROSPECTING.

SOURCE FORMALISM:
[PARAPHRASE]
Chang et al. report interviews, observations, and survey evidence from prolific text-to-image creators, including deliberate search for specialized vocabulary and exploitation of glitches.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
For domain lexicon L, estimate prompt yield Y(t) for t∈L. Lexical prospecting seeks terms with unusually high Y relative to ordinary-language frequency or descriptive obviousness.

TENSION:
Specialized terms may work because they address useful learned associations, but their community prestige can also create ritual vocabulary whose causal contribution is small.

MISSING:
Longitudinal traces from external source vocabulary → first prompt use → replicated effect → community diffusion.

BOUNDARY:
Chang et al. document community practice after Joshua’s interview; this is convergence, not evidence that Joshua influenced those participants or vice versa.

CITATION TRAIL:

TEST:
Collect vocabulary sourced from photography, architecture, cinema, printing, materials, and museum catalogs. Blind-test matched specialized and plain-language terms across models; measure effect size, uniqueness, portability, and community diffusion.
