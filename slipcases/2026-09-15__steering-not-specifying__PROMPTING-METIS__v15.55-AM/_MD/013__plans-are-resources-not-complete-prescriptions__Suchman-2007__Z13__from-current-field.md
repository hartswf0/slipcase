TITLE: Plans are resources for situated action, not complete prescriptions
ORIGINAL ID: Z13
TYPE: NEW ZETTEL

CLAIM
A plan can project and account for action without prescribing every detail of situated conduct.

EVIDENCE
Suchman contrasts a planning view, in which plans prescribe action at every level, with situated action, where the prescriptive significance of intentions is inherently vague and practical activity supplies the rest.

TENSION
Rejecting complete prescription does not imply that plans are irrelevant; they remain practical resources within action.

QUESTION
When is a prompt functioning as a plan, and when is it functioning as a situated move inside a larger activity?
