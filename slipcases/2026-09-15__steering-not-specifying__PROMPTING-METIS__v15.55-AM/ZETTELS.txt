TITLE: Textual identity is not operational identity
ORIGINAL ID: Z01
TYPE: NEW ZETTEL

CLAIM
The visible string can remain unchanged while its operational identity changes because the model, tools, context, interface, and world around it change.

EVIDENCE
User observation: “the prompt lacks stability and changes with different models.” The Bijker analogy treats this not merely as noisy performance but as a sign that the technical object called a prompt has not yet stabilized.

TENSION
Prompt benchmarking often treats the string as the independent variable, while actual operation depends on a larger configuration.

QUESTION
What should count as the same prompt when the inscription is identical but the available operations differ?

TITLE: Responsibility without sovereignty
ORIGINAL ID: Z07
TYPE: NEW ZETTEL

CLAIM
Agency in uncertain systems occupies a middle ground between total authorship and total absolution: actors can be answerable for what they do within conditions they did not choose.

EVIDENCE
The user formulates the distinction as chance = conditions not chosen, consequence = what follows from action within them, and mêtis = the capacity to tell the difference and act anyway. TENSION
Responsibility cannot be assigned solely by subjective intention once downstream effects become visible and further action remains possible.

TITLE: WORLDTEXT is a feedback condition, not a one-way word-to-world arrow
ORIGINAL ID: Z16
TYPE: NEW ZETTEL

CLAIM
When descriptions can alter code, media, simulations, tools, or material processes, language enters a feedback loop in which the changed world becomes the condition for the next description.

EVIDENCE
The field converges on repeated structures of description, interpretation, operation, consequence, perception, and renewed description. House of Dust supplies a historical score-to-world case; Put That There supplies situated executable reference; contemporary generative systems compress more steps into the same interface.

TENSION
“Words make worlds” is too strong if it hides the interpreters, infrastructures, permissions, materials, and failures that mediate realization.

QUESTION
How can WORLDTEXT be operationalized without attributing causal power to language alone?
