TITLE: Commitment needs a party who can be held, so the design account it grounds cannot survive a substrate-agnostic operator

SOURCE: PROGRAMS/dac.json — Design After Cognitivism v1.0, <THESIS> and <CORE_PROBLEM>, after Winograd and Flores

PASSAGE: [QUOTE] "<computer> is not <mind>. <AI output> is not <understanding>. <language> is not <information transfer>. <design> is not <interface decoration>. <design> is <ontological intervention> in <human practice>." [QUOTE] "[rebuild] <computational design> as <accountable coordination of commitments, breakdowns, repairs, and responsibilities>." [QUOTE] "<cognitivism> [misdescribes] <human cognition> as <internal symbol manipulation>. <AI ideology> [extends] this mistake"

RESEARCH OBJECT: The word commitment in the positive programme. A commitment is an obligation someone can be held to; it presupposes a party with standing who can fail and be answerable for failing. Software has no standing. So every loop in this account requires a human somewhere, by construction.

LOCAL MOVE: Four denials clear the cognitivist ground, then design is redefined as coordination of commitments rather than representation of tasks.

SOURCE TERMS: ontological intervention / accountable coordination / commitments / breakdowns / repairs / responsibilities / cognitivist fantasy

WHAT BECAME STRANGE: A system can *carry* a commitment (transmit, record, enforce it) without *making* one. Those are different acts and the same word covers both. Once agents act autonomously the difference stops being academic: an agent that books a flight has carried someone's commitment, and if it books the wrong one nobody has yet decided whose commitment was broken.

QUESTION: Can a system make a commitment, or only carry one — and is there any case where a commitment has been enforced *against* a system rather than against its operator?

DEEPER QUESTION: If systems only carry commitments, then "AI agent" is a category error of the same shape as the cognitivist one this theory attacks — attributing to the machine a capacity that exists only in a social practice. The critique would then apply to the agent paradigm with no modification.

MECHANISM: <SPEAKER MAKES COMMITMENT> -> [SYSTEM CARRIES IT: records, transmits, enforces] -> <ACTION> -> [BREAKDOWN] -> repair is demanded -> <REPAIR DEMANDED OF THE HUMAN, ALWAYS>

FORMAL SHIFT: <COMMITMENT> -> <CARRIED OBLIGATION> -> [BREAKDOWN] -> <REPAIR ADDRESSED TO A PARTY WITH STANDING>

SOURCE FORMALISM: The commitment / breakdown / repair / responsibility quartet.

OUR FORMALIZATION: [OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX] makes_commitment(X) requires: X can be held; X can fail; failure obligates X to repair. Test each on a system. If all three fail, systems are carriers only, and every "autonomous agent" claim inherits a category error.

TENSION: READING A: commitment is irreducibly social; machines carry, never make; the vocabulary must keep a human in every loop. READING B: institutions already hold non-human parties (corporations) to commitments, so standing is conferrable rather than natural, and it could be conferred on systems.

MISSING: Any case of enforcement against a system. Any account of what conferring standing would require.

BOUNDARY: Winograd and Flores wrote before autonomous agents; extending their commitment vocabulary to them is our move and is exactly what needs argument.

CITATION TRAIL: Winograd and Flores, Understanding Computers and Cognition (1986). Speech-act theory on commissives. Corporate personhood as the precedent for conferred standing. [[FORAGE-PT-012]] [[FORAGE-PT-025]]

TEST: Search for a case where an obligation was enforced against a software system as such — not against its vendor, operator, or user. If none exists, "carrier, not maker" holds, and the agent literature has a Winograd-shaped problem.
