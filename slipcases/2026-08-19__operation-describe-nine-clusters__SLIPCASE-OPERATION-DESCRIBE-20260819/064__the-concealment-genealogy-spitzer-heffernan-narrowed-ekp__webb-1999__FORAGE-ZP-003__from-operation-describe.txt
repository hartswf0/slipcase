TITLE: The concealment genealogy: Spitzer/Heffernan narrowed ekphrasis; Webb's enargeia recovers its operativity

SOURCE: Z-Port/final-draft-v2.md §1 (identical in v3, final-assembled §1); Z-Port/final-draft-v4.md ("Operative Ekphrasis and the Imagetext"); Z-Port/deep-research-report (14).md (Thesis bin, Source audit)

PASSAGE: [QUOTE] "As established by Leo Spitzer's influential 1955 analysis of John Keats's 'Ode on a Grecian Urn,' ekphrasis was strictly redefined as the 'poetic description of a pictorial or sculptural work of art.' This formalist constraint, later codified by James A. W. Heffernan as the 'verbal representation of visual representation,' effectively concealed the original operativity of the ancient rhetorical practice." (final-draft-v2.md §1) [QUOTE] "As Ruth Webb (1999) has shown, ancient ekphrasis was defined not by its object but by its function: *enargeia*, or vividness, and its operative capacity to bring a subject — whether a battle, a person, or a place — before the eyes of the audience. It was a technology of production, not merely of representation." (final-draft-v2.md §1) [QUOTE] "Authors of rhetorical treatises from the Empire period, including Theon (1st century), Hermogenes (2nd), Aphthonios (4th), and Nikolaos (5th), treat ekphrasis as the art of enargeia (Webb, 1999; Webb 2016)." (final-draft-v4.md)

RESEARCH OBJECT: A critical-historical genealogy with three stages: (1) ancient progymnasmata: ekphrasis defined by function (enargeia — bringing anything before the eyes), art object not required; (2) twentieth-century narrowing (Spitzer 1955 → Krieger 1992, Heffernan 1993, Mitchell 1994): ekphrasis = words about static visual art, emphasizing medial friction and impossibility; (3) recovery: Webb 1999/2016, Brosch 2018 (digital ekphrasis as "performative strategy"), completed by Bajohr's operative turn. The narrowing is charged with having "concealed" the form's original productivity.

LOCAL MOVE: Turn a definitional dispute in classical philology into the argumentative license for the whole paper: if ancient ekphrasis was always operative, then generative AI is a recovery of the original concept, not a metaphorical stretch of it.

SOURCE TERMS: enargeia; progymnasmata; "poetic description of a pictorial or sculptural work of art" (Spitzer); "verbal representation of visual representation" (Heffernan); formalist narrowing; technology of production

WHAT BECAME STRANGE: The canonical twentieth-century definition of ekphrasis turns out to be a mid-century invention that reversed the ancient one — the tradition's most-cited formula is historically the aberration, and the AI prompt is closer to Theon than Keats criticism is.

QUESTION: Was the narrowing "deliberate" (v2's word: "a profound and deliberate narrowing") — or an emergent effect of rhetoric's cultural decline, as v4's gentler account ("As rhetorical practice and theory became less culturally important...") suggests?

DEEPER QUESTION: Does every media-theoretic concept carry a suppressed operational past that new technology re-exposes — i.e., is "concealment then technical recovery" a general schema for writing media genealogies, and what would falsify such a schema?

MECHANISM: Disciplinary relocation changes definition: when ekphrasis moved from rhetoric (performance training) to literary criticism (poem analysis), its defining property migrated from function (enargeia) to object (artwork), and its operativity became invisible.

FORMAL SHIFT: Definition by function (what description does to an audience) → definition by object (what description is about) → back to definition by operation (what description executes).

SOURCE FORMALISM: Webb's function/object distinction; the v4 list of progymnasmata authors (Theon, Hermogenes, Aphthonios, Nikolaos) as the evidentiary base.

OUR FORMALIZATION: [OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX] Ekphrasis_ancient = {d : d produces enargeia in audience}, object unrestricted. Ekphrasis_modern = {d : object(d) ∈ artworks ∧ d is verbal representation of visual representation}. Ekphrasis_operative = {d : execute(d) yields media object}. Claim: Ekphrasis_operative restores the ancient functional definition with a machine substituted for the audience.

TENSION: READING A (v2/v3/final-assembled): the narrowing was "profound and deliberate" and "fundamentally obscured the productive, world-building capacity of the word" — an indictment. READING B (v4/portugal): the same era "produced rich analyses of literary pictorialism" and the Spitzer quote is sourced neutrally through Webb 1999, 10 — a shift of emphasis, not a suppression. The rhetorical stakes of the genealogy differ by draft.

MISSING: Spitzer's 1955 essay is never quoted beyond the one definitional phrase (itself quoted via Webb); no primary engagement with Heffernan's Museum of Words beyond the formula; the drafts do not test whether the modern narrowing had its own good reasons (e.g., distinguishing ekphrasis from description generally).

BOUNDARY: Genealogy covers Western/Greco-Roman rhetoric through Anglo-American criticism; nothing on non-Western description traditions.

CITATION TRAIL: Theon/Hermogenes/Aphthonios/Nikolaos (via Webb 1999, Webb 2016) → Spitzer 1955 (via Webb 1999, p.10) → Krieger 1992 → Heffernan 1993/2004 → Mitchell 1994 → Clüver 1997, Elleström 2010, Lindhé 2013, Brosch 2018 → Bajohr 2024.

TEST: Verify the Spitzer quotation and Webb's page 10 attribution against Webb 1999 ("Ekphrasis ancient and modern: the invention of a genre"); check whether Webb frames Spitzer as inventor of the narrow definition or as symptom of it.
