ID: FORAGE-PT-012

TITLE: Five of the apparatus's six operations are mechanical and the sixth is moral, and bundling them makes an ethical claim look like a technical finding

SOURCE: PROGRAMS/agential.json — agential-worlds-cybernetic-cuts v2.0, <thesis> and <governing_problem>

PASSAGE: [QUOTE] "<generative_AI_system> is an <apparatus> that [performs] <agential_cuts>, [completes] <cybernetic_circuits>, [compresses] <archives>, [simulates] <worlds>, [modulates] <attention>, and [redistributes] <responsibility>." [QUOTE] "<AI_discourse> often [misdescribes] <generative_AI_system> as <assistant>, <engine>, <model>, or <content_tool>. This [hides] the deeper operation: <system> [makes] <worlds_actionable>."

RESEARCH OBJECT: A six-item list with one item of a different kind. Cutting, circuit-completion, compression, simulation and attention-modulation are things a system does. Redistributing responsibility is something that happens to *people* — it requires institutions, expectations and answerability, none of which the system contains.

LOCAL MOVE: The theory refuses the neutral-tool description and replaces it with an apparatus that acts. Its rhetorical strength comes from the list's momentum; the sixth item rides in on the five.

SOURCE TERMS: apparatus / agential cuts / cybernetic circuits / compresses archives / modulates attention / redistributes responsibility / makes worlds actionable

WHAT BECAME STRANGE: Responsibility-redistribution is presented as an operation of the system, but responsibility can only move if some institution accepts the move. So either the claim is about institutions (and the system is an occasion, not an agent), or it is a metaphor doing the work of an argument.

QUESTION: Is responsibility-redistribution an *effect* of the other five operations, or an independent operation — and is there a case where cuts occur and responsibility does not move?

DEEPER QUESTION: If responsibility only moves when an institution ratifies the move, then the political object of study is the ratification, not the apparatus. That relocates the whole critique from the model to the paperwork around it.

MECHANISM: <SYSTEM MAKES A CUT> -> some distinctions become actionable, others unavailable -> [DECISIONS TAKEN ON THE CUT] -> outcomes -> [INSTITUTION ASSIGNS OR DECLINES BLAME] -> <RESPONSIBILITY LOCATED>. The final arrow is not performed by the system.

FORMAL SHIFT: <TECHNICAL OPERATION> -> <ACTIONABLE WORLD> -> [INSTITUTIONAL RATIFICATION] -> <RESPONSIBILITY MOVED>

SOURCE FORMALISM: The six-operation list; elsewhere in the corpus an auditable cut taxonomy with levels.

OUR FORMALIZATION: [OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX] Separate CUT-OPS = {cut, circuit, compress, simulate, modulate} from RESP-OP = {redistribute}. Then: does RESP-OP ever fire without an institutional actor? If never, it is not an operation of the apparatus but a property of its uptake.

TENSION: READING A (Barad-faithful): the cut *is* ethical — deciding what counts as an object is already a moral act, so the sixth item is not a separate kind. READING B: without an institution that can be held, "responsibility redistributed" names nobody's obligation and functions as critique-flavoured description.

MISSING: A single case traced from a technical cut to an actual shift in who was held answerable. The theory names the operation and never instantiates it.

BOUNDARY: Barad's agential realism supports the entanglement of ethics and ontology; it does not supply an account of institutional liability, and citing her does not provide one.

CITATION TRAIL: Barad, Meeting the Universe Halfway, on agential cuts and ethico-onto-epistemology. Responsibility-gap literature in machine ethics. [[FORAGE-PT-013]] [[FORAGE-PT-026]]

TEST: Find three deployments where a system's classification changed a decision. For each, trace whether any party's answerability actually changed — in policy, contract, or practice. If none did, responsibility is sticky and the sixth operation fails empirically.
