ID: FORAGE-ZP-001

TITLE: The core thesis and its mutation: "word to image" is already obsolete; the decisive shift is word to world model

SOURCE: Z-Port/word-to-world.md ("I. Thesis: Word to World Model Is the Move"); Z-Port/final-draft-v2.md §1; Z-Port/final-draft-v3.md §1; Z-Port/final-draft-v4.md (Abstract + Introduction); Z-Port/final-assembled-draft.md §1

PASSAGE: [QUOTE] "The old ekphrastic question asked whether words could make readers see. Generative AI appears to answer yes: the word becomes the picture. However, that answer is already obsolete. The decisive shift is not from word to image but from word to world model." (final-draft-v2.md §1; repeated in v3 and final-assembled-draft) [QUOTE] "A prompt is not yet a world, but it has stopped being a sentence." (final-draft-v3.md §1 — added in v3, absent from v2 and final-assembled) [QUOTE] "We are no longer reclassifying generative AI from an image-maker to a tool; we are reclassifying it as a world-model operator. The image was never the destination. The world was." (word-to-world.md §I)

RESEARCH OBJECT: The paper's central claim as a moving target: a reclassification thesis (generative AI = world-model operator, not image-maker) whose strength of assertion mutates across draft versions. v2/v3/final-assembled open with the polemical "already obsolete" move; v4 (the Bolter co-authored register) retreats to a modest coincidence framing — "this coincidence invites us to apply literary history and aesthetic theory" — and only reaches the world-model claim late, hedged as "the hybrid path... is closer to the original force of 'operative'."

LOCAL MOVE: Invalidate the celebratory reading of text-to-image AI as the fulfillment of ekphrasis, then immediately reclassify the object of study: the prompt's true referent is not the picture but the operating conditions of an environment.

SOURCE TERMS: word-to-world-model; world-model operator; operative reclassification; threshold (not destination); "description helps constitute the world's operating conditions"

WHAT BECAME STRANGE: The moment of triumph (word finally becomes image) is declared obsolete in the same sentence that announces it. The achievement is used as evidence of its own insufficiency — an argumentative structure where the answered question is the problem.

QUESTION: Which version of the thesis is defensible — the strong reclassification (AI is a world-model operator now) or the weak threshold claim (prompting is *becoming* spatial and procedural)?

DEEPER QUESTION: What does it mean methodologically that the thesis softens precisely when a senior co-author's empirical, historically careful register (v4) takes over — is the strong thesis a rhetorical artifact of the compressed drafts rather than a claim the evidence supports?

MECHANISM: Escalation by supersession: each answered media question (can words make us see? can words make images?) is converted into the premise of a larger question (can words make worlds?), so the argument advances by declaring its own prior stage obsolete.

FORMAL SHIFT: From a two-term relation (word → image) to a three-term staged relation (word → image → world model), with the middle term demoted from destination to "way-station."

SOURCE FORMALISM: Three-threshold structure: (1) progymnasmata performance; (2) operative ekphrasis / imagetext in latent space; (3) worldtext / world-model interface (v2 §4, final-assembled §4).

OUR FORMALIZATION: [OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX] Thesis strength as a function T(v) over draft versions: T(word-to-world)=max (reclassification asserted as fact), T(v2)=T(v3)=high (obsolescence asserted, threshold hedged), T(v4)=low (coincidence + analogy, natural-sign section added), T(final-assembled)=high (restored polemic + D's ethical question). Non-monotone: revision does not converge on stronger claims but oscillates between polemic and defensibility.

TENSION: READING A (v2/v3/final-assembled): the word-to-image answer "is already obsolete"; the field must move now; the shield IS a miniature world model. READING B (v4/portugal-version): the correspondence between prompting and ekphrasis is an "almost uncanny" coincidence to be explored; the analogy must "remain controlled"; the world-model claim is one hedged section, and the natural-sign genealogy (Krieger) does the central work instead. The two readings disagree about whether the paper announces a rupture or interprets a resonance.

MISSING: No draft states criteria for when the threshold counts as crossed — what observable property of a generative system would falsify "the decisive shift is from word to world model." Deep-research-report warns the key terms "are not current consensus terms."

BOUNDARY: The claim is about visual/spatial generative platforms (OpenAI, Gemini, Midjourney, Genie, Marble); none of the drafts claims LLM text generation or code generation is worldtext.

CITATION TRAIL: Bajohr 2024 (operative ekphrasis) → Mitchell 1994 (imagetext) → Ha & Schmidhuber 2018 (world models) → LeCun 2022 → Bruce et al. 2024 (Genie 2) → World Labs Marble.

TEST: Compare the opening 200 words of v2, v3, v4, and final-assembled: if the "already obsolete" sentence survives into the venue version co-signed by both authors, the strong thesis won; if the v4 "coincidence" framing survives, the weak one did.

PLATFORM: Draft corpus of a co-authored media-theory paper (Hartsoe & Bolter), targeting a humanities journal; evaluated internally by rubric graders.
