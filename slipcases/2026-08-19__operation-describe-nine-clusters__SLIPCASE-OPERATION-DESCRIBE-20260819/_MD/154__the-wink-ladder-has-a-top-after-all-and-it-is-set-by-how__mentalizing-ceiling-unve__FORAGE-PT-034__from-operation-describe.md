TITLE: The wink ladder has a top after all, and it is set by how many orders of intentionality a reader can hold

SOURCE: Higher-order intentionality research reporting a limit on how many nested mental states people can track, associated with Robin Dunbar's mentalizing work [UNVERIFIED — specific studies, order counts and effect sizes not retrieved]; read against PROGRAMS/tda.json wink rules

PASSAGE: [PARAPHRASE] This literature reports that performance on tasks requiring nested attribution of mental states degrades sharply beyond a small number of orders, with a commonly cited ceiling in the region of four to five levels for typical adults. [QUOTE] tda.json: "IF <eyelid_contraction> [prepares] <future_parody> THEN OUTPUT <rehearsal>"

RESEARCH OBJECT: An empirical bound on a ladder the rule set leaves open. The parent noted that nothing in the production rules stops rung five. Something outside the rules does: readers cannot reliably hold the stack. Thickness is therefore not unbounded interpretive regress but a quantity capped by the interpreter's capacity — which makes the cap a property of the reader, not of the culture.

LOCAL MOVE: This child closes the parent's open recursion from outside the source, replacing a formal non-termination with an empirical ceiling.

SOURCE TERMS: orders of intentionality / mentalizing / nested mental states / ceiling / rehearsal / parody

WHAT BECAME STRANGE: Geertz's image of interpretation as turtles all the way down becomes false in a specific, testable way. There is a floor to the turtles, and it is cognitive. Any description operating above the ceiling is not thicker — it is unreadable, and its apparent depth is produced by the analyst's notation rather than recovered from the act.

QUESTION: Where exactly does recoverability fail — and is the ceiling the same for producing a depth-n act as for reading one?

DEEPER QUESTION: If production and comprehension ceilings differ, then acts can be performed at depths no audience can recover, and the surplus is systematically lost. That would make a whole class of intentional acts structurally invisible to ethnography, not because ethnographers lack thickness but because everyone does.

FORMAL SHIFT: <UNBOUNDED LADDER> -> <BOUNDED BY READER CAPACITY> -> [MEASURE CEILING] -> <THICKNESS WITH A MAXIMUM>

SOURCE FORMALISM: NONE verified here; the order-counting paradigms exist but are not quoted.

OUR FORMALIZATION: [OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX] Two ceilings: C_read = max depth recoverable from a trace; C_produce = max depth an actor can construct. Thickness of a description is min(depth intended, C_read of the audience). Surplus = depth intended - C_read, and surplus is invisible by construction.

TENSION: READING A: the ceiling is real and thickness is capped, so ethnographic depth claims above four or five orders are notational rather than observational. READING B: the experimental paradigms measure explicit reasoning under artificial load; skilled cultural readers may handle deeper stacks fluently in familiar contexts, so the laboratory ceiling understates situated competence.

Discriminating evidence: test depth recovery in a domain the reader is expert in versus a matched unfamiliar one. If expertise raises the ceiling, Reading B holds and the cap is domain-relative.

MISSING: Verified sources, order counts and paradigms. Any test of whether machine systems have a comparable ceiling, which would be the first substrate comparison on this variable.

BOUNDARY: These results concern explicit nested attribution in experimental tasks. Extending them to ethnographic interpretation is an extrapolation across both task and setting.
