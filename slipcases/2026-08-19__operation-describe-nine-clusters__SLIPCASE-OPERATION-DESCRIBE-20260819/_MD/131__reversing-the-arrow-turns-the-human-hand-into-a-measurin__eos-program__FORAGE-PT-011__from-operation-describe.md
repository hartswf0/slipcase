TITLE: Reversing the arrow turns the human hand into a measuring instrument for machine description

SOURCE: PROGRAMS/eos.json — ELEPHANT_OS, <Initial_Interpretation>

PASSAGE: [QUOTE] "<standard_AI> [flows] <human_prompt -> machine_image> <ELEPHANT_OS> [reverses] <machine_text -> human_image>" [QUOTE] "<human> [is-not] <author> <human> [is] <substrate> <machine> [is-not] <substrate> <machine> [is] <author>" [QUOTE] "<drawing> [is] <descriptive_struggle_made_visible>" [QUOTE] "<epistemic_greed> [physicalizes] <context_window>"

RESEARCH OBJECT: An inversion that yields an instrument. If a machine writes a description and humans render it, then the *spread across human renderings* is a direct measurement of how well that description constrains a scene. The human hand becomes the read-out.

LOCAL MOVE: The theory swaps the agency assignment — machine as author, human as substrate — and makes drawing the visible form of descriptive struggle, which is otherwise private.

SOURCE TERMS: operative ekphrasis reversal / substrate / author / six personas as cultural compilers / descriptive struggle made visible / epistemic greed / closed translation chain

WHAT BECAME STRANGE: This is the classical test for descriptive vividness, rebuilt as a game. Ancient rhetoric judged enargeia by whether the hearer became a spectator — you asked the audience what they saw. Here you *watch them draw it*. Same criterion, physical output, and far harder to fake than a questionnaire.

QUESTION: Can convergence across human drawings serve as a measure of a description's constraining power — and does it agree with convergence across machine renderings of the same text?

DEEPER QUESTION: If human and machine renderings of one description diverge in *different* ways, the divergence pattern is a map of what the two substrates each fail to receive. That is a comparative instrument nobody has built.

MECHANISM: <MACHINE TEXT> -> distributed to N human renderers -> [EACH DRAWS] -> N images -> [MEASURE PAIRWISE AGREEMENT ON SCENE ELEMENTS] -> <CONSTRAINING POWER OF THE TEXT>

FORMAL SHIFT: <DESCRIPTION> -> <N HUMAN RENDERINGS> -> [AGREEMENT MEASUREMENT] -> <THICKNESS AS AN EFFECT, NOT A FORM>

SOURCE FORMALISM: Six personas as cultural compilers; the closed translation chain; the game loop.

OUR FORMALIZATION: [OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX] constrain(D) = mean pairwise agreement over rendered scene elements across renderers. Compute for humans and for model samples; divergence(D) = constrain_human(D) - constrain_machine(D). Descriptions far from zero are the interesting ones.

TENSION: READING A: human is substrate — the drawing is a rendering of the machine's authored description, so the human contributes execution, not authorship. READING B: drawing is irreducibly interpretive; every renderer authors, so "substrate" understates them and the measurement is contaminated by taste. The measurement survives either way; the agency claim does not.

MISSING: A scoring rubric for scene-element agreement across drawings. Any statement of who is accountable for what the human draws — which the substrate framing makes genuinely unclear.

BOUNDARY: This licenses a measurement protocol. It does not establish that the machine is an author in any sense beyond originating the text.

CITATION TRAIL: Webb on enargeia and the hearer-as-spectator; the progymnasmata's ekphrasis exercises. Referential communication games in psycholinguistics. [[FORAGE-PT-003]] [[FORAGE-PT-025]]

TEST: Twelve machine-authored descriptions, twenty human renderers, twenty model samples each. Score element agreement in both populations. Any description with high human and low machine agreement is a case where description routes the two substrates differently — and each such case is a paper.
