TITLE:
EVERY UNWRITTEN PAPER IN THIS ARCHIVE IS THE SAME MISSING VARIABLE — THE ARCHIVE HAS A THEORY OF DESCRIPTION WITHOUT A CLOCK

SOURCE:

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
Across the archive, every formal object is instantaneous. The unit ⟨D, A_route⟩ has no time index. The operator tuple O has no history. The YAML schema has one row per artifact. The encounter trace ⟨A, B, C, R, A'⟩ counts loop iterations but nothing inside them. ΔG is a difference between two states with no duration between them.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
The frontier of this archive is not a list of topics. It is one absence, appearing thirty-six times in different costumes.

Onset, decay, hysteresis, habituation, drift, settling, staleness, half-life, timescale mismatch, path dependence: every serious gap found in this forage is a temporal property of description, and the archive's formalism cannot express any of them.

LOCAL MOVE:
This zettel is a survey, not a forage. Its function is to report the topology discovered by the preceding thirty-six: the frontier is not broad, it is deep in one direction.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The archive's ancestors are all temporal thinkers. Ryle's achievement is a *transition*. Geertz's programs are acquired over a *lifetime*. Ricoeur's trace *outlives* its author. Naur's theory *dies* with the team. Bateson's circuit *loops*. Bush's trail is a *path*.

Six ancestors, six accounts of time, and a formalism with no t in it.

The archive's genealogy is a genealogy of duration, and its model is a snapshot.

QUESTION:
What single formal change would make every registered gap in this archive expressible?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Is "operative description" the right name for a phenomenon whose distinguishing properties turn out to be onset, persistence, and decay? A valve has no half-life. A *dose* does.

TENSION:
READING A: the temporal turn is a generalization, and generalizing a theory that has not yet been demonstrated once is premature. Measure ΔG properly in one case first.
READING B: ΔG cannot be measured properly *without* a clock, because the archive's own evidence shows the effect is not stationary — descriptions decay, operators habituate, contexts accumulate. A static measurement of a non-stationary quantity is not a first step; it is an artifact of sampling.

Reading B is supported by FORAGE-OD-019 and FORAGE-OD-035; Reading A is the safer dissertation. This is a genuine strategic fork and it is the author's to choose, not the archive's to resolve.

MISSING:
A single time-indexed measurement anywhere in the archive. Every one of the nine quantities above is currently at zero observations.

Also missing: any citation between the OPERATION DESCRIBE corpus and the SLIPCASE corpus, which independently recorded the same absence (FORAGE-OD-020).

BOUNDARY:
This is a claim about the archive's formalism and its registered gaps. It is not a claim that a temporal theory of description would be true, only that the archive cannot currently state one and that its own gaps all point there.
