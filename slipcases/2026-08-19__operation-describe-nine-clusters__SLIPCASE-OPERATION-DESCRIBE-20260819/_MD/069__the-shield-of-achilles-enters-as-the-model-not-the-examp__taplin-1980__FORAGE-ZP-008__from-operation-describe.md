TITLE: The Shield of Achilles enters as the model, not the example: cosmology, governance, ecology, culture, and Ocean as boundary condition

SOURCE: Z-Port/final-draft-v2.md §3 (core inventory; identical in final-assembled §3); Z-Port/final-draft-v3.md §3 (adds counter-plot passage); Z-Port/worldtext-after-ekphrasis.md §3; Z-Port/image-is-not-end-of-ekphrasis.md §5; primary object: Homer, Iliad 18.478–608

PASSAGE: [QUOTE] "The Shield of Achilles should not enter this argument as a famous example. It should enter as the model." (final-draft-v2.md §3) [QUOTE] "The shield has cosmology (earth, sky, sea, sun, moon, constellations). It has governance (a legal dispute adjudicated in a public assembly). It has ecology (plowing, harvest, vineyard, cattle herding). It has culture (a wedding procession, festive dancing). And it has a boundary condition — the great river of Ocean at the rim, the edge beyond which the world-model has no data." (final-draft-v2.md §3) [QUOTE] "War is the plot. The shield is the counter-plot. The shield shows the life Achilles will defend, destroy, and never fully inhabit." (final-draft-v3.md §3) [QUOTE] "As classical scholars have remarked, the shield occupies a position both within and beyond the narrative of the poem (Taplin, 1980)." (final-draft-v2.md §3)

RESEARCH OBJECT: A structural rereading of Iliad 18.478–608: the shield is not a depicted scene but a specified world, inventoried as five world-model components (cosmology / governance / ecology / culture / boundary condition), with the macro/microcosm reversal from Taplin 1980 — the Trojan War is one event inside the shield's world, yet the shield sits inside the war ("The whole world appears within the weapon of one man"). The scenes are in motion (procession moves, dancers turn, farmers cut, armies clash), so the object already exceeds the static image. The E research file adds Seaford's "nested oppositions" — the trial scene as "a procedural algorithm for resolving conflict."

LOCAL MOVE: Promote the corpus's most canonical example into the paper's argument-generating machine: every later concept (worldtext, boundary condition, constitution, consequence) is first read off the shield, so antiquity supplies the theory and AI supplies the instance — reversing the usual direction of the analogy.

SOURCE TERMS: enter as the model; counter-plot; boundary condition; Ocean at the rim; macrocosm/microcosm reversal; constitutive not descriptive ("It tells us what kind of world the poem believes can exist"); compressed environment; world-bearing artifact

WHAT BECAME STRANGE: An anachronism is asserted with a straight face — "the edge beyond which the world-model has no data" describes the river Ocean in an ML idiom — and the drafts treat this not as metaphor but as structural identity: the rim of a bronze shield and the support of a training distribution perform the same function (bounding what the world can contain).

QUESTION: Does the shield "organize a totality of relations" (worldtext-after-ekphrasis §4) or does the reading over-systematize an aggregative catalog of scenes — is the five-component inventory Homer's structure or the authors'?

DEEPER QUESTION: The final-assembled conclusion adds: "What is absent — slavery, disease, famine — is a claim about what the available grammar renders unimaginable." If a world-specification is defined as much by its exclusions as its contents, is every world model an implicit politics, and can that be read off its boundary conditions the way Ocean can be read off the shield's rim?

MECHANISM: Compression and scale-reversal: a whole social-cosmic order is folded onto one carried object; motion is implied rather than depicted, so the audience's imagination performs the state transitions the surface cannot.

FORMAL SHIFT: The image/world distinction is redrawn as scene-depiction vs world-specification: "The imagetext freezes a moment; the worldtext implies the procedures that make moments succeed one another" (final-draft-v4.md).

SOURCE FORMALISM: The five-component inventory (cosmology, governance, ecology, culture, boundary); Taplin's within/beyond-narrative position; Seaford's nested oppositions (via E file).

OUR FORMALIZATION: [OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX] Shield as world-spec S = (C, G, E, K, B): cosmological frame C, governance procedures G, ecological cycles E, cultural rituals K, boundary B with support(S) ⊆ B; the Iliad's war narrative is then an event e ∈ trajectories(S), giving the containment inversion: the poem ⊂ war, but war ∈ S ⊂ shield ⊂ poem.

TENSION: READING A (word-to-world.md, A-TEST drafts): "The shield is not the first great ekphrasis. It is a miniature world model" — identity claim. READING B (deep-research-report): that phrasing "risks collapsing an ancient poetic artifact into a contemporary technical term too fast"; the defensible version is "ancient formal analogue or proto-world-model" — analogy claim. The drafts oscillate; v4's "The analogy should remain controlled" is the compromise position.

MISSING: No sustained engagement with the Greek text itself (scenes are inventoried, never quoted); no Auden ("The Shield of Achilles," the modern counter-reading where the world on the shield is replaced by a wasteland) anywhere in the corpus, despite its obvious relevance to "what kind of life has the description authorized"; Hardie's cosmological reading named only in the research report.

BOUNDARY: The reading concerns Iliad 18.478–608 only; the corpus does not extend the world-model reading to other ancient ekphrases (pseudo-Hesiod's Shield of Herakles, Aeneid shield) though Aeschylus' Seven appears in the E file's citation list.

CITATION TRAIL: Homer, Iliad 18.478–608 → Lessing 1766 (process not object) → Taplin 1980 ("The Shield of Achilles within the Iliad") → Hardie (cosmology, via research report) → Seaford (nested oppositions, via E file) → Moretti 1996 ("world text," added in final-assembled) → Ha & Schmidhuber 2018 (the mapped target).

TEST: Close-read 18.478–608 and tag each line as depiction (surface) vs procedure (state transition: trial rules, harvest sequence, battle turns); the worldtext reading predicts procedural lines dominate; a scene-catalog reading predicts the opposite.
