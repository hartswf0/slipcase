TITLE: Service work supplies the unit of intensification, and it was measured thirty years before the claim was made

SOURCE: Robin Leidner, Fast Food, Fast Talk: Service Work and the Routinization of Everyday Life (University of California Press, 1993) [UNVERIFIED pagination]; read against PROGRAMS/thick.json "intensified symbolic-control apparatus"

PASSAGE: [PARAPHRASE] Leidner studies workplaces where interaction itself is scripted — counter service and door-to-door insurance sales — and examines how far employers can specify not only tasks but words, tone and affect, where workers accept or resist the script, and what routinizing interaction does to the self that performs it. [QUOTE] thick.json: "<AI> [is-not] <break from culture> <AI> [is] <intensified symbolic-control apparatus>"

RESEARCH OBJECT: An existing empirical baseline for a quantity the parent said had no unit. Leidner's object is the *script*: an explicit symbolic specification of conduct, imposed by an organisation, with measurable coverage. Scripting coverage — what fraction of an interaction is specified in advance — is the unit intensification needs, and it has a pre-AI measurement in a documented setting.

LOCAL MOVE: This child answers the parent's demand for a unit by finding a discipline that already operationalised symbolic control as scripting and studied its limits and its costs.

SOURCE TERMS: routinization / script / interactive service work / self / resistance / specification of feeling

WHAT BECAME STRANGE: Leidner's finding is not that scripting increases compliance but that it becomes contested precisely where it reaches *identity* — workers accept task scripts and resist scripts that specify who they must appear to be. That gives intensification a threshold rather than a slope: symbolic control extends easily until it reaches the self, and then it meets resistance.

QUESTION: What fraction of an interaction is specified in advance now, compared with the settings Leidner documented — and has the increase been in task coverage or in identity coverage?

DEEPER QUESTION: If resistance concentrates at identity specification, then the operative question about generative systems is not how much conduct they script but whether they script *persona* — and a system that supplies the words the worker must say is doing the thing Leidner found most contested.

SOURCE FORMALISM: NONE — ethnographic study, not formal apparatus.

OUR FORMALIZATION: [OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX] coverage = fraction of utterances in an interaction that are specified in advance; and split it: coverage_task versus coverage_persona. Intensification is then two curves, and the parent's three candidate units reduce to these two plus granularity.

TENSION: READING A: scripting coverage is the right unit and generative systems raise it, so intensification is measurable and the continuum thesis holds. READING B: a generated script differs in kind from a corporate one because it is produced per-interaction rather than imposed uniformly — personalised scripting may not feel like a script at all, and resistance may not trigger, which would defeat the comparison.

Reading B is the sharper worry: Leidner's resistance depended on the script being recognisable as external. A script generated to fit you may be invisible.

MISSING: Verified pagination. Any post-2020 measurement of scripting coverage comparable to Leidner's setting. Any test of whether personalised scripts trigger the resistance uniform ones did.

BOUNDARY: Leidner studies two American service settings in the early 1990s. Coverage figures from those settings are a baseline for comparison, not a general historical measurement.

CITATION TRAIL: [[FORAGE-PT-018]] -> Leidner 1993 -> scripting coverage as the unit -> next: the identity threshold, and whether personalisation defeats recognition — which connects directly to who is answerable when a person speaks a script they did not write.

TEST: Sample recorded service interactions from a scripted setting and a generatively-assisted one. Code each utterance as specified or unspecified, and specified-task or specified-persona. Compare coverage. Then ask workers in each setting whether they experience the specification as external — the recognition variable Reading B turns on.
