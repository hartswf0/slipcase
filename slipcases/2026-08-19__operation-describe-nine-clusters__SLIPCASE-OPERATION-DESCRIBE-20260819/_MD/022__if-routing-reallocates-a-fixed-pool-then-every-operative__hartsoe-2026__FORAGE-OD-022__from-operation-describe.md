TITLE:
IF ROUTING REALLOCATES A FIXED POOL THEN EVERY OPERATIVE DESCRIPTION HAS A VICTIM AND THE ARCHIVE HAS NO TERM FOR IT

SOURCE:
Watson Hartsoe — PAPERS/operation-describe-label-01.md §15 "Power: Asymmetric Discipline" and §9 "Compression"; developed from FORAGE-OD-021

PASSAGE:
[QUOTE]
label-01 §15:
"Operative descriptions reduce complexity for platform controllers while restricting agency for categorized subjects. The efficiency of the system is paid for in the compliance and routing of its users."

[QUOTE]
label-01 §9:
"They collapse high-dimensional, complex realities (e.g., tone, context, history) into low-dimensional, actionable symbols"

RESEARCH OBJECT:
The archive's political account is about the *labeled* subject: who is compressed, who is routed, who is reduced to a symbol.

Under congestion the injured party is often the *unlabeled* one. The issue nobody marked. The comment no classifier flagged for review. The function whose schema description was left thin.

Being described is a form of power over you. Not being described is a different form, and the archive has no vocabulary for it.

LOCAL MOVE:
The compression chapter asks what context disappears *inside* the label. It does not ask what disappears *outside* it.

SOURCE TERMS:
asymmetric discipline

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The archive already has the term. label-00 §8 lists "under-routing" among its error questions and then never develops it.

Under-routing is not a failure mode of a description. It is the *systematic consequence* of any description working correctly in a congested system. It is not error; it is the cost of success, borne elsewhere.

QUESTION:
What is the correct name and unit for the harm suffered by objects that a routing system leaves undescribed?

DEEPER QUESTION:
If routing power is conserved — if salience is zero-sum within a fixed operator budget — then is the archive's central concept better understood as a theory of *deprivation* than a theory of action?

MECHANISM:
<FIXED OPERATOR ATTENTION BUDGET A>
→ description raises salience of subset X
→ [BUDGET CONSTRAINT: Σ salience ≤ A]
→ salience of complement X̄ falls
→ <ACTION ON X̄ DELAYED OR NEVER TAKEN>
→ harm accrues to parties associated with X̄, who were never described at all

FORMAL SHIFT:
<DESCRIPTION OF X>
→ <SALIENCE REALLOCATION UNDER BUDGET>
→ [DEPRIVATION OF X̄]
→ <HARM WITHOUT CATEGORIZATION>

SOURCE FORMALISM:
The archive supplies the budget constraint already: Tax(D,O) > A(O) implies A(O) is finite. Finiteness plus competition is sufficient for zero-sum salience. The archive wrote the premise and not the conclusion.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

  Σ_i salience_i(t) ≤ A(O)

Define the deprivation of an undescribed object j:

  dep_j(D) = salience_j(no D) − salience_j(D)   ≥ 0 under congestion

Then a routing system's total political effect has two components:

  discipline  = harm to the described (the archive's current account)
  deprivation = harm to the undescribed (absent)

And a testable prediction: interventions that *increase* description coverage may increase discipline while reducing deprivation. The two harms trade off, which means there is no policy that minimizes both — a genuine dilemma the archive could own.

TENSION:
READING A: attention is not strictly fixed; more salient issues attract more contributors, so the pool expands and deprivation is small.
READING B: the pool expands far more slowly than description volume, so in practice descriptions compete and deprivation dominates.

Discriminating evidence: does total resolved-issue volume rise with labeling rate, or only its distribution?

MISSING:
Any measurement of the undescribed. Every method in the archive samples on the described: labeled issues, flagged comments, invoked tools. The population that would show deprivation is the one the archive's data collection excludes by design.

BOUNDARY:
This does not show deprivation is larger than discipline. It shows the archive measures only one of two harms and has no term for the other.

TEST:
Sample the undescribed. Take a repository week; take all issues that received no label; measure their outcome distribution against a comparable week with lower labeling intensity.

Any systematic disadvantage is deprivation, measured for the first time in this research programme.
