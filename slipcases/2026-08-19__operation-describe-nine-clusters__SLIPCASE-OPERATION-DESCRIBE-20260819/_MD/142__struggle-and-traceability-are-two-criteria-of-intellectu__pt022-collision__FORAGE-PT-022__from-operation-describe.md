TITLE: Struggle and traceability are two criteria of intellectual honesty that can each be satisfied perfectly while the other is absent

SOURCE: Collision of PROGRAMS/dse.json (answerability as struggle) with PROGRAMS/memex.json (path integrity as auditable traversal)

PASSAGE: [QUOTE] dse.json: "<language> [becomes] <thinking-like-description> only when it is made answerable to: { <inward_pressure>, <memory>, <intention>, <metaphor>, <fact>, <history>, <public_criterion>, <correction>, <ethical_consequence> }" [QUOTE] memex.json: "<AI-assisted scholarship> must be judged by <path integrity>, not by <answer fluency>."

RESEARCH OBJECT: Two rival standards for the same virtue. Struggle is answerability to pressures, most of them internal. Traceability is answerability to a record, entirely external. They are orthogonal: a perfectly logged retrieval chain can involve no struggle at all, and a deeply struggled-over judgment can leave no trail.

LOCAL MOVE: Both instruments displace fluency, and they displace it in opposite directions — one inward toward correction and conscience, one outward toward the record.

SOURCE TERMS: answerable to / inward pressure / correction / ethical consequence / path integrity / auditable traversal / trail as ethical object

WHAT BECAME STRANGE: The two standards recommend different systems, and the difference is visible in current practice. A retrieval system with complete citations and no revision has maximal traceability and zero struggle. A scholar who rewrote a paragraph nineteen times against their own objections has maximal struggle and — because drafts are discarded — no trail.

The field is choosing between these without noticing there is a choice, and it has largely chosen traceability because traceability is cheap to instrument.

QUESTION: Are the two measurably independent, and does any real system score well on both?

DEEPER QUESTION: If struggle is unobservable and traceability is easily faked, then intellectual honesty has no criterion that is both checkable and hard to counterfeit — which would explain why the fluency problem keeps reappearing under new names.

MECHANISM: Traceable-without-struggle: <QUERY> -> [RETRIEVE, CITE, ASSEMBLE] -> complete trail, no revision, no self-objection -> <AUDITABLE, UNTHOUGHT>. Struggled-without-trace: <DRAFT> -> [NINETEEN REVISIONS UNDER CORRECTION] -> drafts discarded -> <THOUGHT, UNAUDITABLE>

FORMAL SHIFT: <HONESTY> -> <TWO ORTHOGONAL AXES> -> [MEASURE BOTH] -> <FOUR QUADRANTS, ONE OF THEM EMPTY?>

SOURCE FORMALISM: dse's nine answerability conditions; memex's four scholarly verbs.

OUR FORMALIZATION: [OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX] Two axes: S = revision depth against recorded objections; T = fraction of claims with resolvable provenance. Four quadrants. Predict most retrieval systems land high-T low-S, most solitary scholarship high-S low-T, and the high-high quadrant is nearly empty — because keeping struggle *and* recording it requires preserving the drafts, which nobody does.

TENSION: READING A: the two are complementary and a good system does both; the empty quadrant is a tooling gap. READING B: they trade off — recording your struggle changes it, because a preserved objection is performed for an audience rather than faced.

MISSING: Any system that preserves objection history as first-class provenance. Drafts are treated as waste everywhere.

BOUNDARY: The orthogonality is our claim; neither file mentions the other.
