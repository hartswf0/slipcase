TITLE: The positive term for distribution already exists — no single component holds the whole representation — and it makes Naur and Hutchins the same claim

SOURCE: Edwin Hutchins, Cognition in the Wild (MIT Press, 1995), the ship-navigation analysis [UNVERIFIED pagination]; read against PROGRAMS/cyber.json seven-verb characterisation and PROGRAMS/theory.json shared theory

PASSAGE: [PARAPHRASE] Hutchins argues that the navigation of a large vessel is accomplished by a system comprising people, instruments and procedures in which no single participant holds the representation that the system computes with; the cognitive unit is the assembly, and the evidence is that the work continues correctly while no individual could perform or state it alone. [QUOTE] theory.json: "<software_development> is <shared_theory_building_under_change>"

RESEARCH OBJECT: A criterion, not a stance. The parent found that distribution had only a negative definition — not Tool, not Author, not Mirror — and no positive term. Hutchins supplies one that is checkable: distribution obtains when the representation the system operates on is held by no single component. That is an inspectable property with a clear failure condition.

LOCAL MOVE: This child converts the parent's ablation proposal into a criterion with an established evidence standard, and in doing so discovers that two instruments in the corpus are making one claim in two vocabularies.

SOURCE TERMS: distributed cognition / cognitive system / representation / propagation across media / shared theory / living possession

WHAT BECAME STRANGE: Naur says the program theory is held by the team and dies when the team disperses. Hutchins says the navigation computation is held by the assembly and stops when the assembly breaks. These are the same structural claim about the same kind of object, arrived at from software engineering and from cognitive anthropology, and neither instrument in the corpus cites the other.

QUESTION: Does the criterion — no single component holds the whole representation — actually hold for a human-model working pair, or does one party hold enough that the pair is a tool relation after all?

DEEPER QUESTION: Hutchins' criterion has a corollary the corpus should want: if no component holds the whole, then the transfer problem is not about media at all. You cannot hand over what nobody holds. Naur's pessimism would follow from the *distribution* rather than from the ineffability of understanding — a much cheaper explanation.

MECHANISM: <TASK> -> [REPRESENTATION SPREAD ACROSS PEOPLE, INSTRUMENTS, PROCEDURES] -> partial states propagate between media -> [NO COMPONENT HOLDS THE WHOLE] -> <SYSTEM COMPUTES; NO PARTICIPANT CAN STATE THE COMPUTATION>

FORMAL SHIFT: <AGENCY LOCATION> -> <REPRESENTATION DISTRIBUTION> -> [CHECK WHETHER ANY COMPONENT HOLDS THE WHOLE] -> <DISTRIBUTION AS A DECIDABLE PROPERTY>

SOURCE FORMALISM: Hutchins' propagation-of-representational-state-across-media account, described but not quoted here.

OUR FORMALIZATION: [OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX] For components c_1..c_n and the representation R the system operates on, distribution holds iff no c_i holds R in full. Operationalise by removal: if removing c_i and asking it to reconstruct R alone fails for every i, R was distributed. This is the parent's ablation with a stated criterion attached.

TENSION: READING A: human-model pairs satisfy the criterion — neither party can state the working representation, so distribution is literal and Naur's pessimism is a distribution effect. READING B: the model holds far more than any navigation instrument ever did, so the pair is closer to an expert consulting a reference than to a distributed assembly, and the criterion is met only trivially.

MISSING: Verified pagination. Any application of Hutchins' criterion to a human-model pair. Any citation between the two instruments, which is the corpus's own oversight.

BOUNDARY: Hutchins studies a bounded task with an external correctness standard. Research work has neither, so the criterion may not transfer even if the analogy is apt.

CITATION TRAIL: [[FORAGE-PT-013]] and [[FORAGE-PT-016]] -> Hutchins 1995 -> the no-single-holder criterion -> next: whether Naur's theory death is a special case of assembly breakdown, which would unify the two instruments and retire one.

TEST: Take a working human-model pair mid-task. Separate them and ask each to reconstruct the working representation alone. Failure by both instantiates distribution; success by either locates the holder and collapses the claim to a tool relation.
