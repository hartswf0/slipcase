TITLE: Disclosure does not undo credence, so labelling generated media is a partial remedy and factual aura has hysteresis

SOURCE: The continued-influence effect — Hollyn Johnson and Colleen Seifert, "Sourcing of discredited information in memory", Journal of Experimental Psychology: Learning, Memory, and Cognition (1994); Stephan Lewandowsky and colleagues on misinformation correction [UNVERIFIED pagination]; read against PROGRAMS/crn.json factual aura

PASSAGE: [PARAPHRASE] This literature reports that information explicitly retracted continues to influence inference and judgement afterwards; correction reduces but does not eliminate reliance on the discredited content, and the residual influence persists even when participants can accurately recall that the information was retracted. [QUOTE] crn.json: "...narrative world-making, factual aura, feedback, and ethical risk"

RESEARCH OBJECT: An asymmetry between acquiring and losing evidential force. The parent proposed measuring aura under frames and asked what happens after disclosure. The continued-influence effect predicts the answer: the curve down is not the curve up. Aura is easier to install than to remove, which makes it a hysteretic quantity rather than a reversible one.

LOCAL MOVE: This child supplies the decay measurement the parent named as missing, and finds a literature that already establishes the decay is incomplete.

SOURCE TERMS: continued influence / retraction / discredited information / correction / source memory / factual aura

WHAT BECAME STRANGE: Provenance labelling is the field's principal remedy for generated media, and it is designed as though credence were reversible. If retraction leaves residue even when the retraction is remembered, then a "generated" label reduces harm without undoing it — and the harm's magnitude depends on the *order* of exposure, which no labelling scheme controls.

QUESTION: How much of the credence installed by an evidential frame survives an explicit generated-content disclosure, and does the residue depend on how long the artifact was believed?

DEEPER QUESTION: If residue grows with the time before disclosure, then the operative variable is *latency to labelling*, not labelling itself — which relocates the whole provenance debate from whether to label to how fast, and makes distribution speed the governing quantity.

MECHANISM: <ARTIFACT IN EVIDENTIAL FRAME> -> [CREDENCE INSTALLED; INFERENCES DRAWN AND STORED] -> [DISCLOSURE: content is generated] -> source tag updated -> [INFERENCES ALREADY DRAWN ARE NOT RETRACTED WITH IT] -> <RESIDUAL INFLUENCE PERSISTS>

FORMAL SHIFT: <CREDENCE> -> <INSTALLED WITH DOWNSTREAM INFERENCES> -> [DISCLOSURE UPDATES THE SOURCE TAG ONLY] -> <HYSTERESIS>

SOURCE FORMALISM: NONE — the effect is reported as behavioural findings, not as a model.

OUR FORMALIZATION: [OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX] h_aura = credence(after disclosure) - credence(never exposed). Reversibility predicts h = 0. Continued influence predicts h > 0 and, plausibly, h increasing in exposure duration and in the number of inferences drawn before disclosure.

TENSION: READING A: aura is a property of the frame and disclosure changes the frame, so credence should fully revert and any residue is a measurement artifact. READING B: credence is not stored as a single tag but distributed across the inferences it licensed, so updating the tag leaves the inferences standing — and the residue is structural.

Reading B is the one the continued-influence findings support, and it predicts something specific: the residue should be larger for artifacts that prompted the viewer to draw explicit conclusions than for those merely seen.

MISSING: Verified pagination. Any application of the paradigm to generated images or video rather than to verbal misinformation. Any measurement of residue against latency to labelling.

BOUNDARY: The effect is established for verbal misinformation in experimental settings. Whether a generated-image disclosure behaves the same way is an extrapolation across both modality and setting.

CITATION TRAIL: [[FORAGE-PT-014]] -> continued-influence literature (retrieve and verify) -> hysteresis in credence -> next: the same asymmetry in label removal generally, where withdrawing a description does not restore the prior route.

TEST: Three groups: unexposed; exposed then disclosed immediately; exposed, allowed to draw explicit inferences, then disclosed. Measure downstream judgements dependent on the content. A gap between group one and group three, larger than between one and two, establishes hysteresis and makes latency the operative variable.
