TITLE: The politics of inhabitation: affordances, and what the shield excludes (slavery, disease, famine)

SOURCE: Dry-Dock/BULKHEAD-07_conclusion.md (Watson's text); Dry-Dock/ARCHIVE/BULKHEAD-03_conclusion.md; KEEL BLOCKS/SYNTH-03_politics-of-inhabitation.md; KEEL BLOCKS/SEED-03_conclusion-evolved.md; KEEL BLOCKS/Ekphrasis to Worldtext_ Generative AI.md (greenhouse politics section)

PASSAGE: [QUOTE, BULKHEAD-07] "The politics of a worldtext will appear not in its explicit content but in its affordances: what it allows, what it remembers, what it makes easy, what it makes impossible, what it treats as natural, and what it renders unthinkable. Homer's shield already stages this. What is present — marriage, law, agriculture, dance — is a claim about what belongs together. What is absent — slavery, disease, famine — is a claim about what the available grammar renders unimaginable." [QUOTE, SYNTH-03] "What is absent — slavery, disease, famine, the lives of women outside ceremony — is a political claim about what can be rendered unthinkable within the available grammar." [QUOTE, SYNTH-03] "The most important power may lie in what a world quietly treats as ordinary, frictionless, and thinkable. When description becomes executable, critique must follow execution all the way down into governance." [QUOTE, SYNTH-03 on the theological register] "The promise of presence and the promise of habitation are, as Davis (2015) argues, theological in disguise." [QUOTE, Ekphrasis-to-Worldtext keel block] "The platform determines the ontology of the possible. If a user attempts to generate an environment that depicts marginalized social structures, unauthorized political assemblies, or un-sanctioned bodies, the platform's safety filters may categorize these as 'impossible actions' and refuse to render them."

RESEARCH OBJECT: A relocation of ideology-critique for generated worlds: from iconography (what the image shows) to affordance (what the world allows) — with Homer's shield as the ancient proof that world-composition is always a selection among forms of life, legible as much in its exclusions as its inclusions. The mechanism of power is named as invisibility: not prohibition but frictionless ordinariness. The Davis line adds a genealogy: the prompt-to-world gesture is the migration of the divine fiat (speaking a world into being) into consumer interfaces.

LOCAL MOVE: Read the shield's famous completeness against itself: list what the "whole Homeric world" omits — slavery, disease, famine, women outside ceremony — and treat each omission as a grammatical impossibility of the worldtext, then map that operation onto safety filters and platform defaults as the modern grammar of the unimaginable.

SOURCE TERMS: affordance (as political category); politics of inhabitation; action grammar; ordinary, frictionless, thinkable; theological in disguise (Davis 2015, TechGnosis); greenhouse politics / algorithmic greenhouse; impossible actions; debris fields of memory (Draft E, replacing "sediment"); sediment; no outside-model (Bajohr).

WHAT BECAME STRANGE: The shield — the paper's emblem of equilibrium and totality — becomes evidence for the opposite: totality is an effect produced by exclusions so deep they read as completeness. The better the worldtext, the more invisible its politics.

QUESTION: Are the shield's exclusions Homer's, the tradition's, or the genre's — and correspondingly: are a platform's exclusions the model's, the corpus's, or the corporation's?

DEEPER QUESTION: Can affordance-critique be practiced from inside a world at all — if power lies in what is frictionless, the critic's own ease of movement is the datum, and the method requires making one's habitation deliberately difficult (a political analog of thick prompting)?

MECHANISM: Training residues + safety filters + physics/movement rules + persistence policies jointly determine permitted life-forms; SYNTH-03's chain: Meyer's "recursive algorithmization of taste" narrows the plausible → Steyerl's mean images instantiate it → Simonen's defaults prove recurrence → world models relocate the narrowing from depiction to affordance ("power enters not only through depiction but through affordance").

FORMAL SHIFT: From representation-critique (decode the image) to governance-critique (audit the action grammar): the critical object is the transition function and its exclusions, not the rendered frame.

SOURCE FORMALISM: The affordance list (allows / remembers / makes easy / makes impossible / treats as natural / renders unthinkable); the presence/absence double claim about the shield.

OUR FORMALIZATION: [OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX] For world W with action grammar A(W): politics(W) = (A(W), cost function over A, complement of A relative to a reference ontology O of social forms). Shield: O = archaic Greek life-forms; complement includes slavery, disease, famine. Platform: O = user-intended worlds; complement = filtered/"impossible actions." Ideology = the subset of the complement not perceived as missing.

TENSION: READING A (Watson, all versions): this is the conclusion's payload — the political argument the whole paper builds toward. READING B (final portugal-draft): the paper ends instead with a media-democratization analogy ("these AI generative systems have done for ekphrasis what the invention of the simple box camera did for photography") and a quality forecast "from mediocre to truly surprising" — a deliberately depoliticized ending. The two endings are incompatible diagnoses of what worldtext ultimately demands: critique of governance vs. history of media expansion.

MISSING: Everything in this zettel is absent from the final draft — Davis, Chun, affordance, the exclusions, "ordinary, frictionless, thinkable," the greenhouse. Also missing everywhere: any classical scholarship on whether slavery IS in fact absent from the shield (the herdsmen and estate laborers are arguably enslaved persons — the exclusion claim needs a philological audit it never receives).

BOUNDARY: The affordance-critique applies to interactive worldtexts; for static images it collapses back into ordinary representation-critique, as the drafts implicitly concede by staging it only in the worldtext sections.

CITATION TRAIL: Davis 2015 (TechGnosis, updated ed.) → Chun 2011 (Programmed Visions: "operation is never innocent") → Bajohr 2024 → Meyer 2025 → Steyerl 2023 → Simonen et al. 2026 → Taplin 1980 (the shield's selectivity).

TEST: Affordance audit of a Genie/Marble world generated from the prompt "Make a Homeric world like the one depicted on the shield of Achilles" (the drafts' own example): enumerate permitted actions and refused generations; compare the refusal set to the shield's exclusion set — the theory predicts structural rhyme, not identity.

PLATFORM: Genie 2/3, World Labs Marble; platform safety-filter layers.
