TITLE:
THE ARCHIVE'S RICOEUR CHAPTER CONTAINS THE DEFINITIONAL CRITERION ITS THEORY CHAPTER HAS BEEN UNABLE TO FIND

SOURCE:
Watson Hartsoe — PAPERS/ricoeur.md §II "From Speech to Text", §III "The Death of Naive Intention", §IV "Action as Inscription" — 2026; and PAPERS/ric-model.md §3 "What Writing Fixes" — 2026

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
The archive's Ricoeur reconstruction holds that inscription detaches an utterance from its speaker, its original audience, and its situation; the inscribed meaning becomes semantically autonomous and available to rival interpretation.

[QUOTE]
ric-model.md §3 heading: "What Writing Fixes"

RESEARCH OBJECT:
Distanciation is not a mood or a literary sensibility. It is a *structural condition*, and it is exactly the condition that separates a description from a stimulus, a label from a symptom, and a routing valve from a causal coupling.

The archive filed it under hermeneutics and left the theory chapter without a genus.

LOCAL MOVE:
The Ricoeur papers in the archive are read for their account of *interpretation after the event*. Their definitional payload — what makes something inscribed at all — is never carried across into the operative-description chapters.

SOURCE TERMS:
distanciation

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
Every property the archive's politics requires is a *consequence* of distanciation, and none of them are available without it:

  error requires a token that can be applied to the wrong object
  contestation requires a token available to a second reader
  authority requires a token whose author is identifiable and answerable
  reversibility requires a token that can be removed while the object remains
  auditability requires a token that persists after the act

The political chapters list these as desiderata (label-01 §16). Ricoeur explains why they come as a package.

QUESTION:
If distanciation is the enabling condition of operativity, is the degree of routing power a function of the *degree* of detachment?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Ricoeur's argument cuts both ways: if the inscribed description is semantically autonomous, then it also escapes the *describer's* authority. Does operative description therefore route action in ways its author cannot control — making the archive's politics of "who controls the categories" only half the story?

SOURCE FORMALISM:
NONE. Ricoeur supplies a conceptual apparatus, not a formalism; the archive's reconstruction preserves that.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

Grade detachment along three independent axes:

  d_author   : can the token act without its author present?
  d_object   : can the token be applied to objects the author never saw?
  d_operator : can the token be read by operators the author never anticipated?

Conjecture: routing *reach* increases with all three, while routing *fidelity* to authorial intent decreases with all three.

That conjecture, if true, is the archive's sharpest political finding, and it inverts its current framing. The description that routes most widely is the one its author controls least.

A tool schema shipped in a public SDK is maximally detached on all three axes. So is a Geertzian "control mechanism." So is COSMIC_LAW.md once a second person reads the repository.

TENSION:
READING A: authorial control and routing reach are independent; a platform can scale a category while retaining full control of it.
READING B: they trade off structurally, because scale requires detachment and detachment forfeits control — so platform power over categories is always partly retrospective repair, not command.

Discriminating evidence: cases where a widely adopted category was used in ways its author publicly disavowed, and what remedy was available.

MISSING:
Any treatment in the archive of the description that escapes its author. The whole political apparatus assumes descriptions obey the powerful. Ricoeur says inscriptions do not obey anyone.

BOUNDARY:
Ricoeur's arguments concern human interpretation of inscribed action. Whether a statistical model's use of a token counts as "interpretation" is exactly what the archive's operator debate is about, and importing Ricoeur does not settle it.
