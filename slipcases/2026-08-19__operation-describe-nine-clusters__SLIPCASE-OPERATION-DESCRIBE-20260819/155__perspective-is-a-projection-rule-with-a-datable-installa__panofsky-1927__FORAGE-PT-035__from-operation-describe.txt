ID: FORAGE-PT-035

TITLE: Perspective is a projection rule with a datable installation, so depiction-as-projection has a history and its first case is a gain rather than a loss

SOURCE: Erwin Panofsky, Perspective as Symbolic Form (Die Perspektive als "symbolische Form", 1927) [UNVERIFIED pagination]; read against PROGRAMS/mmt.json and its central command

PASSAGE: [PARAPHRASE] Panofsky argues that linear perspective is not the discovery of how vision works but a historically specific symbolic convention — a construction that had to be adopted, taught and enforced, and against which earlier and later pictorial systems are not failures but different constructions. [QUOTE] mmt.json: "What disciplined arrangement lets one system be laid against another and tested?"

RESEARCH OBJECT: A projection rule whose adoption is dated. The parent asked what happens when a projection rule is lost and could find no worked case. The stronger case runs the other way: a rule being *installed*. Perspective made pictures measurable against space — you can extract distances and positions from a perspectival image and not from a hieratic one — and that capacity arrived at a particular time, with training, treatises and workshops behind it.

LOCAL MOVE: This child replaces the parent's search for a lost rule with a documented acquisition, which supplies the same evidence with better records.

SOURCE TERMS: symbolic form / perspective construction / convention / pictorial space / projection

WHAT BECAME STRANGE: If perspective is a convention rather than a discovery, then measurability is not a property pictures have but a capacity a culture builds and can decline to build. The parent's claim that a model is only a model while its rule is held becomes historically sharper: for most of the history of images, the rule that would make them measurable did not exist, and nobody experienced this as a lack.

QUESTION: What measurements does a perspectival image license that a pre-perspectival one does not, and can that difference be stated as a set of recoverable quantities?

DEEPER QUESTION: If installing a projection rule is what makes a pictorial system measurable, then the correct historical question about generative models is not what they depict but *which projection rule they have installed* — and unlike perspective, nobody wrote its treatise.

MECHANISM: <SPACE> -> [PERSPECTIVE CONSTRUCTION APPLIED: vanishing point, horizon, foreshortening rules] -> <IMAGE> -> [INVERSE CONSTRUCTION BY A TRAINED READER] -> recovered positions and distances -> <IMAGE FUNCTIONS AS A MEASURE>

FORMAL SHIFT: <PICTURE> -> <PICTURE UNDER AN INSTALLED PROJECTION RULE> -> [INVERSE PROJECTION] -> <RECOVERABLE QUANTITIES>

SOURCE FORMALISM: Panofsky discusses the geometry of the perspective construction historically; no formal apparatus is quoted here.

OUR FORMALIZATION: [OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX] measurable(image) = the set of world quantities recoverable by applying a known inverse projection. Perspective makes that set non-empty for spatial quantities. The question for any depiction system, generative included, is which set is non-empty — and for current image models the honest answer is that no inverse is specified.

TENSION: READING A (Panofsky as used here): perspective is convention, so measurability is culturally installed and the parent's community-indexed account of depiction is vindicated historically. READING B: perspective approximates the actual optics of projection, so its adoption was a discovery constrained by physics rather than a free convention — in which case not all projection rules are equally available and the account needs a realism term.

Panofsky's thesis is itself contested on exactly this point, which is why the tension is live rather than rhetorical.

MISSING: Verified passages. Any statement of which quantities a generative image licenses recovering — the direct analogue question, unasked.

BOUNDARY: Panofsky is a contested art-historical thesis. Using it to license a general claim that projection rules are conventional would overstate a disputed source.

CITATION TRAIL: [[FORAGE-PT-008]] -> Panofsky 1927 -> projection as installed convention -> next: the critical literature on Panofsky's conventionalism, and the technical question of what inverse projection, if any, a diffusion model implements.

TEST: Take one pre-perspectival and one perspectival depiction of a comparable scene. Ask trained readers to recover specified spatial quantities from each. The difference in recoverable set is projection doing work; equal recovery would falsify the parent's account.
