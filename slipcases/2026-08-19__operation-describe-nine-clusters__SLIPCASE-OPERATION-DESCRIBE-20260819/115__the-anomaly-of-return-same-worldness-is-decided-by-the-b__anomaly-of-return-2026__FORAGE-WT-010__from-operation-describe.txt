TITLE: The anomaly of return: same-worldness is decided by the backward glance, scored on Mythos/Topos/Ethos

SOURCE: worldtext/syntheses/anomaly-of-return.md — "Core Thesis", "The Test / Formal Statement", "The Sora Problem", "The Zork Contrast", "The Deeper Claim"

PASSAGE: [QUOTE] "The definitive test of world coherence is not forward motion. It is the backward glance." [QUOTE] "Below the threshold: you have a **slideshow** — a sequence of images sharing an aesthetic. Above the threshold: you have a **world** — a persistent, navigable, inhabitable reality."

RESEARCH OBJECT: A minimum viable definition of "world": a generated construct passes iff, after a sequence of generative operations, the earlier state remains inferrable, no entity has been involuntarily annihilated, no law silently contradicted, and a return probe yields a recognizable re-entry.

LOCAL MOVE: The file inverts the evaluation direction of generative media: instead of judging the newest output (forward fluency), it judges what the newest output did to the *oldest* state — coherence is retrodictive.

SOURCE TERMS: Anomaly of return; return probe; same-worldness; Mythos/Topos/Ethos (from Tosca & Klastrup); entity ledger (identity fields vs. state fields); continuity gate; return index; silent annihilation; retroactive contradiction; style-over-logic illusion; flattening of myth; ontological threshold.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE: The Zork/Sora inversion: [QUOTE] "Zork has zero visual fidelity and absolute topological fidelity. Sora has maximum visual fidelity and zero topological fidelity." A 1977 text adventure outranks a frontier video model on the axis that decides whether there is a world at all. [QUOTE] "visual quality and world coherence are orthogonal properties."

QUESTION: What counts as "recognizable" in the return probe — is recognizability operator-judged (subjective, per-session) or specifiable as invariant preservation (objective, ledger-checkable)?

DEEPER QUESTION: The fourth failure mode, "Flattening of Myth" (systemic pressure resolves all ambiguity into singular facts, destroying a mystery's "mythic function"), implies some world-content must *never* be fully determined. Can a state-machine world (Zork-style, fully enumerated) therefore ever host myth — or is there a formal class of world-facts whose coherence condition is preserved indeterminacy?

MECHANISM: The operational protocol: (1) stop forward motion; (2) issue return prompt "Describe [previous location/character/event] as it exists now"; (3) compare against established world-state; (4) score on three axes — Mythos (backstory holds), Topos (physical laws hold), Ethos (characters remain themselves); (5) any axis fails → continuity fracture → repair or reject. [QUOTE] "Failure on **any single axis** means the artifact does not belong to the world."

FORMAL SHIFT: From world-quality as a scalar aesthetic score to world-existence as a binary threshold predicate evaluated by state-recovery — worldhood becomes testable per artifact.

SOURCE FORMALISM: [QUOTE] "Given a generated world-state **W** at time **t₀**, and a sequence of generative operations **O₁, O₂, ... Oₙ** producing states **W₁, W₂, ... Wₙ**: The world passes the anomaly of return if and only if: **W₀** remains inferrable from **Wₙ**; No entity present in **W₀** has been involuntarily annihilated...; No law governing **W₀** has been silently contradicted...; The operator can issue a **return probe** ... and receive a response that is **recognizable** as belonging to the same world." Plus required architecture: entity ledger, continuity gate, return index.

OUR FORMALIZATION: [OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX] Return(W₀, Wₙ) ≡ ∃ decoder d: d(Wₙ, probe) ≈ W₀|probe under an equivalence ≈ parameterized by the three axes: ≈ = ≈_M ∧ ≈_T ∧ ≈_E. This is a conjunction of per-axis bisimulation-like conditions; "inferrable" makes worldhood a property of the pair (system, interrogation protocol), not of outputs alone.

TENSION: "W₀ remains inferrable from Wₙ" and "no entity involuntarily annihilated" pull apart: a world can preserve inferability while entities legitimately die (voluntary annihilation? narrative cause?). The qualifier "involuntarily" smuggles authorial intent into an otherwise state-based test. Rival reading: the test is really about *cause-tracked change* vs. *silent change*, not persistence per se.

MISSING: Probe sampling policy (how many probes, chosen how, before declaring a pass?); quantitative scoring (the axes are pass/fail per artifact but no corpus-level metric); treatment of worlds whose laws include forgetting (dream logic, unreliable narration).

BOUNDARY: Tests only what the operator thinks to probe — a return index makes retrieval efficient but cannot enumerate the silently contradicted laws nobody re-queries; the test's recall is bounded by probe coverage.

CITATION TRAIL: anomaly-of-return.md → Tosca & Klastrup "Transmedial Worlds" (2004: Mythos/Topos/Ethos) → Wolf, Building Imaginary Worlds (2012: Invention/Completeness/Consistency) → Aarseth, Cybertext (1997: traversal function) → NATURAL SIGN Docs A & B → continuity-debt.md ("Return Probe" as detection strategy).

TEST: Directly checkable: run return probes against Sora-class video models and against stateful text engines; the file predicts categorical failure for the former ("the room behind the camera does not exist") and categorical success for Zork — an executable benchmark contrast.

PLATFORM: Model-agnostic evaluation protocol; requires only prompt access and a recorded world-state history.
