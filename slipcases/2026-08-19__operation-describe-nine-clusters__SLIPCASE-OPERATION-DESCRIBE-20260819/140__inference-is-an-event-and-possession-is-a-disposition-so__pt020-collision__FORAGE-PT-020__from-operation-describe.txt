ZETTEL

ID: FORAGE-PT-020

TITLE: Inference is an event and possession is a disposition, so a system could accumulate achievements it never comes to possess

SOURCE: Collision of PROGRAMS/argue.json (inference as achievement) with PROGRAMS/rcp.json (possession as mastery across an indefinite field); the pairing is registered in PROGRAMS/meta.json <cross_references>

PASSAGE: [QUOTE] meta.json: "argue.json <-> rcp.json (achievement vs. possession)" [QUOTE] argue.json: "<person> [comes-to-see] <something> before <person> [can-publicly-state] <why-it-follows>" [QUOTE] rcp.json: "<concept-possession> [is] <operational-mastery-across-an-indefinite-task-field>"

RESEARCH OBJECT: A type difference the corpus flags and never works out. An achievement is dated and singular: at some moment the conclusion is drawn. A disposition is undated and general: across occasions the actor can handle the unforeseen case. Nothing guarantees that achievements aggregate into a disposition.

LOCAL MOVE: This zettel takes the cross-reference meta.json declares and asks what follows if the two are genuinely different categories rather than two aspects of one thing.

SOURCE TERMS: achievement / comes-to-see / possession / operational mastery / indefinite task field / factor-in-use

WHAT BECAME STRANGE: A system that solves each instance freshly, with no carry-over, would produce a long record of genuine achievements and possess nothing. That is not a deficient thinker — it is a *different* profile from both the competent possessor and the fluent bluffer, and neither instrument in the corpus can name it.

The corpus has two categories where it needs three: possession, bluffing, and achievement-without-accumulation.

QUESTION: Do successes on a concept family correlate across occasions more than chance — and does that correlation grow with exposure, as a disposition should?

DEEPER QUESTION: If achievements never aggregate, then the fluency critique is aimed at the wrong target. The problem would not be that output outruns thought; it would be that thought occurs and does not *stick*, leaving nothing behind to be mastered.

MECHANISM: <TASK> -> [ACHIEVEMENT: conclusion drawn] -> no residue retained -> <NEXT TASK> -> [ACHIEVEMENT AGAIN, FROM SCRATCH] -> record of successes, no disposition formed, no transfer to unforeseen cases

FORMAL SHIFT: <DATED EVENT> -> <ACCUMULATION?> -> [TEST FOR CARRY-OVER] -> <DISPOSITION OR MERE SEQUENCE>

SOURCE FORMALISM: rcp's six possession verbs; argue's separation of inference from argument.

OUR FORMALIZATION: [OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX] Three profiles, distinguished by two measurements — within-family success correlation across occasions (rho) and transfer to out-of-field cases (tau):
POSSESSOR: rho high, tau high. BLUFFER: rho low, tau low, fluency high. ACHIEVER-WITHOUT-POSSESSION: rho low, tau low, *success* high. The third is currently invisible to both instruments.

TENSION: READING A: repeated achievement *is* possession; a disposition is nothing but a reliable pattern of achievements, so the distinction dissolves. READING B: possession requires handling the unforeseen, which no number of in-distribution achievements demonstrates — so the distinction is real and the third profile exists.

MISSING: Any measurement of rho. The corpus tests capability per task and never tests carry-over across tasks.

BOUNDARY: This is a claim about what two instruments can distinguish, not a claim about any system's actual profile.

CITATION TRAIL: Ryle, The Concept of Mind, on dispositions versus occurrences — the distinction this collision turns on. Ryle, "Thinking and Inferring" (1953). [[FORAGE-PT-001]] [[FORAGE-PT-002]]

TEST: One concept family, twenty tasks, repeated sessions with no memory carried between them. Compute success correlation across occasions and transfer to out-of-field items. A high-success, low-correlation, low-transfer profile is the third category, observed.
