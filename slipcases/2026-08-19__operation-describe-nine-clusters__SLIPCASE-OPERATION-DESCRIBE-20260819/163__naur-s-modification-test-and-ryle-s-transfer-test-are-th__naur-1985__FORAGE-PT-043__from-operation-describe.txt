TITLE: Naur's modification test and Ryle's transfer test are the same test, and both are unbounded for the same reason

SOURCE: PROGRAMS/theory.json (modification without breaking coherence) read against PROGRAMS/rcp.json (mastery across an indefinite task field); Naur, "Programming as Theory Building" (1985) and Ryle, The Concept of Mind (1949) [UNVERIFIED pagination]

PASSAGE: [QUOTE] theory.json: "<program_theory> is the living possession that lets <team> [explain], [justify], [modify], [teach" [QUOTE] rcp.json: "<concept-possession> [is] <operational-mastery-across-an-indefinite-task-field>"

RESEARCH OBJECT: An identity between two tests the corpus treats as belonging to different lineages. Modifying a system without breaking its coherence requires handling a change nobody anticipated. Transfer requires handling a task outside the sampled field. Both are demands to perform correctly on an item the tester did not supply, and both are therefore unbounded in exactly the same way.

LOCAL MOVE: This child collapses two criteria into one, which halves the corpus's evaluation problem and doubles its urgency: there is one unsolved measurement, not two.

SOURCE TERMS: modify / explain / justify / teach / coherence / operational mastery / indefinite task field / transfer

WHAT BECAME STRANGE: The corpus has two lineages each independently arriving at an unbounded criterion, and neither notices that the unboundedness is the same. That convergence is stronger evidence for the criterion than either source alone — a demand that shows up in software practice and in philosophy of mind, from different premises, is likelier to be tracking something real than a demand invented in one place.

QUESTION: Is there a single instrument that measures both — a task family where "modify without breaking" and "transfer beyond the sample" are the same operation?

DEEPER QUESTION: If the two are one, then the corpus's four possession verbs and its four practice verbs reduce. Explain, justify and teach are all producible without the capacity; modify and transfer are the same capacity. The set of eight verbs may contain exactly one diagnostic and seven displays.

MECHANISM: <SYSTEM OR CONCEPT HELD> -> [NOVEL DEMAND NOT SUPPLIED BY THE TESTER] -> response must satisfy constraints the holder was never told -> [COHERENCE JUDGED BY THE HOLDER OR THE DOMAIN] -> <CAPACITY EVIDENCED OR DEFEATED>

FORMAL SHIFT: <TWO CRITERIA IN TWO LINEAGES> -> <ONE UNBOUNDED DEMAND> -> [SINGLE INSTRUMENT] -> <ONE MEASUREMENT PROBLEM>

SOURCE FORMALISM: Naur's four verbs; rcp's six verbs. Neither supplies a procedure.

OUR FORMALIZATION: [OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX] One protocol: present an item drawn from outside everything the subject was given, require a response that satisfies unstated coherence constraints, have the constraint-holder judge blind. Score. This is simultaneously a Naur test and a Ryle test, and its result licenses claims in both vocabularies.

TENSION: READING A: the tests are identical and the corpus should retire one vocabulary. READING B: they differ in what supplies the coherence standard — for Naur it is a designed artifact with an author who can adjudicate; for Ryle it is a practice with no adjudicator. Where no one can rule, transfer has no ground truth, and only the software case is actually measurable.

Reading B is the more useful discovery: it means the software case is the *only* one of the two that can be run, which is why the corpus should run it there first.

MISSING: Any instance of either test being run. Any account of who adjudicates coherence when the domain has no author.

BOUNDARY: The identity claimed here is structural. Whether the two traditions would accept the collapse is a separate question neither source addresses.

CITATION TRAIL: [[FORAGE-PT-001]] and [[FORAGE-PT-016]] -> the shared unboundedness -> single protocol -> next: [[FORAGE-PT-026]] on whether a constraint set changes the result, since constraint coverage is precisely what bounds the unforeseen.

TEST: Run the single protocol on a designed artifact with a living author: stranger, novel modification demand, blind coherence judgement. Then attempt the same on a practice with no adjudicator and observe that the scoring step cannot be completed. The asymmetry is the finding.
