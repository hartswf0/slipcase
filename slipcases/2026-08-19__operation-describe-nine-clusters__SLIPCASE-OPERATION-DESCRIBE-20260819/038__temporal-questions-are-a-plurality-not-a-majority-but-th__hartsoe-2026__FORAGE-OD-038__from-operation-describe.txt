RESEARCH OBJECT:
The parent zettel predicted a majority and found a plurality. The interesting finding is not the level but the slope.

Over sixteen days of ingest, the archive's open questions became progressively more temporal. The frontier is not static and mis-specified; it is moving, and it is moving toward the clock.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The archive's earliest questions are about *ontology* — is latent space a place, are world models ekphrastic, is the reader a diffusion engine. Its latest are about *rates* — at what generation rate does epistemic debt become irreversible, when does generation outpace coherence, how much contradiction before collapse.

Nobody decided this. The vocabulary of the questions changed under the pressure of what the archive was building. That drift is itself evidence about how a research programme's problems are routed by its own accumulated objects — which is, precisely, the archive's own thesis operating on the archive.

QUESTION:
Is the drift toward temporal questions caused by the objects the archive built (world bibles, entity ledgers, continuity gates all have state and therefore rates), or by the sources it ingested in late April?

DEEPER QUESTION:
If building stateful objects generates temporal questions, then research problems are routed by artifacts rather than chosen by researchers — and the archive's practice-based method is not a way of investigating routing but an instance of being routed.

OUR FORMALIZATION:

  τ_b = share of temporal questions in batch b

  observed: τ = (0.33, 0.25, 0.40, 0.50, 0.60) over batches 1…5

The 04-13 batch breaks monotonicity, so the honest claim is a rise from batch 2 onward, on five points and small n. This is a suggestive slope, not an established trend, and it should be reported as such.

Falsifiable continuation: any future batch of questions should show τ ≥ 0.6 if the drift is driven by stateful objects, since the objects are still present and accumulating.

TENSION:
READING A (drift is real and object-driven): the archive built stateful things, and stateful things break over time, so the questions followed. Prediction: τ keeps rising, and it rises fastest after commits that add persistence machinery.
READING B (drift is source-driven and coincidental): late April happened to ingest Aarseth, Wolf, and the continuity-debt material, which are temporal literatures. Prediction: τ falls if the next ingests are ontological.

Discriminating evidence: correlate τ per batch against whether that batch's ingest added persistent objects (atlas rows for places, ledgers, gates) versus conceptual entities. The atlas records both, dated.

MISSING:
Question batches after 2026-04-28. The atlas has not been recompiled since; the git history's last commit is 2026-06-04 and the PAPERS corpus grew without the atlas being updated.

So the archive's frontier register stopped four months ago while its paper corpus continued. That divergence is a second finding: the compiled worldtext and the writing practice came apart, and COSMIC_LAW §6 step 10 ("Update atlas.md") stopped routing.

That is a second measured instance of a self-addressed description with ΔG → 0 over time — a decay, not a null. The banned-terms list failed immediately (FORAGE-OD-023); the atlas-update law worked for six weeks and then lapsed.

Two self-addressed laws, two different failure profiles. That is the beginning of an actual dataset.
