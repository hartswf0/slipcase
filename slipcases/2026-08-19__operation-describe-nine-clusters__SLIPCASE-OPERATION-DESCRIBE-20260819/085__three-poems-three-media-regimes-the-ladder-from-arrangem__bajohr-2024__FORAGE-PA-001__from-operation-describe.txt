ID: FORAGE-PA-001

TITLE: Three poems, three media regimes: the ladder from arrangement to execution to correlation

SOURCE: PAPERS/bajohr.md (identical byte-for-byte to PAPERS/tenne.md), section 2 "Three Poems, Three Media Regimes"

PASSAGE: [QUOTE] "The three works therefore mark three media regimes: 1. **Analogue visual poetry:** text becomes image through manual and mechanical arrangement. 2. **Sequential digital poetry:** code performs image through executable syntax. 3. **Connectionist AI image generation:** prompt becomes image through learned multimodal correlation. ... The analogue answer is: through embodied arrangement. The sequential answer is: through executable operation. The connectionist answer is: through artificial semantics."

RESEARCH OBJECT: A three-rung comparative apparatus built from three concrete artworks — Franz Mon's typewriter poem "non/tot" (analogue), Jasmin Meerhoff's script-generated *They Lay* (sequential digital), and Dave Orr's DALL-E 2 output "Stiny Snity Grify" from the prompt "a poem about the singularity written in a serif font" (connectionist). Each rung answers "how can text become image?" with a different mechanism, and the third rung yields asemic pseudo-writing: "the image of textuality without producing coherent text."

LOCAL MOVE: Uses visual poetry as a "compact archive" to periodize digitality itself: instead of one analogue/digital divide, the paper splits the digital into sequential and connectionist paradigms and shows each produces a different ontology of the text-image relation.

SOURCE TERMS: operative ekphrasis; media regimes; sequential paradigm; connectionist paradigm; asemic writing; visual Gestalt of a poem; substrate-aware aesthetics

WHAT BECAME STRANGE: The category "digital" — a shell script arranging fragments and a diffusion model answering a prompt are both "digital" yet belong to different conceptual orders. Also strange: Orr's output "looks like writing while failing as writing," revealing the model knows writing as look before it knows writing as language.

QUESTION: Which regime does a given text-to-image production actually belong to, and what diagnostic (determinism, inspectability, failure signature) settles it?

DEEPER QUESTION: Is asemic output the connectionist regime's native signature — the place where a model's learned Gestalt of a genre becomes visible precisely because its semantics fails?

MECHANISM: Regime 1: bodily/mechanical arrangement (keystrokes, spacing). Regime 2: a hidden operative text (script) deterministically produces a visible text-object; "the visible object is the residue of execution" (via Hayles's processual text). Regime 3: a prompt activates learned image-caption correlations in shared representational space; output is probabilistic synthesis, not rule-following transformation.

FORMAL SHIFT: From a two-term analogue/digital periodization to a three-term regime ladder in which the middle term (executable code) preserves the text/image distinction and the third term weakens it inside the substrate.

SOURCE FORMALISM: The numbered three-regime list and its paired answer-triple (embodied arrangement / executable operation / artificial semantics).

OUR FORMALIZATION: [OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX] regime(w) = ⟨producer, determinism, locus-of-fusion⟩: R1 = ⟨hand+machine, n/a, viewer-perception⟩; R2 = ⟨script, deterministic, viewer-perception⟩; R3 = ⟨model, probabilistic, latent-substrate⟩. The regimes differ in WHERE text/image fuse: for R1–R2 fusion happens in the human viewer; for R3 it happens pre-output, in the embedding.

TENSION: icids-imagetext-to-worldtext.md extends this ladder past the image to a fourth object (worldtext: persistent navigable simulation), while imagetext-to-worldtext-clean.md argues the ancient rhetorical ekphrasis was "already operative" (verbal input producing visual experience via the listener's storehouse of images) — which undercuts bajohr.md's framing that operativity is what's new; on the clean paper's reading the modern representational definition was the anomaly.

MISSING: Any account of hybrid regimes (e.g., a deterministic pipeline invoking a model as one stage — exactly what ripples-perspectival-finitude.md builds); no criteria for outputs that are legible text.

BOUNDARY: The ladder holds for text→image production; it says nothing about image→text (captioning) or text→text generation, where the same three regimes presumably recur but are not mapped.

CITATION TRAIL: Bajohr (operative ekphrasis, artificial semantics); Lessing, *Laocoön*; Heffernan; W.J.T. Mitchell; Hayles (processual electronic text); Franz Mon "non/tot"; Jasmin Meerhoff *They Lay*; Dave Orr "Stiny Snity Grify" (DALL-E 2).

TEST: Take one target artifact and produce it three ways (hand-arranged, scripted, prompted); check whether the predicted failure signatures differ as the ladder claims (R2 fails syntactically/crashes; R3 fails asemically/plausibly).

PLATFORM: Any multimodal text-to-image system (DALL-E, Stable Diffusion, CLIP-guided pipelines); typewriter and shell script as contrast cases.
