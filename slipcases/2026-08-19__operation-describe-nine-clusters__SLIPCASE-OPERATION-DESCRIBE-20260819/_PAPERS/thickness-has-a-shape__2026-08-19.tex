\title{\textbf{Thickness Has a Shape}\\[.35em]\large Depth, Ceiling, and the Inverse Relation Between Rich Description and Established Reference}
\author{Watson Hartsoe}
\date{2026-08-19 \quad\textemdash\quad Working Paper}
\begin{document}\maketitle
\begin{abstract}\noindent Descriptive thickness is used across several literatures as a quality and never as a quantity. This paper gives it a shape. Rendering Geertz's wink as production rules exposes what the prose keeps implicit: the levels are recursive, each rung adding one intention about the rung below, so thickness has a natural unit --- the depth of intentional embedding a correct description requires. Three constraints then follow from work that has never been connected to the concept. Research on nested mental-state attribution reports a ceiling of roughly four to five orders, which means the ladder terminates and ``turtles all the way down'' is false in a testable way. Annotation practice reports that agreement falls as inference about intent deepens, which makes disagreement a candidate estimator of thickness rather than a defect of scheme --- and implies that methods selecting for high reliability systematically select against thick cases. And referential communication research reports that partners describing the same referent repeatedly converge on progressively shorter labels, which inverts the usual valuation: elaborate description is what speakers produce \emph{before} a convention exists, not evidence of care. We conclude that thickness is not a property of a description but of a relation between describer, receiver and shared history, and we identify the timescale band --- the negotiated pact --- that this makes visible.\end{abstract}
\vspace{-.5em}\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} thick description, Geertz, orders of intentionality, inter-annotator agreement, conceptual pacts, enargeia, reference, timescales\par\vspace{1em}
\section{A unit hiding in a rule set}
Geertz's wink is the founding example of thick description and it is usually read as an illustration of interpretive richness. Written as production rules, it turns out to be a recursion.

An eyelid contraction is a physical event. A wink is an intention about that contraction. A parody of a wink is an intention about the wink. A rehearsal of a parody is an intention about the parody. Each rung takes the rung below as its object, and a correct description must hold the whole stack.

That gives thickness a unit: the depth of intentional embedding required. Contraction is zero, wink is one, parody is two, rehearsal is three. Nothing in the rules stops rung five, which is presumably what recommends the image of turtles all the way down.

Two literatures that have never been applied to the concept say the ladder is bounded and that its depth is measurable from disagreement. A third says its value has been misread.

\section{The ladder terminates}
Research on higher-order intentionality reports that performance on tasks requiring nested attribution of mental states degrades sharply beyond a small number of orders, with a commonly cited ceiling in the region of four to five levels for typical adults.

If that holds, interpretation is not an unbounded regress. There is a floor to the turtles and it is cognitive. Any description operating above the ceiling is not thicker but unreadable, and its apparent depth is produced by the analyst's notation rather than recovered from the act.

Two ceilings should be distinguished, and their possible difference is the interesting part. Call $C_{\text{read}}$ the maximum depth recoverable from a trace and $C_{\text{produce}}$ the maximum depth an actor can construct. If production exceeds comprehension, then acts are routinely performed at depths no audience can recover, and the surplus is systematically lost --- not because ethnographers lack thickness but because everyone does.

A serious objection: the experimental paradigms measure explicit reasoning under artificial load, and skilled readers may handle deeper stacks fluently in familiar contexts. The discriminating test is expertise --- if depth recovery rises in a domain the reader knows well, the cap is domain-relative rather than universal.

\section{Disagreement as the estimator}
Annotation practice computes agreement coefficients and treats low agreement as evidence that a category is not reliably applicable. Agreement is routinely high for surface labelling and low for tasks requiring inference about intent, irony or stance.

Placed against the depth account, this admits a different interpretation. Low agreement may not be a defect of the scheme but a \emph{signature of the phenomenon}. A genuinely depth-three act should produce disagreement, because recovering three nested intentions from a trace underdetermines the reading.

If agreement falls monotonically with required depth, two things follow. First, thickness can be estimated \emph{from} disagreement, with no depth annotation at all --- only multiple readers. Second, and more uncomfortably, any method that selects for high reliability selects against thick cases. Quantitative content analysis would then have been filtering out precisely the material that interpretive description exists to reach, and doing so by a criterion it regards as a virtue.

There is a clean discriminating test. If extended training closes the agreement gap on shallow items but not on deep ones, the deep disagreement is real underdetermination rather than a training deficit --- and forcing agreement at depth would mean coercing readers into a convention rather than measuring an act.

A consequence for the validation of interpretation: accounts that require plural rival readings over social time to validate an interpretation will find that their formula returns most readily for shallow traces and diverges for deep ones. The formula works best where least is at stake.

\section{Thickness measures missing convention}
The third constraint reverses the concept's valuation, and it comes from a paradigm that has been running since the 1960s.

In director--matcher tasks, participants describing the same hard-to-name figure across repeated trials converge on progressively shorter labels. The abbreviated label works for the partner who helped establish it and fails for a new partner.

Elaborate description is therefore what speakers produce while a convention is \emph{absent}. Once a pact exists, a short label carries the same referential work. Thickness measures the lack of shared history, not the presence of care.

This is the same shape as a finding from an entirely different literature: where a name is already legible to a receiver, adding description contributes almost nothing, and description does its work precisely where the name fails. Psycholinguistics and machine tool-selection have arrived at one relation from opposite directions.

The consequence is that thickness cannot be a property of a description. It is a property of the triple: describer, receiver, and history between them. No measure that scores a text alone can capture it, and the six-layer constraint stacks that circulate as method are startup costs rather than achievements.

The counter-reading is worth preserving. Compression may discard content that mattered --- the short label may work only across a narrow range and fail on a variant of the referent where the elaborate description would have held. That is testable: after a pact forms, try the abbreviation on a variant.

\section{The band this makes visible}
Accounts of symbolic control in this area run between two timescales with nothing in between. At one end, a single act is read now. At the other, cultural programs acquired across a lifetime complete an unfinished animal. Both are called programs, and the word carries an eight-order-of-magnitude equivocation: one sense is closer to a compiler, the other to an instruction.

Pacts occupy the missing band. They form in minutes, compress reference for particular partners, and dissolve when the partnership changes. And they have a property neither neighbour has: they are \emph{jointly authored}. A wink is read; a culture is inherited; a pact is built by two parties who could each have proposed otherwise.

That makes the middle band the only scale at which both vocabularies apply --- someone is steering and something is being negotiated. It also predicts an observable transition. A prompt library is a sedimented pact: a formula that outlived its negotiation and is now inherited by users who did not build it. The move from the pact band to the constitutive band should therefore be visible in prompt-sharing communities, where a jointly refined formula becomes an inherited convention with no negotiator present.

\section{An instrument the tradition already had}
If thickness is convergence across receivers rather than structure in the text, the measurement is old.

Enargeia was not defined as a property of words but by an effect on an audience: the hearer is brought to the condition of a spectator. The criterion came with a procedure --- ask what they saw. That is a public test for descriptive power, and it predates every apparatus currently used to evaluate prompts.

Operationalised, it gives thickness as specificity times concordance across receivers, computable for human readers and for repeated machine samples. The cross-substrate quantity is then the divergence between the two, and every description far from the diagonal is a case where the same words route two kinds of receiver differently.

It also puts literary and instructional value on a collision course. Barthes valorised the writerly text, which maximises divergence among readers. Prompt design valorises convergence. If thickness is convergence, then literary value and instructional value point in opposite directions, and a project that wants both is pursuing two goals under one word.

\section{Limitations}
The depth unit is legible in a rule set because the rule set was written; Geertz does not quantify, and the recursion is our reading of a formalisation rather than his claim.

The mentalizing ceiling, the agreement findings and the pact results are all invoked at the level of recurring reported patterns. Specific studies, order counts, coefficients and pagination were not retrieved for this paper, and each is marked accordingly in the source map. No depth-stratified agreement study exists to our knowledge; the monotonic prediction is untested.

The pact paradigm concerns human dyads with a shared task, mutual feedback and a fixed referent. Extending it to open-ended generative prompting crosses several boundaries at once, and whether a model participates in ratification or merely accommodates is unresolved --- which is exactly what the joint-authorship claim turns on.
