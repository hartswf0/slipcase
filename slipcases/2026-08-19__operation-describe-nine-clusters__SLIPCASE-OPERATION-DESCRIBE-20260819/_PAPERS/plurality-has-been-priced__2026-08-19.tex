\title{\textbf{Plurality Has Been Priced}\\[.35em]\large Corroboration Rules as Estimates of How Many Independent Readings Validation Requires}
\author{Watson Hartsoe}
\date{2026-08-19 \quad\textemdash\quad Working Paper}
\begin{document}\maketitle
\begin{abstract}\noindent A well-known formula for interpreting inscribed action holds that an action, plus its trace, plus social time, plus rival readings, produces a validated interpretation --- with the honest addition that validation never reaches certainty. This paper takes the formula literally and observes that two of its four arguments are unavailable to a single reader working now, which means a solitary immediate interpretation is not an invalid interpretation but not yet an interpretation at all. That indicts a great deal of contemporary reading practice without naming it. But the formula leaves its two crucial parameters unspecified: how many independent readings, and how much time. We argue that both have already been estimated, repeatedly and expensively, by the one institution that must reconstruct vanished events and pays for getting it wrong. Adversarial testimonial procedure --- multiple independent accounts, cross-examination, corroboration thresholds, standards of proof, and mechanisms for impeaching a single account --- is not a metaphor for the formula but an implementation of it with the parameters set by rule. Reading the procedure as an estimate yields a further result the formula lacks: traces are treated as differentially reliable rather than as uniform rescues, which the hermeneutic account has no term for.\end{abstract}
\vspace{-.5em}\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} hermeneutics, validation, corroboration, testimony, inter-annotator agreement, Ricoeur, inscription, interpretation\par\vspace{1em}
\section{A formula with missing arguments}
The account runs: an event disappears, a trace remains, the trace becomes readable, the reading becomes contested, contestation demands validation, and validation never becomes absolute certainty. Compressed: action plus trace plus social time plus rival readings yields a validated interpretation.

Social time and rival readings are not desiderata in this formula. They are arguments. Remove them and the function does not return.

The consequence is unflattering and unstated. A single reader producing an interpretation in one sitting has supplied two of four arguments. Their output is not a poor interpretation; by the formula's own structure it has not yet entered the process that would make it one. This applies as much to a scholar working alone with a text as to a machine-assisted reading produced in a session, and the formula was written long before the second case existed.

What the formula does not supply is the two numbers: the minimum count of genuinely independent readings, call it $k$, and the minimum elapsed time, call it $\tau$.

\section{The rescue with no stated loss}
Behind the formula sits a stronger claim: speech vanishes as event and writing rescues what was said; action vanishes as event and the trace rescues what was done; meaning does not live inside intention but survives as inscribed public consequence.

Stated with that symmetry, it is a conservation law, and the loss term is never written. Every trace is partial by construction --- a record holds what it was built to hold. So what is rescued is not the deed but a projection of it, and the projection was chosen by whoever built the recorder.

This matters for the formula. If the invariant across independent traces is large, plural reading converges and validation is cheap. If it is thin, agreement across traces may reflect shared recording conventions rather than the event, and plurality is doing less work than it appears to.

The size of that invariant is an empirical question. It has an answer wherever one event is multiply recorded, and nobody in this literature has gone to look.

\section{The institution that set the parameters}
The hermeneutic tradition invokes a courtroom of interpretation as a figure for validation. The figure is closer to the truth than it is usually taken to be: the courtroom is not a metaphor for the formula but an implementation of it, and the two missing parameters are set there by rule.

Legal systems that must reconstruct a vanished event do not rely on a single record. They take multiple independent accounts, subject each to adversarial questioning, and treat agreement across independent accounts as the ground of reliability while treating any single account as insufficient. Corroboration requirements and standards of proof are stated rules; they vary by jurisdiction and matter type; and they were arrived at by adjustment against error over a long period.

Read as estimates, a corroboration rule is a value for $k$ and a standard of proof is a threshold on what plural agreement licenses. On that reading $k$ is not unknown. It has been set, revised and litigated for centuries in the institution that bears the cost of being wrong.

The existence and elaborateness of the procedure is itself evidence about the invariant. If a single trace rescued the deed, cross-examination would be redundant and corroboration pointless. Practitioners who must actually reconstruct events found the single-trace invariant too thin to rely on.

Two things must be conceded. Legal reliability rules are shaped by policy, cost and rights as much as by epistemics, so reading them as measurements of an epistemic quantity requires an argument this paper sketches rather than completes. And the procedure is adversarial partly because parties have interests: remove the incentive to distort and a single honest record might be adequate, in which case the procedure measures adversarial pressure rather than inscriptional loss. That is a real rival and it is testable --- compare independent contemporaneous records of an uncontested event and see how much they overlap.

\section{What the formula lacks}
Legal procedure supplies one thing the hermeneutic account has no term for: a mechanism for impeaching a trace.

Testimony can be discredited. Some accounts are treated as more reliable than others, on grounds of position, interest, contemporaneity and consistency. The procedure therefore does not treat traces as uniform rescues; it weights them.

The formula has no weight. Action plus trace plus time plus rival readings treats every trace alike, and every reading as one vote. Adding a reliability weight is not a technical refinement; it changes what validation is. With weights, agreement among three well-placed accounts can outrank agreement among ten poor ones, and the count $k$ stops being the operative parameter.

That points at what the account needs next, and it is not more philosophy. It is a criterion for what sets a trace's weight --- and the candidates are all properties the recording apparatus fixes, which returns the argument to the unstated loss term.

\section{Where plurality stops working}
One more constraint bears on $k$, and it comes from the practice that has measured plural reading most systematically.

Annotation work reports agreement coefficients and treats low agreement as evidence that a category is not reliably applicable. Agreement is high for surface labelling and low where inference about intent is required.

If agreement falls as interpretive depth rises, then the formula returns most readily for shallow traces and diverges for deep ones. Validation by plurality succeeds where nothing interpretive is at stake and fails where interpretation is the point --- which means the formula is best satisfied in exactly the cases nobody needed it for.

That is not a refutation. It suggests instead that persistent disagreement at depth is a property of the material rather than a failure of procedure, and that a hermeneutics honest about this would report depth alongside agreement rather than seeking convergence at every level. The honest clause in the original formula --- validation never reaches certainty --- turns out to be doing more work than its authors could show.

\section{Limitations}
No corroboration rule is quoted here, no jurisdiction is named, and no holding is cited. The reading of legal procedure as parameter estimation is structural and would need doctrinal work to sustain.

The agreement findings are invoked as a recurring reported pattern; no coefficients or studies were retrieved for this paper, and no depth-stratified agreement study is known to us.

The invariant under inscription is not measured. The paper's central suggestion --- that a multiply-recorded uncontested event would let the invariant be computed directly --- is an available and unperformed experiment, and until it is run the choice between a thin and a substantial invariant remains open.
