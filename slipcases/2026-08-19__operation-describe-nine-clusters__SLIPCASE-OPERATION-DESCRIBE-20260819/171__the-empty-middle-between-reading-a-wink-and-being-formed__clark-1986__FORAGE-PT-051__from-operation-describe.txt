TITLE: The empty middle between reading a wink and being formed by a culture is occupied by pacts — locally negotiated conventions with a lifespan

SOURCE: [[FORAGE-PT-024]] (the timescale gap) and [[FORAGE-PT-038]] (conceptual pacts, after Clark and Wilkes-Gibbs 1986); read against PROGRAMS/thick.json and PROGRAMS/tda.json

PASSAGE: [PARAPHRASE] Conceptual pacts are conventions established between particular partners over a handful of exchanges, which compress reference for those partners and fail for others. They are neither momentary readings nor lifelong programs; they form in minutes and dissolve when the partnership changes. [QUOTE] thick.json: "<culture> [is] <extra-genetic instruction system>"

RESEARCH OBJECT: A populated middle. The timescale index ran from a single act to a lifetime with nothing in between, and the missing band is where conventions are *negotiated* rather than read or inherited. Pacts sit at roughly the scale of a conversation or a session, they have a formation cost, a compression benefit, and a partner-specific validity — three properties neither the momentary nor the constitutive account has.

LOCAL MOVE: This child fills the gap the parent named, using the mechanism the sibling child found, and thereby connects the two Geertzes through a third object rather than choosing between them.

SOURCE TERMS: conceptual pact / entrainment / partner-specific / precedent / extra-genetic instruction system / meaningful action

WHAT BECAME STRANGE: Pacts have a property neither neighbouring scale has: they are *jointly authored*. A wink is read; a culture is inherited; a pact is built by two parties who could each have proposed otherwise. That makes the middle band the only scale at which the corpus's routing vocabulary and its hermeneutic vocabulary both apply — someone is steering and something is being negotiated.

QUESTION: What is the lifespan of a pact in a human-model interaction, and does it dissolve on context clearing, on model update, or on task change?

DEEPER QUESTION: If pacts are jointly authored, then a prompt library is a *sedimented pact* — a convention that outlived its negotiation and is now inherited by users who did not build it. That is the transition from the middle band to the constitutive one, observable in real time, which the corpus's timescale index predicts but has no case for.

SOURCE FORMALISM: NONE — the pact literature reports behavioural measures, not a formal model of lifespan.

OUR FORMALIZATION: [OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX] Three timescale bands with distinct authorship: READ (single act, author elsewhere), PACT (session, jointly authored), PROGRAM (lifetime, inherited). Predict that the corpus's contested terms sort cleanly — routing applies in READ, steering in PACT, constitution in PROGRAM — and that most of its confusions come from claims made in one band with evidence from another.

TENSION: READING A: pacts are a genuine third scale and the index now covers the range. READING B: a pact is just a short program — the same mechanism at a shorter duration — so adding a band multiplies entities without dividing the mechanism, and the parent's one-mechanism reading survives.

Discriminating evidence: whether pact formation shows the *joint* signature. If either party can unilaterally impose the convention, it is a short program; if it requires ratification by the other, it is a distinct kind.

MISSING: Any lifespan measurement for human-model pacts. Any documented case of a pact sedimenting into an inherited prompt convention, though prompt-sharing communities should be full of them.

BOUNDARY: The pact literature concerns human dyads with a shared task and mutual feedback. Whether a model participates in ratification or merely accommodates is unresolved and is exactly what Reading B turns on.

CITATION TRAIL: [[FORAGE-PT-024]] and [[FORAGE-PT-038]] -> pacts as the middle band -> next: prompt-sharing communities as the sedimentation site, where a jointly negotiated formula becomes an inherited convention with no negotiator present.

TEST: Track a working formula in a prompt-sharing community from first posting to widespread reuse. Record whether early adopters modify it collaboratively and later ones inherit it unchanged. That transition is sedimentation, observed, and it dates the move from PACT to PROGRAM.
