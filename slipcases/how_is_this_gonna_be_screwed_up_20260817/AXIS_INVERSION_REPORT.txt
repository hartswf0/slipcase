TITLE
How Is This Gonna Be Screwed Up?
Patent Drafting and the Cultural Formation of Prompt Expertise

THE_NARCISSISM_DELETED
- Removed the old paper's "This Article takes..." explanatory scaffolding as an opening move.
- Removed the tourist walk through patent doctrine before the reader had a reason to care.
- Removed "prompt common law" as a self-justifying conceptual novelty; it survives only where it explains the lifecycle from recurring failure to workaround to feature.
- Removed the inventory-style "deeper questions" section. Questions now emerge only where they threaten or extend the argument.
- Removed the flattering but weak proposition that lawyers are natural leaders of prompt language.
- Removed the gap claim. The paper does not argue that prior scholarship forgot one more antecedent. It argues that starting the clock at the interface produces a systematic measurement error.
- Removed "ancient master" from the scholarly claim. Shambibble is treated as evidence, not mascot.

THE_TARGET_COMMUNITY
Primary: HCI and human-AI interaction researchers studying prompting, prompt literacy, end-user prompt engineering, controllability, and natural-language interfaces.
Secondary: copyright and AI-authorship scholars who use "the prompt" as a unit for locating human control.

THE_ACCEPTED_REALITY
Prompting is commonly studied as a model-facing technique: users enter an interface, formulate natural-language instructions, observe failures, and acquire prompt literacy through interaction, scaffolding, examples, or iteration.

THE_ANOMALY
Shambibble explicitly located part of his unusual early Midjourney facility in a preexisting professional practice: patent law. What transferred was not legal vocabulary but a trained sensitivity to alternate construction and consequential misinterpretation. Baxandall and Geertz supply the missing mechanism: practical life can cultivate discriminations that later travel into apparently unrelated domains.

THE_ERROR_PIVOT
If prompt expertise is measured only after the user encounters the model, the field starts the causal history too late. It mistakes inherited professional discrimination for unexplained individual aptitude and overvalues interface-specific syntax.

THE_COST
- HCI can mismeasure expertise by treating "AI experience" or "prompt literacy" as sufficient predictors.
- Training can overfit users to model-version-specific incantations instead of durable diagnostic practices.
- Interface design can miss expertise already present in law, programming, editing, directing, translation, and other practices.
- Copyright analysis can mistake a final prompt string for the full human control path.
- Organizations can recruit for current tool familiarity rather than the discriminations that survive tool change.

THE_RESCUING_SOLUTION
Study prompt expertise as culturally formed professional vision. Track which distinctions were trained where, how generative systems make them newly consequential, how communities retune them, and where prior expertise becomes fixation. In Shambibble's case, patent drafting supplies adversarial construction; PromptCraft and the image-prompting manual transform that inherited habit into multimodal adversarial empiricism.
