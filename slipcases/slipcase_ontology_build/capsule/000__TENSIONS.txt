RANK 1 — T-01 — WHERE DOES PROMPT CAUSALITY ACTUALLY LIVE?

RIVAL CLAIM A:
The human-readable prompt is the primary causal control object. Its words are interpreted and their semantic content guides generation.

RIVAL CLAIM B:
The human-readable prompt is only an upstream intervention. The effective control may live elsewhere: in prompt rewriting, compiled demonstrations, token/embedding geometry, denoising dynamics, random initial state, model priors, or post-generation selection.

EVIDENCE FOR A:
- WORKWORDS-PROMPT-001: developer practice often treats prompt wording as implementation.
- WORKWORDS-PROMPT-007: LMQL makes natural-language prompting an explicit computational component inside a program.
- DEFAULT-IMAGES-CHI26-A: changing known/unknown prompt terms changes generated imagery.

EVIDENCE FOR B:
- DEFAULT-IMAGES-CHI26-B-1: a documented DALL-E 3 pipeline rewrites user ideas before image generation.
- WORKWORDS-PROMPT-004: DSPy can compile effective prompts from declarations and metrics.
- WORKWORDS-PROMPT-002: soft prompts can control models without human-readable words.
- DEFAULT-IMAGES-CHI26-C-1: initial latent noise can exhibit content-generation preferences.
- DEFAULT-IMAGES-CHI26-A-1: concepts visible early in denoising can later disappear.

SOURCES:
Liang et al. 2025; Beurer-Kellner et al. 2023; OpenAI 2023; Khattab et al. 2023;
Qin & Eisner 2021; Mao et al. 2023; Agarwal et al. 2023; Simonen et al. 2026.

WHAT BOTH READINGS EXPLAIN:
Prompt edits often correlate with output changes.

WHAT NEITHER READING EXPLAINS:
How causal responsibility should be partitioned when several stages are simultaneously necessary and none is sufficient.

HIDDEN ASSUMPTION:
That “the prompt” names one stable object. The field instead contains user text, rewritten text, compiled demonstrations, discrete tokens, continuous vectors, latent state, attention trajectories, and evaluator-mediated descendants.

THIRD POSSIBILITY:
Prompt causality is not located at one layer. It is interactional: wording changes the system by perturbing a trajectory whose response depends on transformation, representation, stochastic state, learned priors, and selection.

QUESTION MADE HARDER:
When we say “this prompt caused this output,” what intervention are we actually claiming made the difference?

DECISIVE EVIDENCE:
Instrument an open pipeline end-to-end. Hold user-visible text constant while independently intervening on rewrite, tokenization/embedding, seed/initial latent, denoising attention, and selection. Then hold those layers constant while changing text. Estimate which interventions change the output distribution and which prompt clauses are causally inert.

CONSEQUENCE:
If causal control is distributed, prompt-response pairs are inadequate evidence for prompt effects. Prompt engineering, authorship, reproducibility, and criticism all need stage-specific causal claims.


RANK 2 — T-02 — CAN MORE SPECIFICATION PRODUCE LESS CONTROL?

RIVAL CLAIM A:
Prompting becomes more reliable as requirements are stated explicitly and precisely.

RIVAL CLAIM B:
Explicit requirements compete. Adding instructions can decrease compliance with instructions already present; omission can sometimes yield better behavior because the model supplies defaults.

EVIDENCE FOR A:
- WORKWORDS-PROMPT-001: prompt programming treats requirements as part of implementation.
- WORKWORDS-PROMPT-007: formal constraints can improve reliability in LMQL.

EVIDENCE FOR B:
- WORKWORDS-PROMPT-003: Yang et al. find unspecified requirements can be satisfied by default, while specifying more requirements does not monotonically improve performance.
- DEFAULT-IMAGES-CHI26-A: known concepts can dominate unknown terms in larger prompts, masking their failure.
- DEFAULT-IMAGES-CHI26-A-1: multiple concepts can compete spatially and one can be ignored.

SOURCES:
Yang et al. 2025; Liang et al. 2025; Beurer-Kellner et al. 2023; Simonen et al. 2026; Agarwal et al. 2023.

WHAT BOTH READINGS EXPLAIN:
Prompt wording matters.

WHAT NEITHER READING EXPLAINS:
A general rule for deciding which requirements should be explicit, delegated, separated, constrained formally, or tested externally.

HIDDEN ASSUMPTION:
That specification is additive: each new clause contributes control without changing the effectiveness of other clauses.

THIRD POSSIBILITY:
Prompt specification behaves like a limited-capacity competitive system. Reliability depends not on amount of description but on allocation of control across channels: text, examples, constraints, defaults, and evaluators.

QUESTION MADE HARDER:
What does “fully specified” mean when making a requirement explicit can reduce the probability that it is obeyed?

DECISIVE EVIDENCE:
Factorial ablation of prompt requirements while measuring each requirement separately. Compare prose-only, separated-stage, formal-constraint, and evaluator-enforced versions of the same requirement set.

CONSEQUENCE:
The software-engineering ideal of exhaustive specification may not transfer cleanly to model-mediated execution. Prompt omission may be bug, delegation, or an engineered resource.


RANK 3 — T-03 — WHAT LANGUAGE DOES THE MODEL ACTUALLY OBEY?

RIVAL CLAIM A:
Prompt semantics are grounded primarily in the human-language meanings of the written words.

RIVAL CLAIM B:
Operational meaning is model-relative. Human nonwords can be effective; legitimate words can be weak; single Unicode substitutions can redirect outputs; continuous vectors can replace words altogether.

EVIDENCE FOR A:
- Most prompt interfaces and prompt-programming practices are organized around natural-language instructions.
- DEFAULT-IMAGES-CHI26-F assumes low-resource words may fail because relevant associations are weak.

EVIDENCE FOR B:
- WORKWORDS-PROMPT-002: soft prompts are arbitrary continuous vectors.
- DEFAULT-IMAGES-CHI26-F-2: macaronic nonce strings can robustly evoke visual concepts.
- DEFAULT-IMAGES-CHI26-F-1: single non-Latin homoglyphs can shift cultural attributes through encoder geometry.

SOURCES:
Qin & Eisner 2021; Millière 2022; Struppek et al. 2023; Simonen et al. 2026.

WHAT BOTH READINGS EXPLAIN:
Some textual interventions have stable effects.

WHAT NEITHER READING EXPLAINS:
How to translate between human semantic categories and the model’s operative geometry without assuming one mirrors the other.

HIDDEN ASSUMPTION:
That “word known to the model” is a lexical property rather than a relation among tokenization, embedding geometry, training history, and downstream generation.

THIRD POSSIBILITY:
Natural language is a human-readable control surface over a nonlinguistic representational system. Its practical power comes from partial alignment, not identity, between human and model semantics.

QUESTION MADE HARDER:
What counts as an instruction when the most effective control object need not be a sentence—or even a word?

DECISIVE EVIDENCE:
Create matched controls crossing human lexicality and model conditioning strength:
human word/high model association; human word/low association; nonword/high association; nonword/low association; soft vector/high association.
Compare output control, transfer, and interpretability.

CONSEQUENCE:
“Prompt literacy” cannot be reduced to better prose. It also concerns the mismatch between visible language and latent control geometry.


RANK 4 — T-04 — DOES A MODEL FAIL TO UNDERSTAND, OR UNDERSTAND AND THEN LOSE?

RIVAL CLAIM A:
Missing content indicates failure of initial semantic representation.

RIVAL CLAIM B:
A requested concept can be represented during early denoising and then be overwritten, competed away, or forgotten before the final output.

EVIDENCE FOR A:
- DEFAULT-IMAGES-CHI26-ROOT/F: default images are associated with unknown or weakly represented inputs.

EVIDENCE FOR B:
- DEFAULT-IMAGES-CHI26-A-1: A-STAR reports concepts present in early cross-attention that are not retained to later denoising steps.
- DEFAULT-IMAGES-CHI26-A: known terms can dominate unknown terms in larger prompts.

SOURCES:
Simonen et al. 2026; Agarwal et al. 2023.

WHAT BOTH READINGS EXPLAIN:
Final images can omit requested concepts.

WHAT NEITHER READING EXPLAINS:
Whether recurrent default motifs are present from the beginning, emerge only after requested semantics decay, or co-exist and win a competition.

HIDDEN ASSUMPTION:
That final output faithfully reveals the system’s earlier representational state.

THIRD POSSIBILITY:
Semantic control is temporal. “Understanding” is not a one-time event but a trajectory of acquisition, localization, retention, competition, and extinction.

QUESTION MADE HARDER:
At what timestep does a prompt stop controlling what the image will become?

DECISIVE EVIDENCE:
Track token-specific conditioning across denoising for default-triggering prompts and matched known prompts. Identify first representation, peak influence, decay, and emergence of fallback motifs.

CONSEQUENCE:
Prompt adherence requires temporal diagnostics; final-output evaluation alone cannot distinguish absence from extinction.


RANK 5 — T-05 — WHEN LANGUAGE WEAKENS, DOES RANDOMNESS BECOME MEANING?

RIVAL CLAIM A:
Random seed is stochastic variation around semantics supplied by the prompt.

RIVAL CLAIM B:
Initial random state contains structured content affordances relative to a trained generator, and its causal influence increases when semantic conditioning weakens.

EVIDENCE FOR A:
- Standard prompt practice treats seed as a reproducibility/variation parameter.

EVIDENCE FOR B:
- DEFAULT-IMAGES-CHI26-C: seed changes which default motifs appear.
- DEFAULT-IMAGES-CHI26-C-1: blocks of initial latent noise have content-generation preferences and can be relocated to steer output.

SOURCES:
Simonen et al. 2026; Mao et al. 2023.

WHAT BOTH READINGS EXPLAIN:
Changing seed changes outputs.

WHAT NEITHER READING EXPLAINS:
How prompt strength and initial-state affordances quantitatively trade causal influence.

HIDDEN ASSUMPTION:
That “random” means semantically neutral.

THIRD POSSIBILITY:
Randomness is neutral only before it enters a trained dynamical system. Once interpreted by the generator, different random initial conditions have unequal downstream affordances.

QUESTION MADE HARDER:
Is semantic underspecification best understood as missing instruction, or as ceding control to latent initial conditions and priors?

DECISIVE EVIDENCE:
Cross semantic-familiarity levels with identical seed sets, then causally transplant latent blocks between seeds. Estimate how the marginal effect of seed changes as prompt control weakens.

CONSEQUENCE:
The causal story of prompt generation must include state, not just text.


RANK 6 — T-06 — CAN THE DETECTOR CREATE THE ATTRACTOR IT CLAIMS TO FIND?

RIVAL CLAIM A:
Visually convergent clusters across dissimilar prompts reveal recurrent generative defaults.

RIVAL CLAIM B:
Part of the apparent convergence may be induced or amplified by the embedding geometry used to detect similarity.

EVIDENCE FOR A:
- DEFAULT-IMAGES-CHI26-ROOT/E: manual affinity grouping and large-scale CLIP clustering both find recurrent motifs.

EVIDENCE FOR B:
- DEFAULT-IMAGES-CHI26-E-1: contrastive embedding spaces can exhibit hubness, where some candidates are anomalously near many queries.

SOURCES:
Simonen et al. 2026; Chowdhury et al. 2024.

WHAT BOTH READINGS EXPLAIN:
Some outputs appear unusually central in learned similarity space.

WHAT NEITHER READING EXPLAINS:
How much of cluster stability is generator behavior versus measurement geometry.

HIDDEN ASSUMPTION:
That the metric used to observe convergence does not possess a convergence pathology of its own.

THIRD POSSIBILITY:
Default images are real but their measured frequency, boundaries, and membership are representation-dependent.

QUESTION MADE HARDER:
How do we distinguish a generative attractor from an observational attractor?

DECISIVE EVIDENCE:
Replicate clustering across independent visual representations, hub-normalized metrics, and human judgments; report cluster persistence rather than one clustering solution.

CONSEQUENCE:
A benchmark of default images must include detector invariance, not only generator recurrence.

RANK 8 — T-08 — WHERE DOES AUTHORSHIP MOVE WHEN PROMPTS REWRITE THEMSELVES?

RIVAL CLAIM A:
Prompt authorship resides primarily in selecting and composing the instruction.

RIVAL CLAIM B:
In compiled, optimized, or evolutionary prompting, authorship migrates toward objectives, examples, metrics, mutation rules, and selection environments.

EVIDENCE FOR A:
- WORKWORDS-PROMPT-001: prompt developers iteratively write and revise prompts.

EVIDENCE FOR B:
- WORKWORDS-PROMPT-004: DSPy compiles prompt realizations from declarative programs and metrics.
- WORKWORDS-PROMPT-005: ProTeGi rewrites prompts using textual gradients and metric-guided search.
- WORKWORDS-PROMPT-006: Promptbreeder evolves both task prompts and mutation prompts.

SOURCES:
Liang et al. 2025; Khattab et al. 2023; Pryzant et al. 2023; Fernando et al. 2023.

WHAT BOTH READINGS EXPLAIN:
Humans still set some initial conditions and goals.

WHAT NEITHER READING EXPLAINS:
How to attribute contribution when wording, optimization, and selection are distributed across humans and models.

HIDDEN ASSUMPTION:
That authorship tracks textual production.

THIRD POSSIBILITY:
Authorship in prompt systems is partly ecological: choosing the fitness landscape can matter more than writing any surviving sentence.

QUESTION MADE HARDER:
If the instruction is machine-generated but the criterion of survival is human-chosen, who wrote the behavior?

DECISIVE EVIDENCE:
Compare outcomes from fixed human prompts, machine-optimized prompts under human metrics, and human-selected outputs from unoptimized prompt populations. Track which intervention explains the largest variance in judged quality.

CONSEQUENCE:
Provenance must record evaluators and selection conditions, not merely prompt text.


RANK 9 — T-09 — DOES FORMALIZING PROMPTING FORMALIZE THE WORDS?

RIVAL CLAIM A:
Prompt languages such as LMQL make natural-language interaction programmable by adding control flow, constraints, and formal semantics.

RIVAL CLAIM B:
Formal scaffolding leaves the central natural-language operation model-interpreted and probabilistic; the words themselves still lack conventional formal semantics.

EVIDENCE FOR A:
- WORKWORDS-PROMPT-007: LMQL generates constrained inference procedures from prompts, control flow, and output constraints.

EVIDENCE FOR B:
- WORKWORDS-PROMPT-007 TENSION: the model-interpreted natural-language transition remains qualitatively different from deterministic program transitions.
- WORKWORDS-PROMPT-002: continuous controls can substitute for language entirely.

SOURCES:
Beurer-Kellner et al. 2023; Qin & Eisner 2021.

WHAT BOTH READINGS EXPLAIN:
Prompt systems can be embedded in reliable software structures.

WHAT NEITHER READING EXPLAINS:
What semantic theory should govern a program containing both formally defined transitions and learned interpretive transitions.

HIDDEN ASSUMPTION:
That putting an ambiguous operation inside a formal language makes that operation itself formal.

THIRD POSSIBILITY:
Prompted systems require a mixed semantics that explicitly distinguishes defined, constrained, sampled, interpreted, and delegated transitions.

QUESTION MADE HARDER:
What kind of program contains steps whose meaning is not specified by the language but inferred by another learned system?

DECISIVE EVIDENCE:
Model one LMQL program as a state machine and perturb only the prose fragments across model versions. Measure which claimed program invariants survive.

CONSEQUENCE:
“Prompting is programming” becomes an empirical and semantic question rather than a metaphor.


RANK 10 — T-10 — CAN PROMPT PRACTICE BECOME EXPERIMENTAL WITHOUT DESTROYING WHAT IT IS TRYING TO STUDY?

RIVAL CLAIM A:
Prompting should be treated as controlled hypothesis testing: compare variants, models, and outputs.

RIVAL CLAIM B:
Creative prompt practice is path-dependent; criteria change after seeing outputs, and serendipity is part of the work. Experimental control may erase the evolving purpose of the practitioner.

EVIDENCE FOR A:
- WORKWORDS-PROMPT-008: ChainForge supports prompt variation, model comparison, and hypothesis testing.

EVIDENCE FOR B:
- WORKWORDS-PROMPT-008 TENSION: controlled comparison can discard situated judgment and criteria discovered during exploration.
- WORKWORDS-PROMPT-003: not all omitted requirements are failures; some behavior is delegated or emergent.

SOURCES:
Arawjo et al. 2023; Yang et al. 2025.

WHAT BOTH READINGS EXPLAIN:
Iterative prompting learns from differences.

WHAT NEITHER READING EXPLAINS:
How to preserve causal evidence when the evaluation criterion itself changes during the experiment.

HIDDEN ASSUMPTION:
That the dependent variable is stable before inquiry begins.

THIRD POSSIBILITY:
Creative prompting needs reflexive experiments that version both the prompt and the criterion by which outputs are judged.

QUESTION MADE HARDER:
Can we infer what a prompt did when seeing the output changed what the user wanted?

DECISIVE EVIDENCE:
Record prompt interventions and criterion revisions separately during real creative sessions. Test whether retrospective judgments can reconstruct the criteria actually in force at each decision.

CONSEQUENCE:
A prompt history should preserve changing evaluation functions, not just text revisions.
