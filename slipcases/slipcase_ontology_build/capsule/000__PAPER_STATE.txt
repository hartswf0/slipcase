PAPER STATE: QUESTION-PAPER

TITLE:
The Vanishing Prompt: Operational Language and the Manufacture of Causal Certainty

CENTRAL QUESTION:
What kind of thing is a prompt if making it causally adequate makes it cease to be a prompt?

CURRENT WAGER:
Prompt scholarship has a boundedness–causality dilemma. The thin prompt is bounded, portable, and experimentally convenient but causally incomplete. The thick prompt absorbs the conditions needed to explain behavior but expands toward situated execution and loses the identity that made it a prompt. This is not proposed as a solved ontology. It is a testable pressure on current ontologies.

STRANGE STEPPING STONE:
The field may manufacture causal certainty by allowing the visible instruction to inherit causal privilege because it is the easiest component to archive, annotate, version, and publish.

NOT YET EARNED:
- “The prompt does not exist.” Too strong.
- “The execution lineage is the real prompt.” Rejected; merely replaces one totality with another.
- “Context is the real prompt.” Rejected; expands rather than solves the boundary.
- “Words are not causes.” Rejected; evidence shows words can be consequential.

OPEN FAILURE CONDITION:
If a stable prompt identity can be shown to preserve high causal invariance across changes in surrounding runtime conditions, the boundedness–causality dilemma weakens substantially.
