ARRANGEMENT 004 — RIVAL MECHANISMS: WHEN WORDS STOP BEING THE CAUSE

Purpose:
Read the field as a causal-attribution problem rather than as a progression toward one new definition of “prompt.”

01 DEFAULT-IMAGES-CHI26-B
   The behavioral phenomenon is stronger than its causal explanation.

02 DEFAULT-IMAGES-CHI26-B-1
   User-visible text can differ from executed text.

03 WORKWORDS-PROMPT-004
   Executed wording can be compiled from declarations and metrics.

04 WORKWORDS-PROMPT-002
   Effective control can survive after readable words disappear.

05 DEFAULT-IMAGES-CHI26-F-2
   Human nonwords can be model-significant.

06 DEFAULT-IMAGES-CHI26-F-1
   A single character can exert unintended model-relative cultural force.

07 DEFAULT-IMAGES-CHI26-A-1
   A concept can enter generation and later lose control.

08 DEFAULT-IMAGES-CHI26-C
   Weak semantic guidance increases the visible role of seed.

09 DEFAULT-IMAGES-CHI26-C-1
   Initial latent state has structured downstream affordances.

10 WORKWORDS-PROMPT-003
   More explicit specification can create instruction interference.

11 WORKWORDS-PROMPT-005
   Prompt wording can become an effect of metric-guided search.

12 WORKWORDS-PROMPT-006
   The mechanism that rewrites prompts can itself evolve.

13 WORKWORDS-PROMPT-008
   Prompt effects require contrastive evidence, but creative goals may move during the comparison.

READING QUESTION:
At each step ask: what variable would have to be held fixed before the effect could legitimately be attributed to the visible words?
