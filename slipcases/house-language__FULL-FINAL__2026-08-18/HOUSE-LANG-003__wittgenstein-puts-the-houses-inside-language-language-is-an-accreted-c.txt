ZETTEL

ID:
HOUSE-LANG-003

TITLE:
WITTGENSTEIN PUTS THE HOUSES INSIDE LANGUAGE: LANGUAGE IS AN ACCRETED CITY, NOT A SINGLE HOUSE.

SOURCE:
Ludwig Wittgenstein — Philosophical Investigations — 1953; 4th ed. 2009 — §18.

PASSAGE:
[QUOTE]
“Our language can be regarded as an ancient city: a maze of little streets and squares, of old and new houses.”

RESEARCH OBJECT:
The scale of the architectural metaphor for language.

LOCAL MOVE:
Wittgenstein answers a question about whether a language must be complete by comparing language to a historically layered city containing old houses, additions, and new regular suburbs.

SOURCE TERMS:
language; ancient city; streets; squares; old houses; new houses; extensions; suburbs

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
“House of Language” may be too architecturally singular. Wittgenstein’s image is LANGUAGE = CITY CONTAINING HOUSES OF DIFFERENT AGES AND FORMS.

QUESTION:
What changes if the fundamental object is not the house of language but the city of language through which houses are added, inherited, renovated, standardized, and abandoned?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Could a house produced through contemporary prompt systems be understood as a new “suburb” attached to older linguistic and architectural districts rather than something language created ex nihilo?

MECHANISM:
Accumulation without total replacement: new forms appear beside inherited forms and historical heterogeneity remains materially present.

FORMAL SHIFT:
<LANGUAGE AS UNITARY CONTAINER>
→ <LANGUAGE AS HISTORICAL CITY>
→ [ADDITION / EXTENSION / OBSOLESCENCE]
→ <HETEROGENEOUS PRESENT>

SOURCE FORMALISM:
NONE

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
LANGUAGE(t+1) = LANGUAGE(t) + additions + new districts - obsolete uses, without requiring complete replacement.

TENSION:
“The House That Words Built” gives words compact collective agency; Wittgenstein’s city suggests a layered inheritance.

MISSING:
Who modifies the city? What persists when a practice becomes obsolete? What corresponds architecturally to a new suburb of language?

BOUNDARY:
§18 is a comparison in an inquiry into language; it does not claim literal cities are linguistic systems or that architectural and linguistic change are mechanically equivalent.

CITATION TRAIL:
Philosophical Investigations §§18–23; Descartes on planned vs inherited towns; language-games; forms of life.

TEST:
Trace the historical entry of vocabulary required to describe one contemporary house and ask whether the resulting house is produced by one language or by historically incompatible linguistic districts.
