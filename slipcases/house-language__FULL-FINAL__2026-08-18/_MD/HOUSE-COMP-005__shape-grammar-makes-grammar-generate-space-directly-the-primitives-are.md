HOUSE-COMP-005

TITLE:
SHAPE GRAMMAR MAKES GRAMMAR GENERATE SPACE DIRECTLY: THE PRIMITIVES ARE SHAPES, NOT WORDS.

SOURCE:
George Stiny and James Gips — “Shape Grammars and the Generative Specification of Painting and Sculpture” — 1972 — Information Processing 71 — pp. 1460–1465.

PASSAGE:
[QUOTE]
“A method of shape generation using shape grammars which take shape as primitive and have shape specific rules is presented.”

RESEARCH OBJECT:
GRAMMAR WHOSE SYMBOLIC OPERATIONS ARE SPATIAL TRANSFORMATIONS.

LOCAL MOVE:
Stiny and Gips take the generative idea of formal grammar and alter the primitive: the basic object is not a character string but SHAPE.

SOURCE TERMS:
shape; grammar; generation; rules; generative specification; painting; sculpture; language

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The phrase “architectural language” can cease to be metaphorical without becoming verbal: a grammar can literally produce spatial forms.

QUESTION:
Does the genealogy of “The House That Words Built” need to split LANGUAGE into NATURAL LANGUAGE, PROGRAMMING LANGUAGE, FORMAL GRAMMAR, SHAPE LANGUAGE?

DEEPER QUESTION:
What happens when a natural-language system proposes or modifies the rules of a shape grammar rather than directly predicting finished geometry?

MECHANISM:
An initial shape is transformed through applicable shape rules; repeated rule application generates further shapes.

FORMAL SHIFT:
<INITIAL SHAPE>
→ <SHAPE REPRESENTATION>
→ [MATCH + APPLY SHAPE RULE]
→ <GENERATED SHAPE>

SOURCE FORMALISM:
SHAPE GRAMMAR: shapes are primitives and production rules operate over shapes.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
S₀ → r₁(S₀) → r₂(...) → ... → Sₙ. The crucial difference from string grammar: rule matching is spatial.

TENSION:
The House That Words Built privileges words; shape grammar suggests operativity may increase when the system leaves words behind.

MISSING:
Material properties; structure; construction sequence; cost; site; use; fabrication; semantics of inhabitation.

BOUNDARY:
The 1972 paper demonstrates generative specification of paintings and sculptures; later architectural applications require separate evidence.

CITATION TRAIL:
George Stiny; James Gips; Stiny and William J. Mitchell — Palladian villa grammar; procedural modeling; parametric design.

TEST:
Describe one house as natural-language instructions and as a shape grammar; compare what must become explicit and what kinds of ambiguity each permits.
