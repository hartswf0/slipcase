TITLE:
Differential evaluation gives the human choice only after the agent has chosen what can be chosen.

SOURCE:
David Heinemeier Hansson and Lex Fridman — Lex Fridman Podcast #501 — 2026 — 00:59:21 onward.

PASSAGE:
[QUOTE]
“Differential evaluation. You give me three options, I pick one of them.”

RESEARCH OBJECT:
This initially sounds like a strong account of human agency:

agent generates;
human chooses.

But the more consequential selection may occur one step earlier.

Someone—or some model—decides which three possibilities become visible.

LOCAL MOVE:
DHH identifies selection as the human comparative advantage.

The hidden operation is candidate-set construction.

SOURCE TERMS:
three options
pick
taste
snap judgments
gut
differential evaluation

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
Perfect taste cannot recover an alternative that was never generated.

QUESTION:
Who has more design power: the actor choosing among three candidates or the actor deciding which three candidates exist?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Does agentic design require a theory of candidate-set politics?

MECHANISM:
huge possibility space
→ agent samples three candidates
→ human compares
→ winner conditions subsequent generation
→ unexplored regions disappear further from the trajectory

FORMAL SHIFT:

→ [HUMAN SELECTION]
→ <SEARCH TRAJECTORY>

OUR FORMALIZATION:

Ω = possible designs

G(Ω) = {a,b,c}

The human can control H perfectly while G still governs what is thinkable.

TENSION:
DHH's account foregrounds human taste.

The architecture gives the generator agenda-setting power.

MISSING:
