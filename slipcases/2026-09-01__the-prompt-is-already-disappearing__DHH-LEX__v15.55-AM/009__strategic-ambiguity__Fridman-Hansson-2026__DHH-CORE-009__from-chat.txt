TITLE:
Natural-language ambiguity is not replacing formal precision; it is choosing where interpretation happens.

SOURCE:
Lex Fridman and David Heinemeier Hansson — Lex Fridman Podcast #501 — 2026 — 04:02:28–04:05:51.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
Lex proposes “strategic use of ambiguity”: express style, metaphor, and direction without specifying implementation. DHH agrees and argues that nondeterminism is central rather than defective.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
Their strongest prompting idea is not “English is a programming language.”

It is that natural language can intentionally leave interpretive work undone.

A programming language pushes ambiguity toward zero before execution.

Their prompting practice sometimes preserves ambiguity specifically so the agent must resolve it.

LOCAL MOVE:
Lex treats poetic compression as high-bandwidth guidance.

DHH links the resulting interpretive freedom to nondeterministic variation.

SOURCE TERMS:
ambiguity

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
Ordinary specification theory treats unresolved interpretation as a defect.

Here unresolved interpretation is part of the control strategy.

QUESTION:
How much intent can be preserved while deliberately refusing to specify its execution?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Can we distinguish productive ambiguity from simple missing information?

MECHANISM:
human supplies semantically rich but incomplete expression
→ several plausible completions remain
→ agent resolves under context
→ one completion becomes concrete
→ human judges it
→ subsequent language reduces or redirects the ambiguity

FORMAL SHIFT:
<REMOVE AMBIGUITY BEFORE EXECUTION>
→ <PRESERVE CONTROLLED AMBIGUITY>
→ [INTELLIGENT INTERPRETATION]
→ <CONCRETE VARIANT>

SOURCE FORMALISM:
NONE

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

Good prompt ≠ interpretation count of 1.

Possible target:

1 < |I(P)| < uncontrolled

where I(P) is the set of plausible interpretations consistent with the desired semantic region.

TENSION:
Nondeterminism is not equivalent to creativity.

Random errors are nondeterministic too.

The important variable is whether variation remains inside an intelligible intention envelope.

MISSING:
A measurable distinction among:
ambiguity,
underspecification,
metaphor,
stochasticity,
contradiction,
and procedural freedom.

BOUNDARY:
This is a strong hypothesis from practice, not yet a demonstrated general principle.

TEST:
Hold underlying design intention constant while varying only how much procedural interpretation the prompt leaves open.

Map where novelty rises without intent collapsing.
