RESEARCH OBJECT:
[OUR INFERENCE]
AUTHORSHIP IS EXPERIENCED ASYMMETRICALLY ACROSS INSTRUCTION AND EXECUTION.

LOCAL MOVE:
Martina distinguishes the prompt as "something that you did" and "only your text" from the image as "something that the AI did."

SOURCE TERMS:
"selling the image"; "something that the AI did"; "selling the prompt"; "only your text"

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
Felt authorship attaches to the instruction even though the mature instruction itself contains inherited vocabulary and discoveries from earlier model outputs.

QUESTION:
What kind of authorship survives when instruction is human-authored but realization resolves consequential properties absent from the instruction?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Would decision provenance provide a more precise account than single-author attribution?

MECHANISM:
Instruction establishes some constraints; model resolves additional properties; user later accepts/rejects outputs; retained emergent properties become subsequent constraints.

FORMAL SHIFT:
AUTHORSHIP != sole causal production; decision authority is distributed across a trajectory.

SOURCE FORMALISM:
[PARAPHRASE]
Martina explicitly locates originality more strongly in prompt text than in generated image.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
For feature f, record provenance stage: SPECIFIED / SYSTEM-RESOLVED / RETAINED-AFTER-EMERGENCE / COMMUNITY-INHERITED / TEMPLATE-PROMPTED.

TENSION:
Decision provenance does not settle legal copyright or moral authorship.

MISSING:
Feature-level provenance across several generations and edits.

BOUNDARY:
Keep legal ownership distinct from felt authorship and process authorship.

CITATION TRAIL:
Transcript 22:05 -> instruction/execution split -> Goodman/Suchman -> decision provenance.

TEST:
Trace 30 visible properties of a final artifact backwards through prompts and generations; classify where each property became fixed.

PLATFORM:
Midjourney; authorship; prompt markets
