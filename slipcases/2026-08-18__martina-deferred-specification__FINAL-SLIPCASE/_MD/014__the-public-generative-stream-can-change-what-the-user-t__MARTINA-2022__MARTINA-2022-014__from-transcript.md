RESEARCH OBJECT:
[OUR INFERENCE]
VISIBLE ALTERNATIVES CAN CONTAMINATE PREFERENCE FORMATION.

LOCAL MOVE:
Martina says the newbie channel made her unsure about what she created and tempted her toward what looked cooler; moving to her own chat let her refine rather than compete.

SOURCE TERMS:
"not so sure about what I created"; "competing with anonymous people"; "maybe what I had in mind is not good enough"; "refine things"

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The same public openness that teaches prompt technique can destabilize the user's criterion of what counts as good for her project.

QUESTION:
When does exposure to alternatives expand possibility, and when does it substitute social comparison for project-directed judgment?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Can private generation preserve a more stable evaluative trajectory by reducing exogenous preference shocks?

MECHANISM:
Public feed supplies salient alternatives; social comparison changes perceived quality; user may adopt stylistic direction not previously connected to project.

FORMAL SHIFT:
PUBLIC FEED -> COMPARISON -> CRITERION SHIFT -> STYLE DRIFT.

SOURCE FORMALISM:
[PARAPHRASE]
Martina contrasts stressful public-channel comparison with private refinement.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
JUDGMENT_t = f(project criterion, generated candidate, salient others).

TENSION:
Isolation can also reduce productive exposure and reinforce narrow taste.

MISSING:
Within-subject sessions in public-feed and private-feed conditions with tracked preference changes.

BOUNDARY:
One participant cannot establish general effects of feed visibility.

CITATION TRAIL:

TEST:
Have users pre-rank design directions, work under public-feed versus private conditions, then re-rank and explain changes; distinguish learned technique from copied taste.
