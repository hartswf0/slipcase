TITLE:
Prompting requires a practical theory of what the model knows

SOURCE:
Martina interview with Watson Hartsoe, October 10, 2022. Unpublished transcript.

SOURCE URL:
NONE (private local source; preserved under _RESOURCES/PINK_MJ_Interview1.wh_Martina.pages and _RESOURCES/martina_transcript.txt)

PASSAGE:
[QUOTE]
"I didn't know what the AI knows. So I can make the most of this." (25:12)

RESEARCH OBJECT:
[OUR INFERENCE]
MODEL-OF-THE-MODEL AS PRACTICAL PROMPT KNOWLEDGE.

LOCAL MOVE:
Effective address requires Martina to infer which categories, styles, and associations are behaviorally available to the model.

SOURCE TERMS:
"what the AI knows"; "make the most"; "limit"

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
Prompting involves inquiry into the addressee, not only articulation of the self.

QUESTION:
What minimum working model of a generative system is required for competent direction?

DEEPER QUESTION:
When does a prompt community become a vernacular empirical science of an undocumented and changing machine?

MECHANISM:
Observe output under varied descriptions; infer model-relevant distinctions; retain successful hypotheses in personal/community practice.

FORMAL SHIFT:
USER INTENT + MODEL-OF-MODEL -> PROMPT CHOICE.

SOURCE FORMALISM:
[PARAPHRASE]
Martina explicitly frames prompt improvement as learning what the AI knows and where its limits may be.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
P(prompt | goal) is conditioned by user belief B about model response surface.

TENSION:
The anthropomorphic vocabulary of "knows" need not correspond to model internals; it is a practitioner-level predictive theory.

MISSING:
Longitudinal record of hypotheses about model behavior and their predictive success after model updates.

BOUNDARY:
No claim that model knowledge is humanlike.

CITATION TRAIL:
Transcript 25:12 -> model-of-model -> public prompt observation -> current tool surfaces make capability legibility explicit.

TEST:
Ask practitioners to predict outputs from unseen prompt variants, then measure whether explicit model theories predict performance beyond general visual expertise.

Midjourney; model legibility; prompt community
