TITLE:
Judgment labor becomes more consequential as candidate production gets cheaper

SOURCE:
Martina interview with Watson Hartsoe, October 10, 2022. Unpublished transcript.

SOURCE URL:
NONE (private local source; preserved under _RESOURCES/PINK_MJ_Interview1.wh_Martina.pages and _RESOURCES/martina_transcript.txt)

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
Across 38:13 and 56:53 Martina describes rapid generation of sketches while also spending up to an hour and a half being "very picky with results."

RESEARCH OBJECT:
[OUR INFERENCE]
GENERATIVE ABUNDANCE RECOMPOSES EXPERTISE AROUND DISCRIMINATION.

LOCAL MOVE:
This card crosses time compression with Martina's description of being highly selective.

SOURCE TERMS:
"hours instead of days"; "very picky with results"

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
Faster production does not remove expertise; it can shift scarce effort toward evaluation, comparison, and retention.

QUESTION:
What becomes the bottleneck when candidate generation is abundant?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Can judgment be treated as a primary productive capability rather than secondary filtering?

MECHANISM:
Candidate cost falls -> candidate volume rises -> attention and evaluative discrimination become limiting resources.

FORMAL SHIFT:
CHEAPER MAKING -> MORE CANDIDATES -> SCARCER JUDGMENT.

SOURCE FORMALISM:
[PARAPHRASE]
The interview jointly supports faster candidate generation and intensive selection.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
Total project quality is bounded by max(evaluation throughput, candidate relevance), not generation throughput alone.

TENSION:
Abundance may also reduce average deliberation per candidate.

MISSING:
Quantitative observation of candidate/evaluation ratios.

BOUNDARY:
No claim that evaluation is uniquely human or impossible to automate.

CITATION TRAIL:
[[MARTINA-2022-020]] + transcript 56:53 -> production/selection shift -> direction as consequential discrimination.

TEST:
Instrument sessions to record candidate count, dwell time, rejection reasons, retained features, and downstream quality.
