RESEARCH OBJECT:
[OUR INFERENCE]
PROMPT VOCABULARY CARRIES PROVENANCE AND ETHICAL FORCE.

LOCAL MOVE:
Martina avoids "in the style of" another artist in personal work because it feels like a bad copy rather than merely a neutral control phrase.

SOURCE TERMS:
"This is not my style"; "in the style of"; "bad copy"; "doesn't feel right"

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
An operative phrase can be technically useful while making authorship feel less legitimate.

QUESTION:
When does an effective control phrase become unacceptable because of the history it invokes?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Can prompt provenance be represented at the level of borrowed stylistic operations rather than only final image attribution?

MECHANISM:
Named artist reference compresses a set of learned visual associations into a control token; Martina interprets the same compression as carrying another creator's authorship.

FORMAL SHIFT:
CONTROL POWER and AUTHORSHIP LEGITIMACY can diverge.

SOURCE FORMALISM:
[PARAPHRASE]
Martina distinguishes ordinary influence from literally prompting "in the style of" an artist.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
Operational efficiency(reference) does not imply ethical neutrality(reference).

TENSION:
The interview records Martina's ethical judgment, not a universal standard.

MISSING:

BOUNDARY:
