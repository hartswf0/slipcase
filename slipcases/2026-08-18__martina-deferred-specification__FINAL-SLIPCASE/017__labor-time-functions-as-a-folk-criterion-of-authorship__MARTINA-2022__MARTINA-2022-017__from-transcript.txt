RESEARCH OBJECT:
[OUR INFERENCE]
FELT AUTHORSHIP IS PARTLY TIED TO INVESTED CRAFT LABOR.

LOCAL MOVE:
Martina explains that an image does not feel fully hers because she did not draw it or spend a month making the drawing, despite spending substantial time being picky with results.

SOURCE TERMS:
"not really mine"; "I didn't draw it"; "spend ... a month"; "very picky with results"

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
Selection labor is experienced as weaker evidence of authorship than manual execution labor.

QUESTION:
Why does an hour and a half of consequential selection count differently from an hour and a half of drawing?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Does generative practice produce a new hierarchy between execution labor and judgment labor?

MECHANISM:
Existing craft norms make manual execution salient as evidence of ownership; generative selection redistributes labor toward evaluation without automatically inheriting equivalent authorship status.

FORMAL SHIFT:
LABOR_TYPE matters, not only LABOR_TIME.

SOURCE FORMALISM:
[PARAPHRASE]
Martina contrasts manual drawing labor with time spent selecting and refining generations.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
FELT_AUTHORSHIP = f(execution labor, specification, selection, transformation, originality norms), not simply hours.

TENSION:
Her account may change as generative practices normalize.

MISSING:
Longitudinal and cross-practice comparison of felt authorship.

BOUNDARY:
No claim that labor morally determines authorship.
