TITLE:
The guide stores descriptive competence outside unaided memory

SOURCE:
Martina interview with Watson Hartsoe, October 10, 2022. Unpublished transcript.

SOURCE URL:
NONE (private local source; preserved under _RESOURCES/PINK_MJ_Interview1.wh_Martina.pages and _RESOURCES/martina_transcript.txt)

PASSAGE:
[QUOTE]
"I cannot really remember all of them." (42:55) [Transcript passage discusses artistic movements, medium, perspective, aesthetics, and atmospheres available through the guide.]

RESEARCH OBJECT:
[OUR INFERENCE]
FUNCTIONAL PROMPT COMPETENCE EXCEEDS UNAIDED RECALL.

LOCAL MOVE:
Martina uses the guide because the actionable vocabulary is larger than what she can keep available in memory.

SOURCE TERMS:
"artistic movement"; "medium"; "perspective"; "aesthetics"; "atmospheres"; "remember"

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
A person can competently use a distinction she cannot independently recall because the technical artifact keeps it available.

QUESTION:
Where does prompt competence reside when the user cannot internally remember the vocabulary required to exercise it?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Can a self-authored external memory later surprise its author and become a source of new possibilities?

MECHANISM:
External store preserves categories; recognition at point of use substitutes for unaided recall; selection reactivates a forgotten distinction.

FORMAL SHIFT:
PAST INSCRIPTION -> PERSISTENCE -> FUTURE ENCOUNTER -> CURRENT OPTION.

SOURCE FORMALISM:
[PARAPHRASE]
Martina reports using the guide because she cannot remember all relevant descriptive categories.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
AVAILABLE_KNOWLEDGE = recall(user) union lookup(template) union recognition(examples).

TENSION:
External storage alone does not prove distributed cognition in the strong philosophical sense.

MISSING:
Behavioral comparison of performance with guide available, removed, and replaced.

BOUNDARY:
Functional distribution is the defensible claim; extended-mind ontology remains open.

CITATION TRAIL:
Transcript 42:55 -> external vocabulary -> Hutchins/Clark-Chalmers -> test system-level remembering.

TEST:
Ask users to construct prompts with guide, without guide, and with unfamiliar taxonomy; record omissions, retrieval time, and conceptual branching.

PLATFORM:
Notion; external memory; prompt vocabulary
