RESEARCH OBJECT:
[OUR INFERENCE]
LEGAL OR PLATFORM OWNERSHIP DOES NOT DETERMINE EXPERIENCED AUTHORSHIP.

LOCAL MOVE:
Martina notes that terms may make the user the owner while simultaneously reporting that she does not feel the work is fully her creation.

SOURCE TERMS:
"never really felt that it was my creation"; "in the terms, you are the owner"

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
Ownership can be granted formally while authorship remains experientially unresolved.

QUESTION:
What process conditions make a generated artifact feel ownable independent of contractual rights?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Is felt authorship tied to labor, control, decision provenance, originality, execution, or accountability?

MECHANISM:
Martina compares formal ownership with her sense that the machine performs too much of the realization.

FORMAL SHIFT:
CONTRACTUAL_OWNER can differ from FELT_AUTHOR.

SOURCE FORMALISM:
[PARAPHRASE]
The source directly contrasts terms-based ownership with weak felt ownership.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
OWNERSHIP_LEGAL != AUTHORSHIP_EXPERIENTIAL != DECISION_PROVENANCE.

TENSION:
Felt authorship may reflect historically contingent norms about labor and craft rather than a stable criterion.

MISSING:
Comparative cases varying labor, specification, selection, editing, and attribution.

BOUNDARY:
No legal conclusion is drawn.

CITATION TRAIL:
Transcript 19:28 -> legal/felt divergence -> 56:53 labor theory -> 22:05 prompt/image split.

TEST:
Create matched artifacts under varying degrees of human specification and post-editing; ask creators to allocate authorship and explain decisive factors.
