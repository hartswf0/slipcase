TITLE:
The functional unit of remembering can include people and technological devices

SOURCE:
Edwin Hutchins, "How a Cockpit Remembers Its Speeds," Cognitive Science 19(3), 1995.

SOURCE URL:
https://doi.org/10.1207/s15516709cog1903_1

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
Hutchins argues that outcomes in aviation cannot be inferred from individual information processing alone and takes a distributed socio-technical system as the unit of analysis.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
[PARAPHRASE]
MEMORY CAN BE A PROPERTY OF AN ORGANIZED PERSON-ARTIFACT SYSTEM.

LOCAL MOVE:
Follow [[MARTINA-2022-006]]: Martina cannot recall all prompt vocabulary but the user-template system can make it available at the moment of need.

SOURCE TERMS:
"distributed"; "socio-technical system"; "unit of analysis"; "technological devices"

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
Forgetting inside the person need not equal loss of functional knowledge at the system level.

QUESTION:
Does expertise with a prompt guide consist partly in knowing how to navigate a system that remembers for you?

DEEPER QUESTION:
What dependencies reveal whether an external guide is constitutive of performance or merely convenient?

MECHANISM:
Information relevant to performance is represented and transformed across coordinated human and technical components.

FORMAL SHIFT:
UNIT OF ANALYSIS: user -> user+template+interface for some functional questions.

SOURCE FORMALISM:
[PARAPHRASE]
Hutchins explicitly proposes a distributed socio-technical unit of analysis for cognition in cockpit work.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
FUNCTIONAL_MEMORY(system) can exceed INTERNAL_RECALL(user).

TENSION:
A Notion guide is far less integrated and safety-critical than an aircraft cockpit.

MISSING:
Dependence and coordination evidence in actual prompt sessions.

BOUNDARY:
No strong extended-mind conclusion follows automatically.

CITATION TRAIL:

TEST:
Remove or alter the guide and measure which operations disappear, slow, or change; compare with unaided experts.
