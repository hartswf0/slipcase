ZETTEL

ID:
IMG-JEPA-04-A

TITLE:
Description has an addressee that alignment has no place for

SOURCE:
W. J. T. Mitchell — Ekphrasis and the Other — 1994 — §18
Randall Balestriero and Yann LeCun — Proving and Improving the Platonic Representation Hypothesis with JEPAs — 2026 — §§3 and 5.1

PASSAGE:
[QUOTE — Mitchell] “the relation of the speaker and the audience or addressee of the ekphrasis”

RESEARCH OBJECT:
The addressee as a variable in deciding what a description should do.

LOCAL MOVE:
Mitchell inserts a third position into the encounter between a describer and a depicted object. The description goes to someone whose reception is part of the event.

SOURCE TERMS:
Addressee, ekphrastic hope, description, ventriloquism.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
[OUR INFERENCE] The JEPA captioner makes the image-to-sentence step executable. Mitchell’s next step happens in reception. A sentence can retrieve the correct photograph while leaving a reader unable to imagine the relation for which the photograph was described.

QUESTION:
When is a good caption a good description for a particular person?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Can an imagetext work depend on giving one reader something to notice that another reader already sees?

MECHANISM:
In the JEPA evaluation, a candidate can succeed by ranking its associated image or caption above alternatives. In Mitchell’s account, a description also mediates between the depicted object and a listener or reader. What the reader already knows changes what the description can accomplish.

FORMAL SHIFT:
NONE

SOURCE FORMALISM:
NONE

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
Evaluate the same image–description pair on two axes. Measure whether it identifies the image among alternatives and whether it helps a specified reader recover a specified relation.

TENSION:
A caption that names Achilles may distinguish a Shield image in an archive. A caption that locates the elders relative to the disputants may better enable a reader to reconstruct its composition. The two tasks can favor different sentences.

MISSING:
A recipient, a purpose for describing, and evidence of what the recipient came to see.

BOUNDARY:
The comparison brings Mitchell’s account of reception to the JEPA evaluation. It establishes no line of influence between the authors.

CITATION TRAIL:
[[IMG-JEPA-04]] → Mitchell §18 on the ekphrastic triangle → §§21 onward on cups and shields.

TEST:
Write two length-matched descriptions of each Shield image, one identifying the narrative and one locating the figures. Have one group retrieve the image among distractors and another reconstruct its blocking from text alone. Compare caption rankings across the tasks.
