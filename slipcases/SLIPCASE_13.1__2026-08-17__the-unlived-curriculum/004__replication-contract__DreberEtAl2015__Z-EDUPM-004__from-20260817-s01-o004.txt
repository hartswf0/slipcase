TITLE:
SCHOLARSHIP BECOMES A LITERAL PREDICTION MARKET ONLY AFTER A CLAIM IS CONVERTED INTO A SETTLEABLE EVENT: “WILL THIS RESULT REPLICATE?”

SOURCE:
Anna Dreber et al. — “Using Prediction Markets to Estimate the Reproducibility of Scientific Research” — 2015 — Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences 112(50), 15343–15347.

SOURCE URL:
https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1516179112

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE] The researchers created prediction markets concerning the replication outcomes of psychology studies. Market participants traded on whether results would replicate; for the studies with observed replication outcomes, the markets correctly classified 29 of 41, approximately 71 percent.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
A real case in which scientific judgment is converted into a tradeable forecast with explicit settlement.

LOCAL MOVE:
The experiment does not treat citations, publication counts, or peer-review scores as markets.

It constructs an actual market around a newly specified future event:
replication success.

SOURCE TERMS:
prediction markets
reproducibility
replication
market beliefs
survey beliefs
replication outcomes
scientific research

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
“The scientific literature is a prediction market” is too loose.

But researchers can build a prediction market on top of the literature by transforming papers into contracts concerning future tests.

That transformation requires something intellectually violent and productive:

a scientific claim must become settleable.

QUESTION:
What must be removed from a scientific result before researchers can trade a binary contract on whether it “replicates”?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Does making science market-compatible require turning an open-ended claim about the world into an institutionally defined settlement event?

SOURCE FORMALISM:
Prediction markets were constructed around replication outcomes and compared with survey forecasts.

The experiment provides:
- contracts,
- traders,
- prices,
- future events,
- realized outcomes.

These are precisely the elements missing from metaphorical descriptions of citations or grades as prediction markets.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

PAPER
≠ MARKET

PAPER
→ SETTLEMENT QUESTION
→ CONTRACT
→ MARKET

TENSION:
A replication result is not equivalent to the truth of a scientific theory.

Binary settlement may aggregate beliefs effectively while compressing context, effect sizes, protocol dependence, and degrees of evidential support.

MISSING:
A theory of what scientific information cannot survive conversion into a binary replication contract.

BOUNDARY:
The study demonstrates prediction-market forecasting of replication outcomes. It does not establish that prediction markets determine scientific truth or should replace peer review.

CITATION TRAIL:
Camerer et al. — replication forecasting in experimental economics / social science.
Van Bavel et al. — contextual sensitivity in replication.
Yang et al. — estimating deep replicability.
Robin Hanson — idea futures.

TEST:
Take one disputed result and construct three settlement contracts:

A. exact replication success
B. effect direction preserved
C. effect size remains within a specified interval

Compare market prices.

The divergence measures how much “will it replicate?” hides.
