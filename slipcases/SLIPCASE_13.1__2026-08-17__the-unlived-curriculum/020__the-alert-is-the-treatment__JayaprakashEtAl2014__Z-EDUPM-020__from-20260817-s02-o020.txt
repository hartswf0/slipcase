TITLE:
THE EARLY-ALERT SCORE BECOMES A TREATMENT WHEN IT TRIGGERS ACTION: THE SYSTEM CHANGES BOTH GRADES AND THE BEHAVIOR THAT PRODUCES THEM.

SOURCE:
Sandeep M. Jayaprakash, Erik W. Moody, Eitel J. M. Lauría, James R. Regan, and Joshua D. Baron — Early Alert of Academically At-Risk Students: An Open Source Analytics Initiative — 2014 — Journal of Learning Analytics 1(1), 6–47.

SOURCE URL:
https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1127037.pdf

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE] The Open Academic Analytics Initiative used predictive models to identify at-risk students and evaluated intervention strategies. It reports improved course outcomes associated with intervention and also unintended behavior, including warnings contributing to some early withdrawals.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
An early-alert model becomes part of a treatment once its classification is coupled to a notification or support protocol.

LOCAL MOVE:
Jayaprakash et al. supply an educational implementation in which prediction, intervention, and outcome are operationally connected.

SOURCE TERMS:
early alert
at-risk students
predictive model
intervention
control group
student performance
withdrawal

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The same alert can improve outcomes for some students and alter exit behavior for others. The score enters the trajectory it is later used to evaluate.

QUESTION:
What is the object being evaluated: the risk model, the message, the support protocol, or the coupled prediction-intervention system?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Can model accuracy remain primary when deployment changes both the outcome and its meaning?

MECHANISM:
learning-system data → risk model → classification → notification/intervention → changed behavior → changed course outcome

FORMAL SHIFT:
<ACADEMIC RISK ESTIMATE>
→ <ALERT>
→ [INTERVENTION / RESPONSE]
→ <CHANGED BEHAVIOR>
→ <CHANGED OUTCOME>

SOURCE FORMALISM:
The article evaluates predictive-model portability and intervention effectiveness in a multi-institutional early-alert initiative; it does not supply a formal performativity model.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
Observed outcome after an alert is a function of score, message, recipient, response, and institutional policy, not only pre-alert features.

TENSION:
Positive average effects do not imply uniform benefit. Alerts can change help-seeking, withdrawal, anxiety, or disengagement in different directions.

MISSING:
An event-level record linking model version and score to exact intervention, recipient response, timing, and outcome.

BOUNDARY:
The article supports prediction-intervention coupling and reports intervention effects; it does not itself use the language of performative prediction.

CITATION TRAIL:
[[Z-EDUPM-003]] → early warning as intervention → [[Z-EDUPM-016]] → intervention changes later labels → Jayaprakash et al. 2014

TEST:
Reanalyze early alerts at event level, distinguishing score, message, intervention, student response, withdrawal timing, and final outcome.
