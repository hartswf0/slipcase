TITLE:
MICRO-CREDENTIALS DO NOT PROVE THE OLD CREDENTIAL HAS FAILED; THEY DECOMPOSE ONE LONG-HORIZON CLAIM OF FITNESS INTO SMALLER CLAIMS WITH THEIR OWN ASSESSMENT AND VERIFICATION EVENTS.

SOURCE:
Beverley Oliver — Towards a Common Definition of Micro-credentials — 2022 — UNESCO.

SOURCE URL:
https://unesdoc.unesco.org/ark:/48223/pf0000381668

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE] UNESCO’s proposed definition treats a micro-credential as a record of focused learning achievement that verifies what a learner knows, understands, or can do; it includes assessment against defined standards, is awarded by a trusted provider, has standalone value, and may contribute to larger credentials.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
Credential proliferation can be read as temporal and epistemic unbundling rather than simply credential inflation.

LOCAL MOVE:
The definition makes the credential smaller while demanding explicit assessment, standards, provider trust, and a specified learning achievement.

SOURCE TERMS:
micro-credential
focused learning achievement
assessment

trusted provider
standalone value
quality assurance
learning outcomes

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The bachelor’s degree bundles thousands of claims into one delayed settlement:

THIS PERSON IS EDUCATED.

A micro-credential attempts a much smaller claim:

THIS PERSON CAN DO X,
tested under Y standard,
issued by Z provider.

The proliferation of credentials may therefore indicate a demand for more frequent and granular settlement of competence claims.

QUESTION:
Is hyper-credentialism an inflation of signals—or a response to the inability of large credentials to expose which particular claim is being certified?

DEEPER QUESTION:
What happens to education when every competence must become small enough to be separately assessed, verified, transported, and recombined?

MECHANISM:
large bundled qualification
→ decompose learning outcomes
→ define smaller achievement
→ assess against standard
→ issue portable record
→ optionally stack into larger credential

SOURCE FORMALISM:
UNESCO’s definition supplies constitutive criteria:
- focused learning achievement,
- verification,
- assessment,
- standards,
- trusted provider,
- standalone or combinable value,
- quality assurance.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

DEGREE:
  one large signal
  / long horizon
  / many latent competencies

MICRO-CREDENTIALS:
  C1(skill_a, issuer_x, standard_q, date_t1)
  C2(skill_b, issuer_y, standard_r, date_t2)
  C3(skill_c, issuer_z, standard_s, date_t3)

The credential ecology gains resolution while multiplying verification events.

TENSION:
The initial hypothesis reads endless micro-credentials as evidence that the old degree has lost predictive value.

UNESCO gives another surviving interpretation:
smaller credentials may be designed for flexibility, portability, focused learning, and lifelong education even where conventional degrees retain value.

MISSING:
Evidence establishing why a particular labor market adopts micro-credentials:
signal failure,
skills change,
institutional marketing,
regulation,
learner flexibility,
or employer demand.

BOUNDARY:
The UNESCO definition does not claim that micro-credentials emerged because degrees stopped predicting worker quality. That genealogy remains unverified.

CITATION TRAIL:
European Council definition of micro-credentials.
OECD alternative credentials.
Credential ecology / stackability research.
Labor-market studies comparing employer interpretation of degrees versus granular credentials.
Randall Collins on credentialism — but only after separating his sociological mechanism from contemporary micro-credential architecture.

TEST:
Show employers equivalent candidate profiles containing:

A. degree only;
B. micro-credentials only;
C. degree + micro-credentials;
D. direct work sample.

Measure predicted competence, interview selection, wage offer, and confidence.

Then test whether micro-credentials substitute for, complement, or merely decorate the degree signal.
