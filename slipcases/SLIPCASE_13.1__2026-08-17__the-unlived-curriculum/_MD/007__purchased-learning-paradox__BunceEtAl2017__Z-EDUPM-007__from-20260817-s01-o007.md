ZETTEL

ID:
Z-EDUPM-007

TITLE:
“STUDENT AS CONSUMER” IS NOT YET “STUDENT BUYS AN A”: CONSUMER ORIENTATION MAY INSTEAD WEAKEN THE LEARNER IDENTITY REQUIRED TO PRODUCE THE PERFORMANCE BEING PURCHASED.

SOURCE:
Louise Bunce, Amy Baird, and Siân E. Jones — “The Student-as-Consumer Approach in Higher Education and Its Effects on Academic Performance” — 2017 — Studies in Higher Education 42(11), 1958–1978.

SOURCE URL:
https://doi.org/10.1080/03075079.2015.1127908

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE] In a survey of 608 undergraduates in England, greater consumer orientation was associated with lower academic performance and mediated relationships among learner identity, grade goals, and performance.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
Consumerization may alter the subjectivity with which the student approaches learning before it alters professors’ willingness to award grades.

LOCAL MOVE:
The study operationalizes “student as consumer” as a measurable orientation rather than assuming that paying tuition automatically produces entitlement.

SOURCE TERMS:
student-as-consumer
consumer orientation
learner identity
grade goal
academic performance
tuition fees

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The commodity relation may not simply mean:

I PAID
→ GIVE ME THE CREDENTIAL.

It may also mean:

I PAID
→ EDUCATION IS SOMETHING DELIVERED TO ME
→ MY ROLE AS PRODUCER OF MY OWN LEARNING WEAKENS.

The consumer model can therefore damage the very asset the consumer believes they are purchasing.

QUESTION:
Does marketization primarily corrupt grading standards, or does it first change the student’s theory of where learning comes from?

DEEPER QUESTION:
What happens when an institution sells an outcome whose production still requires uncompensated participation by the buyer?

MECHANISM:
fee responsibility / marketized environment
→ consumer orientation
↔ learner identity
→ engagement / academic behavior
→ performance

FORMAL SHIFT:
<STUDENT>
→ <CUSTOMER SUBJECT POSITION>
→ [REINTERPRET EDUCATIONAL RELATION]
→ <CHANGED LEARNER ORIENTATION>
→ <CHANGED PERFORMANCE>

SOURCE FORMALISM:
The source uses survey measures and mediation analysis connecting:
consumer orientation,
learner identity,
grade goal,
and self-reported academic performance.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

EDUCATION is an unusual commodity because:

PURCHASE(ACCESS)
does not imply
PURCHASE(LEARNING).

Production remains partly co-produced by the purchaser.

TENSION:
The source supports concern about consumer orientation but pressures the stronger story that students simply “pay and receive an A.”

Its empirical pattern instead associates stronger consumer orientation with poorer performance.

MISSING:
Direct causal identification.
Evidence from U.S. universities.
Direct measures of grading pressure on faculty.
Administrative rather than self-reported performance.

BOUNDARY:
The study is observational, based on students in England, and uses self-reported academic performance. It cannot establish that consumer orientation causes lower grades or that tuition policy causes faculty grade inflation.

CITATION TRAIL:
Naidoo & Jamieson — student consumerism.
Molesworth, Nixon & Scullion — marketisation and student subjectivity.
Research on academic entitlement.
Love & Kotchen — grade/evaluation incentive coupling.

TEST:
Measure separately:

A. consumer orientation,
B. academic entitlement,
C. learner identity,
D. willingness to complain about grades,
E. actual grade appeals,
F. faculty grading response.

Determine whether “consumerism” reaches grades through student disengagement, complaint pressure, faculty incentives, or distinct pathways.
