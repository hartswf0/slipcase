===== 001 Z-SLIP-001 =====

ZETTEL

ID:
Z-SLIP-001

TITLE:
A research file must preserve the next possible move, not merely the previous statement

SOURCE:
Watson Hartsoe — SLIPCASE — 2026 — “The problem,” “Every run ends in files,” “The paper is a wager”

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
SLIPCASE asks what must be true of a file for research to survive the software that produced it. Cards preserve not only claims but provenance, relations, unresolved addresses, questions, and tests.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
Durability in research archives may depend less on preserving content than on preserving affordances for continuation.

LOCAL MOVE:
The specification shifts archival survival from “can this document still be read?” to “can another intelligence reconstruct what could reasonably be done next?”

SOURCE TERMS:
research checkpoint
zettel
ghost
PLATFORM
LINKS
provenance
reconstruction state
open edges

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
A perfectly preserved note may still be intellectually dead if its unresolved dependencies, questions, and possible tests have disappeared.

QUESTION:
What is the minimum information a research object must contain for an unfamiliar future reader or machine to continue the inquiry rather than merely recover its conclusions?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Could “continuability” become a stronger archival criterion than readability or format longevity?

MECHANISM:
Research survives when the file carries enough evidence of its position in an inquiry—identity, origin, links, unresolved edges, and possible tests—to regenerate future operations.

FORMAL SHIFT:
<research statement>
→ <addressable research object with provenance and unresolved edges>
→ [RECONSTRUCT / LINK / FORAGE / TEST]
→ <continuable inquiry>

SOURCE FORMALISM:
NONE

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
SURVIVAL ≠ readability alone

SURVIVAL =
READABILITY
+ ADDRESSABILITY
+ PROVENANCE
+ RELATIONAL RECOVERY
+ CONTINUATION POTENTIAL

TENSION:
More continuation metadata may make the supposedly simple durable file increasingly dependent on a schema.

MISSING:
A failure test demonstrating when a readable file can no longer support reconstruction of its inquiry.

BOUNDARY:
The SLIPCASE specification proposes features of continuable research objects but does not establish which are strictly necessary or sufficient.

CITATION TRAIL:
Digital preservation literature on significant properties; archival diplomatics; provenance models; executable and reproducible research objects.

TEST:
Delete all derived SLIPCASE machinery and give only root-level cards to an unfamiliar researcher. Measure which intended research operations they can reconstruct.

PLATFORM:
[[research objects that survive their software]]

LINKS:

[[significant properties]]
[[reconstruction state]]
[[archive as executable possibility]]

A ghost is not missing information but a declared future object

SOURCE:
Watson Hartsoe — SLIPCASE — 2026 — “The relations get compiled”

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
An unresolved declared address becomes a ghost. Ghosts are published rather than hidden, and those receiving multiple inbound links become promising forage targets.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
An unresolved reference can become a positive research object when the system preserves its identity and inbound demand.

LOCAL MOVE:
SLIPCASE converts failed resolution from an error state into prospective structure.

SOURCE TERMS:
ghost
unresolved
address
inbound links
conceptual attractor
forage target
broken link

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The archive may know that an idea is important before anyone has written the note describing it.

QUESTION:
Can an archive discover a concept before it possesses content for that concept?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Is a sufficiently connected absence already a weak form of knowledge?

MECHANISM:
Independent cards emit the same unresolved address; relation compilation aggregates those references; repeated convergence produces a visible empty node; that node becomes a candidate for investigation.

FORMAL SHIFT:
<unwritten but repeatedly named concept>
→ <persistent unresolved address>
→ [INVERT LINKS + COUNT INBOUND REFERENCES]
→ <visible conceptual attractor>

SOURCE FORMALISM:
ghost = declared [[ADDRESS]] with no conservatively resolvable card

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

GHOST(g) :=
unresolved(g) ∧ indegree(g) ≥ 1

ATTRACTOR(g) :=
GHOST(g) ∧ independent_origins(g) ≥ 2

LIVE_EDGE(g) :=
ATTRACTOR(g) ∧ occurrence_in(g,
 {QUESTION, DEEPER_QUESTION, MISSING, CITATION_TRAIL, TEST})

TENSION:
High indegree can reflect repeated vocabulary, bad naming practice, or copied assumptions rather than genuine conceptual importance.

MISSING:
A notion of independent origin strong enough to distinguish genuine convergence from copying or template inheritance.

BOUNDARY:
Link frequency alone does not establish scholarly significance.

CITATION TRAIL:
Information retrieval work on query expansion and latent needs; graph-theoretic treatments of missing nodes; Luhmann on references to unwritten continuations; open-world knowledge representation.

TEST:
Compare the top ten high-indegree ghosts against expert-chosen future research questions across several mature checkpoints.

ZETTEL

ID:
Z-SLIP-004

TITLE:
The epistemic boundary should run between judgment and enumeration

SOURCE:
Watson Hartsoe — SLIPCASE — 2026 — “The model judges; the tool counts”

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
Interpretive operations belong to the model; deterministic operations such as hashing, counting, parsing, extraction, resolution, compilation, and verification belong to scripts. No quantity should be reported unless a command produced it.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
Hybrid research systems need an explicit allocation of epistemic authority between probabilistic interpretation and deterministic computation.

LOCAL MOVE:
SLIPCASE converts a practical anti-hallucination measure into an architectural rule.

SOURCE TERMS:
model judges
tool counts
deterministic operation
verification
fabrication surface

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
“Use tools” is underspecified. The important question is not whether tools exist but which claims each component is permitted to make.

QUESTION:
Can every output claim in an AI research system be assigned to the weakest mechanism capable of establishing it?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Should provenance record not just where information came from but which computational authority licensed each assertion?

MECHANISM:
Semantic interpretation is delegated to a language model; enumerable and verifiable properties are delegated to programs; final artifacts retain the distinction.

FORMAL SHIFT:
<mixed research operation>
→ <typed epistemic responsibilities>
→ [MODEL JUDGMENT | DETERMINISTIC COMMAND]
→ <claims with differentiated authority>

SOURCE FORMALISM:
“No number is reported that no command produced.”

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

ASSERTION_TYPES:

SEMANTIC → model permitted
ENUMERATIVE → command required
IDENTITY → deterministic resolution preferred
VERIFICATION → command required
INTERPRETIVE_SYNTHESIS → model permitted + provenance required

TENSION:
Many operations—entity resolution, citation matching, duplicate detection, even deciding what counts as a zettel—sit between purely semantic and purely deterministic categories.

MISSING:
A treatment of mixed operations and uncertainty thresholds.

BOUNDARY:
Deterministic production does not make the underlying categories correct; a script can count precisely what was classified badly.

CITATION TRAIL:
Human-in-the-loop systems; proof-carrying data; data provenance; typed effect systems; reproducible computational research.

TEST:
Annotate every field in one checkpoint with the mechanism authorized to produce it and identify fields whose authority cannot be cleanly assigned.

PLATFORM:
[[epistemic division of labor]]

LINKS:
[[model judges tool counts]]
[[typed assertions]]
[[fabrication surface]]
[[mechanical reconciliation]]
