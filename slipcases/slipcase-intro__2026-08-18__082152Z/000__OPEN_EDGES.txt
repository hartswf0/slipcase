CARD FRONTIERS

Z-SLIP-001 — A research file must preserve the next possible move, not merely the previous statement
QUESTION: What is the minimum information a research object must contain for an unfamiliar future reader or machine to continue the inquiry rather than merely recover its conclusions?
MISSING: A failure test demonstrating when a readable file can no longer support reconstruction of its inquiry.
TEST: Delete all derived SLIPCASE machinery and give only root-level cards to an unfamiliar researcher. Measure which intended research operations they can reconstruct.

Z-SLIP-002 — A ghost is not missing information but a declared future object
QUESTION: Can an archive discover a concept before it possesses content for that concept?
MISSING: A notion of independent origin strong enough to distinguish genuine convergence from copying or template inheritance.
TEST: Compare the top ten high-indegree ghosts against expert-chosen future research questions across several mature checkpoints.

Z-SLIP-003 — The strongest research queue may be compiled from what the archive fails to resolve
QUESTION: What happens if the next research question is selected from unresolved relational pressure rather than proposed by the researcher from scratch?
MISSING: A source of novelty not reducible to graph recurrence.
TEST: Run two research branches from the same checkpoint: one following top-ranked ghosts, another following human-selected strange low-frequency edges. Compare novelty and usefulness after three generations.

Z-SLIP-004 — The epistemic boundary should run between judgment and enumeration
QUESTION: Can every output claim in an AI research system be assigned to the weakest mechanism capable of establishing it?
MISSING: A treatment of mixed operations and uncertainty thresholds.
TEST: Annotate every field in one checkpoint with the mechanism authorized to produce it and identify fields whose authority cannot be cleanly assigned.

Z-SLIP-005 — Completeness has at least three independent dimensions
QUESTION: What classes of completeness need to remain orthogonal in AI-mediated research?
MISSING: A vocabulary for unknown denominators, where even the amount of unseen material cannot be estimated.
TEST: Take a checkpoint currently described as “complete” and score its three dimensions independently. Observe whether materially different states were hidden by the single label.

Z-SLIP-006 — Root placement can encode irreversibility rather than category
QUESTION: Would organizing artifacts by recoverability produce more durable research repositories than organizing them by document type or topic?
MISSING: A test for whether an artifact is genuinely derivable rather than merely reproducible in rough form.
TEST: Delete every underscore directory from a mature checkpoint and rebuild it using only root objects and retained compiler instructions. Diff the results.

Z-SLIP-007 — A disposable paper can protect the archive from its own argument
QUESTION: What changes if papers are treated as compiled views over a persistent field of research objects?
MISSING: A method for distinguishing legitimate alternative compilations from cherry-picked arguments using the same card field.
TEST: Compile two explicitly opposed papers from the same checkpoint while forbidding either process from deleting or rewriting source cards.

Z-SLIP-008 — Disclosure becomes more informative when its unit is the production chain
QUESTION: What events in a mixed human-model production chain are consequential enough to require explicit disclosure?
MISSING: A principled definition of consequential control.
TEST: Show readers a binary AI label and a chain-style public file for the same artifact. Test which supports more accurate judgments about control, verification, and responsibility.

Z-SLIP-009 — Low-definition research artifacts can deliberately transfer completion to the reader
QUESTION: How much incompleteness is necessary for an archive to invite intellectual participation without becoming merely confusing?
MISSING: An account of who is capable of participating and what prerequisite knowledge the archive assumes.
TEST: Build three versions of the same checkpoint interface with increasing explanatory completion. Compare reader-generated questions, cross-links, and successful reconstructions.

Z-SLIP-010 — A vocabulary that never converges may reveal either intellectual breadth or addressing failure
QUESTION: How can a research system distinguish conceptual novelty from accidental synonymy?
MISSING: A reversible mechanism for proposing equivalence without silently merging identities.
TEST: Have independent reviewers classify 100 near-match platform names as SAME, RELATED, DISTINCT, or UNCERTAIN and compare those judgments to automated normalization.

Z-SLIP-011 — Lineage addresses make intellectual descent visible but may confuse descent with identity
QUESTION: Can a research object simultaneously have a stable identity and an identifier that encodes its evolving genealogy?
MISSING: A merge semantics for cards with multiple intellectual ancestors.
TEST: Construct adversarial merge histories—two-parent merges, split/recombine, duplicate discovery, independent rediscovery—and attempt to preserve stable IDs without losing ancestry.

Z-SLIP-012 — A sendable research object is stronger than a shareable interface
QUESTION: What is the smallest self-contained artifact from which a research field can be reconstructed after its original tooling disappears?
MISSING: A separation between “single-file transmission object” and “canonical storage form.”
TEST: Air-gap a checkpoint, remove the original repository, and ask another researcher to reconstruct every mechanically derivable artifact using only the transmitted file.

Z-SLIP-013 — Recursive research objects require a second state that must never become part of the object
QUESTION: What is the minimum external state required to turn recursively forageable research objects into sustained inquiry?
MISSING: A classification of which orchestration state is mechanically reconstructible and which constitutes consequential research history.
TEST: Run the same ZETTEL graph twice, once with preserved daemon state and once after reconstructing state only from the cards. Compare the next ten selected research edges.

Z-SLIP-014 — Research direction becomes an operation over the whole graph rather than inheritance from the latest note
QUESTION: What should determine the allocation of research attention when lineage, novelty, uncertainty, and potential consequence point toward different edges?
MISSING: A novelty mechanism capable of surfacing edges that are low-connectivity, low-frequency, or poorly represented but potentially field-changing.
TEST: Compare global-frontier steering against depth-first lineage following and random edge selection on the same checkpoint. Measure how often each strategy changes multiple existing interpretations rather than merely adding leaves.

Z-SLIP-015 — A research archive can be monotonic in evidence while remaining nonmonotonic in interpretation
QUESTION: Could a research system support radical theoretical revision while maintaining an append-only evidentiary substrate?
MISSING: A distinction between immutable source payload and the revisable admission decision that made it a research object.
TEST: Take a checkpoint whose central thesis has been defeated. Recompile a contradictory interpretation without modifying or deleting any admitted evidence and record exactly where the old architecture obstructs the new reading.

Z-SLIP-016 — A prompt can be causally consequential to scholarship without becoming evidence for the scholarship
QUESTION: How should research provenance distinguish artifacts that caused inquiry from artifacts that justify its conclusions?
MISSING: A vocabulary for recording consequential prompt interventions without drowning the archive in every trivial instruction.
TEST: For each major paper claim in one checkpoint, trace both SUPPORT and PRODUCTION ancestry. Identify cases where the production chain materially shaped the claim but is invisible in the evidence chain.

Z-SLIP-017 — A ghost can govern research attention while remaining forbidden as evidence
QUESTION: What kinds of non-evidence may legitimately determine where research effort is spent?
MISSING: A decay or skepticism mechanism for ghosts repeatedly investigated without yielding evidence.
TEST: Track high-ranking ghosts through several forage generations. Record which become evidenced research objects, which dissolve through renaming, and which continue consuming attention without evidentiary yield.

Z-SLIP-018 — Autonomous inquiry requires a principled condition for surrendering priority back to a human
QUESTION: How can a research system detect the transition from an epistemic decision to a value-laden priority decision?
MISSING: Observable indicators that distinguish “hard research problem” from “human priority decision.”
TEST: Collect daemon stopping decisions across a large graph and ask independent researchers whether each frontier required more evidence or a substantive choice of values/priorities. Compare classifications.

Z-SLIP-019 — The research tree disappears when children can pressure ancestors
QUESTION: What relation should represent the event in which later evidence changes how an earlier research object must be read?
MISSING: A relation vocabulary for retrospective qualification that preserves the distinction between explicit card links and compiler interpretation.
TEST: Select ten cases where later zettels appear to alter earlier ones. Have multiple compilers independently classify the relation as SUPPORTS, QUALIFIES, CONTRADICTS, SPLITS, or UNRELATED and compare disagreement.

Z-SLIP-020 — The full system is not a forager but a research metabolism
QUESTION: What is the correct unit of analysis for a research system whose important property is continued transformation rather than any individual output?
MISSING: A benchmark for research-system quality that evaluates transformation across generations rather than the apparent quality of one final paper.
TEST: Evaluate the system after five checkpoint generations using two criteria: quality of the latest paper and recoverability/change-history of the whole field. Determine whether these rankings diverge.
