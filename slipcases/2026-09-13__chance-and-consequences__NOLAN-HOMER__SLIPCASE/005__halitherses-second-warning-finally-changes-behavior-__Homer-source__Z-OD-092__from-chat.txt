TITLE:
Halitherses’ second warning finally changes behavior, so warning failure is conditional rather than structural

SOURCE:
Homer — Odyssey 24.450–471.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
After the massacre Halitherses warns the Ithacans not to attack Odysseus lest they draw further evil upon themselves. More than half of the assembly immediately abandon the retaliatory expedition; the rest follow Eupeithes and die or are stopped.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
The poem contains a controlled contrast in which the same seer is ignored early and obeyed late.

LOCAL MOVE:
BOOK 2:
Halitherses warns.
Audience ignores.
Predicted destruction occurs.

BOOK 24:
Halitherses warns.
Majority obeys.
They exit the new revenge sequence.

SOURCE TERMS:
Halitherses
πείθω
κακόν
τίσις
assembly

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The poem does not teach that warnings inevitably fail.

It asks what conditions make the same kind of warning actionable.

After the massacre the evidence environment has changed:
the earlier prophecy has been vindicated,
bodies are visible,
and Halitherses has accumulated epistemic authority.

QUESTION:
What changed between Book 2 and Book 24 that converted warning into action?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Does causal knowledge become behaviorally effective only after sufficient consequences have already occurred?

MECHANISM:
W₁
+ weak credibility / high confidence of audience
→ ignored.

W₁ fulfilled
→ source credibility updated.

W₂ from same interpreter
+ visible catastrophe
→ majority compliance.

FORMAL SHIFT:
<WARNING WITHOUT ESTABLISHED AUTHORITY>
→ <FULFILLED CONSEQUENCE>
→ [UPDATED TRUST]
→ <WARNING THAT CHANGES ACTION>

SOURCE FORMALISM:
Book 24 explicitly says more than half of the assembly depart after Halitherses’ speech.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

Trust(Halitherses,t₂)
>
Trust(Halitherses,t₀)

because:

Prediction₁ → Fulfilled.

Therefore:

P(obey Warning₂)
>
P(obey Warning₁).

TENSION:
Those who obey may act from fear rather than genuine causal learning.

MISSING:
Whether the poem marks explicit recognition that Halitherses had been correct before.

BOUNDARY:
This does not prove Bayesian updating as an ancient cognitive model.
