TITLE:
τίσις is not merely a consequence; it is a narrative that selects an offense, victim, avenger, and equivalence

SOURCE:
Alexander C. Loney — The Ethics of Revenge and the Meanings of the Odyssey — 2019 — Chapters 1–3.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
Loney treats tisis as negative reciprocity with temporal, calculative, and adjudicative dimensions and then explicitly analyzes tisis in the Odyssey as a narrative form. Different vengeance narratives can manipulate these structures and illuminate one another ironically.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
Retributive consequence requires narrative assembly.

LOCAL MOVE:
A death does not become τίσις merely because it follows an injury.

A tisis account must specify:

WHAT WAS THE PRIOR OFFENSE?
WHO WAS HARMED?
WHO MAY REPAY?
WHAT COUNTS AS EQUIVALENT?
WHEN IS THE DEBT COMPLETE?

SOURCE TERMS:
τίσις
τίνω
ἀποτίνω
reciprocity
temporality
calculation

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
“Consequence” sounds event-like.

τίσις is story-like.

It needs antecedent selection and moral roles.

The same physical killing can therefore belong to incompatible narratives:

JUST REPAYMENT
or
NEW OFFENSE.

QUESTION:
Is vengeance causal because of what physically precedes it, or because a community accepts a particular narrative linking the acts?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Who possesses authority to stabilize one revenge narrative when several valid sequences overlap?

MECHANISM:
event₀
→ remembered injury
→ narrative role assignment
→ equivalence calculation
→ retaliatory event₁
→ event₁ becomes available to rival narrative.

FORMAL SHIFT:
<PHYSICAL EVENT SEQUENCE>
→ <ROLE-ASSIGNED STORY>
→ [TISIS CLASSIFICATION]
→ <AUTHORIZED RETALIATION>

SOURCE FORMALISM:
Loney explicitly calls tisis a narrative and emphasizes temporality, calculation, and judgment in negative reciprocity.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

event sequence ≠ necessarily tisis.

TENSION:
The narrative framing may not be arbitrary; social norms can constrain which tisis stories are plausible.

MISSING:
What institutional or communal processes in Homeric society authorize the “judge” role when Zeus is absent.

BOUNDARY:
Narrativity does not make vengeance merely fictional.
