TITLE:
After Book 13, Odysseus begins programming other people’s consequence conditions

SOURCE:
Homer — Odyssey 13; 16; 19; 21–22.
Jackie Murray — “Book 13” — The Oxford Critical Guide to Homer’s Odyssey — 2025 — pp. 169–180.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
After returning to Ithaca, Odysseus and Athena plan his disguised infiltration and tests of the suitors. Subsequent books progressively control who knows his identity, who possesses weapons, who controls doors, who receives the bow, and when recognition is allowed to occur.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
Causal design after Book 13 operates through information permissions.

LOCAL MOVE:
Athena changes Odysseus’s appearance.

Odysseus selectively reveals identity.

Telemachus removes weapons.

Eumaeus and Philoetius receive authenticated access.

Doors are closed.

The bow is routed into Odysseus’s hands.

The suitors remain informationally behind the state of the world they inhabit.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The revenge plan is partly an access-control system.

The decisive resource is not merely superior strength.

It is asymmetric knowledge of:
identity,
weapons,
doors,
alliances,
and timing.

QUESTION:
Does the Odyssey turn information asymmetry itself into an instrument of consequence?

DEEPER QUESTION:
What distinguishes clever causal staging from morally problematic entrapment?

MECHANISM:
control information
→ control expectations
→ control allocation of resources
→ constrain opponents’ possible responses
→ trigger reveal
→ engineered consequence.

SOURCE FORMALISM:
Book 13 centers on disguise, deception, infiltration, and testing; Book 21 explicitly coordinates the bow transfer and locking of doors after Odysseus authenticates himself to Eumaeus and Philoetius with the scar.
