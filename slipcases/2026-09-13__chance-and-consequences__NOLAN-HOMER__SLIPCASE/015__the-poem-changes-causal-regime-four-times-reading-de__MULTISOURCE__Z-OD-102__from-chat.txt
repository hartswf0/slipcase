TITLE:
The poem changes causal regime four times: reading, designing, verifying, then stopping consequences

SOURCE:
Homer — Odyssey 1–24.
The Oxford Critical Guide to Homer’s Odyssey — Joel P. Christensen, ed. — 2025.
Alexander C. Loney — The Ethics of Revenge and the Meanings of the Odyssey — 2019.

PASSAGE:
NONE

RESEARCH OBJECT:
The 24-book poem may be organized by transformations in what characters do with consequences.

LOCAL MOVE:
BOOKS 1–12:
READ CONSEQUENCE.

Agents encounter warnings, failures, prophecies, divine interventions, and outcomes whose causes must be understood.

BOOKS 13–22:
DESIGN CONSEQUENCE.

Athena and Odysseus manipulate disguise, information, signs, tests, weapons, doors, and timing to produce a desired terminal state.

BOOK 23:
VERIFY CONTINUITY.

Penelope determines whether the victorious agent is genuinely continuous with the husband who left twenty years earlier and whether their relation can resume.

BOOK 24:
STOP CONSEQUENCE.

The slaughter becomes a new antecedent for revenge and requires an externally imposed stopping rule.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The poem does not have one “theory of consequence.”

It successively changes the operation applied to consequence:

INTERPRET
→ ENGINEER
→ AUTHENTICATE
→ TERMINATE.

QUESTION:
Is this operational progression a better description of the Odyssey’s architecture than wanderings / return / revenge?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Does Odysseus’s education fail if he becomes excellent at engineering consequences without learning how to stop them?

MECHANISM:
Phase I:
outcome → explanation.

Phase II:
desired outcome → backwards plan.

Phase III:
claimed identity → verification against history.

Phase IV:
completed revenge → recursion → stopping rule.

TENSION:
The phases overlap heavily: prophecy continues after Book 13, planning occurs before it, recognition pervades the entire poem, and revenge structures begin in Book 1.

MISSING:
A book-by-book quantitative test of dominant operation.

BOUNDARY:
This is a structural hypothesis, not a source claim.
