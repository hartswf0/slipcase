TITLE:
Books 9–12 make the main witness to causation the same person whose responsibility is under dispute

SOURCE:
Homer — Odyssey 9–12.
Elizabeth Minchin — “Book 12” — The Oxford Critical Guide to Homer’s Odyssey — 2025 — pp. 158–168.
Alexander C. Loney — 2015.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
Odysseus narrates the Wanderings himself to the Phaeacians. Recent scholarship emphasizes both the authority and self-conscious craft of his first-person narrative and the impossibility of independently establishing the “truth” of the adventures; Loney separately interrogates Odysseus’s presentation of responsibility for the companions.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
The causal archive of the Wanderings is controlled by an interested survivor.

LOCAL MOVE:
The person supplying:

warnings,
crew speeches,
his own motives,
divine communications,
and causal sequence

is also:

commander,
sole survivor,
beneficiary of the story,
and object of possible blame.

SOURCE TERMS:
ἀπόλογοι
ἀοιδός
ἀτασθαλία
ἑταῖροι
storytelling

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
Coding the Wanderings directly as events hides the most important metadata:

SOURCE = ODYSSEUS.

A causal edge such as:

CREW DISOBEYED → CREW DIED

is not delivered by a neutral recorder.

QUESTION:
How should causal coding represent interested narration?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Can the poem simultaneously authorize Odysseus as storyteller and invite suspicion about the causal account he provides?

MECHANISM:
multi-agent event
→ all other witnesses die
→ survivor narrates
→ selects speech, antecedents, and motives
→ audience reconstructs causality from survivor-controlled archive.

SOURCE FORMALISM:
Book 12’s recent critical guide explicitly foregrounds Odysseus’s self-awareness as storyteller, narrative authority, purpose in self-presentation, and problems concerning the truth of his tale.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

Every coded edge from Books 9–12 should include:

EDGE_SOURCE = Odysseus
CORROBORATION = narrator / god / none
SELF_INTEREST = possible
ALTERNATIVE_WITNESS = mostly absent.

TENSION:
The external narrator’s proem independently attributes the companions’ deaths to their own ἀτασθαλίαι, partially corroborating Odysseus.

MISSING:
Which causal claims in the Apologoi receive external-narrator confirmation.

BOUNDARY:
Interested testimony is not false testimony.
