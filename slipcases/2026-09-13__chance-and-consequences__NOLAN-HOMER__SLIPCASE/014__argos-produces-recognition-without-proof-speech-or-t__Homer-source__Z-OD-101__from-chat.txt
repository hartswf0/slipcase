TITLE:
Argos produces recognition without proof, speech, or test

SOURCE:
Homer — Odyssey 17.290–327.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
Argos recognizes the disguised Odysseus, moves his ears and tail, lacks the strength to approach, and dies shortly afterward. No scar is shown, no private story is exchanged, and no human interpretive procedure mediates the recognition.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
The Odyssey contains recognition outside its explicit evidentiary machinery.

LOCAL MOVE:
Elsewhere:

uncertain identity
→ σῆμα
→ test
→ recognition.

presence
→ recognition.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
If recognition is causal inference, Argos is an anomaly.

The dog appears to retain direct relational continuity through transformed appearance without requiring the proof structures humans need.

QUESTION:
What does Argos preserve that humans have lost?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Does the poem distinguish embodied familiarity from propositional evidence?

SOURCE FORMALISM:
The narrator presents Argos as recognizing Odysseus while the disguised hero remains unrecognized by the humans around him.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

Human recognition often requires:

Evidence → Inference → Recognition.

Argos suggests:

RelationMemory + Encounter → Recognition.

TENSION:
The poem does not explain the sensory mechanism of Argos’s recognition.

MISSING:
Whether smell, movement, voice, or narrative convention supplies the recognition channel.

BOUNDARY:
Argos cannot establish a Homeric theory of animal memory.
