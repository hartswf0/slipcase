TITLE:
Odyssean objects do not merely preserve memory; they store capacities for later action

SOURCE:
Homer — Odyssey 21 and 23.
“Odysseus and His Bed: From Significant Objects to Thing Theory in Homer” — Classical Quarterly — 2020.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
Scholarship on the bed, bow, and orchard argues that these objects are first shaped by human action yet later become plot-producing agents: the bed defines Odysseus as husband, the bow as hero, and the orchard as heir.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
Material objects can preserve causal history in executable form.

LOCAL MOVE:
Odysseus builds the bed.

Years later:
the bed enables reunion.

Odysseus owns and masters the bow.

Years later:
the bow enables identity disclosure and slaughter.

The orchard embodies a history of inheritance and cultivation.

Years later:
it participates in Laertes’ recognition.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
Memory need not reside primarily in a mind.

An object can carry forward:

production history,
social role,
skill requirements,
private knowledge,
and future affordances.

QUESTION:
When does a trace become an actor in the next causal sequence?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Is Odysseus’s identity distributed across material systems that remember him more reliably than people do?

MECHANISM:
human action at t₀
→ object transformed
→ durable structure persists
→ later situation activates stored structure
→ new action becomes possible.

SOURCE FORMALISM:
The cited study explicitly describes bed, bow, and orchard as both patients of human agency and later agents in the plot, connected to distributed personhood.

TENSION:
Calling objects “agents” can obscure the human interpretive and practical work required to activate them.

MISSING:
A distinction between:
stored information,
affordance,
symbolic value,
and causal agency.

BOUNDARY:
Homer does not articulate a theory of distributed cognition or thing agency.
