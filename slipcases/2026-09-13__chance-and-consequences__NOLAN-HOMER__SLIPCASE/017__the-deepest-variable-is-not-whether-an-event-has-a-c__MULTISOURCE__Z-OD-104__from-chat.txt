TITLE:
The deepest variable is not whether an event has a cause but whether its causal account becomes actionable

SOURCE:
Homer — Odyssey 1–24.
Alexander C. Loney — The Ethics of Revenge and the Meanings of the Odyssey — 2019.
Bruno Currie — “Recognizing Odysseus, reading Penelope” — 2022.

PASSAGE:
NONE

RESEARCH OBJECT:
Across warning, recognition, revenge, and peace, information repeatedly changes status from merely available to action-authorizing.

LOCAL MOVE:
A prophecy can be known but ignored.

A dream can be interpreted but not believed.

A scar can prove identity and activate alliance.

A remembered offense can authorize revenge.

A remembered offense can later be prevented from authorizing revenge.

The crucial transition is therefore not:

UNKNOWN → KNOWN

but:

KNOWN → ACTIONABLE.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The poem’s epistemology and ethics appear coupled through permissions.

Knowledge alone does little.

Knowledge becomes consequential when it changes what an actor regards as:

possible,
permitted,
required,
or owed.

QUESTION:
What turns causal knowledge into authorization?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Is the Odyssey less concerned with causal truth than with governance of what truths are allowed to do?

MECHANISM:
information
→ interpretation
→ belief
→ normative status
→ action permission
→ consequence.

SOURCE FORMALISM:
NONE

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

For proposition P:

Know(P)
≠
Believe(P)
≠
MayActOn(P)
≠
MustActOn(P).

The Odyssey repeatedly manipulates transitions among these states.

Examples:

Halitherses:
Know/claim → not acted on → later acted on.

Penelope:
interpretable dream → suspended belief.

Eumaeus/Philoetius:
scar → authenticated alliance → coordinated action.

Suitor kin:
remembered death → revenge claim.

Zeus:
remembered death → claim disabled.

TENSION:
Formal permission language risks turning poetic, affective, religious, and kinship relations into artificial state machines.

MISSING:
The Homeric verbs and social practices corresponding to transitions among:
KNOW,
BELIEVE,
TRUST,
OWE,
MAY,
MUST.

BOUNDARY:
“Actionable causal knowledge” is our analytic object.
