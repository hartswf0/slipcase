ID: PF-082

TITLE:
Prompt sophistication does not guarantee expressive control.

SOURCE:
U.S. Copyright Office — Copyright and Artificial Intelligence, Part 2 — 2025.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE] The Copyright Office specifically rejects the proposition that increasingly detailed prompts necessarily give users sufficient control over resulting expressive elements.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
Generic versus elaborate prompting is not by itself a sufficient authorship distinction.

LOCAL MOVE:
The source directly pressures one of the field's most intuitive gradations.

SOURCE TERMS:
detailed prompts; control; expressive elements; human authorship.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
A thousand-word prompt can be more intellectually sophisticated without being more determinative of expression.

QUESTION:
What dimensions of prompt sophistication matter for scholarship if detail is not a reliable proxy for expressive control?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Could a very short but formally operative prompt program exert more control than a long prose specification?

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
DETAIL / CONTROL / CONTRIBUTION are independent axes.

TENSION:
The prompt-forward argument must stop treating prompt length or specificity as automatic evidence of authorship.

MISSING:
Direct measures of control.

BOUNDARY:
The report's conclusion is about copyrightability under current systems.

CITATION TRAIL:
LMQL; constrained decoding; DSPy Assertions.

TEST:
