ZETTEL

ID: PF-088

TITLE:
Contributor vocabularies already demonstrate the power of verbs before verdicts.

SOURCE:
NISO — CRediT Role Descriptors — n.d.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE] CRediT describes research through operations such as conceptualization, methodology, investigation, validation, writing, supervision, and software.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
Operational role vocabularies can precede and support higher-level credit judgments.

LOCAL MOVE:
The taxonomy offers a model for describing practice without pretending all actions are equivalent.

SOURCE TERMS:
conceptualization; methodology; investigation; validation; writing; software.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
A prompt-specific vocabulary may not need to begin from zero.

QUESTION:
Which prompt-forward verbs map cleanly onto CRediT, and which are genuinely new?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Are “specify, constrain, branch, reject, recurse, orchestrate” new scholarly roles or lower-level operations inside existing ones?

MECHANISM:
<fine-grained operations>
→ <role grouping>
→ [contribution statement]
→ <credit/accountability>

FORMAL SHIFT:
NONE

SOURCE FORMALISM:
CRediT taxonomy.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
PROMPT VERBS → CRediT roles → unresolved residue.

TENSION:
Inventing too many new verbs can create a taxonomy that describes every click but explains nothing.

MISSING:
Granularity rule.

BOUNDARY:
CRediT roles are intentionally broader than individual workflow actions.

CITATION TRAIL:
Human-computer interaction task taxonomies; provenance ontologies.

TEST:
Code one hundred prompt interactions with both fine-grained verbs and CRediT roles; identify stable correspondences.
