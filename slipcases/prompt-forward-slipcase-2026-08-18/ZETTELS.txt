ZETTEL

ID: PF-090

TITLE:
The prompt may be an epistemic instrument precisely where it cannot be reduced to either prose or output.

SOURCE:
Davis Baird — Thing Knowledge: A Philosophy of Scientific Instruments — 2004.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE] Baird argues that scientific knowledge is not exhausted by propositions; instruments and material artifacts can embody epistemically significant know-how.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
The question “what kind of scholarly object is an instruction that changes what a generative machine can do?” can be displaced into philosophy of scientific instruments.

LOCAL MOVE:
Baird makes room for knowledge-bearing artifacts that are neither theories nor ordinary textual claims.

SOURCE TERMS:
thing knowledge; instrument; epistemology; material artifact.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The prompt is made of language, yet its important property may be neither what it says nor the text it produces, but the repeatable operation it enables.

QUESTION:
Can there be a class of **operative scholarly writing** whose epistemic content is demonstrated by what it makes a system reliably do?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Would the prompt then be closer to a protocol, algorithm, instrument, score, or a genuinely new hybrid object?

MECHANISM:
<instruction artifact>
→ <generative machine>
→ [reliably alter capability]
→ <epistemically usable operation>

FORMAL SHIFT:
<linguistic instruction>
→ <versioned executable specification>
→ [model-mediated operation]
→ <demonstrated capability>

SOURCE FORMALISM:
NONE

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
PROMPT-AS-SCHOLARLY-OBJECT requires:
1. specified operation,
2. reproducible behavioral difference,
3. articulated boundary,
4. provenance of construction,
5. epistemic use.

TENSION:
Baird deliberately expands epistemology toward material things; converting his argument back into language risks losing the force of the move. Copyright doctrine adds another pressure: consequential instructions need not amount to expressive authorship.

MISSING:
A genealogy linking scientific protocol, program text, executable specification, prompt program, and epistemic instrument without pretending they are identical.

BOUNDARY:
No source yet establishes “prompt = scholarly object.” The source merely makes the question sharper and harder.

CITATION TRAIL:

Hacking — Representing and Intervening.
Beurer-Kellner, Fischer, Vechev — Prompting Is Programming.
U.S. Copyright Office — Copyright and Artificial Intelligence, Part 2.

W3C PROV.

Take one prompt claimed as a scholarly contribution. Remove its rhetoric and test only five things: whether it specifies an operation, changes capability reproducibly, survives model/context variation, records its own construction, and enables an epistemic result unavailable without it. Then ask which existing scholarly artifact class can describe all five without residue.
