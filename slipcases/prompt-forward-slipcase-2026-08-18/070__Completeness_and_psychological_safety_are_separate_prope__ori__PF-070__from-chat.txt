ZETTEL

ID: PF-070

TITLE:
Completeness and psychological safety are separate properties of a research record.

SOURCE:
U.S. Office of Research Integrity — Laboratory Notebooks — n.d.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE] ORI recommends detailed records sufficient for others to reconstruct what was done and for results to be validated.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
A notebook can be epistemically complete without being institutionally private.

LOCAL MOVE:
The source pressures the assumption that exploratory safety requires incomplete records.

SOURCE TERMS:
complete; reproducibility; validation; laboratory notebook.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The unresolved variable is not record completeness but who may inspect the record, when, and for what purpose.

QUESTION:
Could prompt workspaces remain complete while access is purpose-limited?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Is access governance more important than data minimization for protecting exploratory inquiry?

MECHANISM:
<complete record>
→ <access rules>
→ [authorized inspection]
→ <accountability without universal exposure>

FORMAL SHIFT:
NONE

SOURCE FORMALISM:
NONE

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
RECORD COMPLETENESS × ACCESS CONTROL.

TENSION:
Keeping sensitive material creates breach and subpoena/investigation risks even if routine access is limited.

MISSING:
Institutional access architecture for prompt histories.

BOUNDARY:
ORI does not address AI conversation privacy.

CITATION TRAIL:
Electronic lab notebook permissions; IRB data governance.

TEST:
Compare selective-retention and complete-but-access-controlled provenance designs against a set of realistic audit scenarios.
