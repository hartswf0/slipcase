ZETTEL

ID: PF-025

TITLE:
Capability and compliance evaluate causal influence under different norms.

SOURCE:
Khattab et al. — DSPy — 2023; Georgia Institute of Technology — Interim AI Policy — 2026.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE] DSPy optimizes language-model pipelines against task metrics; Georgia Tech makes the magnitude and significance of AI use relevant to disclosure.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
The same fact—an intervention materially changed an outcome—can be relevant to both engineering success and disclosure without carrying the same value judgment.

LOCAL MOVE:
One regime optimizes causal efficacy; the other uses significance to decide whether transparency obligations increase.

SOURCE TERMS:
metric; optimization; magnitude; significance; disclosure.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
Opposite administrative consequences do not necessarily mean opposite judgments of moral worth.

QUESTION:
Is “capability versus contamination” too strong a formulation for a policy that may merely require disclosure?

DEEPER QUESTION:
What empirical evidence would show that disclosure itself functions as stigma rather than neutral provenance?

MECHANISM:
<large causal effect>
→ engineering: [reward performance]
→ governance: [increase disclosure relevance]
→ <different institutional action>

FORMAL SHIFT:
NONE

SOURCE FORMALISM:
DSPy optimization to a metric; qualitative disclosure factors.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
SAME CAUSAL FACT ≠ SAME NORMATIVE FUNCTION.

TENSION:
The draft turns difference in administrative purpose into a stronger cure/poison opposition.

MISSING:
Evidence of sanctions, reputational costs, or negative evaluations attached to disclosed high-impact AI use.

BOUNDARY:
Disclosure relevance is not itself a finding of misconduct.

CITATION TRAIL:
Publisher disclosure practices; reader perceptions of AI disclosure.

TEST:
Compare evaluations of identical work under otherwise equivalent disclosure descriptions varying only magnitude of AI involvement.
