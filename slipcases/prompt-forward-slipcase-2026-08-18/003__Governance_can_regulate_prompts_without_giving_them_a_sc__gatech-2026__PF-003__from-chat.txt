ZETTEL

ID: PF-003

TITLE:
Governance can regulate prompts without giving them a scholarly ontology.

SOURCE:
Georgia Institute of Technology — Interim Artificial Intelligence (AI) in Academic & Research Contexts — 2026.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE] Georgia Tech regulates disclosure and responsibility for AI-assisted scholarly work without developing a taxonomy of prompts as scholarly artifacts.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
Regulatory visibility and scholarly recognition are separate operations.

LOCAL MOVE:
The policy asks how AI use should be handled, not what every AI-facing linguistic object positively is.

SOURCE TERMS:
AI use; disclosure; substantive; responsibility.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The absence of “prompt as scholarship” may be intentional scope rather than conceptual blindness.

QUESTION:
What ontology must governance possess in order to regulate an activity fairly?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Can governance remain artifact-agnostic while still distinguishing trivial prompting from methodologically consequential prompting?

MECHANISM:
<heterogeneous AI practices>
→ <policy category AI use>
→ [apply disclosure/accountability rules]
→ <institutional judgment>

FORMAL SHIFT:
NONE

SOURCE FORMALISM:
NONE

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
REGULATION(prompting) does not imply THEORY(prompting).

TENSION:
The original aphorism treats missing positive ontology as a defect; a governance document may reasonably defer that ontology to scholarly communities.

MISSING:
Evidence that this deferral causes actual misclassification.

BOUNDARY:
Policy silence does not establish institutional denial of prompts as intellectual work.

CITATION TRAIL:
Georgia Tech disciplinary guidelines; ACM/IEEE artifact policies; methodological descriptions of prompt-based research.

TEST:
Compare how the same prompt artifact is classified by a policy office, an IRB, a software venue, and a dissertation committee.
