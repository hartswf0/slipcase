ZETTEL

ID: PF-043

TITLE:
Orchestration can be a contribution role without becoming writing authorship.

SOURCE:
NISO — CRediT Role Descriptors — n.d.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE] CRediT separates supervision, project administration, methodology, and writing into different contribution categories.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
Coordinating a process need not be assimilated to writing in order to count.

LOCAL MOVE:
The taxonomy institutionalizes plural contribution forms.

SOURCE TERMS:
supervision; project administration; methodology; writing.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The conductor analogy may prove contribution while failing to prove the particular category “author.”

QUESTION:
What kind of contribution is prompt orchestration most analogous to: methodology, supervision, software, writing, or a new role?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Could “orchestration” be decomposed into several existing roles instead of introduced as a new authorship category?

MECHANISM:
<multiple components>
→ <human coordination>
→ [sequence/control]
→ <integrated output>

FORMAL SHIFT:
NONE

SOURCE FORMALISM:
CRediT roles.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
ORCHESTRATION = sequencing + constraint + delegation + evaluation.

TENSION:
Calling orchestration “authorship” may obscure already available contribution vocabularies.

MISSING:
Empirical mapping of AI workflows to CRediT roles.

BOUNDARY:
CRediT expressly does not determine authorship.

CITATION TRAIL:
Production studies; directing; research supervision.

TEST:
Have expert panels classify prompt-orchestration tasks using existing CRediT categories and record irreducible residue.
