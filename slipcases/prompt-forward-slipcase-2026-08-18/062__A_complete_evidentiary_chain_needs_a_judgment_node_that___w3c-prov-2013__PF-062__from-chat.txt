ZETTEL

ID: PF-062

TITLE:
A complete evidentiary chain needs a judgment node that ordinary provenance does not supply.

SOURCE:
Moreau and Missier, eds. — PROV-DM — 2013.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE] PROV models entities, activities, agents, and their production relations.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
The desired chain—interaction → artifact → disclosure → institutional judgment → rationale—contains both provenance and adjudication.

LOCAL MOVE:
PROV can represent production history but not the normative sufficiency of a disclosure by itself.

SOURCE TERMS:
entity; activity; agent; provenance.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The “complete chain” crosses from descriptive lineage into institutional reasoning.

QUESTION:
How should an adjudication be represented alongside provenance without pretending it is another causal event?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Could provenance systems encode reasons and standards as first-class entities?

MECHANISM:
<interaction>
→ <artifact>
→ <disclosure>
→ [institutional rule/judgment]
→ <decision>

FORMAL SHIFT:
NONE

SOURCE FORMALISM:
PROV graph.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
PROVENANCE LAYER + NORMATIVE LAYER.

TENSION:
Combining both layers may falsely naturalize a contestable judgment as part of “what happened.”

MISSING:
Representation of rules, reasons, and appeals.

BOUNDARY:
PROV is descriptive infrastructure, not jurisprudence.

CITATION TRAIL:
Argumentation models; legal provenance; policy-as-code.

TEST:
Model one hypothetical AI-disclosure case with separate provenance and adjudication graphs and test where evaluators disagree.
