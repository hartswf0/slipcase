ZETTEL

ID: PF-085

TITLE:
Before saying governance cannot calibrate “too much,” identify what object its threshold actually targets.

SOURCE:
Georgia Institute of Technology — Interim AI Policy — 2026.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE] The policy speaks of magnitude and significance of AI use rather than of prompt quantity specifically.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
The threshold target is broader than prompts.

LOCAL MOVE:
The source breaks the aphorism's implied equation between AI use and prompting.

SOURCE TERMS:
AI use; magnitude; significance.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
A policy may intentionally regulate a heterogeneous activity class rather than define each component technology.

QUESTION:
Is “AI use” too broad to support reliable judgment, or appropriately broad because the governed risk cuts across techniques?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Which distinctions materially change the policy outcome?

MECHANISM:
<heterogeneous AI activities>
→ <umbrella category>
→ [qualitative assessment]
→ <disclosure>

FORMAL SHIFT:
NONE

SOURCE FORMALISM:
NONE

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR INFERENCE]
Ontology should split only distinctions that change adjudication.

TENSION:
A complete theory of prompting may be intellectually valuable but unnecessary for some governance tasks.

MISSING:
Decision-sensitive feature analysis.

BOUNDARY:
Do not assume governance must define prompts in order to regulate AI use at all.

CITATION TRAIL:
Risk-based regulation; technology-neutral law.

TEST:
Ask adjudicators whether detailed prompt-role information changes decisions compared with broad AI-use descriptions.
