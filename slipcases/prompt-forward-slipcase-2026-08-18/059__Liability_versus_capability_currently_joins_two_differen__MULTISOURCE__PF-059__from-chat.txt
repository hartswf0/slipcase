ZETTEL

ID: PF-059

TITLE:
“Liability versus capability” currently joins two different institutional questions too quickly.

SOURCE:
Georgia Institute of Technology — Interim AI Policy — 2026; Khattab et al. — DSPy — 2023.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE] Georgia Tech regulates disclosure and responsibility for AI use; DSPy optimizes model pipelines for capability.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
Governance asks who must disclose or answer for an activity; engineering asks whether the activity works.

LOCAL MOVE:
The sources expose different predicates applied to overlapping technical objects.

SOURCE TERMS:
disclosure; responsibility; metric; pipeline.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
Liability is not simply the negative version of capability.

QUESTION:
Where is there actual evidence that prompt effectiveness increases legal or disciplinary liability?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Would “responsibility/capability” be a more accurate opposition until misconduct cases are found?

MECHANISM:
<prompt intervention>
→ engineering [evaluate efficacy]
→ governance [evaluate obligations]
→ <two descriptions>

FORMAL SHIFT:
NONE

SOURCE FORMALISM:
NONE

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR INFERENCE]
CAPABILITY and LIABILITY are orthogonal variables.

TENSION:
The sharper aphoristic formulation may be rhetorically stronger than the evidence.

MISSING:
Cases in which prompt capability itself increased sanction or liability.

BOUNDARY:
Current sources support different institutional functions, not direct inversion.

CITATION TRAIL:
Misconduct decisions; contract/IP disputes involving prompts.

TEST:
Search adjudicated AI cases for whether effective prompting is explicitly treated as aggravating evidence.
