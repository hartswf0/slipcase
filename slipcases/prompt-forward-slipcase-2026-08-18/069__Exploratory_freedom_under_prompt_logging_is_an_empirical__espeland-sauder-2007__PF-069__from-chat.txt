ZETTEL

ID: PF-069

TITLE:
Exploratory freedom under prompt logging is an empirical governance variable.

SOURCE:
Espeland and Sauder — Rankings and Reactivity — 2007.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE] Actors modify conduct when they know they are subject to consequential measurement.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
“Researchers should be allowed to ask the ugliest question” predicts that traceability can alter inquiry content.

LOCAL MOVE:
The source supplies a general reactivity mechanism but not the AI-specific effect.

SOURCE TERMS:
reactivity; public measure; evaluation.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The relevant outcome may not be fewer prompts but less epistemic variance in prompts.

QUESTION:
Does audit visibility reduce adversarial, speculative, taboo, or counter-normative exploration?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Which kinds of inquiry are selectively chilled?

MECHANISM:
<anticipated review>
→ <self-censorship>
→ [narrow prompt distribution]
→ <reduced exploratory space>

FORMAL SHIFT:
NONE

SOURCE FORMALISM:
NONE

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR INFERENCE]
AUDIT VISIBILITY → entropy reduction in exploratory prompting.

TENSION:
Logging can also make researchers more rigorous and less reckless.

MISSING:
Measured benefits and chilling effects in the same experiment.

BOUNDARY:
Reactivity literature alone does not establish chilling.

CITATION TRAIL:
Research ethics; surveillance effects; self-censorship.

TEST:
Measure semantic diversity and risk profiles of prompts under private, team-visible, and committee-visible conditions.
