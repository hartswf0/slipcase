TITLE:
Metalanguage can precede object theory when institutions govern by standards rather than definitions.

SOURCE:
Georgia Institute of Technology — Interim AI Policy — 2026.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE] The policy employs open-textured terms such as magnitude, significance, and disciplinary appropriateness.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
The “sentencing guidelines before defining the offense” metaphor assumes governance must proceed from complete ontological definition.

LOCAL MOVE:
The source instead resembles standards-based governance.

SOURCE TERMS:
magnitude; significance; discipline norms; appropriate disclosure.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
Many institutional rules govern through evaluative standards whose application is stabilized by precedent and professional judgment.

QUESTION:
Is the core failure absent definition or absent precedent?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Would adjudicated examples solve more than a philosophical ontology of prompting?

MECHANISM:
<open standard>
→ <case>
→ [professional interpretation]
→ <precedent/calibration>

FORMAL SHIFT:
NONE

SOURCE FORMALISM:

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
OBJECT THEORY and CASE CALIBRATION are substitute mechanisms only in some respects.

TENSION:
Without enough shared understanding of the object, precedent can become inconsistent.

MISSING:
Case history.

BOUNDARY:
The policy's qualitative vocabulary does not prove conceptual inversion.

CITATION TRAIL:
Standards versus rules in law; professional ethics adjudication.

TEST:
Compare consistency gains from giving evaluators definitions versus giving them adjudicated exemplars.
