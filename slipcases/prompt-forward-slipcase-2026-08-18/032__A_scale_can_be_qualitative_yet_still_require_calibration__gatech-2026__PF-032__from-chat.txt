ID: PF-032

TITLE:
A scale can be qualitative yet still require calibration.

SOURCE:
Georgia Institute of Technology — Interim Artificial Intelligence (AI) in Academic & Research Contexts — 2026.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE] The policy relies on magnitude and significance judgments rather than numerical thresholds.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
The issue is not absence of units but absence or presence of shared calibration practices.

LOCAL MOVE:
The source redirects critique from “no ruler markings” toward “how do evaluators stabilize judgment?”

SOURCE TERMS:
magnitude; significance; appropriate.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
Many mature institutional standards operate without numerical thresholds.

QUESTION:
What would count as adequate calibration for qualitative AI-use judgments?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Are exemplars, precedent, rubrics, and professional norms functional substitutes for units?

MECHANISM:
<qualitative predicate>
→ <examples/precedent>
→ [community calibration]
→ <judgment>

FORMAL SHIFT:
NONE

SOURCE FORMALISM:
NONE

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR INFERENCE]
Reliability depends on shared calibration, not numericality.

TENSION:
The original “ruler without inches” metaphor risks mistaking qualitative judgment for measurement failure.

MISSING:
Inter-rater reliability data.

BOUNDARY:
The policy does not report calibration performance.

CITATION TRAIL:
Assessment rubrics; qualitative coding reliability; disciplinary ethics standards.

TEST:
Have evaluators independently classify a fixed set of AI-use scenarios before and after exposure to shared exemplars.
