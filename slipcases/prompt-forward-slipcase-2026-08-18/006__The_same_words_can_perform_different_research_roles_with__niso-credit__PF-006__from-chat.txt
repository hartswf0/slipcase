ZETTEL

ID: PF-006

TITLE:
The same words can perform different research roles without having the same institutional status.

SOURCE:
NISO — CRediT Role Descriptors — n.d.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE] CRediT distinguishes conceptualization, methodology, investigation, software, validation, and writing as different contribution roles within research.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
Institutional function is assigned by use in a workflow, not by linguistic material alone.

LOCAL MOVE:
The taxonomy recognizes that contribution type depends on what an activity does in the research process.

SOURCE TERMS:
methodology; conceptualization; software; investigation; writing.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
A string of prose can plausibly perform methodology in one placement and writing in another.

QUESTION:
Can prompt classification be made relational—by workflow position and operation—rather than lexical?

DEEPER QUESTION:
What must remain invariant for “the same linguistic act” to genuinely count as the same act across protocol, code, interview instrument, and prompt?

MECHANISM:
<linguistic artifact>
→ <workflow placement>
→ [perform research operation]
→ <contribution role>

FORMAL SHIFT:
NONE

SOURCE FORMALISM:
Contributor-role taxonomy.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
ROLE = f(artifact, placement, operation), not f(text alone).

TENSION:
Calling identical words “the same act” may erase differences in addressee, execution environment, reversibility, and accountability.

MISSING:
A cross-context comparison of identical instructions.

BOUNDARY:
CRediT does not classify prompts.

CITATION TRAIL:
Speech-act theory; workflow provenance; executable specifications.

TEST:
Place one instruction verbatim into a protocol, code comment, research-assistant memo, interview guide, and LLM prompt; trace what each actually causes.
