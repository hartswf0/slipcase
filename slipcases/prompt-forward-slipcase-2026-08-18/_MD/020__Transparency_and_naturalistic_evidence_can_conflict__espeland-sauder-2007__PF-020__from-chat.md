ID: PF-020

TITLE:
Transparency and naturalistic evidence can conflict.

SOURCE:
Espeland and Sauder — Rankings and Reactivity — 2007.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE] Measurement systems can change the behavior and organizational practices they render visible.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
Transparency has an observational cost when the observed actor anticipates evaluation.

LOCAL MOVE:
The source destabilizes the assumption that more visible process automatically means truer process evidence.

SOURCE TERMS:
reactivity; measurement; evaluation.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
A perfectly traceable workflow may be less representative of unobserved inquiry than an incomplete one.

QUESTION:
What degree of process capture yields useful accountability without substantially reorganizing the process?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Is there an analogue of observer effect for scholarly provenance systems?

MECHANISM:
<visibility>
→ <anticipated judgment>
→ [behavior changes]
→ <trace differs from unobserved practice>

FORMAL SHIFT:
NONE

SOURCE FORMALISM:
NONE

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
TRACE FIDELITY has two axes:
event completeness / behavioral naturalism.

TENSION:
Without process capture, evaluators may have no basis for distinguishing workflows at all.

MISSING:
A measured transparency–reactivity curve.

BOUNDARY:
The tradeoff is inferred, not quantified by the source.

CITATION TRAIL:
Humanly; HaLLMark; audit studies.

TEST:
Increase trace visibility incrementally across experimental conditions and measure behavioral drift.
