ID: PF-075

TITLE:
“Brainstorming” hides interaction structure that process datasets can recover.

SOURCE:
Lee, Liang, and Yang — CoAuthor — 2022.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE] CoAuthor records request, suggestion, insertion, revision, and timing behavior across large numbers of human–LM writing sessions.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
A single disclosure noun can collapse heterogeneous interaction sequences.

LOCAL MOVE:
The dataset makes those sequences available for analysis.

SOURCE TERMS:
collaborative writing; interaction; dataset; replay.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
“AI was used for brainstorming” may describe workflows whose machine contribution and human control differ radically.

QUESTION:
Which process features should replace broad labels such as brainstorming, editing, or drafting?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Could disclosures be generated from traces rather than self-selected category names?

MECHANISM:
<interaction log>
→ <process features>
→ [classify]
→ <specific disclosure>

FORMAL SHIFT:
NONE

SOURCE FORMALISM:
CoAuthor interaction dataset.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
DISCLOSURE should describe operations, not broad activity nouns.

TENSION:
Highly detailed process disclosure may be unreadable.

MISSING:
A compact vocabulary grounded in actual trace variation.

BOUNDARY:
CoAuthor does not provide a compliance schema.

CITATION TRAIL:
Humanly; process mining; CRediT.

TEST:
Derive five to ten operational disclosure labels from CoAuthor-style traces and test whether readers infer contribution more accurately.
