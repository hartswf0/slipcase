ID: PF-071

TITLE:
Failed prompts fit ordinary research-record logic better than the exceptionalism thesis suggests.

SOURCE:
U.S. Office of Research Integrity — Laboratory Notebooks — n.d.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE] Good research notebooks preserve what was done with enough detail to support validation and later reconstruction.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
Failures and abandoned paths are ordinary parts of research records.

LOCAL MOVE:
The source normalizes keeping unsuccessful experiments rather than treating failure traces as a uniquely AI problem.

SOURCE TERMS:
records; methods; data; reproducibility; validation.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
A prompt history full of failures may resemble a laboratory notebook more than a polished manuscript.

QUESTION:
Which aspects of failed prompting are genuinely novel relative to existing recordkeeping practice?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Does conversational generativity change the evidentiary status of failure, or only its volume and intimacy?

TENSION:
The more prompts resemble experiments, the weaker the claim that their messy failures must remain private by default.

MISSING:
Comparison of failure-record norms across sciences, design, humanities, and AI research.

BOUNDARY:
Notebook norms do not automatically justify publishing failures.

CITATION TRAIL:
Negative results; lab notebook history; design process documentation.

TEST:
Compare disclosure expectations for failed code experiments, failed wet-lab experiments, and failed prompt experiments.
