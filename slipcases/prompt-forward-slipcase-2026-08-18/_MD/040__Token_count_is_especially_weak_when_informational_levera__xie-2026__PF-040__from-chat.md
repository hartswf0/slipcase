ZETTEL

ID: PF-040

TITLE:
Token count is especially weak when informational leverage is concentrated.

SOURCE:
Xie et al. — Measuring Human Contribution in AI-Assisted Content Generation — 2026.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE] Human contribution is modeled through information conveyed to the result, not by raw input length.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
Textual mass and conditional influence are separable.

LOCAL MOVE:
Information theory supplies a mechanism beneath the aphorism's gear metaphor.

SOURCE TERMS:
mutual information; human contribution; self-information.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
A tiny prompt can have large output influence, but a large influence can still be intellectually trivial.

QUESTION:
How should leverage be separated from intellectual difficulty?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Can a three-word instruction be both causally dominant and scholarly negligible?

MECHANISM:
<short input>
→ <large conditional shift>
→ [information transmission]
→ <high causal/informational leverage>

FORMAL SHIFT:
NONE

SOURCE FORMALISM:
Information-theoretic contribution measure.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
LEVERAGE ≠ LENGTH; LEVERAGE ≠ MERIT.

TENSION:
Replacing token count with mutual information solves only one of two problems.

MISSING:
A theory of scholarly significance layered over causal contribution.

BOUNDARY:
High measured human contribution need not imply high-quality research.

CITATION TRAIL:
Philosophy of scientific contribution; novelty metrics.

TEST:
Construct high-leverage trivial prompts and low-leverage conceptually sophisticated prompts; ask experts to score contribution separately.
