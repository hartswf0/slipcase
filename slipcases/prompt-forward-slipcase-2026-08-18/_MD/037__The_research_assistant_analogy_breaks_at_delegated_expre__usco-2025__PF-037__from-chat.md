ID: PF-037

TITLE:
The research-assistant analogy breaks at delegated expressive judgment.

SOURCE:
U.S. Copyright Office — Copyright and Artificial Intelligence, Part 2 — 2025.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE] The Office distinguishes human instructions from human authorship where another process determines expressive execution.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
Giving intellectually rich instructions is not equivalent to personally exercising all downstream judgment.

LOCAL MOVE:
The report destabilizes analogies that treat prompting as straightforward delegation to another human worker.

SOURCE TERMS:
instructions; expressive elements; control; authorship.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
A research assistant can answer for reasons, negotiate ambiguity, and receive authorship credit; a model occupies a different accountability structure.

QUESTION:
Which parts of the research-assistant analogy survive, and which collapse, when the recipient is a generative model?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Is the crucial difference agency, legal personhood, epistemic accountability, execution control, or something else?

MECHANISM:

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
INSTRUCTIONAL SIMILARITY does not imply DELEGATION EQUIVALENCE.

TENSION:
The analogy remains useful for showing that instructions can themselves contain intellectual work.

MISSING:
A precise comparison of human and machine delegation.

BOUNDARY:
Copyright doctrine does not determine scholarly collaboration norms.

CITATION TRAIL:
Delegated research; ghostwriting; principal-agent theory; AI agency.

TEST:
Compare identical research instructions given to an RA and an LLM; trace where clarification, discretion, verification, and responsibility differ.
