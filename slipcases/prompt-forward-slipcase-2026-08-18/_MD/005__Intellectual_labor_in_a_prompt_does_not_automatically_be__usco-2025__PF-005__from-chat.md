ID: PF-005

TITLE:
Intellectual labor in a prompt does not automatically become copyright authorship.

SOURCE:
U.S. Copyright Office — Copyright and Artificial Intelligence, Part 2: Copyrightability — 2025 — pp. 25–26.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE] The Copyright Office concludes that even detailed prompts ordinarily do not provide sufficient control over the expressive elements generated by current systems.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
Intellectual specification and legally cognizable expressive control can diverge.

LOCAL MOVE:
The report compares prompts to instructions that state desired outcomes while leaving execution to another producer.

SOURCE TERMS:
prompts; control; expressive elements; instructions; human authorship.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
A prompt may contain sophisticated intellectual work yet fail a legal authorship test.

QUESTION:
What forms of scholarly contribution do prompts perform that copyright's expressive-control test intentionally ignores?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Are “authorship,” “intellectual work,” “method,” and “copyrightable expression” being falsely treated as interchangeable?

MECHANISM:
<detailed human instruction>
→ <model interpretation>
→ [stochastic generation]
→ <expression not sufficiently controlled by prompter>

FORMAL SHIFT:
NONE

SOURCE FORMALISM:
NONE

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
INTELLECTUAL SPECIFICATION ⊬ EXPRESSIVE AUTHORSHIP.

TENSION:
The prompt-forward thesis becomes weaker if it needs every consequential prompt to count as authorship, but stronger if it distinguishes multiple kinds of contribution.

MISSING:
A positive scholarly category for sophisticated specification that is not final-expression authorship.

BOUNDARY:
The report addresses U.S. copyrightability, not doctoral contribution or research methodology.

CITATION TRAIL:
Commissioned-art doctrine; software specifications; research protocols; CRediT methodology.

TEST:
Classify the same complex prompt under copyright, research-method, software, and dissertation-contribution criteria.
