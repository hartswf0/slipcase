ZETTEL

ID: PF-076

TITLE:
Human and AI contribution can be measured separately rather than treated as complements.

SOURCE:
Xie et al. — Measuring Human Contribution in AI-Assisted Content Generation — 2026.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE] The study focuses specifically on estimating human contribution in AI-assisted generation rather than merely detecting machine-produced text.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
“More AI” does not logically entail “less human” on a single zero-sum axis.

LOCAL MOVE:
The source foregrounds the human side as its own object of measurement.

SOURCE TERMS:
human contribution; AI-assisted generation; information.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
A workflow can increase both machine causal activity and human technical work.

QUESTION:
Should disclosure report machine contribution and human contribution as independent dimensions?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Can both be high simultaneously?

MECHANISM:
<human input + model>
→ <joint process>
→ [estimate human information contribution]
→ <nonbinary attribution>

FORMAL SHIFT:
NONE

SOURCE FORMALISM:
Information-theoretic human contribution.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
HUMAN CONTRIBUTION and AI CONTRIBUTION are not necessarily complements summing to 100%.

TENSION:
Some measurement schemes may still normalize contributions into a closed total.

MISSING:
A non-zero-sum empirical attribution framework.

BOUNDARY:
The source's metric alone does not settle all contribution types.

CITATION TRAIL:
Distributed cognition; team contribution.

TEST:
Construct workflows where human effort and AI computation both increase together and test whether evaluators still force zero-sum judgments.
