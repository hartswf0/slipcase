ZETTEL

ID: PF-034

TITLE:
Ablation establishes causal dependence, not scholarly contribution.

SOURCE:
Park et al. — Generative Agents: Interactive Simulacra of Human Behavior — 2023 — ablation evaluation.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE] The authors ablate memory, reflection, and planning components to test whether those architectural elements materially contribute to agent behavior.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
Removing a component and observing degradation establishes load-bearing function.

LOCAL MOVE:
Ablation supplies an operational answer to “does this component matter?”

SOURCE TERMS:
ablation; reflection; planning; memory; behavior.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The proposed prompt test—remove it and see whether capability disappears—answers a causal question but not who deserves scholarly credit.

QUESTION:
What extra evidence converts causal necessity into intellectual contribution?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Can a trivial constant be causally indispensable without being a scholarly contribution?

MECHANISM:
<system>
→ [remove component]
→ <performance change>
→ <causal contribution estimate>

FORMAL SHIFT:
NONE

SOURCE FORMALISM:
Component ablation.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
CAUSAL LOAD-BEARING ≠ NOVELTY ≠ AUTHORSHIP.

TENSION:
Ablation is necessary evidence for “this prompt creates capability” but insufficient for “this prompt is scholarship.”

MISSING:
Novelty, intentionality, difficulty, generality, and explanatory role.

BOUNDARY:
Do not use ablation alone as an authorship test.

CITATION TRAIL:
Software contribution studies; causal attribution; invention criteria.

TEST:
Compare indispensable but trivial prompt components with dispensable but conceptually novel ones.
