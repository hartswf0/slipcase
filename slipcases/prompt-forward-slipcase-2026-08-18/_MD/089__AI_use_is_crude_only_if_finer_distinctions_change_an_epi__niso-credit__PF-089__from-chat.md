ID: PF-089

TITLE:
“AI use” is crude only if finer distinctions change an epistemic or institutional decision.

SOURCE:
NISO — CRediT Role Descriptors — n.d.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE] CRediT replaces undifferentiated contribution with role-specific descriptions.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
The “glass use” analogy becomes testable as a question of discriminatory value.

LOCAL MOVE:
The source demonstrates why role-specific description can matter.

SOURCE TERMS:
contributor role; methodology; validation; writing; software.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
A coarse label is not intrinsically bad; it is bad when it collapses distinctions relevant to the decision at hand.

QUESTION:
Which AI-use distinctions change judgments of reproducibility, originality, responsibility, or disclosure?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Can the dissertation identify the smallest vocabulary that preserves those decision-relevant differences?

MECHANISM:
<coarse category>
→ <split into roles>
→ [compare judgments]
→ <retain only consequential distinctions>

FORMAL SHIFT:

OUR FORMALIZATION:

TENSION:
A maximal taxonomy would reproduce the whole interaction instead of clarifying it.

MISSING:
Decision-sensitive ontology testing.

BOUNDARY:
CRediT's success does not prove an analogous AI taxonomy will be useful.

CITATION TRAIL:

TEST:
Iteratively remove AI-workflow categories until evaluator accuracy meaningfully drops.
