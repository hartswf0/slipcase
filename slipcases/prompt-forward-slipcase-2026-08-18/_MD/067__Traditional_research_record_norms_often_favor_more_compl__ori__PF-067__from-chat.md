ID: PF-067

TITLE:
Traditional research-record norms often favor more completeness than prompt-minimalism assumes.

SOURCE:
U.S. Office of Research Integrity — Laboratory Notebooks: Notebook and Data Management — n.d.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE] ORI guidance emphasizes sufficiently complete records to permit validation, reproducibility, reporting, and assignment of credit.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
Selective provenance is in tension with established norms of comprehensive research recording.

LOCAL MOVE:
The source pressures the claim that researchers should preserve only contribution-relevant residue.

SOURCE TERMS:
notebook; complete records; reproducibility; validation; credit.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
“Do not submit your epistemic childhood” conflicts with a longstanding scientific norm that notebooks should err toward completeness.

QUESTION:
Why should AI prompt records be treated differently from laboratory notebooks?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Is the relevant difference privacy, conversational intimacy, scale, reactivity, or the model's role as interlocutor?

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR INFERENCE]
Prompt-minimalism requires a principled exception to notebook-completeness norms.

TENSION:
Comprehensive AI logs can contain far more personal, speculative, or irrelevant material than conventional notebooks.

MISSING:
Comparative ethics of AI traces versus lab notebooks.

BOUNDARY:
ORI guidance is laboratory-oriented and not automatically applicable to prompt conversations.

CITATION TRAIL:
Electronic lab notebooks; qualitative fieldnotes; privacy-by-design.

TEST:
Compare what information conventional notebook standards would require versus what full prompt histories contain.
