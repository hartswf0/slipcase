ID: PF-007

TITLE:
The blueprint analogy overstates how much a prompt controls execution.

SOURCE:
U.S. Copyright Office — Copyright and Artificial Intelligence, Part 2: Copyrightability — 2025 — pp. 25–26.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE] The Office argues that detailed prompting can specify desired attributes without determining how the model renders those attributes expressively.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
Specification can be causally consequential without fixing execution.

LOCAL MOVE:
The report separates desired outcome from control of expressive realization.

SOURCE TERMS:
instructions; control; expressive elements; prompts.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
Calling a prompt a blueprint quietly assumes a level of determinate translation that current generative systems often lack.

QUESTION:
What physical artifact is a better analogy for a prompt: blueprint, brief, score, constraint set, seed, or experimental intervention?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Can the analogy be replaced by an operational measure of constraint rather than another metaphor?

MECHANISM:
<prompted attributes>
→ <model-internal generation>
→ [non-deterministic realization]
→ <variable expression>

FORMAL SHIFT:

SOURCE FORMALISM:
NONE

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
SPECIFICATION STRENGTH = reduction in permissible output space.

TENSION:
A prompt can materially narrow outcomes while still leaving decisive expressive choices unresolved.

MISSING:
A metric for how much an instruction actually contracts an output distribution.

BOUNDARY:
The Copyright Office does not claim prompts have no causal effect.

CITATION TRAIL:
Constraint programming; information theory; controllable generation.

TEST:
Estimate output diversity before and after progressively detailed prompts while keeping model and sampling fixed.
