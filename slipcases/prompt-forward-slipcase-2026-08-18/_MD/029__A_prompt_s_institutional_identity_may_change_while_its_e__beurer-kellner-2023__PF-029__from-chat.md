ID: PF-029

TITLE:
A prompt's institutional identity may change while its executable identity remains fixed.

SOURCE:
Beurer-Kellner, Fischer, and Vechev — Prompting Is Programming — 2023.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE] LMQL treats prompt-centered model interaction as a programmable artifact with constraints and control flow.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
Technical status can be established operationally even when legal, employment, or governance status varies.

LOCAL MOVE:
The paper gives prompt programs a computational identity independent of institutional valuation.

SOURCE TERMS:
query language; programming; constraints; language models.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The cross-context table should distinguish changes in what the artifact does from changes in what institutions call it.

QUESTION:
Which institutional category changes are merely labels, and which alter actual rights, obligations, or evidentiary use?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Can one prompt artifact be traced across software, publication, IP, employment, and compliance regimes without assuming those regimes should converge?

MECHANISM:
<same executable artifact>
→ <different institutional frames>
→ [apply local rules]
→ <different statuses>

FORMAL SHIFT:

OUR FORMALIZATION:

EXECUTABLE IDENTITY / INSTITUTIONAL IDENTITY.

TENSION:
The original table risks presenting speculative categories such as “moat” or “evidence of skill” as if they were formally equivalent statuses.

MISSING:
Actual documents from each institutional regime.

BOUNDARY:
LMQL establishes programmability, not employment or IP valuation.

CITATION TRAIL:
Prompt-related patents; employment portfolios; licensing; governance.

TEST:
Choose one published prompt program and document how five institutional regimes formally classify it.
