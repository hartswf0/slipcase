ZETTEL

ID: PF-030

TITLE:
Technical ontology cannot simply be imported into governance unchanged.

SOURCE:
Khattab et al. — DSPy — 2023.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE] DSPy represents language-model pipelines as declarative modules and compiles them to improve task metrics.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
Engineering ontologies are optimized for building systems, not necessarily for assigning scholarly responsibility.

LOCAL MOVE:
DSPy makes model-facing instructions manipulable as program components.

SOURCE TERMS:
modules; compiler; pipeline; metric; declarative.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The very ontology that recognizes prompts technically may omit concepts governance needs: responsibility, disclosure, consent, authorship, and misconduct.

QUESTION:
What should governance import from technical AI practice, and what must it refuse?

DEEPER QUESTION:
What is the minimum shared ontology that lets engineers and governance officers refer to the same underlying operations?

MECHANISM:
<technical artifact>
→ <engineering representation>
→ [optimize capability]
→ <system behavior>

FORMAL SHIFT:
NONE

SOURCE FORMALISM:
DSPy programming model.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
SHARED OBJECTS, DIFFERENT PREDICATES.

TENSION:
“Governance failed to import engineering ontology” presupposes that engineering categories are normatively appropriate.

MISSING:
A translation layer rather than wholesale import.

BOUNDARY:
DSPy cannot tell institutions what counts as scholarly responsibility.

CITATION TRAIL:
Responsible AI standards; software accountability; CRediT.

TEST:
Map the objects recognized by DSPy against the objects required by an AI disclosure policy and identify unmatched categories in both directions.
