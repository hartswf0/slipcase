ZETTEL

ID: PF-083

TITLE:
A prompt must earn epistemic-object status rather than receive it by metaphor.

SOURCE:
Davis Baird — Thing Knowledge — 2004.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE] Baird argues that instruments and material products of science can themselves bear epistemic significance.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
The prompt-as-instrument claim can be transformed into a harder question about what makes an artifact epistemic.

LOCAL MOVE:
Baird challenges proposition-centered epistemology by granting material instruments a knowledge-bearing role.

SOURCE TERMS:
thing knowledge; instrument; epistemology.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
Prompts are symbolic and executable but not obviously “things” in Baird's sense.

QUESTION:
What properties of scientific instruments—reliability, intervention, embodied technique, calibration, material stability—can prompts satisfy?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Could there be “instruction knowledge” analogous to thing knowledge?

MECHANISM:
<artifact>
→ [reliable epistemic operation]
→ <knowledge-producing intervention>

FORMAL SHIFT:
NONE

SOURCE FORMALISM:
NONE

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR INFERENCE]
PROMPT becomes epistemic artifact only if its operation carries reproducible know-how not exhausted by propositional description.

TENSION:
A prompt is already language; Baird's argument partly gains force by moving beyond language-centered accounts of knowledge.

MISSING:
A bridge from material to executable-linguistic artifact.

BOUNDARY:
Baird does not support the prompt claim directly.

CITATION TRAIL:
Scientific software epistemology; computational notebooks; operational knowledge.

TEST:
Compare a prompt, algorithm, laboratory protocol, and physical instrument against Baird-inspired epistemic criteria.
