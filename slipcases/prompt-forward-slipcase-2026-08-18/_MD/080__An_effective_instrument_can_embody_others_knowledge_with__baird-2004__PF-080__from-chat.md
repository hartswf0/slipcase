ZETTEL

ID: PF-080

TITLE:
An effective instrument can embody others' knowledge without transferring their contribution to its user.

SOURCE:
Davis Baird — Thing Knowledge — 2004.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE] Baird treats instruments as epistemically significant artifacts rather than transparent extensions of individual users.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
Powerful tools complicate, rather than erase, distributed contribution.

LOCAL MOVE:
The source blocks the reverse error: if a prompt system is epistemically productive, its user does not automatically deserve all of that productivity as personal contribution.

SOURCE TERMS:
instrument; thing knowledge; epistemic artifact.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
A prompt may embody work from model builders, training data, prior prompt designers, libraries, and the current operator simultaneously.

QUESTION:
What portion of a prompt-mediated capability belongs to the current researcher rather than the inherited instrument?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Can contribution analysis separate instrument-building knowledge from instrument-using knowledge?

MECHANISM:
<inherited apparatus>
+ <operator intervention>
→ [use]
→ <result>

FORMAL SHIFT:
NONE

SOURCE FORMALISM:
NONE

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
CAPABILITY provenance spans instrument makers and operators.

TENSION:
The prompt-forward account often zooms tightly onto the operator and model while backgrounding the infrastructure that makes prompting work.

MISSING:
Upstream technical provenance.

BOUNDARY:
Baird does not provide a quantitative allocation of credit.

CITATION TRAIL:
Infrastructure studies; model supply chain; software dependency attribution.

TEST:
Trace one prompt capability backward through prompt, framework, model, fine-tuning, and training infrastructure.
