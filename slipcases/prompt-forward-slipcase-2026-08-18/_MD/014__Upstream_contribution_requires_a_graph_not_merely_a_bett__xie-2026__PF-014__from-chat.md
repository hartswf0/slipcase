ZETTEL

ID: PF-014

TITLE:
“Upstream” contribution requires a graph, not merely a better percentage.

SOURCE:
Xie et al. — Measuring Human Contribution in AI-Assisted Content Generation — 2026.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE] The proposed metric estimates human contribution from relationships between human input and generated output, while acknowledging the inadequacy of output-only judgments.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
Moving upstream from output still does not automatically capture the workflow.

LOCAL MOVE:
The paper makes input consequential to attribution but keeps a relatively compact human-input/output model.

SOURCE TERMS:
human input; output; contribution; information.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The researcher’s architecture may be spread across multiple prompts, source choices, retries, and selections rather than located in a single input.

QUESTION:
What representation is needed when “the human input” is itself a temporally distributed program of interventions?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Should attribution move from scalar contribution to topology of contribution?

MECHANISM:
<distributed interventions>
→ <interaction graph>
→ [propagate dependencies]
→ <contribution topology>

FORMAL SHIFT:
<human-AI history>
→ <graph>
→ [trace dependencies]
→ <role-specific attribution>

SOURCE FORMALISM:
Information-theoretic scalar contribution.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
SCALAR → GRAPH.

TENSION:
A graph gains descriptive fidelity but may make comparison and policy thresholds much harder.

MISSING:
A graph-based scholarly contribution model.

BOUNDARY:
Nothing in Xie et al. requires a provenance graph; this is a reframe generated by the limitation.

CITATION TRAIL:
W3C PROV; workflow systems; causal graphs.

TEST:
Encode the same ten workflows as both scalar contribution scores and provenance graphs; identify distinctions lost by each.
