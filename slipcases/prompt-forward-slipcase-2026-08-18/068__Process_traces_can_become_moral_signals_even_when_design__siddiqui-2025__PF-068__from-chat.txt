ZETTEL

ID: PF-068

TITLE:
Process traces can become moral signals even when designed as descriptive evidence.

SOURCE:
Siddiqui et al. — DraftMarks — 2025.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE] DraftMarks visually surfaces where AI entered writing, where revision occurred, and how collaboration unfolded.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
A descriptive trace is interpreted through culturally loaded visual signs of effort, revision, and machine contribution.

LOCAL MOVE:
The interface makes process visible through familiar material metaphors.

SOURCE TERMS:
eraser crumbs; masking tape; smudges; transparency; co-writing.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
Representing revision intensity can quietly turn “effort” into evidence of virtue.

QUESTION:
Do provenance visualizations cause readers to reward visible labor independently of quality or intellectual contribution?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Can a process interface avoid smuggling a moral economy of sweat into authorship judgments?

MECHANISM:
<process trace>
→ <visual encoding>
→ [reader interpretation]
→ <ownership/effort judgment>

FORMAL SHIFT:
NONE

SOURCE FORMALISM:
Skeuomorphic encoding.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR INFERENCE]
TRACE DESIGN mediates normative inference.

TENSION:
Without legible encodings, process evidence may remain unusable.

MISSING:
Controlled studies separating trace appearance from actual contribution.

BOUNDARY:
DraftMarks does not imply that effort should determine authorship.

CITATION TRAIL:
Labor signaling; proof-of-work metaphors; visualization rhetoric.

TEST:
Manipulate visual evidence of revision while holding actual workflow constant and measure perceived authorship.

[[The Moral Rhetoric of Provenance]]
