ZETTEL

ID: PF-042

TITLE:
Selection counts as authorship only when the selection itself is sufficiently creative.

SOURCE:
U.S. Copyright Office — Copyright and Artificial Intelligence, Part 2 — 2025 — pp. 31–32.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE] The Office recognizes that human selection, coordination, arrangement, or modification of AI-generated material may contain protectable human authorship.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
Selection is not automatically intellectual authorship; its expressive character matters.

LOCAL MOVE:
The report gives positive legal status to some post-generation human operations.

SOURCE TERMS:
selection; coordination; arrangement; modification; human authorship.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The sculptor metaphor makes rejection look inherently creative, but routine choice among alternatives may not cross any meaningful threshold.

QUESTION:
What distinguishes creative selection from mere preference or quality control?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Do scholarship and copyright need different thresholds for consequential selection?

MECHANISM:
<candidate outputs>
→ <human selection/arrangement>
→ [creative decisions]
→ <human-authored expressive contribution>

FORMAL SHIFT:
NONE

SOURCE FORMALISM:
NONE

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
SELECTION becomes contribution when the rule/arrangement materially constitutes the scholarly object.

TENSION:
Tacit taste can be consequential without being explainable as an explicit rule.

MISSING:
A scholarly—not copyright—test for selection.

BOUNDARY:
The report does not say every selection earns copyright.

CITATION TRAIL:
Compilation authorship; editorial practice; curatorial authorship.

TEST:
Compare evaluator judgments across random selection, quality filtering, theory-driven selection, and compositional arrangement.
