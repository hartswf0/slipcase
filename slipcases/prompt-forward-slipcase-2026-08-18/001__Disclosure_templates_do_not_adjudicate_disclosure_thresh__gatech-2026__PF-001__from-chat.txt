ZETTEL

ID: PF-001

TITLE:
Disclosure templates do not adjudicate disclosure thresholds.

SOURCE:
Georgia Institute of Technology — Interim Artificial Intelligence (AI) in Academic & Research Contexts — 2026 — Disclosure of AI Use.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE] Georgia Tech makes disclosure depend on the magnitude of AI use, the significance of the work product, and relevant disciplinary norms.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
A policy can specify factors for judgment without supplying cases that show how those factors are adjudicated.

LOCAL MOVE:
The policy delegates calibration to context rather than defining a universal quantitative threshold.

SOURCE TERMS:
magnitude; significance; disciplinary norms; disclosure.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The absence of adjudicated exemplars may be less a missing definition than a missing institutional genre between policy and case law.

QUESTION:
What artifact teaches researchers how qualitative AI-disclosure standards are actually applied?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Does AI governance need adjudicated exemplars, or are discipline-specific precedents supposed to perform that work?

MECHANISM:
Abstract threshold → local interpretation → disclosure decision.

FORMAL SHIFT:
<AI use>
→ <magnitude/significance judgment>
→ [apply disciplinary norm]
→ <disclose / do not disclose>

SOURCE FORMALISM:
NONE

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
POLICY STANDARD ≠ ADJUDICATED EXAMPLE.

TENSION:
Demanding case-level calibration from a general policy may ask the policy to perform the work of committees, disciplines, publishers, or precedent.

MISSING:
Published examples containing interaction, artifact, disclosure decision, and rationale.

BOUNDARY:
The policy's lack of adjudicated exemplars does not establish that no such precedents exist elsewhere at Georgia Tech.

CITATION TRAIL:
College-level guidance; graduate-school procedures; research-integrity decisions; publisher AI policies.

TEST:
Find ten real disclosure decisions and compare which properties actually predict the judgment.
