ZETTEL

ID: PF-056

TITLE:
Explicit selection rules are not necessary for selection to be creative.

SOURCE:
U.S. Copyright Office — Copyright and Artificial Intelligence, Part 2 — 2025.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE] The report recognizes potentially copyrightable human selection, coordination, and arrangement of AI-generated material.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
Selection may embody creative judgment without the selector being able to formalize a deterministic rule.

LOCAL MOVE:
The source grants possible expressive significance to selection itself.

SOURCE TERMS:
selection; coordination; arrangement; modification.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The aphorism's demand that the researcher “state and defend the rule” may privilege explicit criteria over tacit judgment.

QUESTION:
How can prompt-forward scholarship document tacit selection without pretending that taste was an algorithm?

DEEPER QUESTION:
What evidence of intellectual selection is available when the criterion is recognitional rather than propositional?

MECHANISM:
<candidates>
→ <human evaluative judgment>
→ [select/arrange]
→ <composite work>

FORMAL SHIFT:
NONE

SOURCE FORMALISM:
NONE

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR INFERENCE]
SELECTION EVIDENCE may be contrastive explanation rather than prior rule.

TENSION:
Without articulated criteria, selection can become impossible to distinguish from arbitrary preference.

MISSING:
Methods for eliciting retrospective but non-fabricated selection rationale.

BOUNDARY:
Copyright's creativity threshold is not identical to scholarly rigor.

CITATION TRAIL:
Polanyi; connoisseurship; design critique; curatorial practice.

TEST:
Ask experts to perform selections first and articulate criteria afterward; compare with predeclared-rule selections.
