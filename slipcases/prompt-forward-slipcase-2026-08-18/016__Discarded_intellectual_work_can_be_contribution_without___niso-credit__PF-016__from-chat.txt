ID: PF-016

TITLE:
Discarded intellectual work can be contribution without becoming authorship.

SOURCE:
NISO — CRediT Role Descriptors — n.d.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE] CRediT recognizes conceptualization, methodology, investigation, and validation independently of writing the published draft.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
Contribution need not survive verbatim in the final manuscript.

LOCAL MOVE:
The taxonomy separates research work from textual retention.

SOURCE TERMS:
conceptualization; methodology; investigation; validation; writing.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The discarded eighteen pages in the aphorism need not be rescued as “authorship” to remain research work.

QUESTION:
Which prompt-mediated activities should be credited as conceptualization or methodology even when no generated text survives?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Would contribution-role reporting resolve more of the AI dispute than arguments over who wrote the sentences?

MECHANISM:
<research activity>
→ <conceptual/methodological effect>
→ [contribute to study]
→ <creditable role without textual inheritance>

FORMAL SHIFT:
NONE

SOURCE FORMALISM:
CRediT taxonomy.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
RETENTION is neither necessary nor sufficient for CONTRIBUTION.

TENSION:
Contribution recognition and authorship qualification are deliberately distinct in CRediT.

MISSING:
Prompt-specific descriptions of conceptualization and methodology.

BOUNDARY:
The taxonomy does not say every abandoned note deserves formal credit.

CITATION TRAIL:
Authorship criteria; CRediT implementation studies.

TEST:
Ask research teams to classify discarded AI-mediated work using CRediT and compare those classifications with final-text-based AI disclosures.
