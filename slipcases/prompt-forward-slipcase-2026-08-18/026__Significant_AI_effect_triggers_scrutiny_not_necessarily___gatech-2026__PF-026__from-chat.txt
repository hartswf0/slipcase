ID: PF-026

TITLE:
Significant AI effect triggers scrutiny, not necessarily condemnation.

SOURCE:
Georgia Institute of Technology — Interim Artificial Intelligence (AI) in Academic & Research Contexts — 2026.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE] The policy uses magnitude and significance to determine whether and how AI use should be disclosed.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
Disclosure thresholds and prohibitions are different governance instruments.

LOCAL MOVE:
The policy makes significance relevant to transparency rather than automatically invalidating the work.

SOURCE TERMS:
magnitude; significance; disclose; appropriate.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The aphorism's “excellent versus problematic” symmetry overstates what the policy itself says.

QUESTION:
When does institutional disclosure become de facto negative classification even if the written policy is neutral?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Is the real conflict located in policy language, or in how audiences interpret disclosed AI assistance?

MECHANISM:

FORMAL SHIFT:

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR INFERENCE]
DISCLOSURE may produce reputational effects not specified by policy.

TENSION:
A disclosure requirement can coexist with full acceptance of the underlying research.

MISSING:
Reader, committee, and reviewer response data.

BOUNDARY:
Do not call significant AI influence “contamination” on the basis of the Georgia Tech policy alone.

CITATION TRAIL:
