# Paper Trail — The Prompt Is Not the Author

A provisional reading path supporting the working paper:

- [[PF-001]] Disclosure templates do not adjudicate disclosure thresholds.
- [[PF-004]] Contribution taxonomies already fracture the simple human-author category.
- [[PF-005]] Intellectual labor in a prompt does not automatically become copyright authorship.
- [[PF-008]] “Prompt” hides a boundary between instruction and prompt program.
- [[PF-014]] “Upstream” contribution requires a graph, not merely a better percentage.
- [[PF-038]] Contribution can be decomposed without forcing the verbs back into one author noun.
- [[PF-041]] Natural-language constraints are not the same thing as executable constraints.
- [[PF-044]] Recursive prompting converts attribution from a sentence problem into a workflow problem.
- [[PF-052]] Calibration turns “prompt as instrument” into an ensemble claim.
- [[PF-015]] Provenance is selectable description, not necessarily total conversational capture.
- [[PF-062]] A complete evidentiary chain needs a judgment node that ordinary provenance does not supply.
- [[PF-018]] Auditability can make the record reactive.
- [[PF-025]] Capability and compliance evaluate causal influence under different norms.
- [[PF-031]] Qualitative governance predicates may depend intentionally on disciplinary interpretation.
- [[PF-079]] Scientific instruments can themselves embody knowledge rather than merely subtract from human knowledge.
- [[PF-083]] A prompt must earn epistemic-object status rather than receive it by metaphor.
- [[PF-090]] The prompt may be an epistemic instrument precisely where it cannot be reduced to either prose or output.
