ZETTEL

ID: PF-048

TITLE:
A prompt can be a contribution without being a sufficient authorship claim.

SOURCE:
NISO — CRediT Role Descriptors — n.d.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE] CRediT explicitly distinguishes describing contribution from determining authorship.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
The admission test for prompt scholarship should not collapse into “does this make me the author?”

LOCAL MOVE:
CRediT gives a precedent for recognizing valuable roles without awarding every role identical status.

SOURCE TERMS:
contribution; authorship; conceptualization; methodology.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
Prompt scholarship can be defended more narrowly and more strongly by abandoning universal prompt authorship.

QUESTION:
What are the minimum conditions for a prompt to count as a scholarly contribution?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Should those conditions differ for methodology, software, conceptualization, and writing?

MECHANISM:
<prompt practice>
→ <role-specific evaluation>
→ [admission test]
→ <recognized / routine contribution>

FORMAL SHIFT:
NONE

SOURCE FORMALISM:
CRediT roles.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
PROMPT CONTRIBUTION is role-relative.

TENSION:
A contribution taxonomy may still leave graduate-school requirements about student authorship unresolved.

MISSING:
Role-specific prompt admission tests.

BOUNDARY:
Not every prompt, like not every line of code, is scholarly contribution.

CITATION TRAIL:
CRediT; software artifacts; methods papers.

TEST:
Develop separate criteria for prompt-as-method, prompt-as-software, prompt-as-writing, and prompt-as-conceptualization; apply them to published cases.
