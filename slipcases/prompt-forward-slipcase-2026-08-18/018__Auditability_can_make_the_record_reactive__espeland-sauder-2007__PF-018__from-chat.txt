ZETTEL

ID: PF-018

TITLE:
Auditability can make the record reactive.

SOURCE:
Espeland and Sauder — Rankings and Reactivity: How Public Measures Recreate Social Worlds — 2007.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE] Espeland and Sauder show that people and organizations alter behavior in response to being publicly measured and evaluated.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
A provenance requirement can become an intervention in the practice it records.

LOCAL MOVE:
The paper identifies reactivity as a consequence of measurement systems.

SOURCE TERMS:
reactivity; commensuration; self-fulfilling prophecy; public measures.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
A prompt log cannot automatically be treated as an untouched window into research once researchers know it will be judged.

QUESTION:
Does anticipated audit change how researchers prompt?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Which forms of provenance minimize reactivity without sacrificing accountability?

MECHANISM:
<anticipated evaluation>
→ <behavioral adaptation>
→ [produce auditable record]
→ <changed research practice>

FORMAL SHIFT:
NONE

SOURCE FORMALISM:
Two mechanisms of reactivity are analyzed: self-fulfilling prophecy and commensuration.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
RECORDING RULE → OPERATOR STRATEGY → RECORDED BEHAVIOR.

TENSION:
All research records may be reactive to some degree; the relevant question is whether AI traces create a distinctive or harmful form.

MISSING:
Empirical evidence from actual prompted research.

BOUNDARY:
Espeland and Sauder study rankings, not AI provenance.

CITATION TRAIL:
Hawthorne effects; audit cultures; Goodhart's law; research notebooks.

TEST:
Randomly assign researchers to “private prompt history” versus “history will be reviewed” conditions and compare exploratory behavior.
