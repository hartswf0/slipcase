ZETTEL

ID: PF-004

TITLE:
Contribution taxonomies already fracture the simple human-author category.

SOURCE:
NISO — CRediT Role Descriptors — n.d.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE] CRediT separates conceptualization, methodology, software, validation, investigation, supervision, writing, and other contribution roles while explicitly stating that the taxonomy does not determine authorship.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
“Human contribution” is already institutionally decomposed into heterogeneous operations.

LOCAL MOVE:
CRediT separates contribution description from authorship adjudication.

SOURCE TERMS:
conceptualization; methodology; software; validation; writing; supervision.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The human/AI/tool/output ontology is too coarse even before AI enters the picture.

QUESTION:
Could AI-assisted scholarship be described first through contribution roles rather than percentage-of-AI labels?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Which existing CRediT roles change meaning when the operation is mediated through a generative model?

MECHANISM:
<research activity>
→ <role decomposition>
→ [attribute contributions]
→ <contribution statement>

FORMAL SHIFT:
NONE

SOURCE FORMALISM:
14 contributor-role categories.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
AUTHORSHIP ≠ Σ CONTRIBUTION ROLES.

TENSION:
Role decomposition can describe work more accurately while deliberately refusing to settle authorship.

MISSING:
Roles for prompt specification, iterative orchestration, machine-output validation, and generative selection.

BOUNDARY:
CRediT cannot simply be treated as an AI authorship standard.

CITATION TRAIL:
CRediT governance; journal authorship policies; human-AI contribution taxonomies.

TEST:
Take twenty AI-assisted research workflows and attempt to encode them using only current CRediT roles; record recurrent residue.
