ZETTEL

ID: PF-038

TITLE:
Contribution can be decomposed without forcing the verbs back into one author noun.

SOURCE:
NISO — CRediT Role Descriptors — n.d.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE] CRediT describes distinct research contributions and explicitly warns that the taxonomy is not designed to determine authorship.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
Role decomposition is an alternative to searching for one sovereign act called writing.

LOCAL MOVE:
The taxonomy gives institutional precedent for describing what people did rather than reducing all labor to author/non-author.

SOURCE TERMS:
conceptualization; methodology; validation; writing; supervision.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The prompt debate may not need a better definition of “author” first.

QUESTION:
Can AI scholarship report operations with sufficient precision that the authorship binary becomes secondary?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Which operations matter for responsibility even when they do not count as writing?

MECHANISM:
<research process>
→ <operation roles>
→ [attribute]
→ <contribution description>

FORMAL SHIFT:
NONE

SOURCE FORMALISM:
Contributor-role taxonomy.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
SPECIFY / GENERATE / SELECT / VERIFY / DEFEND as candidate roles.

TENSION:
Institutions still eventually need authorship decisions for degrees, bylines, and copyright.

MISSING:
A human-AI role taxonomy linked to those downstream decisions.

BOUNDARY:
Role decomposition cannot abolish authorship requirements.

CITATION TRAIL:
CRediT adoption; authorship criteria; provenance.

TEST:
Replace binary AI-authorship disclosures with operation-role statements and test whether evaluators make more consistent judgments.
