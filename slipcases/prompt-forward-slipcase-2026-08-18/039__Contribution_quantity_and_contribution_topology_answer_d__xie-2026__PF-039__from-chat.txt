ZETTEL

ID: PF-039

TITLE:
Contribution quantity and contribution topology answer different questions.

SOURCE:
Xie et al. — Measuring Human Contribution in AI-Assisted Content Generation — 2026.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE] The paper proposes a continuous information-theoretic measure of human contribution.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
A scalar can distinguish amounts while remaining blind to where in the process leverage occurs.

LOCAL MOVE:
The source makes “how much” more rigorous but does not eliminate “what kind.”

SOURCE TERMS:
human contribution; information theory; degree.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
Rejecting “how much AI?” entirely may throw away a useful question; the error is asking only that question.

QUESTION:
What should be measured on separate axes: magnitude, role, timing, control, or responsibility?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Can a contribution vector outperform both binary disclosure and a single scalar?

MECHANISM:
<workflow>
→ <multiple dimensions>
→ [measure]
→ <contribution profile>

FORMAL SHIFT:
NONE

SOURCE FORMALISM:
Scalar information-theoretic contribution.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
PROFILE = (magnitude, role, topology, control, validation).

TENSION:
More dimensions may make policy unusable.

MISSING:
Evidence that each proposed axis changes judgments.

BOUNDARY:
The source does not advocate topology.

CITATION TRAIL:
Multidimensional attribution; CRediT; provenance graphs.

TEST:
Compare prediction of expert contribution judgments using scalar versus multidimensional representations.
