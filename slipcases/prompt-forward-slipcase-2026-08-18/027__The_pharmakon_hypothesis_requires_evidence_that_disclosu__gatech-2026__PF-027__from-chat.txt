ZETTEL

ID: PF-027

TITLE:
The pharmakon hypothesis requires evidence that disclosure functions as poison.

SOURCE:
Georgia Institute of Technology — Interim Artificial Intelligence (AI) in Academic & Research Contexts — 2026.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE] Significant AI use may increase disclosure obligations, but the policy does not define such use as scholarly contamination.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
The cure/poison metaphor currently mixes causal efficacy with an unverified social judgment.

LOCAL MOVE:
The policy weakens the claim that the same causal power is institutionally valued positively in engineering and negatively in scholarship.

SOURCE TERMS:
significance; magnitude; disclosure.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
Perhaps the genuine pharmakon is not the prompt but transparency itself: disclosure can protect trust while potentially altering how the work is judged.

QUESTION:
What exactly is poisonous—AI influence, undisclosed AI influence, or the social interpretation of disclosure?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Can the pharmakon be located empirically rather than rhetorically?

MECHANISM:
<AI influence>
→ <disclosure>
→ [? audience interpretation ?]
→ <acceptance / suspicion>

FORMAL SHIFT:
NONE

SOURCE FORMALISM:
NONE

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR INFERENCE]
The evaluative sign enters after disclosure, not necessarily at causal influence.

TENSION:
The earlier metaphor gives the prompt itself the ambiguity that may actually belong to institutional framing.

MISSING:
Evidence on evaluators' reactions.

BOUNDARY:
The policy cannot support “prompt = poison.”

CITATION TRAIL:
AI stigma; disclosure effects; Derrida/Plato only after the institutional mechanism is established.

TEST:
Measure whether disclosed prompt-forward methods receive lower authorship judgments than equivalent undisclosed or conventionally assisted methods.
