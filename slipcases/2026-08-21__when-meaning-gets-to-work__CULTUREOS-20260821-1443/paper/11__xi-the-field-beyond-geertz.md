# XI. The Field Beyond Geertz

The broad corpus that grew from the cultural-system question is useful not because it proves a single lineage but because it shows how many different problems have been hidden inside the same phrase.

Kinship, in Schneider, produces a relatively autonomous symbolic account of relations among persons while simultaneously raising the question of how those symbols articulate with social practice. [@schneider1968american] Medicine, in Kleinman, joins meaning to institutional sectors and interpersonal relations. [@kleinman1980patients] Sport becomes entangled with policy, ethnicity, and governance. [@arnaud2002sport] Accounting reveals how representations loop into organizational decisions. [@mouritsen1989accounting] Food can be treated structurally, semiotically, politically, and materially, with Appadurai's gastro-politics reminding us that classification and distribution are also relations of power. [@appadurai1981gastro]

Mathematics becomes an evolving cultural system capable of selection, diffusion, resistance, and consolidation. [@wilder1981mathematics] Music becomes an institution that trains the capacities it later labels as talent. [@kingsbury1984music] Cannibalism becomes an embodied cultural logic. [@sanday1986divine] Caregiving becomes a patterned organization of dependency and obligation. [@albert1990caregiving] The university defines itself by repeatedly drawing distinctions around knowledge. [@considine2006university] Gender begins as a Geertzian cultural system and becomes an argument for grounding symbolic organization in practice and power. [@ortnerwhitehead1981sexual]

Theater makes the audience part of a multimodal sign system; dance makes the body itself a temporally organized medium; television makes technical transmission compatible with divergent decoding. [@fischerluchte1992semiotics; @blacking1984dance; @servaeslie1996television] Time becomes not merely a content of culture but a scheduler of social possibility. [@hall1983dance; @thompson1967time] The phrase "cultural system" therefore names not a settled theoretical object but a historical zone in which scholars repeatedly try to specify how meaning is organized, distributed, reproduced, embodied, institutionalized, and made consequential.

That heterogeneity is not an embarrassment to be cleaned up. It is the empirical history of the concept. The recurring title formula tempts us toward a single genealogy, but the actual field is a braid: Parsons and action theory, Sorokin's integration, White's energetics, Geertzian semiotics, cognitive anthropology, structuralism, ecological systems, practice theory, semiotics, media studies, infrastructure studies, STS. Similar titles can hide different machines.

The practical gain of the runtime question is therefore comparative rather than imperial. It allows us to ask the same family of questions across rival traditions without declaring them identical. Where is the relevant state? What must be learned? What can be externalized? Which relations are required for action to proceed? What is merely represented, and what is institutionally coupled to consequence? What becomes visible only when the arrangement breaks? Which repairs preserve the name of the system while changing its actual operation? Which phenomena leak across system boundaries and should be typed differently?

These questions do not replace interpretation. They make interpretation answer for its conditions of efficacy.
