# VIII. Failure as an Instrument

Successful cultural action is analytically stingy. It hides the agreements, translations, permissions, material supports, trained perceptions, and repair work that let the action appear obvious. Failure is more generous.

Geertz's 1957 funeral is one instance. Garfinkel turned the principle into method. By making ordinary scenes troublesome, breaching experiments forced the "seen but unnoticed" background expectancies of everyday life into visibility. [@garfinkel1967studies] The important result was not only the discovery of hidden rules. A breach could disturb the very scheme by which participants knew what sort of event they were in. Recognition itself had practical conditions.

This is why the language of debugging and fuzzing is tempting. One sends an unexpected input and watches which assumption fails first. A form receives a value outside its categories. A participant refuses an assigned role. A legal case does not fit precedent. A conversational answer obeys the literal question while violating the practical expectation. A body does not fit the architecture. An institution produces an exception it has no authorized procedure for handling.

But ethnography cannot simply import the engineer's right to break things. Human relationships are not test environments. Deliberate breaches can embarrass, harm, expose, or redistribute risk. The more defensible research strategy begins with failures that have already occurred: archival disputes, public breakdowns, appeals, error logs, help-desk records, maintenance histories, ritual interruptions, contested classifications. The task is not to manufacture trouble indiscriminately but to learn from the moments in which the system has already disclosed its hidden dependencies.

Suchman adds another kind of failure: the gap between plan and situated action. Plans are resources for action rather than scripts that strongly determine its course. [@suchman1987plans] The omission is not merely an engineering defect. Abstraction is what allows a plan to travel across circumstances, while local actors supply information the plan could not have anticipated. The better the ethnographer specifies the explicit rule, the more visible the residual work of execution can become.

Pickering's "mangle" adds material resistance. Resistance is relational - a block relative to an attempted goal - and accommodation changes apparatus, concepts, routines, or purposes. [@pickering1993mangle] The same material behavior can be irrelevant under one project and decisive under another. A theory of cultural failure must therefore ask not only what went wrong but what actors were trying to do, what the world refused, and how the subsequent adjustment altered the field of future action.

Failure, in this sense, is not the opposite of system. It is one of the principal ways a system becomes observable.
