RESEARCH OBJECT:
LAWYER AS INTERPRETIVE ENGINEER.

LOCAL MOVE:
The easy thesis is that lawyers are good at prompting because lawyers write carefully. Shambibble's practice supports a stronger and less flattering account. The useful lawyer is trained to expect language to be read by someone else, against interest, under rules not controlled by the drafter, after circumstances change.

Patent practice adds habits unusually aligned with consequential AI instruction: define scope, distinguish open from closed language, establish antecedents, anticipate broad constructions, record amendments, preserve exceptions, and admit what the text cannot settle. Shambibble adds empirical discipline: test the construction the machine actually makes.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The lawyer's comparative advantage may appear exactly where “prompt engineering” stops being about clever phrases and becomes governance of interpretation under consequence.

QUESTION:
What professional practices already train people to author language whose meaning will be operationalized by an interpreter they do not control?

DEEPER QUESTION:
If lawyers become leaders in prompt language, what parts of legal craft deserve transfer—and which pathologies of legal drafting, such as verbosity, strategic ambiguity, and inaccessible jargon, must be refused?

MECHANISM:
Translate legal habits into operational prompt practice: claim desired scope; define terms; bind references; test broad constructions; enumerate exceptions; version amendments; preserve reasons; verify actual execution; stop at reasonable certainty rather than imaginary completeness.

FORMAL SHIFT:
PROMPT ENGINEER AS WORDSMITH

becomes

PROMPT COUNSEL AS INTERPRETIVE ENGINEER

SOURCE FORMALISM:
Patent law supplies formal doctrines of claim scope, definiteness, construction, and amendment history. Shambibble supplies a first-person account of those habits unexpectedly becoming useful in 2022 image prompting.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

LAWYERLY PROMPTING = adversarial drafting + scope discipline + interpretive record + empirical execution testing.

Not: legal vocabulary transplanted wholesale into prompts.

TENSION:
Legal practice can also reward strategic ambiguity, complexity, adversarial excess, and exclusionary language. The claim that lawyers possess transferable skills does not imply that legal prose should become the stylistic model for AI interfaces.

MISSING:
Comparative evidence: do patent attorneys actually outperform matched technical writers, programmers, or designers on consequential prompt-specification tasks after controlling for domain expertise?

BOUNDARY:
This zettel positions patent lawyers as a promising source of methods, not as owners or historical inventors of prompt engineering.
