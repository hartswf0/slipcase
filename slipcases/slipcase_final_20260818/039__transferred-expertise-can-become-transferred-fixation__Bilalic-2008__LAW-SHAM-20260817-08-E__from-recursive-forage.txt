TITLE:
2026-08-17 — The professional eye that sees one failure earlier may become blind to another.

SOURCE:

PASSAGE:

“the first idea that comes to mind, triggered by familiar features of a problem, prevents a better solution being found.” ([sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0010027708001133?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

RESEARCH OBJECT:
TRANSFERRED EXPERTISE CAN BECOME TRANSFERRED FIXATION.

LOCAL MOVE:
[[LAW-SHAM-20260817-08-A]] gives the paper a powerful proposition:

professional discriminations can travel.

Bilalić, McLeod, and Gobet introduce the necessary loyal opposition.

Expertise does not merely improve perception.

Familiar structures can capture attention.

In their chess experiments, experts who believed they were searching for superior alternatives continued visually attending to features associated with the familiar solution. ([eric.ed.gov](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ810141&utm_source=chatgpt.com))

Therefore the patent attorney's trained sensitivity to:

scope
definitions
ambiguity
adversarial interpretation
textual amendment

may help on some prompt problems while making other prompt possibilities harder to notice.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The same fact that makes the Geertzian transfer argument plausible makes its danger plausible.

A trained eye is selectively blind.

QUESTION:
Which prompt failures become easier for patent attorneys to detect, and which become harder because legal training directs attention toward language rather than other control surfaces?

DEEPER QUESTION:
When generative systems accept images, schemas, tools, examples, state, code, and interaction histories, can an expert in words become trapped by the assumption that the problem should be solved in words?

MECHANISM:
Familiar problem feature appears.

Professional schema activates.

Schema supplies plausible solution.

Attention remains attached to schema-relevant evidence.

Alternative representation receives less attention.

Expert persists with an adequate but inferior approach.

SOURCE FORMALISM:
The authors study the Einstellung effect with expert chess players.

The proposed mechanism is attentional: a familiar solution schema continues directing visual attention even while the expert reports searching for another solution. ([eric.ed.gov](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ810141&utm_source=chatgpt.com))
