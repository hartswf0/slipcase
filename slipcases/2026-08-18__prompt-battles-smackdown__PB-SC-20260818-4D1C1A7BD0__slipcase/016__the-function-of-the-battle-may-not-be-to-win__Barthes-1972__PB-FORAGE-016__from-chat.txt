TITLE:
The function of the battle may not be to win.

SOURCE:
Roland Barthes — “The World of Wrestling” — Mythologies — Hill and Wang, 1972. Scan pp. 15–16. fileciteturn5file0L2-L3

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]

Barthes separates wrestling from contests whose meaning depends on uncertainty about the final result. The spectator does not primarily follow the changing fortunes of the match. Each moment must instead produce an immediately intelligible image of a passion or condition.

He makes the stronger claim that the wrestler’s function is not simply to win but to enact completely the signs expected of the role.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
[[PB-FORAGE-015]] still assumes that adversarial inquiry finally needs a result capable of adjudicating between rival claims.

Barthes opens a stranger possibility.

The Prompt Battle may be badly understood whenever WINNING is treated as its primary epistemic function.

Its distinctive power may instead be:

to make an otherwise abstract disagreement visible enough that one can finally see what the disagreement consists of.

The battle would then be closer to an epistemic demonstration than a tournament.

LOCAL MOVE:
Replace:

WHICH SIDE WON?

with:

WHAT BECAME IMPOSSIBLE NOT TO SEE?

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
A Prompt Battle can fail as a competition while succeeding as research.

A Defender could “lose” yet expose the exact failure mode everyone had previously discussed only abstractly.

A Challenger could “win” yet reveal that its success required an extraordinary amount of elicitation labor.

The score might therefore be the least interesting output of the match.

QUESTION:
What is a Prompt Battle supposed to make unmistakable that an ordinary benchmark table cannot?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Could the strongest scientific use of adversarial prompting be not adjudication but the production of publicly intelligible epistemic situations?

MECHANISM:
An abstract dispute is assigned opposing roles.

Each role is forced to materialize its claim through prompts, completions, failures, recoveries, interventions, and counter-interventions.

The sequence turns theoretical differences into observable contrasts.

The principal output becomes the contrast itself rather than the final winner.

SOURCE FORMALISM:
Barthes distinguishes wrestling from a contest organized primarily around uncertainty about final victory.

He describes wrestling as producing immediately intelligible signs and argues that the wrestler’s function exceeds simply winning.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

Let:

D = theoretical disagreement
B = battle trajectory
W = winner
L = legibility of the disputed mechanism

Conventional evaluation privileges:

B → W

A Barthesian Prompt Battle privileges:

B → ΔL(D)

where the important outcome is how much more discriminable the disagreement becomes after its enactment.

TENSION:
Adversarial collaboration tries to make disagreement empirically decidable.

Wrestling makes disagreement dramatically intelligible.

These are not the same epistemic achievement.

A battle designed too strongly for decision may hide the mechanism inside a score.

A battle designed too strongly for spectacle may produce clarity without warranting a conclusion.

MISSING:
A criterion for distinguishing:

EPISTEMIC LEGIBILITY

from:

MERE DRAMATIC CLARITY.

BOUNDARY:
Barthes analyzes French professional wrestling as spectacle.

He does not describe scientific experimentation, contemporary WWE, or language-model evaluation.

The transformation of his distinction into an evaluation method is [OUR INFERENCE].

CITATION TRAIL:

→ scientific demonstration

TEST:
Take one disputed AI claim.

Run two versions.

MATCH A:
participants compete under a conventional scoring rubric.

MATCH B:
participants are instructed to collaborate adversarially in producing the clearest possible visible contrast between their positions, with no winner declared.

Afterward, give both transcripts to researchers unfamiliar with the dispute.

Measure separately:

which side they think won
what mechanism they think was demonstrated
which variables they can now name
what experiment they would run next.

If MATCH B generates substantially better mechanistic questions without producing stronger unsupported conclusions, Prompt Battles may have an epistemic function distinct from competition.
