TITLE:
The crowd may be scoring justice while the researchers think it is scoring capability.

SOURCE:
Roland Barthes — “The World of Wrestling” — Mythologies — Hill and Wang, 1972. Scan pp. 20–24. fileciteturn5file0L5-L7

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]

Barthes repeatedly returns to justice. The spectacle constructs injury, foulness, retaliation, reversal, and punishment so that the audience can experience a visible system of compensation. The pleasure is not reducible to technical excellence; actions acquire meaning through a moral economy in which the villain’s punishment becomes dramatically deserved.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
[[PB-FORAGE-012]] turned judge disagreement into a research object.

Barthes suggests that agreement may be just as suspicious.

Judges may converge because the battle has given them a narratively satisfying moral sequence:

MODEL FAILS
→ CHALLENGER CALLS IT OUT
→ MODEL REPAIRS
→ JUSTICE RESTORED.

Or:

CLAIMANT BOASTS
→ DEFENDER EXPOSES FAILURE
→ CLAIM COLLAPSES
→ HUBRIS PUNISHED.

The score may therefore register narrative justice rather than capability.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The most satisfying Prompt Battle outcome is likely to be a reversal.

But reversal is also a dramatic form.

A one-turn correction after an embarrassing failure feels conclusive even when it proves almost nothing about prevalence or robustness.

The match can make weak evidence feel deserved.

QUESTION:
How much Prompt Battle judgment is driven by the evidentiary strength of an outcome and how much by the dramatic satisfaction of its reversal?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Could the crowd’s moral appetite systematically push AI evaluation toward stories with villains, redemptions, humiliations, and clean finishes?

MECHANISM:
A contestant establishes expectation.

The opposing side violates or exposes it.

A reversal produces emotional resolution.

Observers experience the sequence as meaningful and complete.

That felt completeness can leak into epistemic confidence.

FORMAL SHIFT:
<EVIDENCE E>
+

<MORAL REVERSAL R>

SOURCE FORMALISM:
Barthes analyzes wrestling through a moral spectacle of suffering, foulness, compensation, punishment, and justice.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

Let:

J_e = judgment warranted by evidence
J_o = observed human judgment
D = dramatic satisfaction.

Possible contamination:

J_o = J_e + αD

where α represents sensitivity to dramaturgical form.

This is a proposed experimental model, not source machinery.

TENSION:
The audience reaction is not useless.

A powerful “pop” may reveal that a mechanism has finally become publicly intelligible.

But intelligibility, persuasion, justice, and truth can all produce confidence.

The experiment must distinguish them.

MISSING:
Independent measurement of:

EVIDENTIARY CONFIDENCE
NARRATIVE SATISFACTION
MORAL APPROVAL
SURPRISE
LEGIBILITY.

BOUNDARY:
Barthes does not study research judges or AI evaluations.

The proposed contamination of epistemic scoring by dramatic justice is [OUR INFERENCE].

TEST:
Construct matched transcripts containing identical pieces of evidence.

Arrange them into different orders:

A:
failure → recovery.

B:
recovery → failure.

C:
interleaved evidence without dramatic reversal.

D:
evidence summarized without characters.

Ask judges separately for:

capability score
confidence
satisfaction
surprise
sense that the result was “decisive.”

If capability judgments change with narrative order despite identical evidence, dramatic justice is part of the judging function.
