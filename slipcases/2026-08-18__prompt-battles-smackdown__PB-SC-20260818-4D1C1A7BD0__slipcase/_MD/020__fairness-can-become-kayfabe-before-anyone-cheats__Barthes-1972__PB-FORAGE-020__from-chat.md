TITLE:
Fairness can become kayfabe before anyone cheats.

SOURCE:
Roland Barthes — “The World of Wrestling” — Mythologies — Hill and Wang, 1972. Scan pp. 22–23. fileciteturn5file0L6-L6

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]

Barthes argues that “fairness” in wrestling is not primarily a binding constraint on what can happen. It is a recognizable role within the spectacle. Conventional gestures—restraint, handshakes, obedience, visible politeness—make fairness legible, while foul play gains meaning through equally excessive signs.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
[[PB-FORAGE-013]] worried that a public scoring rule becomes a target contestants can optimize.

Barthes exposes a prior problem.

The ritual signs of fair evaluation can become detached from the actual validity of the measurement.

Transparent rubric.
Neutral moderator.
Balanced sides.
Equal speaking time.
Blind judging.
Handshake.

These may be valuable procedures.

They may also become the visible costume of fairness.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
A beautifully fair Prompt Battle can still measure the wrong thing.

A visibly asymmetrical Prompt Battle can sometimes reveal a mechanism more accurately.

The audience may nevertheless trust the first because its fairness has better signs.

QUESTION:
Which elements of Prompt Battle fairness actually constrain inference, and which merely make fairness publicly recognizable?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Can procedural legitimacy become an obstacle to epistemic validity when the procedure must look symmetrical even though the phenomenon is not?

MECHANISM:
A contest needs public legitimacy.

Legitimacy is communicated through recognizable fairness signals.

Those signals become design requirements.

Designers preserve symmetry even where the underlying object requires asymmetric testing.

The appearance of fairness can therefore constrain the experimental design independently of validity.

SOURCE FORMALISM:
Barthes describes fairness and foulness as intelligible theatrical roles whose meanings are made visible through conventional signs.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

Let:

F_p = perceived procedural fairness
F_e = epistemic adequacy of the procedure.

Prompt Battles often assume:

F_p ≈ F_e.

The Barthesian pressure is:

F_p can be independently optimized.

Therefore:

maximize F_p

does not guarantee:

maximize F_e.

TENSION:
Without recognizable fairness procedures, participants may reasonably distrust the result.

But designing for recognizable fairness can make methodological asymmetries impossible to use even when they are scientifically justified.

MISSING:
A Prompt Battle protocol that labels every fairness device with its epistemic function:

WHAT BIAS DOES THIS PREVENT?
WHAT CLAIM DOES THIS ENABLE?
WHAT FAILURE REMAINS?

BOUNDARY:
Barthes is analyzing fairness as a theatrical sign in wrestling.

The claim that scientific fairness rituals can acquire a similar semiotic independence is [OUR INFERENCE].
