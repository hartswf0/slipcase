TITLE:
Judge disagreement may be data, not a defect in the scoring system.

SOURCE:
PB_PRIME — internal working archive — PB_18 / PB_28 / PB_69 — author and date not supplied.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
One stage of the archive treats ambiguous judging as a methodological weakness to be repaired through standardized rubrics, public criteria, judges, crowdsourcing, and even automated scoring.

A later stage proposes “judging the dance”: valuing depth, adaptability, innovation, collaborative synergy, and the evolving process rather than the final completion.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
These are not two versions of the same scoring system.

They imply different theories of evidence.

LOCAL MOVE:
Stop treating the later rubric as an optimization of the earlier rubric.

Preserve the contradiction.

SOURCE TERMS:
objective scoring
standardized scoring rubric
ambiguity in winning

depth

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The archive first tries to remove disagreement from judgment.

Later it makes emergence, divergence, and interpretive depth central to what counts.

If the object is genuinely emergent, evaluator disagreement may reveal the object’s multiple interpretations rather than measurement noise.

QUESTION:
What if the most valuable output of judging is not the winner but the structure of disagreement among judges?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Does Prompt Battle need measurement reliability, or does it need a disciplined method for preserving incompatible readings long enough to learn from them?

MECHANISM:
Fixed rubrics compress heterogeneous judgments into a common scalar.

Explanatory judgments preserve why observers disagree.

Those disagreements may correlate with:

    expertise
    epistemic camp
    values
    expectations
    model familiarity
    interpretation of the flag.

SOURCE FORMALISM:
Early scoring:
direct hit / near miss / off target.

Later scoring:
depth / innovation / synergy / adaptability / coherence.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

Instead of only:

    score = mean(judge ratings)

retain:

    J = {rating_i, rationale_i, background_i}

and analyze:

    disagreement = structure(J)

The dependent variable may be the topology of disagreement rather than consensus.

TENSION:
Reproducible benchmarks need stable criteria.

Generative inquiry may be destroyed by criteria stable enough to force every observation into a predetermined construct.

MISSING:
A declared answer to:

    WHAT IS THE SCORE FOR?

ranking?
model evaluation?
participant skill?
discovery?
interpretive disagreement?
research-question generation?

BOUNDARY:
Judge disagreement is not automatically informative. It can also be noise, confusion, poor instructions, or unreliable evaluation.

CITATION TRAIL:
Construct validity.
Inter-rater disagreement as signal.
Disagreement-based annotation.
Pluralistic evaluation of generative systems.

TEST:
Run the same battle with 30 judges.

Collect:

    numeric score
    written rationale
    confidence
    AI expertise
    epistemic orientation.

Analyze once by consensus score.

Analyze again by clusters of rationales.

Ask whether the second analysis exposes a stable disagreement that the first erases.
