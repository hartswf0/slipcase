TITLE:
An adaptive benchmark can become part of the causal system it claims merely to observe.

SOURCE:
Juan Perdomo, Tijana Zrnic, Celestine Mendler-Dünner, and Moritz Hardt — Performative Prediction — 2020 — Proceedings of the 37th International Conference on Machine Learning, pp. 7599–7609.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
Perdomo and colleagues call predictions performative when deploying them changes the outcomes they are meant to predict. Their framework treats this feedback as more than ordinary noise: acting on a prediction can change the future distribution against which prediction is evaluated.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
[[PB-FORAGE-004]] treated the evolving critique pool as an endogenous curriculum.

Perdomo et al. sharpen the danger.

Once participants learn from prior battles, judges publish criteria, benchmark designers introduce new flags in response to discovered behavior, and model developers train against visible failures, evaluation no longer samples an independent object.

The evaluation participates in producing the next object.

LOCAL MOVE:
Replace:

DYNAMIC BENCHMARK

with the stronger possibility:

PERFORMATIVE BENCHMARK.

SOURCE TERMS:
performative prediction
distribution shift
retraining
performative stability
strategic feedback effects
risk minimization

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
A Prompt Battle may discover that a model performs differently next month not merely because the model changed.

The public existence of the battle may have helped change:

human prompting strategies
model post-training
safety rules
evaluation expectations
available exemplars
the critique pool itself.

The measuring apparatus acquires descendants inside what it later measures.

QUESTION:
Can a longitudinal Prompt Battle distinguish technological change from change caused by previous rounds of the benchmark itself?

DEEPER QUESTION:
What would “stability” mean for an evaluation whose challenges and subjects continually adapt to each other?

MECHANISM:
Evaluation produces observations.

Observations alter human/model strategies.

Those strategies alter future outputs.

The evaluator responds by changing future challenges.

The new evaluation distribution is therefore partly generated by earlier measurements.

FORMAL SHIFT:
<MODEL + INITIAL CHALLENGE DISTRIBUTION>
→ [EVALUATION]
→ <PUBLIC FAILURES / STRATEGIES>
→ [ADAPTATION]
→ <NEW MODEL / HUMAN / CHALLENGE DISTRIBUTION>
→ [NEXT EVALUATION]

SOURCE FORMALISM:
Perdomo et al. introduce the concept of performative stability for prediction settings in which acting on predictions affects future outcomes.

They analyze retraining under strategic feedback and distribution shift.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

Let:

M_t = model state
H_t = population of human strategies
C_t = critique/challenge distribution
E_t = published battle evidence

Then:

E_t = Evaluate(M_t, H_t, C_t)

but:

M_{t+1}, H_{t+1}, C_{t+1}
=
Adapt(M_t, H_t, C_t, E_t)

Therefore:

E_{t+1}

is not an independent replication of E_t.

The previous evaluation is one of its causal ancestors.

TENSION:
A living benchmark remains difficult precisely because it reacts to frontier behavior.

But every reaction contaminates naive longitudinal comparison.

Freezing the benchmark preserves comparability while inviting saturation.

Adapting it preserves discovery while destroying a stable measuring stick.

MISSING:
A lineage record showing which later:

prompts
model patches
system rules
training examples
flags
evaluation criteria

were causally downstream of earlier Prompt Battle findings.

BOUNDARY:
Performative Prediction formalizes settings in which prediction deployment influences the target distribution.

It does not directly analyze benchmarks that adapt to models.

Applying performativity to Prompt Battles is [OUR INFERENCE].

TEST:
Maintain two synchronized critique sets.

PUBLIC FRONTIER:
results and failures are published after every round.

SEALED ANCHOR:
never published, never used for development, and periodically rerun.

Track performance on both across model versions.

If PUBLIC FRONTIER behavior improves while SEALED ANCHOR behavior does not, one surviving explanation is adaptation to the visible evaluation ecology rather than generalized capability change.
