TITLE:
The scientific part of a Prompt Battle may have to happen before the fighting begins.

SOURCE:
Clark, Costello, Mitchell, and Tetlock — Keep Your Enemies Close: Adversarial Collaborations Will Improve Behavioral Science — 2022 — Journal of Applied Research in Memory and Cognition 11(1):1–18.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
Adversarial collaboration asks scholars who disagree to jointly construct tests of their competing hypotheses rather than independently producing demonstrations optimized for their own position.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
PB_PRIME supplies adversaries but not yet the strongest requirement of adversarial collaboration:

    the opponents must share ownership of the test.

LOCAL MOVE:
Move competition from producing persuasive completions to negotiating mutually dangerous evidence.

SOURCE TERMS:
adversarial collaboration
competing hypotheses
co-develop tests
self-correction

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
In the Prompt Battle, Defender and Challenger often inherit their jobs:

    prove the critique
    defeat the critique.

That creates pressure to win.

But a scientific adversary must participate in designing a result that could make their own position lose.

QUESTION:
Before a flag is admitted to battle, should both opponents have to state what completion or pattern of completions would make them abandon their assigned side?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Can a battle produce knowledge when neither participant is required to be vulnerable to the evidence?

MECHANISM:
Competitive role assignment can convert inquiry into search:

    Defender searches failure space.
    Challenger searches success space.

Without shared adjudication criteria, each can always move to another example.

The disagreement becomes indefinitely renewable.

SOURCE FORMALISM:

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

Before round r:

    D states evidence E_D that would weaken DEFEND.
    C states evidence E_C that would weaken CHALLENGE.

Battle is admissible only if there exists:

    E*

that both agree would discriminate between the live readings.

No E*:
the flag is rhetorical, not experimental.

TENSION:
The performative energy of a battle depends on trying to win.

The epistemic strength of adversarial collaboration depends on designing conditions under which your own side can lose.

MISSING:
Pre-battle crux statements.

The archive contains rebuttal, role reversal, reflection, and judging, but not a compulsory jointly authored statement of discriminating evidence.

BOUNDARY:
Adversarial collaboration is a scientific-dispute methodology, not evidence that competitive performance formats are inherently epistemically superior.

CITATION TRAIL:
Kahneman on adversarial collaboration.
Cowan et al. on extended adversarial collaboration.
Severe testing.
Preregistration of rival predictions.

TEST:
Before revealing which participant will defend which side:

1. Both independently operationalize the critique.
2. They reconcile those definitions.
3. They jointly write:
       what supports the critique,
       what weakens it,
       what would remain ambiguous.
4. Only then randomly assign Defender and Challenger.

Compare this condition against ordinary Prompt Battles.

Measure whether post-battle disagreement becomes more specific rather than merely stronger.
