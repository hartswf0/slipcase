TITLE:
A rigged Prompt Battle may be more honest than a supposedly neutral one.

SOURCE:
Roland Barthes — “The World of Wrestling” — Mythologies — Hill and Wang, 1972. Scan pp. 15, 18–20. fileciteturn5file0L2-L5

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]

Barthes argues that asking whether wrestling is “rigged” mistakes the nature of its spectacle. The important issue for the audience is not whether an uncertain sporting contest has been secretly manipulated but whether the performance completely renders its meanings visible.

Later he compares wrestling to theatre: what matters is the intelligible representation of situations through exaggerated exterior signs.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
Prompt Battles repeatedly inherit the prestige of the uncontrolled contest:

two opponents
one arena
rules
judges
uncertain outcome.

But the strongest research version may require the opposite.

The match may need to be BOOKED.

The Defender and Challenger could jointly construct the sequence most capable of exposing the real disagreement.

The rig would no longer be corruption of the experiment.

The rig would be the experimental apparatus.

LOCAL MOVE:
Split:

SECRET RIGGING
→ manipulation concealed from participants or audience

from:

→ deliberate construction of a sequence designed to expose a mechanism.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
Scientific respectability makes the staged encounter look suspicious.

Yet experiments themselves are staged situations.

Variables are selected.
Conditions are constructed.
Treatments are imposed.
Contrasts are engineered.

The relevant question may therefore not be:

IS THIS BATTLE NATURAL?

but:

WHAT EXACTLY HAS BEEN STAGED, AND WHAT DOES THAT STAGING LICENSE US TO CLAIM?

QUESTION:
What would become possible if Prompt Battles openly declared themselves booked demonstrations rather than pretending to be naturally occurring contests?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Can choreography increase epistemic rigor when the choreography is preserved as part of the method?

MECHANISM:
Researchers identify a disputed mechanism.

Opposing sides jointly design a sequence that should make rival predictions maximally distinguishable.

Prompts, model conditions, reversals, and stopping points are deliberately arranged.

Every intervention is disclosed.

The performance is therefore artificial but reproducible.

The epistemic claim is not:

B reveals naturally occurring frequency.

It is:

B constitutes a controlled demonstration that mechanism M can be produced under conditions C.

Thus:

STAGED
≠
FALSE.

But:

STAGED
≠
REPRESENTATIVE.

TENSION:
The unbooked battle preserves discovery.

The booked battle preserves interpretability.

Too little choreography produces anecdotes whose important variables are unclear.

Too much choreography can manufacture a phenomenon that disappears outside the ring.

MISSING:
A vocabulary distinguishing:

DEMONSTRATION
STRESS TEST
REPRESENTATIVE EVALUATION
CAUSAL INTERVENTION
PUBLIC SPECTACLE.

Prompt Battles currently slide among these.

BOUNDARY:
Barthes does not argue that scientific experiments should be rigged.

The analogy between theatrical construction and experimental control is [OUR INFERENCE].

TEST:
Choose one already-observed Prompt Battle failure.

Produce:

UNBOOKED VERSION
participants independently search for victory.

BOOKED VERSION
participants jointly design the strongest sequence for revealing the contested mechanism.

Publish the complete booking instructions before execution.

Then test both sequences on:

new model versions
new operators
new seeds
new prompt paraphrases.

If the booked sequence reveals a stable conditional mechanism across these substitutions, staging has increased experimental clarity.

If it collapses outside its exact choreography, the rig produced an artifact.
