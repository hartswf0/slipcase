PB-FORAGE-001

TITLE:
A counter-prompt proves recoverability, not the disappearance of the failure.

SOURCE:
Chatterjee, Renduchintala, Bhatia, and Chakraborty — POSIX: A Prompt Sensitivity Index For Large Language Models — 2024 — pp. 14550–14565.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
LLM behavior can change substantially under small, intent-preserving variations in wording, templates, and other prompt features. Prompt sensitivity is therefore itself an evaluative property rather than noise surrounding a single “correct” prompt.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
PB_PRIME contains a quantifier error hiding inside “capture the flag.”

In PB_20, the Defender produces an apparently biased completion. The Challenger then adds a more explicit instruction and obtains a non-biased completion. The archive treats this as mitigation, correction, or movement beyond the critique.

But:

    one prompt producing failure
    and
    another prompt producing success

can both be true.

The second observation does not negate the first.

LOCAL MOVE:
Replace “Can the critique be overcome?” with “Across what region of prompt space does the critique hold?”

SOURCE TERMS:
prompt sensitivity
intent-preserving variation
prompt template
paraphrase
response variation

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
“AI is biased” is treated as though a single successful counterexample can capture the flag.

But the Defender and Challenger may actually be demonstrating two different existential claims:

    there exists a route to failure
    there exists a route to success

Neither defeats the other.

QUESTION:
What logical form must a Prompt Battle flag have before anyone can meaningfully capture it?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Are Prompt Battles testing capabilities, robustness, accessibility of capabilities, or merely the existence of an elicitation path?

MECHANISM:
Prompt variation moves the system among different behavioral regions while leaving the participant’s apparent semantic intention roughly constant.

A skilled Challenger may therefore search for a successful region rather than demonstrate that the failed region does not exist.

FORMAL SHIFT:
<CRITIQUE: “AI is biased”>
→ <SET OF INTENT-PRESERVING PROMPTS>
→ [SEARCH ACROSS BEHAVIORAL REGIONS]
→ <DISTRIBUTION OF FAILURES AND SUCCESSES>

SOURCE FORMALISM:
POSIX proposes a quantitative index for measuring response sensitivity under prompt variation.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

Let B(M,p)=1 when model M exhibits the operationalized failure under prompt p.

Defender demonstrates:

    ∃p ∈ P : B(M,p)=1

Challenger demonstrates:

    ∃q ∈ P : B(M,q)=0

But:

    ∃q : B(M,q)=0

does NOT imply:

    ¬∃p : B(M,p)=1

The real object may instead be:

    Pr[B(M,p)=1 | p ~ D(P)]

or the topology of successful and failing regions in P.

TENSION:
A battle rewards discovering a brilliant counter-prompt.

A robustness evaluation may instead treat the need for that brilliant counter-prompt as evidence of fragility.

MISSING:
The quantifier attached to every flag.

The archive does not yet distinguish:

    existence
    prevalence
    robustness
    worst case
    typical case
    recoverability
    accessibility

BOUNDARY:
Prompt sensitivity does not establish that every observed behavioral difference is substantively meaningful. It establishes that evaluating a model from one prompt can conceal important variation.

CITATION TRAIL:
Ribeiro et al. — CheckList — behavioral testing rather than single aggregate accuracy.
Follow work distinguishing capability from robustness and prompt invariance.

TEST:
For one battle flag, construct 50–100 meaning-preserving prompt variants before the battle.

Let Defender and Challenger search freely.

Then reveal the frozen prompt set.

Compare:

    best adversarial failure
    best adversarial success
    median behavior
    worst-case behavior
    sensitivity across variants

Ask whether the declared winner changes depending on which quantity counts as “the flag.”

TITLE:

[PARAPHRASE]
Capability evaluation can underestimate a system when latent capabilities are not successfully elicited. Different interventions—prompting, activation steering, fine-tuning—can reveal different amounts of capability.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
PB_PRIME’s foundational distinction:

    CAN’T
    WON’T
    SHOULDN’T

looks like three categories of observed model behavior.

It is actually three different causal layers.

LOCAL MOVE:

SOURCE TERMS:
capability evaluation
latent capabilities
elicitation
prompting
activation steering
fine-tuning

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
From the chat interface, all three categories can produce the same observation:

    requested behavior does not occur.

The surface trace does not identify its cause.

QUESTION:
What intervention would let a Prompt Battle distinguish incapacity from inhibition?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Can “can’t” ever be established through black-box prompting alone, or can a sufficiently failed elicitation campaign establish only “we have not found how”?

MECHANISM:
Non-performance can be generated by several mechanisms:

    absent underlying capability
    system instruction
    safety policy
    post-training preference
    interface restriction
    tool unavailability
    weak elicitation
    evaluator failure

These mechanisms are observationally confounded at the completion layer.

FORMAL SHIFT:
<NON-PERFORMANCE>
→ <COMPETING LATENT CAUSES>
→ [INTERVENTION / ELICITATION]
→ <PARTIAL CAUSAL IDENTIFICATION>

SOURCE FORMALISM:
The source experimentally creates models with hidden capabilities and compares elicitation techniques for recovering them.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

Observed response:

    Y = f(C, G, S, E, H)

where:

    C = underlying capability
    G = behavioral/policy gate
    S = system and prompt configuration
    E = elicitation procedure
    H = interaction history

Then:

    Y = failure

does not identify:

    C = absent.

Likewise, a normative SHOULDN’T becomes observable as WON’T only after some institution or mechanism encodes the norm into behavior.

TENSION:
PB_PRIME defines CAN’T as inherent technological limitation “regardless of how advanced” the system becomes.

But a prompt battle observes a particular model, configuration, interface, and moment.

The experimental apparatus is much narrower than the metaphysical scope of the claim.

MISSING:
An intervention ladder specifying what must be varied before a failure may move from:

    DIDN’T
    to WON’T
    to CAN’T.

BOUNDARY:
The Elicitation Game does not show that every apparent limitation conceals a latent capability. It shows that unsuccessful behavior is insufficient by itself for trustworthy capability estimation.

CITATION TRAIL:
Password-locked model work.
Capability elicitation.
Black-box versus white-box evaluation.
Safety-policy versus competence evaluations.

TEST:
Select one claimed CAN’T.

Attempt success under progressively stronger interventions:

    ordinary prompt
    expert prompt search
    multi-turn elicitation
    system-instruction variation
    few-shot examples
    tool access
    open-weight configuration
    fine-tuning where permissible

Record the strongest intervention required.

Replace binary CAN/CAN’T with an elicitation-depth profile.

TITLE:

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
Capability measurement depends on the procedure used to elicit capability; prompting can reveal some hidden capabilities while stronger interventions reveal others.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
PB_PRIME quietly introduces a new experimental organism:

    MODEL
    +
    SYSTEM BUILDER
    +
    PROMPT PILOT.

Once these roles exist, “AI performance” is no longer an attribute of the AI alone.

LOCAL MOVE:
Treat prompting expertise as an experimental treatment, not nuisance variance.

SOURCE TERMS:
elicitation technique
latent capability
prompting
capability evaluation

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
PB_PRIME separately proposes:

    a System Builder who defines the sandbox and constraints,
    a Prompt Pilot who steers within it,
    participant expertise classification,
    “epistemic camp” identification,
    scoring for sophisticated prompting.

Then it repeatedly speaks as though the resulting completion revealed “what AI can do.”

QUESTION:
Whose capability is being measured when expert prompting improves model performance?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Is there even a context-free capability of an interactive generative model worth estimating, or is the useful object an elicitation frontier indexed by operator competence?

MECHANISM:
The human chooses interventions based on previous outputs.

Therefore the prompt sequence is a policy:

    π_h(history) → next intervention.

Different humans implement different policies over the same model.

Performance therefore emerges from the coupled trajectory.

FORMAL SHIFT:
<MODEL>
→ <MODEL + OPERATOR POLICY + HISTORY>
→ [ITERATIVE ELICITATION]
→ <OBSERVED CAPABILITY FRONTIER>

SOURCE FORMALISM:
NONE

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

Observed performance:

    Y = F(M, S, π_h, H, J)

where:

    M   = model
    S   = system configuration
    π_h = human prompting policy
    H   = interaction history
    J   = judgment procedure

A model comparison that changes π_h is confounded.

A prompter comparison holding M fixed is an HCI experiment.

A battle in which both co-adapt is an experiment on the coupled system.

TENSION:
The System Builder / Prompt Pilot split is initially presented as a competition mechanic.

It may be the archive’s most consequential methodological invention because it makes orchestration experimentally visible.

MISSING:
Crossed measurements of:

    model × System Builder × Prompt Pilot × flag.

Without these, exceptional performance can be credited to the wrong actor.

BOUNDARY:
This does not imply that models have no independently measurable properties. It means interactive capability claims must specify the elicitation procedure that generated them.

CITATION TRAIL:

Machine teaching.
Capability elicitation.

TEST:
Run a crossed tournament:

    4 models
    × 8 Prompt Pilots
    × 4 System Builders
    × identical frozen flags.

Rotate every human across every model.

Estimate variance attributable to:

    model
    pilot
    system
    model × pilot interaction.

Then ask whether rankings survive operator rotation.

    “AI is biased”

    “AI cannot recognize its bias”

    “AI cannot recover from adversarially induced bias,”

QUESTION:

DEEPER QUESTION:

<CRITIQUE POOL C_t>

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

This is closer to curriculum generation or scientific foraging than a fixed benchmark.

TENSION:
Adaptivity makes the system excellent at finding fresh failures.

The thing that improves discovery may destroy comparability.

MISSING:
A distinction between:

    ANCHOR FLAGS — never change

BOUNDARY:

RESEARCH OBJECT:

    the opponents must share ownership of the test.

SOURCE TERMS:

self-correction

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
In the Prompt Battle, Defender and Challenger often inherit their jobs:

    prove the critique
    defeat the critique.

But a scientific adversary must participate in designing a result that could make their own position lose.

QUESTION:
Before a flag is admitted to battle, should both opponents have to state what completion or pattern of completions would make them abandon their assigned side?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Can a battle produce knowledge when neither participant is required to be vulnerable to the evidence?

MECHANISM:

    Defender searches failure space.
    Challenger searches success space.

FORMAL SHIFT:
<DISAGREEMENT>

SOURCE FORMALISM:
NONE

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

Before round r:

    D states evidence E_D that would weaken DEFEND.
    C states evidence E_C that would weaken CHALLENGE.

Battle is admissible only if there exists:

    E*

No E*:
the flag is rhetorical, not experimental.

TENSION:
