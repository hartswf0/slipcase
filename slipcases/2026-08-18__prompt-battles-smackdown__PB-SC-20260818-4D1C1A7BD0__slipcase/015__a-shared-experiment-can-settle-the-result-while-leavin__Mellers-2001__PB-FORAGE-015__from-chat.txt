ID:
PB-FORAGE-015

TITLE:
A shared experiment can settle the result while leaving the disagreement alive.

SOURCE:
Barbara Mellers, Ralph Hertwig, and Daniel Kahneman — Do Frequency Representations Eliminate Conjunction Effects? An Exercise in Adversarial Collaboration — 2001 — Psychological Science 12(4):269–275.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
Mellers, Hertwig, and Kahneman jointly designed empirical tests around a disagreement concerning conjunction effects, with Mellers serving as arbiter. The experiments produced findings accepted as observations, but Hertwig and Kahneman still offered different interpretations of those findings.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
[[PB-FORAGE-005]] proposed that adversaries should jointly define evidence capable of making their own positions lose.

The canonical adversarial-collaboration example reveals a deeper problem.

Agreement on the experiment does not guarantee agreement on what the result means.

LOCAL MOVE:
Split the empirical crux into:

OUTCOME CRUX
what observation occurs

and:

INTERPRETATION CRUX
what change in theory that observation licenses.

SOURCE TERMS:
adversarial collaboration
empirical tests
dispute
arbiter
predictions
different interpretations

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
Two adversaries can agree completely about:

procedure
data
observed result

and still disagree theoretically.

Therefore “we agreed on the test beforehand” is not sufficient to make a battle decisive.

The elasticity may simply migrate from experimental design into interpretation.

QUESTION:
Must a Prompt Battle preregister not only predicted outcomes but how each possible outcome should change each side’s claim?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Can genuine surprise be preserved if interpretation itself is precommitted?

MECHANISM:
Competing accounts generate predictions.

A jointly accepted experiment yields observation E.

But each account retains an interpretation function mapping E into theoretical consequences.

If those mappings remain unconstrained, the same E can update the two theories differently.

FORMAL SHIFT:
<RIVAL CLAIMS>
→ <JOINT EMPIRICAL TEST>
→ [OBSERVED RESULT E]
→ <RIVAL INTERPRETATION FUNCTIONS>
→ <PERSISTENT OR TRANSFORMED DISAGREEMENT>

SOURCE FORMALISM:
NONE

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

Before battle:

A predicts P_A(E)
B predicts P_B(E)

But decisive collaboration also requires attention to:

U_A(E) = how A says evidence E should update A
U_B(E) = how B says evidence E should update B.

Even when:

E is shared,

it need not follow that:

U_A(E) = U_B(E).

The residual disagreement may therefore live in update rules rather than factual predictions.

TENSION:
Precommitting interpretive consequences makes post-hoc rescue harder.

But complete precommitment can make inquiry stupid.

An unexpected result may expose a distinction neither side knew before the experiment.

A method designed to prevent reinterpretation can also prevent conceptual learning.

MISSING:
A protocol distinguishing:

LEGITIMATE REFRAMING
because the evidence reveals a new variable

from:

POST-HOC IMMUNIZATION
where a theory changes only to avoid losing.

BOUNDARY:
Mellers, Hertwig, and Kahneman do not formalize “interpretation functions.”

That distinction is [OUR FORMALIZATION] generated from the fact that their jointly conducted experiments still permitted different interpretations.

CITATION TRAIL:
[[PB-FORAGE-005]]
→ Mellers, Hertwig, and Kahneman
→ adversarial-collaboration protocol
→ later extended adversarial collaborations
→ philosophy of auxiliary hypotheses
→ investigate when reinterpretation is discovery versus escape.

TEST:
Before a Prompt Battle, require each side to write:

1. predicted outcome distribution
2. what observation would strengthen its position
3. what observation would weaken its position
4. expected magnitude of belief change
5. observations presently considered ambiguous.

After the battle, permit revisions.

For every revision, require:

NEW EVIDENCE THAT FORCED REVISION:
NEW DISTINCTION INTRODUCED:
WHY THIS DISTINCTION WAS NOT IN THE ORIGINAL UPDATE RULE:

Compare ordinary battles with this condition.

The key outcome is not convergence.

It is whether surviving disagreements become narrower and more empirically discriminable.
