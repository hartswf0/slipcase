TITLE:
A consulting firm can change which proposal gets approved without changing the proposal

SOURCE:
Steve Denning — Why Business Schools And Consulting Firms Are Adding So Little Value — 2026 — §6.

PASSAGE:
[QUOTE] “for legitimacy and political cover”

RESEARCH OBJECT:
The effect of consultant attribution on approval when the recommendation itself is held constant.

LOCAL MOVE:
Denning gives a reason to hire consultants that operates through the organization’s approval process.

SOURCE TERMS:
“legitimacy”; “political cover”; “surge analytical capacity”; “proprietary benchmarks.”

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
A deck can look analytically redundant and still perform consequential work. Its signature can change who feels able to authorize the decision.

QUESTION:
At which approval step does outside endorsement change what an executive can get through?

DEEPER QUESTION:
When does the signature convey credible verification, and when does it redistribute blame for a decision already favored internally?

MECHANISM:
[OUR INFERENCE] An internal proposal stalls. An outside firm endorses it. Reviewers revise their belief in the analysis, their exposure if it fails, or both. The proposal clears the gate.

FORMAL SHIFT:
Internal proposal → externally attributed recommendation → [approval review] → possible change in approval or accountability.

SOURCE FORMALISM:
NONE

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX] Separate three effects: a change to the recommendation, a change to confidence in its evidence, and a change to who expects to answer for failure.

TENSION:
The same unchanged recommendation could reflect independent corroboration or a purchase of cover. Approval alone cannot distinguish them.

MISSING:
Versioned recommendations, the review sequence, and evidence of what persuaded the reviewer who had been blocking the decision.

BOUNDARY:
Denning proposes a motive. The passage leaves its prevalence and its effect on decision quality unresolved.

CITATION TRAIL:
Follow the internal proposal, procurement brief, consultant deliverable, and approval minutes for one engagement.

TEST:
Find an engagement where the recommendation survives intact. Compare the objection recorded before the engagement with the rationale recorded at approval. Code whether new evidence, independent verification, or shifted accountability addressed the objection.
