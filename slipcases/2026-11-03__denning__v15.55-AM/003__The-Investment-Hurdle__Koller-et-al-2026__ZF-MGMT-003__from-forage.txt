TITLE:
A customer outcome can appear in the argument and still lose the investment decision

SOURCE:
Tim Koller, Whitney Zimmerman, and Andy West — Why you can’t separate strategy from value—and vice versa — 2026 — response to the question about AI.
Steve Denning — Why Business Schools And Consulting Firms Are Adding So Little Value — 2026 — §3.

PASSAGE:
[QUOTE — Koller] “It allows you to innovate faster, perhaps provide better customer service, lower your manufacturing costs, your distribution costs, and so on.”
[QUOTE — Denning] “with (so far) no mention of the customer.”

RESEARCH OBJECT:
The difference between mentioning customer benefit and giving it independent weight in capital allocation.

LOCAL MOVE:
Koller lists better service among the effects AI can enable, then frames competitive differentiation in terms of returns on capital or revenue growth.

SOURCE TERMS:
“customer service”; “differentiate”; “return on capital”; “grow my revenues faster.”

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The authors’ interview supplies the missing word while leaving open whether the customer has any standing when the financial case weakens.

QUESTION:
Would the framework fund a measurable customer improvement whose expected financial return stays below the hurdle rate?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Can a customer outcome function as a commitment, a constraint, or a decision criterion when it cannot be converted into a credible revenue forecast?

MECHANISM:
[OUR INFERENCE] A project’s customer effects may enter a capital-allocation decision through projected retention, growth, pricing, or cost. Whether those effects can independently justify approval remains open.

FORMAL SHIFT:
Customer improvement → forecast business effects → [apply investment criteria] → approve, redesign, or reject.

SOURCE FORMALISM:
NONE

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX] Hold risk, cost, and financial return constant across two projects. Vary the customer outcome and observe whether the ranking changes.

TENSION:
Denning addresses the book’s public framing. The interview is a separate artifact. It establishes that customer service enters the authors’ account while leaving its decision weight unresolved.

MISSING:
A worked case in which a customer outcome and a financial hurdle point toward opposite decisions.

BOUNDARY:
The interview supports a distinction between rhetorical presence and decision authority. It leaves the book’s full allocation rules and client practice open.

CITATION TRAIL:
Compare the book’s capital-allocation examples with the authors’ public interview. Search for rejected or redesigned projects as well as approved ones.

TEST:
Find one below-hurdle project approved for a documented customer outcome. Check whether the outcome carried independent weight or whether the team revised its financial projections before approval.
