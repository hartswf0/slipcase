TITLE:
Denning’s own client list crosses the divide his argument depends on

SOURCE:
Steve Denning — About — public professional biography.
Steve Denning — Why Business Schools And Consulting Firms Are Adding So Little Value — 2026 — §§6–7.

PASSAGE:
[QUOTE — Denning biography] “His clients have included”
[PARAPHRASE] The list includes McKinsey as well as Microsoft and Netflix, two firms Denning uses as exemplars of the customer-value paradigm. In Forbes, Denning says leading customer-value firms often rely less on the major consultancies for core strategy.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
The engagement as the unit of analysis for an argument drawn around whole institutions.

LOCAL MOVE:
Denning’s biography names professional relationships across both sides of his distinction. It gives no dates, scopes, deliverables, or decision rights for those relationships.

SOURCE TERMS:
“clients”; “core strategy”; “rely less”; “radical management.”

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The same person can sell management advice to a consultancy and to the companies offered as evidence that the consultancy’s model is obsolete. A client list proves contact and leaves open what actually crossed the boundary.

QUESTION:
Which kinds of external advice do exemplar firms buy, and which decisions do they retain the capacity to make and revise themselves?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Does the dividing line run between firms, or between engagements that build the client’s judgment and engagements that sell a decision the client can hide behind?

MECHANISM:
[OUR INFERENCE] External advice can supply evidence, teach a method, confer cover, or take over a decision process. Its organizational effect depends on who owns the model afterward, who can challenge it, and who can act when it fails.

FORMAL SHIFT:
Firm–advisor relationship → engagement scope and decision rights → [classify the work performed] → evidence of learning, dependency, authorization, or some combination.

SOURCE FORMALISM:
NONE

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX] For each engagement, record who framed the question, who could reject the recommendation, who retained the working materials, and who handled post-decision evidence.

TENSION:
Reading A: A common advisor can carry practices across a porous institutional boundary. Reading B: Speaking or training work can coexist with substantial differences in who controls core strategy. The biography leaves both readings alive.

MISSING:
Engagement scopes and a comparison group for “rely less.” The client list supplies neither.

BOUNDARY:
The overlap does not establish that Denning advised any named firm on core strategy, that McKinsey adopted his approach, or that the professional relationship affected his article.

CITATION TRAIL:
[[ZF-MGMT-006]] and [[ZF-MGMT-011]] → Denning’s public client list → dated event programs, statements of work, and accounts from the people who owned the resulting decisions.

TEST:
Recover one documented engagement on each side of the divide. Classify both using the same criteria. Then seek one engagement at an exemplar firm where an outside advisor owned a decision model the client could not independently revise. That case would sharpen what “rely less” has to mean.
