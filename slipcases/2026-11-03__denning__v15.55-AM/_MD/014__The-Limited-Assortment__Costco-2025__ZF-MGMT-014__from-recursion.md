ZF-MGMT-014

TITLE:
Costco makes customer value by refusing to serve some customer preferences

SOURCE:
Costco Wholesale Corporation — 2025 Form 10-K — Item 1, “General.”
Steve Denning — Why Business Schools And Consulting Firms Are Adding So Little Value — 2026 — §§5 and 7.

PASSAGE:
[QUOTE — Costco] “We seek to limit most items to fast-selling models, sizes, and colors.”
[PARAPHRASE] Costco links a restricted assortment and low prices to volume and rapid inventory turnover. It identifies purchasing, distribution, and reduced handling as ways the model supports lower merchandise margins.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
The customer requests a business must decline to preserve its customer promise.

LOCAL MOVE:
Costco explains its economics through a deliberate assortment constraint. A smaller set of items is part of how the company can afford the offer it makes to members.

SOURCE TERMS:
“fast-selling”; “limited selection”; “rapid inventory turnover”; “gross margins.”

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The firm may have to disappoint a customer asking for more choice to protect low prices for the membership. Learning what customers want and deciding which wants the business can afford to serve are different operations.

QUESTION:
At what point does adding a locally desired item weaken the value members get from the system as a whole?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Where does the cost of the restricted assortment land among members whose needs fit the fast-selling sizes and members whose needs regularly fall outside them?

MECHANISM:
[OUR INFERENCE] Restricting variants can concentrate purchasing volume and reduce handling. The resulting savings can support lower prices. Members who cannot find a suitable variant may have to spend additional time or money elsewhere.

FORMAL SHIFT:
Competing assortment requests → item economics and member needs → [retain or exclude a variant] → changes in price, availability, and shopping burden.

SOURCE FORMALISM:
The filing defines gross margin as net sales less merchandise costs. Its account of assortment, volume, turnover, and operating efficiency is qualitative.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX] Measure the customer effect of adding or removing a variant through basket price, failed shopping trips, and spending elsewhere. Break out households whose requirements are affected.

TENSION:
Denning emphasizes continuous adaptation. Costco describes a stable constraint that can make selective adaptation valuable. The two can coexist, but a generic instruction to deliver more of what customers want cannot resolve a conflict between breadth and price.

MISSING:
A documented assortment decision with internal item economics and independent evidence about shoppers who could not complete the intended purchase.

BOUNDARY:
The filing describes Costco’s model. It leaves the causal effect of assortment limits and the distribution of gains across members to be tested.

CITATION TRAIL:
[[ZF-MGMT-010]] → Costco’s 10-K description of assortment → dated item additions or removals and shopping records that include trips ending without purchase.

TEST:
Find one removed variant. Compare basket costs and completion rates before and after removal for households that depended on it and households that did not. Check whether the reported performance metric registers the first group.
