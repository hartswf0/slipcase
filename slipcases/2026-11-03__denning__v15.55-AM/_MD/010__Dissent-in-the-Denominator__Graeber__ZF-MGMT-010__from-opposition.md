TITLE:
The dashboard can give dissent a place to die

SOURCE:
David Graeber — ‘I had to guard an empty room’: the rise of the pointless job — “Box-tickers.”
Dan Davies — Taming the Unaccountability Machine — “Control and communication.”
Steve Denning — Why Business Schools And Consulting Firms Are Adding So Little Value — 2026 — §§5, 7–8.

PASSAGE:
[QUOTE — Graeber] “allow an organisation to be able to claim it is doing something that, in fact, it is not doing”
[QUOTE — Denning] “metrics focused on customer outcomes”

RESEARCH OBJECT:
A metric that certifies customer-centeredness while discarding the evidence that could force a change.

LOCAL MOVE:
Graeber asks what an organization can claim because a document exists. Davies asks what gets lost as information is made manageable. Together they put Denning’s proposed outcome metrics under pressure.

SOURCE TERMS:
“box-tickers”; “customer outcomes”; “the decided-upon.”

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
A team can replace a margin target with a customer outcome target and preserve the same decision architecture. Whoever defines the denominator still decides whose experience is admissible.

QUESTION:
Can people excluded from the metric introduce evidence that compels the organization to change the service or the metric?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Who gets counted after they give up, leave, or fail to get through the front door?

MECHANISM:
[OUR INFERENCE] The dashboard includes completed journeys. The users most impeded by the process disappear before measurement. Staff produce evidence of improved outcomes for the remaining group. Leadership treats that improvement as permission to scale.

FORMAL SHIFT:
Attempted use → eligible measured cases → [aggregate recorded outcomes] → performance claim.

SOURCE FORMALISM:
NONE

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX] Recompute the metric for everyone who attempted to use the service. Keep abandonment, refusal, and unknown outcomes visible as separate categories.

TENSION:
A dashboard can reveal failure when it preserves exceptions and links them to review. The same instrument can hide failure when excluded people have no standing to contest its categories.

MISSING:
Denominator rules, abandoned journeys, cases coded out of scope, and the authority that can change those definitions.

BOUNDARY:
This is a test of Denning’s proposed paradigm. It establishes no measurement practice at any of his named firms.

CITATION TRAIL:
Follow one customer outcome claim back through the dashboard specification, source data, complaint records, and the decision made from it.

TEST:
Rebuild one celebrated outcome metric with abandoned cases visible. Take one material discrepancy to the metric owner. Observe whether it can alter the published claim or the decision made from it.
