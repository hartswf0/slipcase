ZF-MGMT-015

TITLE:
Denning’s earlier model can hear the client without hearing the people affected

SOURCE:
Stephen Denning — What Is Radical Management? — 2010 — opening Total Attorneys example, author-hosted version.
Dan Davies — Taming the Unaccountability Machine — “Control and communication.”

PASSAGE:
[QUOTE — Denning] “At the end of each cycle, the work is demonstrated to clients”
[PARAPHRASE] Denning reports that client feedback enters the next cycle. Davies asks whether people living with the consequences of decisions can communicate what standardized representations leave out.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
The membership rule for a working feedback loop.

LOCAL MOVE:
Denning’s earlier account supplies an operating mechanism missing from his 2026 examples: demonstrate work, solicit feedback, and feed it into the next cycle. Davies shifts attention to who can enter that cycle.

SOURCE TERMS:
“clients”; “cycles”; “feedback”; “the decided-upon.”

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
A service can get better at satisfying the purchasing client while becoming harder for others to live with. In a legal-services workflow, the firm purchasing the software, the worker using it, and the person whose case passes through it can have different complaints and unequal access to the team.

QUESTION:
Who can get a backlog item changed when the paying client likes the feature and someone downstream bears the cost?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Can a loop that increases responsiveness to the purchaser also increase organizational ignorance about everyone the purchaser can speak for?

MECHANISM:
[OUR INFERENCE] The purchaser sees the demonstration and submits feedback. The team reprioritizes work against that feedback. Harm experienced elsewhere may remain invisible if the affected person lacks direct access or standing to trigger review.

FORMAL SHIFT:
Product demonstration → admitted feedback → [reprioritize the next cycle] → revised service for the people represented in the loop.

SOURCE FORMALISM:
Denning describes cross-functional teams, prioritized work in cycles, demonstrations to clients, and incorporation of feedback. The account supplies no adjudication rule for conflicting feedback.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX] For each affected role, record access to the demonstration, ability to submit evidence, authority to trigger review, and power to reverse a release.

TENSION:
The paying client can be an effective proxy when its interests and downstream users’ interests align. The decisive case is a documented conflict in which the proxy has reason to suppress or discount the signal.

MISSING:
A demonstration roster, raw feedback, and a backlog decision where the purchaser’s preference conflicts with evidence from a worker or service recipient.

BOUNDARY:
Denning reports a client feedback loop. The legal-services conflict is a test case. The source does not establish that Total Attorneys excluded any particular group.

CITATION TRAIL:
[[ZF-MGMT-009]] and [[ZF-MGMT-010]] → Denning’s 2010 operating example → the roles present at a demonstration and the records of whose feedback changed the next cycle.

TEST:
Trace one workflow change from demo through release and subsequent complaint. Identify whether a person outside the purchaser’s organization could introduce evidence, trigger review, and get a harmful feature changed.
