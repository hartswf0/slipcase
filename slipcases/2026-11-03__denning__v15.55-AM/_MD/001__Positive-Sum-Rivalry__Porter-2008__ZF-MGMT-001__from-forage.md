ZF-MGMT-001

TITLE:
Porter gives customer needs a role in expanding the market

SOURCE:
Michael E. Porter — The Five Competitive Forces That Shape Strategy — 2008 — “Rivalry Among Existing Competitors.”
Steve Denning — Why Business Schools And Consulting Firms Are Adding So Little Value — 2026 — §4.

PASSAGE:
[QUOTE — Porter] “Such competition can not only support higher average profitability but also expand the industry, as the needs of more customer groups are better met.”
[QUOTE — Denning] “there is no mechanism for systematically raising the value delivered to customers.”

RESEARCH OBJECT:
The difference between a mechanism for better serving customer segments and a routine for discovering what customers will need next.

LOCAL MOVE:
Porter identifies an exception to zero-sum rivalry. Denning asks whether the framework can make continuous improvement an operating practice.

SOURCE TERMS:
“positive sum”; “customer segments”; “average profitability”; “systematically.”

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The text used to indict Five Forces for dividing an existing pie explicitly allows competition to expand it. The disagreement shifts to how managers would repeatedly find and act on unmet needs.

QUESTION:
Does Porter’s account of differentiated rivalry help managers discover neglected needs, or does it explain the benefits once firms have already identified them?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Where does the capacity to learn enter a framework organized around industry structure?

MECHANISM:
[PARAPHRASE] Firms tailor different mixes of price, features, services, or products to different customer segments. Better coverage of those needs can expand the industry and support higher average profitability.

FORMAL SHIFT:
Customer segments with different needs → differentiated offers → [firms compete on different dimensions] → possible market expansion.

SOURCE FORMALISM:
NONE

OUR FORMALIZATION:
NONE

TENSION:
Porter explains a way customer needs can be better met. Denning’s narrower challenge concerns a recurring process for sensing, testing, and revising what firms deliver.

MISSING:
An explicit update rule linking observed customer outcomes to the next change in the offer.

BOUNDARY:
The passage establishes that Porter recognizes positive-sum rivalry. It leaves the comparative effectiveness of Five Forces as a management practice open.

CITATION TRAIL:
Read Porter’s Paccar example under “Positioning the Company” for the operating practices that sustain a differentiated offer.

TEST:
Give two teams the same unfamiliar market case. Ask each to specify its next customer observation, decision owner, and revision trigger. Give one team Porter’s article and the other a customer-discovery protocol. Compare the resulting decision sequences.
