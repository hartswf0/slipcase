TITLE:
An investor return cannot distinguish customer enablement from customer dependence

SOURCE:
Steve Denning — Why Business Schools And Consulting Firms Are Adding So Little Value — 2026 — §§5, 8.

PASSAGE:
[QUOTE] “extraordinary 10-year total returns”

RESEARCH OBJECT:
The use of investor returns as evidence for a management model defined by customer outcomes.

LOCAL MOVE:
Denning places observed investor returns alongside his account of customer enablement and long-term reinvestment at selected firms.

SOURCE TERMS:
“customer enablement”; “long-term reinvestment”; “customer outcomes”; “10-year total returns.”

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
A product can expand what a customer is able to do while increasing what it costs that customer to leave. Both changes can support the same investor return.

QUESTION:
In one named firm, what evidence would distinguish repeat purchasing driven by better outcomes from repeat purchasing driven by switching costs?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Can enablement today produce dependence tomorrow through the same investment in training, integrations, or accumulated workflows?

MECHANISM:
[OUR INFERENCE] Adoption builds useful capabilities and firm-specific knowledge. Customers gain from using the product, while the cost of rebuilding those capabilities elsewhere can rise. Retention can reflect both effects.

FORMAL SHIFT:
Investor return → candidate operating explanations → [compare task outcomes and ability to switch] → evidence separating benefit from dependence.

SOURCE FORMALISM:
NONE

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX] Track three outcomes independently: performance on the customer’s task, cost to preserve that performance after switching, and investor return.

TENSION:
Customer enablement and bargaining power can coexist inside a single product decision. The stock-return figure supplies no way to apportion their contributions.

MISSING:
Customer-level task outcomes, switching costs, and a comparable group facing different switching conditions.

BOUNDARY:
The selected firms generate hypotheses about operating practices. Their returns leave the causal contribution of those practices and their transferability unresolved.

CITATION TRAIL:
Pair Porter’s discussion of switching costs with product migration records and customer procurement decisions for one firm Denning names.

TEST:
Find a change that lowered the cost of switching for some customers while leaving core functionality stable. Compare retention and task performance for affected customers and a comparable unaffected group.
