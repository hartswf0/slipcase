TITLE:
A new CEO can change who shares in the next windfall

SOURCE:
Daron Acemoglu, Alex Xi He, and Daniel le Maire — Eclipse of Rent-Sharing — NBER working paper issued 2022, forthcoming journal version — abstract.

PASSAGE:
[QUOTE] “Exploiting exogenous export shocks, we establish that a key mechanism for these wage effects is changes in rent-sharing practices”

RESEARCH OBJECT:
The response of worker pay to a favorable demand shock after a leadership change.

LOCAL MOVE:
The authors use export shocks to examine how gains are distributed, moving from a wage outcome to a candidate mechanism.

SOURCE TERMS:
“business degree”; “exogenous export shocks”; “rent-sharing practices”; “labor share.”

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
A management appointment can change the rule by which workers participate in gains. A snapshot of wages alone can miss that change.

QUESTION:
Which pay-setting decision weakens the transmission of favorable demand shocks into wages?

DEEPER QUESTION:
How much of the difference travels through classroom instruction, manager selection, or the board’s mandate at appointment?

MECHANISM:
[PARAPHRASE] The authors link wage effects to changes in rent-sharing after business managers take over. They present shareholder-value prioritization as a suggested explanation.

FORMAL SHIFT:
Export-demand shock → comparison of wage responses across manager types → [estimate shock-to-wage transmission] → evidence on rent-sharing.

SOURCE FORMALISM:
The abstract describes an instrumental-variable strategy using board composition at appointment and a separate analysis exploiting export shocks.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX] Separate the change in baseline pay after appointment from the change in how strongly pay responds to favorable demand.

TENSION:
The wage evidence puts pressure on management practices. Marquis’s curriculum argument requires a further link from educational exposure to those practices.

MISSING:
A trace from a specific curricular exposure to a specific pay-setting decision, with evidence about the board’s mandate.

BOUNDARY:
The reported estimates concern CEO business-degree status and firm outcomes in the US and Denmark. They leave the contribution of any individual course or doctrine unresolved.

CITATION TRAIL:
Follow the published article’s supplemental appendix and the working paper’s export-shock specifications.

TEST:
In one documented succession, examine compensation rules and board instructions before and after appointment. Look for a change to bonuses, bargaining, or wage progression that would predict a smaller worker share of the next comparable demand gain.
