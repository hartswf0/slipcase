TITLE:
The consultant can get paid again when the specification fails

SOURCE:
Dan Davies — Taming the Unaccountability Machine — discussion immediately preceding “The unaccountability machine.”

PASSAGE:
[QUOTE] “future ‘changes of specification’ can be billed at the full hourly rate.”

RESEARCH OBJECT:
The price difference between winning the initial contract and repairing an inadequate specification.

LOCAL MOVE:
Davies identifies an incentive for a vendor with better foresight to bid low at entry and earn more when the agreement has to change.

SOURCE TERMS:
“changes of specification”; “full hourly rate”; “cognitive deficit.”

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
A bad specification can look like a procurement win at award and become a source of repeat revenue after award. The buyer can meet its savings target at the exact moment it commits to an expensive dependency.

QUESTION:
Which foreseeable uncertainties did the vendor leave outside the base scope, and who later paid to bring them in?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Does repeated outsourcing erode the buyer’s ability to detect the next under-specified bid?

MECHANISM:
[PARAPHRASE] The better-informed party can price the initial contract aggressively and bill subsequent changes at higher rates. [OUR INFERENCE] If the buyer also loses internal expertise, it may become less able to specify or contest the next engagement.

FORMAL SHIFT:
Uncertain operating need → fixed bid specification → [price later deviations separately] → cheap award with potentially expensive adaptation.

SOURCE FORMALISM:
NONE

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX] Classify change orders by whether the need was unforeseeable, disclosed before award, or reasonably inferable from information available to the vendor.

TENSION:
Paid adaptation can be a sensible response to genuine uncertainty. The harder case is a recurring, foreseeable omission that one side is systematically better positioned to price.

MISSING:
Bid assumptions, pre-award questions, change orders, margins, and evidence of the buyer’s internal capacity over time.

BOUNDARY:
This mechanism comes from Davies’s discussion of public contracting. Its application to private strategy engagements requires contract-level evidence.

CITATION TRAIL:
Davies’s citation to Mariana Mazzucato and Rosie Collington’s The Big Con. Follow one original bid and its change-order history.

TEST:
Pick the costliest change order. Look for the same requirement in pre-award communications or prior engagements. Compare who knew, who could have specified it, and who profited when it resurfaced.
