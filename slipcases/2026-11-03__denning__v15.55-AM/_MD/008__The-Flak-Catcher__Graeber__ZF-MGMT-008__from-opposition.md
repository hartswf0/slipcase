TITLE:
The complaint handler is cheaper than fixing what people complain about

SOURCE:
David Graeber — The Rise of Bullshit Jobs — interview with Suzi Weissman — university carpenter example.

PASSAGE:
[QUOTE] “Be a flak-catcher, effectively.”

RESEARCH OBJECT:
A worker paid to receive the anger created by a capacity decision they lack authority to change.

LOCAL MOVE:
Graeber’s anecdote puts an apologetic intermediary between a broken shelf and a university with insufficient repair capacity.

SOURCE TERMS:
“flak-catcher”; “duct-taper”; “carpenter.”

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The intermediary may be excellent at helping people and still make the underlying failure easier for management to live with. Their competence can be what keeps the problem below the threshold for intervention.

QUESTION:
Does complaint handling reduce the underlying failure rate or mainly reduce the chance that the person who controls capacity has to encounter it?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Does the organization count complaints as evidence of a capacity shortfall or as work items to close?

MECHANISM:
[OUR INFERENCE] Understaffing produces repeated failures. A coordinator absorbs the resulting complaints. Management receives a count of closed tickets while the same failure keeps recurring.

FORMAL SHIFT:
Recurring failure → complaint queue → [acknowledge and close] → lower reported backlog with possible persistence of the failure.

SOURCE FORMALISM:
NONE

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX] Track complaint closure, recurrence of the underlying problem, and whether the budget owner received evidence of recurrence.

TENSION:
A coordinator can aggregate incidents and make the case for capacity that an individual user could never make. That role becomes a sensor if someone with authority must act on what it detects.

MISSING:
What the coordinator escalated, who could authorize another hire, and how recurrence entered the budget process.

BOUNDARY:
Graeber gives an anecdote and a staffing counterfactual. Applying Davies requires separate evidence that the organizational arrangement intercepted corrective feedback.

CITATION TRAIL:
Graeber’s interview on duct-tapers. Davies’s section “Control and communication.” Follow raw tickets into capacity reviews.

TEST:
Trace three repetitions of the same failure. If the handler closed each ticket and no record of recurrence reached the budget owner, inspect who chose the reporting rule and whether that rule can be challenged.
