TITLE:
Everybody can sign the decision and nobody can reverse it

SOURCE:
Dan Davies — Taming the Unaccountability Machine — “The unaccountability machine.”

PASSAGE:
[QUOTE] “the feedback link is broken between the consequences of decisions and the body responsible for making them”

RESEARCH OBJECT:
The path from a documented harm to someone with authority to change the decision that caused it.

LOCAL MOVE:
Davies locates the failure in the return path from consequences to decision authority.

SOURCE TERMS:
“accountability sinks”; “feedback link”; “blank contractual wall.”

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
A decision can leave an immaculate audit trail and still be practically unappealable. Every signature can establish that someone performed their assigned step while leaving the affected person without a route to correction.

QUESTION:
When a worker challenges a consultant-designed staffing cut using evidence of harm, where does the evidence stop moving?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Who designed the jurisdictional boundaries that let every participant give a locally accurate reason why the decision belongs somewhere else?

MECHANISM:
[OUR INFERENCE] The consultant points to the client’s sign-off. The executive points to the approved benchmark. HR points to policy. The appeal closes because each participant can explain their own step and none accepts the power to reopen the whole decision.

FORMAL SHIFT:
Harm → complaint → [route through limited mandates] → case closure or authorized reconsideration.

SOURCE FORMALISM:
NONE

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX] Draw a directed path from the affected person to a role able to receive the original evidence, reopen the case, and amend the rule for future cases. Mark where any of those powers disappears.

TENSION:
Dividing responsibilities can make decisions more accountable when escalation has a real endpoint. The same organizational chart can support either result.

MISSING:
Appeal records, actual override rights, and evidence that a successful appeal can change the rule as well as the individual outcome.

BOUNDARY:
The staffing example is a hypothetical application of Davies. Delay or multiple handoffs alone establish friction. The stronger claim requires a broken route to corrective authority.

CITATION TRAIL:
Davies’s discussion of outsourcing and democratic feedback. Follow a consulting recommendation into the client’s delegation matrix and appeals procedure.

TEST:
Take one upheld complaint. Check who heard it, who could change the individual outcome, and who could change the next decision. A reasoned reopening by an empowered owner defeats the strong sink diagnosis for that route.
