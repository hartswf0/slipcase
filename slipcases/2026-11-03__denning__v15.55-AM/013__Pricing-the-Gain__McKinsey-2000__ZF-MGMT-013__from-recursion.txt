ZF-MGMT-013

TITLE:
McKinsey had a way to price customer benefit decades before the alleged break

SOURCE:
McKinsey & Company — Delivering Value to Customers — 2000 — discussion of economic value to the customer.
Steve Denning — Why Business Schools And Consulting Firms Are Adding So Little Value — 2026 — §3.

PASSAGE:
[QUOTE — McKinsey] “quantify the additional value a product brings to customers above what they already receive from their present suppliers.”
[PARAPHRASE] McKinsey’s retrospective traces customer price–value models to its own staff papers from 1979 and 1981.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
A consulting instrument that makes the customer’s improvement legible as a quantity the seller can use.

LOCAL MOVE:
The article makes the customer’s existing supplier a baseline for evaluating the benefit of switching. It then connects the value proposition to the price charged to a segment.

SOURCE TERMS:
“economic value to the customer”; “present suppliers”; “value proposition”; “customer segment.”

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
Customer benefit has a history inside the very consultancy Denning casts as the carrier of an older paradigm. Putting that benefit into a model opens a further question: how much of the improvement is the model designed to leave with the customer?

QUESTION:
When the model finds additional customer value, does management change the product, the price, or the share of the gain it intends to keep?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Did this customer-centered vocabulary coexist with finance-led governance because it gave the seller a better way to calculate what it could capture?

MECHANISM:
[OUR INFERENCE] A supplier estimates the customer’s gain relative to an existing offer. The estimate can guide redesign or show how far price can rise before the customer loses the incentive to switch. The same evidence supports both operations.

FORMAL SHIFT:
Existing supplier and proposed offer → quantified difference in customer value → [inform design or pricing] → revised offer and division of gains.

SOURCE FORMALISM:
The article supplies a comparative baseline and a price–value framework. The alternative uses of that estimate are separated here for analysis.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX] Record incremental customer benefit before price, incremental price, and the residual benefit to the customer. Track which of the three the recommendation seeks to increase.

TENSION:
The historical source pressures a story about when consultancies discovered customer value. Denning’s stronger claim can survive as a claim about which decision criteria routinely win in practice.

MISSING:
Original staff papers and a client engagement that shows whether the model shifted engineering investment, pricing, or both.

BOUNDARY:
The retrospective establishes a published consulting lineage. It leaves the prevalence and influence of that lineage inside McKinsey and among its clients unresolved.

CITATION TRAIL:
[[ZF-MGMT-001]] and [[ZF-MGMT-003]] → McKinsey’s retrospective → its cited 1979 equipment model and Harvey Golub and Jane Henry’s 1981 “Market strategy and the price-value model.”

TEST:
Recover one worked recommendation and vary only the estimated customer benefit. Observe which output changes first: the product specification, target price, or expected margin.
