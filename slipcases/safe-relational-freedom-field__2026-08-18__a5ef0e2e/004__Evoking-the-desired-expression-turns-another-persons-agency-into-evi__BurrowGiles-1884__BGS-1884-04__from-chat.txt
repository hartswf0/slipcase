BGS-1884-04

TITLE:
“Evoking the desired expression” turns another person’s agency into evidence of the photographer’s authorship

SOURCE:
U.S. Supreme Court — Burrow-Giles Lithographic Co. v. Sarony — 1884 — 111 U.S. 53, 55, 60. fileciteturn0file1L23-L27 fileciteturn0file1L79-L81

PASSAGE:
[QUOTE]
Among the acts supporting Sarony’s authorship was “suggesting and evoking the desired expression.” fileciteturn0file1L79-L81

RESEARCH OBJECT:
One of the Court’s affirmative examples of authorship consists not in manipulating matter directly but in eliciting behavior from another agent.

LOCAL MOVE:
The factual findings treat successful direction of Wilde’s expressive performance as one of Sarony’s image-producing operations.

SOURCE TERMS:
suggesting
evoking
desired expression
arranging the subject
produced
original mental conception

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
An authored element of the photograph can physically originate in someone else’s face.

QUESTION:
When does eliciting another agent’s contribution become part of the director’s authorship rather than the contributing agent’s authorship?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Can authorship reside in steering a responsive system whose local actions are not literally executed by the author?

MECHANISM:
Sarony communicates or induces a desired state; Wilde performs or realizes that state; the resulting expression enters the photograph; the Court credits that sequence to Sarony’s production of the image.

FORMAL SHIFT:
<DESIRED EXPRESSION>
→ <DIRECTION / ELICITATION>
→ [RESPONSIVE AGENT ACTS]
→ <VISIBLE EXPRESSION IN WORK>

SOURCE FORMALISM:
NONE

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

DIRECTOR
→ instruction / elicitation
→ responsive agent
→ contingent performance
→ artifact

The intermediate agent does not automatically break attribution.

TENSION:
Wilde is not merely inert material. His expression depends on capacities and actions belonging to him, yet the opinion describes that expression as evidence of Sarony’s authorship.

MISSING:
The source never asks how much autonomous contribution by the responsive agent would make attribution genuinely joint, divided, or indeterminate.

BOUNDARY:
The case does not establish a general doctrine that commanding another agent transfers authorship of everything that agent produces. The quoted language concerns one component within a much larger pattern of Sarony’s control.

CITATION TRAIL:
Joint authorship doctrine
Director-performer authorship disputes
Nottage v. Jackson
Cases involving assistants executing another person’s artistic instructions
Human-computer interactive creation

TEST:
Compare Sarony’s direction of Wilde with increasingly autonomous performers or production agents. Hold the initial instruction constant while varying how much expressive determination occurs downstream.
