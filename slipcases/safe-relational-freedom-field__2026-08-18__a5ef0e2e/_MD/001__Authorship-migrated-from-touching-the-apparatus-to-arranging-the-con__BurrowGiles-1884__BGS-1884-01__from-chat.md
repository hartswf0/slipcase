TITLE:
Authorship migrated from touching the apparatus to arranging the conditions of the image

SOURCE:
U.S. Supreme Court — Burrow-Giles Lithographic Co. v. Sarony — 1884 — 111 U.S. 53, 60. fileciteturn0file1L77-L81

PASSAGE:
[QUOTE]
Sarony gave his conception visible form by “posing” Wilde, “selecting and arranging” costume and accessories, disposing “light and shade,” and “suggesting and evoking the desired expression.” fileciteturn0file1L79-L81

RESEARCH OBJECT:
The legally significant act was not simply operating the camera. The Court located authorship across a sequence of environmental interventions performed before the photographic exposure.

LOCAL MOVE:
The Court answers the claim that photography is merely mechanical by enumerating operations through which Sarony caused this particular image to exist.

SOURCE TERMS:

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
Why should authorship be identified with making marks directly on a medium? Sarony’s hand does not inscribe the photograph. He authors by configuring a situation from which the photograph emerges.

QUESTION:
When an artifact is generated by an apparatus, which upstream interventions are sufficiently causally specific to count as authorship?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Could authorship be better understood as the construction of generative conditions than as the physical execution of the final artifact?

MECHANISM:
Sarony changes the state of the scene—body, costume, accessories, illumination, affect—until the photographic apparatus records the configured state.

FORMAL SHIFT:

→ <CONFIGURED PHOTOGRAPHIC SITUATION>

SOURCE FORMALISM:
NONE

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

AUTHORSHIP ≠ final mechanical operation alone

AUTHORSHIP MAY RESIDE IN:
conception
+ selection
+ arrangement
+ direction
+ production of consequential conditions

TENSION:
The photographer does not determine every visible feature. Wilde’s body, the optical apparatus, chemistry, illumination, and physical causation all participate in producing the result.

MISSING:
The Court supplies no threshold for deciding how much arrangement is enough. It identifies abundant control in Sarony’s case but does not isolate the minimum sufficient operation.

BOUNDARY:
The decision does not establish that every person who gives instructions to an image-producing system is therefore an author. It establishes authorship only for the concrete configuration proved in this case.

CITATION TRAIL:
Nottage v. Jackson, 11 Q.B.D. 627 (1883)
Later cases distinguishing idea, direction, fixation, and authorship
Copyright disputes involving highly automated image-production systems

TEST:
Remove Sarony’s operations one at a time—posing, costume, lighting, expression, framing—and ask at what point the surviving causal contribution ceases to support the Court’s account of authorship.
