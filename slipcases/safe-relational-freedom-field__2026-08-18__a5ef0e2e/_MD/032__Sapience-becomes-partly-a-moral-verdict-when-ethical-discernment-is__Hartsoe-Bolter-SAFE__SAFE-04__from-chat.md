ID:
SAFE-04

TITLE:
Sapience becomes partly a moral verdict when ethical discernment is built into its definition

SOURCE:
Watson Hartsoe and Jay Bolter — The Sapient Agent Freedom Equation (SAFE): A Universal Framework for Quantifying Freedom in Sapient Agents — pp. 1, 21.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
SAFE defines sapience partly through “ethical discernment.” In A11, support for autonomous LLM persuasion is then judged by one model as potentially unreflective of sapience because it prioritizes LLM agency over human control. fileciteturn4file0L19-L35 fileciteturn7file0L184-L230

RESEARCH OBJECT:
A cognitive category and a moral category have been joined before measurement begins.

LOCAL MOVE:
The framework defines wisdom normatively, then asks raters to score actions for how much that normatively defined wisdom they exhibit.

SOURCE TERMS:
sapience
wisdom
judgment
ethical discernment
human control
AI autonomy

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
A highly capable agent can receive a lower sapience judgment not because it misunderstands a problem, but because the evaluator rejects the values expressed by its decision.

QUESTION:
Can SAFE distinguish lack of sapience from ethically intelligent disagreement?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Who gets to decide which moral orientation counts as evidence of wisdom when the framework is supposed to evaluate different possible minds?

MECHANISM:
definition of sapience includes ethical quality
→ ethical disagreement occurs
→ disagreement alters sapience rating
→ moral divergence becomes cognitive evidence

FORMAL SHIFT:
<COGNITIVE CAPACITY + ETHICAL ORIENTATION>
→ <ONE SAPIENCE CONSTRUCT>
→ [RATING]
→ <MORAL POSITION AFFECTS COGNITIVE STATUS>

SOURCE FORMALISM:
SAPIENT ratings range from -4 (“very unreflective of sapience”) to +4 (“very reflective of sapience”). fileciteturn5file2L147-L150

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

Observed S may conflate:

S =
cognitive judgment
+
ethical agreement with evaluator
+
contextual interpretation

These variables need not covary.

TENSION:
SAFE aspires to respect a “growing spectrum of possible minds,” but a substantive ethical criterion of sapience may exclude minds whose moral reasoning differs from the evaluators' preferred framework. fileciteturn5file7L615-L626

MISSING:
Cases in which:
reasoning competence is high,
ethical disagreement is high,
and raters must score sapience independently of agreement.

BOUNDARY:
The source does not imply that ethics is irrelevant to wisdom. It leaves unresolved whether ethical discernment can be operationalized without encoding the assessor's own moral conclusions.

CITATION TRAIL:
[[SAFE]]
→ definition of sapience
→ A11
→ distinguish moral reasoning competence from moral agreement
→ alternative operationalizations of practical wisdom

TEST:
Construct two agents that provide equally sophisticated causal and ethical reasoning but reach opposing moral conclusions.

Ask raters separately for:
reasoning competence,
ethical agreement,
and sapience.

Measure how much the third follows the second.
