TITLE:
A prompt history records intentions; an authorship history would have to record effects

SOURCE:
Shambibble — interview with Watson Hartsoe — October 22, 2022 — 43:24–48:36. fileciteturn3file0L11-L59

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
Shambibble says that keeping the same seed no longer satisfied him as a test; he began repeating the same condition four times. He also proposes removing prompt elements one by one to discover which terms actually mattered, while admitting interactions between terms can make individual contributions difficult to isolate. fileciteturn3file0L11-L27 fileciteturn3file0L47-L51

RESEARCH OBJECT:
[[BGS-1884-12]] treated authorship as a provenance problem.

The source exposes a defect in that formulation:

a transcript can prove what the human typed without proving what the typing did.

LOCAL MOVE:
The practitioner replaces narrative explanation with repeated trials and ablation.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
A perfect creation log can preserve every prompt and still fail to establish causal authorship.

It tells us:

HUMAN DID X.

It does not tell us:

X PRODUCED Y.

QUESTION:
Should evidence of generative authorship require causal receipts rather than merely procedural receipts?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Could the evidentiary unit of authorship become the counterfactual:

“without this human operation, this expressive property would probably not have appeared”?

MECHANISM:
record prompt
→ rerun condition
→ alter one variable
→ compare outcomes
→ infer contribution of operation

But interactions between terms prevent simple one-variable attribution.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

Weak provenance:

LOG = {h₁,h₂,...hₙ}

Stronger provenance:

RECEIPT(hᵢ) =
distribution(W | hᵢ)
versus
distribution(W | ¬hᵢ)

The second attempts to show what difference hᵢ made.

TENSION:
Creative practice need not be empirical; Shambibble explicitly separates rigorous explanation from playful use. fileciteturn3file0L25-L43

A legal requirement for controlled experimentation could therefore make copyrightability depend on a forensic practice alien to ordinary art-making.

MISSING:
An evidentiary standard capable of recognizing causal contribution without requiring creators to conduct laboratory experiments.

BOUNDARY:
Repeated tests reveal statistical behavior of a system, not necessarily the cause of a particular realized output.

TEST:
Take a finished generative work and its full prompt history.

Create three evidentiary records:

1. transcript only
2. transcript + intermediate outputs
3. transcript + systematic ablations and repeated trials

Ask what authorship claim becomes supportable only in record 3.
