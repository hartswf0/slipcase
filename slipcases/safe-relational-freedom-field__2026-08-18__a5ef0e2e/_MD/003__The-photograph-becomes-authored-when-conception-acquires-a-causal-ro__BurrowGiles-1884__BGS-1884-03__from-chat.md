TITLE:
The photograph becomes authored when conception acquires a causal route into visible form

SOURCE:
U.S. Supreme Court — Burrow-Giles Lithographic Co. v. Sarony — 1884 — 111 U.S. 53, 58–60. fileciteturn0file1L65-L81

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
The Court defines constitutional “writings” broadly as forms through which ideas in an author’s mind receive visible expression, then relies on the finding that Sarony gave his “original mental conception” visible form through specific acts of arrangement. fileciteturn0file1L65-L69 fileciteturn0file1L79-L81

RESEARCH OBJECT:
The opinion contains a two-part architecture of authorship: conception alone is insufficiently visible, while mechanical fixation alone may be insufficiently intellectual. Authorship appears in the successful passage between them.

LOCAL MOVE:
The Court connects an enlarged constitutional definition of “writing” to a factual account of how an internal conception became an external artifact.

SOURCE TERMS:
ideas in the mind
visible expression
original mental conception
visible form
produced

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The key object may not be either the idea or the photograph. It may be the transformation that makes one consequential for the other.

QUESTION:
What must exist between a mental conception and a generated artifact for the artifact to be attributable to that conception?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Is authorship fundamentally a relation of causal traceability between conception and artifact?

MECHANISM:
Conception becomes legally relevant because a chain of decisions changes the eventual visible result.

TENSION:
Many production processes contain a conception but produce results whose details cannot be predicted or controlled. Conversely, highly controlled production can occur without a rich antecedent conception.

MISSING:
The opinion does not tell us whether correspondence between intention and result must be predictable, measurable, or merely demonstrated retrospectively.

BOUNDARY:
The source does not establish “mental conception” as independently copyrightable. It repeatedly requires that conception take visible form in a produced work.

CITATION TRAIL:
Constitutional definitions of “author” and “writings”
Idea-expression doctrine
Cases concerning accidental, aleatory, procedural, and computational creation

TEST:
Compare works with identical initial intentions but progressively less predictable production mechanisms. Determine whether subsequent doctrine tracks intention, intervention, predictability, or resulting expressive form.
