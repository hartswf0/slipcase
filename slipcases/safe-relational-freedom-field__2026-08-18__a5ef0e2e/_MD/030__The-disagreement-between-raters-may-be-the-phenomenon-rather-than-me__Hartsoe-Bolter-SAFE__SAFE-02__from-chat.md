ID:
SAFE-02

TITLE:
The disagreement between raters may be the phenomenon rather than measurement error

SOURCE:
Watson Hartsoe and Jay Bolter — The Sapient Agent Freedom Equation (SAFE): A Universal Framework for Quantifying Freedom in Sapient Agents — pp. 7–9.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
SAFE reports that models systematically prioritize human control, AI autonomy, technological progress, power, and risk differently. It then argues that disagreement may be advantageous because it preserves competing perspectives and warns that privileging one model could bias governance. fileciteturn5file10L791-L813 fileciteturn5file5L391-L414

RESEARCH OBJECT:
Inter-rater variance may be normative plurality disguised as measurement variance.

LOCAL MOVE:
The study begins by seeking disagreement between model ratings and discovers distinct political interpretations embedded in the ratings themselves.

SOURCE TERMS:
disagreement
human control
AI autonomy
power
interpretive framework
diversity
consensus

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
A freedom instrument normally wants raters to converge.

SAFE's most interesting result appears exactly where convergence fails.

QUESTION:
What if calibration destroys the information SAFE is uniquely capable of revealing?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Can a measure of freedom remain valid if freedom itself is a contested political concept whose disagreement cannot be calibrated away?

MECHANISM:

→ high disagreement
→ underlying normative conflict becomes visible

SOURCE FORMALISM:

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

R_ij =
measurement_j(object_i)
+
normative_frame_j(object_i)

reducing variance may erase rather than improve measurement.

TENSION:
The paper simultaneously describes disagreement as potentially productive and calls for future work toward “more aligned and consistent AI evaluative frameworks.” fileciteturn5file5L397-L427

MISSING:
A rule for distinguishing:
measurement error,
model bias,
reasonable normative disagreement,
and genuinely different objects of evaluation.

BOUNDARY:
Not every disagreement is philosophically meaningful. Some variance may still arise from misunderstanding, instability, or model failure.

TEST:
Before averaging any ratings, cluster raters by the reasons they provide.

Determine whether high variance decreases after controlling for differing interpretations of whose agency or freedom is being evaluated.
