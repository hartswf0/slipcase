ZETTEL

ID:
BGS-1884-12

TITLE:
Selecting elements into a work and selecting a finished work from options are different operations

SOURCE:
U.S. Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit — Urantia Foundation v. Maaherra — 1997 — 114 F.3d 955.
U.S. Copyright Office — Copyright and Artificial Intelligence, Part 2: Copyrightability — 2025.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
Urantia treats human selection and arrangement of allegedly nonhuman revelations as potentially copyrightable compilation authorship. The Copyright Office, by contrast, concludes that choosing one AI-generated output from uncontrolled options does not by itself constitute authorship.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
“Selection” is hiding two different machines.

SELECTION A:
elements → human constructs arrangement.

SELECTION B:
completed candidates → human chooses preferred candidate.

The same verb obscures a major difference in where expressive structure is produced.

LOCAL MOVE:
Reading the two sources together splits “selection” into compositional selection and terminal selection.

SOURCE TERMS:
selection
arrangement
coordination
uncontrolled options
authorship by adoption
creative act

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
We cannot ask whether selection “counts” as authorship until we specify what is being selected and what selection changes.

Choosing twelve passages and arranging them into a book can create new structure.

Choosing one already completed image from twelve candidates may create no new internal structure in that image.

QUESTION:
When does selection manufacture expressive relations rather than merely identify an already produced object?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Is the missing unit of authorship not “choice” but choice that changes relations among expressive elements?

MECHANISM:
COMPOSITIONAL SELECTION:
candidate elements
→ choose subsets
→ establish relations/order
→ new expressive whole

TERMINAL SELECTION:
candidate wholes
→ choose one
→ chosen whole remains internally unchanged

FORMAL SHIFT:
<CHOICE>
→ <LOCATE LEVEL OF CHOICE>
→ [ELEMENT-LEVEL COMPOSITION OR WHOLE-OUTPUT ACCEPTANCE]
→ <POSSIBLE AUTHORSHIP / MERE ADOPTION>

SOURCE FORMALISM:
NONE

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

Let W be a work.

Terminal selection:
{W1,W2,...Wn} → Wk

No required transformation:
Δstructure(Wk) = 0

Compositional selection:
{e1,e2,...en}
→ select + relate
→ W

where
RELATIONS(W)
are partly human-originated.

TENSION:
A generative workflow can blur these categories.

A user might select one output, crop it, combine it with another, use it as an image-conditioning input, regenerate locally, reject regions, preserve others, and iterate.

At what point does terminal selection become compositional selection?

MISSING:
A representation of generation history capable of locating whether human choices operated between whole candidates or inside the evolving work.

BOUNDARY:
The Copyright Office’s 2025 conclusion addresses current generally available generative technology and does not declare that all acts called “selection” are nonauthorial.

TEST:
Take one generative workflow and implement two versions:

A: generate 100 complete outputs and choose one.
B: construct one output through 100 human selections among partial elements and intermediate states.

Hold final visual quality constant.

Ask where legally relevant human expression becomes identifiable.
