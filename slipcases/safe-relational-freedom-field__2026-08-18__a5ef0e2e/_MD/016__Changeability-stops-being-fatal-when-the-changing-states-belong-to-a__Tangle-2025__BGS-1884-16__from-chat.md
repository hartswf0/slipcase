ZETTEL

ID:
BGS-1884-16

TITLE:
Changeability stops being fatal when the changing states belong to an authored possibility space

SOURCE:
U.S. Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit — Tangle, Inc. v. Aritzia, Inc. — 2025.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
The Ninth Circuit rejected the idea that a movable sculpture cannot be fixed merely because its pose changes. It reasoned that a human author can conceive a moving or shape-shifting work and that a material embodiment need not remain physically static to satisfy fixation.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
Tangle prevents us from learning the wrong lesson from Kelley.

The problem is not simply:

THE WORK CHANGES.

A changing work can remain authored.

The deeper issue may be whether the work’s changes instantiate an authored range of possibilities or whether outside processes continually introduce form not contained in that range.

LOCAL MOVE:
The court separates material stasis from fixation and allows a work to persist across multiple configurations.

SOURCE TERMS:
moveable
fixed
mental conception
moving
shape-shifting
material object
different poses

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
A work may be one work precisely because it can become many visible states.

This reverses the intuitive model in which authorship points toward one final artifact.

QUESTION:
Can the object of authorship be a space of possible configurations rather than a single configuration?

DEEPER QUESTION:
What would it mean to author not an output but the lawful transformations through which outputs may vary?

MECHANISM:
human conceives transformable object
→ material structure embodies constraints
→ user / motion changes pose
→ new configuration
→ configuration remains an instance of the same authored work

FORMAL SHIFT:
<SINGLE FIXED APPEARANCE>
→ <FIXED TRANSFORMATIONAL STRUCTURE>
→ [MOVE WITHIN PERMITTED CONFIGURATIONS]
→ <MULTIPLE STATES OF ONE WORK>

SOURCE FORMALISM:
NONE

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

WORK ≠ one state S

WORK may equal:

W = {S | S satisfies authored constraint system C}

Authorship could therefore target C,
not each particular S.

TENSION:
Kelley’s garden also changes through time.

The difference cannot therefore be simple mutability.

Candidate distinction:

TANGLE:
change occurs within human-authored structural constraints.

KELLEY:
natural processes contribute much of the changing perceptible form.

But the cases do not themselves provide this formal distinction.

MISSING:
A test for identifying whether a state belongs to an authored possibility space or contains independently originated expressive structure introduced by the transforming process.

BOUNDARY:
Tangle concerns a physical movable sculpture and fixation. It does not decide authorship of generative-model state spaces.

CITATION TRAIL:
[[BGS-1884-15]]
→ Kelley
→ Tangle
→ distinguish mutable work from independently generative medium

TEST:
Represent Kelley and Tangle as sets of reachable states.

Ask whether a human could specify the invariant constraints that make all Tangle states instances of one conceived work, then test whether the same can be done for the garden without trivializing the description.
