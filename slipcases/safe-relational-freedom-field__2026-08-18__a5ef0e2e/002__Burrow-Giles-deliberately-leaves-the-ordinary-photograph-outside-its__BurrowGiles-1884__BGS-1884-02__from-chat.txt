ID:
BGS-1884-02

TITLE:
Burrow-Giles deliberately leaves the “ordinary photograph” outside its holding

SOURCE:
U.S. Supreme Court — Burrow-Giles Lithographic Co. v. Sarony — 1884 — 111 U.S. 53, 59. fileciteturn0file1L71-L77

PASSAGE:
[QUOTE]
After describing the argument that photography can be merely mechanical, the Court says: “This may be true in regard to the ordinary production of a photograph, and, further, that in such case a copyright is no protection. On the question as thus stated we decide nothing.” fileciteturn0file1L71-L73

RESEARCH OBJECT:
The canonical photography-authorship case contains an unresolved category: a photograph produced so mechanically that copyright might not protect it.

LOCAL MOVE:
Rather than prove photography intrinsically creative, the Court narrows the issue to Sarony’s unusually demonstrable acts of conception and arrangement.

SOURCE TERMS:
ordinary production
merely mechanical
no originality of thought
no novelty
accuracy
copyright is no protection
decide nothing

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
Burrow-Giles is commonly available as an argument that technologically mediated images can be copyrighted, but its reasoning simultaneously preserves the possibility of technologically mediated images without protectable authorship.

QUESTION:
What distinguishes Sarony’s authored photograph from the “ordinary production of a photograph” the Court refuses to decide?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Is copyrightability determined by the class of technology being used, or by the structure of human intervention in a particular production?

MECHANISM:
The Court avoids settling whether photography as such is authorship. It performs a case-specific differentiation:

mechanical reproduction?
→ unresolved

demonstrated conception + arrangement + direction?
→ authorship established

TENSION:
Only six lines later, the Court declares Sarony’s photograph an “original work of art.” The opinion therefore establishes a positive case without supplying a rule capable of sorting all neighboring cases.

MISSING:
A criterion separating ordinary mechanical production from intellectual production.

BOUNDARY:
Burrow-Giles cannot, by itself, support the proposition that all photographs are copyrightable merely because a human initiated their production.

CITATION TRAIL:
Cases applying Burrow-Giles to routine photography

TEST:
Build a gradient of image-production cases from automatic exposure to Sarony-level orchestration and identify whether subsequent courts ever specify where the constitutional boundary actually falls.
