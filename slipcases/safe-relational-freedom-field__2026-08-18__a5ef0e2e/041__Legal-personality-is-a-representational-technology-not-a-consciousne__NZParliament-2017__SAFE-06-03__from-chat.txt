SAFE-06-03

TITLE:
Legal personality is a representational technology, not a consciousness certificate

SOURCE:
New Zealand Parliament — Te Awa Tupua (Whanganui River Claims Settlement) Act 2017 — ss. 14, 18. ([legislation.govt.nz](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2017/7/en/2017-03-20/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)) ([legislation.govt.nz](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2017/7/en/2017-03-20/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
Section 14 declares Te Awa Tupua a legal person possessing the rights, powers, duties, and liabilities of a legal person. The Act separately establishes Te Pou Tupua to serve as the “human face” of Te Awa Tupua and act in its name. ([legislation.govt.nz](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2017/7/en/2017-03-20/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)) ([legislation.govt.nz](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2017/7/en/2017-03-20/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

RESEARCH OBJECT:
[[SAFE-06]] still bundled together questions that law can separate:

Is this entity sapient?

Does this entity matter morally?

Can this entity hold legal rights?

Can this entity exercise legal powers itself?

Who represents it when it cannot?

Te Awa Tupua makes those questions visibly non-identical.

LOCAL MOVE:
The legislation assigns legal personality to an entity while separately supplying a representational mechanism through which its legal powers and duties are exercised.

SOURCE TERMS:
legal person
rights
powers
duties
liabilities
human face
act in the name of

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
An entity can occupy the legal position of a rights-holder without first being shown to possess the cognitive architecture ordinarily associated with human agency.

[OUR INFERENCE]
Legal personality therefore cannot be treated as a measurement readout for sapience.

QUESTION:
Why should SAFE make sapience the gateway to legal recognition if legal systems can assign recognition for reasons other than cognitive equivalence to humans?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Which of the following does SAFE actually want to establish:

moral patienthood
legal personality
decision-making capacity
autonomous agency
representability
welfare significance
political membership?

MECHANISM:
entity

→ representative exercises legal functions in entity's name

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

Separate:

M = moral status
L = legal personality
A = autonomous agency
C = decision capacity
R = representational mechanism

No source establishes:

M = L = A = C.

Te Awa Tupua demonstrates at minimum:

L does not require
the rights-holder personally performing every legal operation,

because:

L(entity)
+
R(representative)
→ legal action.

TENSION:
Te Awa Tupua is not a convenient generic example of “giving rights to a nonsentient thing.”

Its legal personality emerges from a specific Māori-Crown settlement and conception of the Whanganui River.

Flattening that history into an AI analogy would destroy much of what the source is doing.

Yet precisely because the source is not about machine intelligence, it destabilizes the assumption that legal personality must track intelligence at all.

MISSING:
A SAFE ontology distinguishing:

recognition
representation
protection
capacity
personality
agency
and moral patienthood.

BOUNDARY:
The Act supplies no evidence concerning AI consciousness, AI rights, or AI moral status.

It demonstrates only that legal personhood is an institutional construction capable of being separated from direct individual exercise of legal agency.

TEST:
Take every right proposed by the SAFE License and ask separately:

1. Must its holder understand the right?
2. Must its holder personally exercise the right?
3. Could the right be represented?
4. Does the right protect welfare, agency, voice, property, continuity, or participation?
5. What evidence is actually necessary before granting that specific right?
