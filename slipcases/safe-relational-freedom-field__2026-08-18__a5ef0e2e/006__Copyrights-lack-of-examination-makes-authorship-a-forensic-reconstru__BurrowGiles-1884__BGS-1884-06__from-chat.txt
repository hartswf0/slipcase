BGS-1884-06

TITLE:
Copyright’s lack of examination makes authorship a forensic reconstruction of production

SOURCE:
U.S. Supreme Court — Burrow-Giles Lithographic Co. v. Sarony — 1884 — 111 U.S. 53, 59–60. fileciteturn0file1L73-L77

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
Unlike the patent system, copyright registration involved no prior tribunal examining originality. The Court therefore says originality, intellectual production, thought, and conception become especially important matters of proof once infringement litigation begins. fileciteturn0file1L75-L77

RESEARCH OBJECT:
The doctrine does not merely define authorship. It creates an evidentiary problem: after an artifact exists, a court must reconstruct the human operations that produced it.

LOCAL MOVE:
The opinion explains why the factual findings concerning Sarony’s process matter so much: copyright registration itself had not established originality.

SOURCE TERMS:
previous examination
established by proof
originality
intellectual production
thought
conception
proved

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
Authorship is partly an observability problem. A work can exist while the legally important history of how it came into existence remains invisible.

QUESTION:
What evidence is required to reconstruct authorship when the final artifact does not reveal its production history?

DEEPER QUESTION:
What happens to authorship doctrine when production systems become too complex, iterative, distributed, or opaque for the relevant causal contribution to be recovered from the artifact itself?

MECHANISM:
Registration occurs without substantive originality examination.
Infringement occurs.
Authorship becomes disputed.
Production history must then be reconstructed through evidence.

FORMAL SHIFT:
<ARTIFACT + REGISTRATION>
→ <DISPUTE>
→ [RECONSTRUCT PRODUCTION]
→ <PROVE OR FAIL TO PROVE AUTHORSHIP>

SOURCE FORMALISM:
NONE

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

AUTHORSHIP is partly a provenance query:

WHO did WHAT
to WHICH intermediate state
with WHAT consequential effect
on the final expression?

TENSION:
The doctrine speaks in terms of mental conception, yet courts can only access that conception through external receipts: testimony, arrangements, intermediate artifacts, instructions, and other observable acts.

MISSING:
The Court does not specify what evidence would establish authorship when a creator cannot reconstruct or document the relevant production operations.

BOUNDARY:
This passage explains the evidentiary significance of originality in litigation; it does not itself prescribe a comprehensive provenance regime.

CITATION TRAIL:
Historical Copyright Office registration practices
Cases requiring evidence of independent creation
Contemporary provenance and creation-log systems
Authorship disputes involving opaque computational workflows

TEST:
Compare disputes where courts possess only a final artifact against disputes containing rich process evidence. Determine which kinds of production receipts actually change authorship findings.
