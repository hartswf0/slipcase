TITLE:
Unpredictability does not defeat authorship; uncontrolled expressive determination might

SOURCE:
U.S. Copyright Office — Copyright and Artificial Intelligence, Part 2: Copyrightability — 2025.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
In comparing generative AI with processes such as nature photography and splatter painting, the Office distinguishes unpredictability from control. It states that some randomness can coexist with authorship and frames the relevant issue as the degree of human control rather than mere predictability of outcome.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
[[BGS-1884-03]] asked whether authorship requires a predictable correspondence between conception and result.

The stronger answer from this source is:

wrong variable.

A work may outrun prediction while still remaining within a human-authored process.

LOCAL MOVE:
The Office refuses to make foreseeability of exact outcome the decisive authorship criterion.

SOURCE TERMS:
randomness
control
predictability
execution
determination
expressive elements

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
Surprise and authorship are not opposites.

An author can create a process specifically because it produces things the author cannot foresee.

This means “the model surprised me” proves almost nothing.

QUESTION:
What is controlled when the exact resulting form is not?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Can authorship reside in controlling a space of permissible transformations rather than controlling the realized endpoint?

MECHANISM:
human establishes:
constraints
materials
operations
parameters
location of intervention

process introduces:
contingency / randomness

result:
not fully forecast

SOURCE FORMALISM:
NONE

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

PREDICT(W) ≠ CONTROL(PROCESS)

Possible authorship relation:

Human chooses boundary B
Process samples within B
Exact x ∈ B is unknown beforehand

The important question becomes:
who authored B?

TENSION:
Current text-prompt systems may let users state desired properties without actually determining the operative constraint boundary used by the model.

An apparent instruction can therefore look like control linguistically while functioning only probabilistically.

MISSING:
A measurable distinction between:
requested constraint,
effective constraint,
and observed regularity.

BOUNDARY:
The Office does not say unpredictability is irrelevant in every case. It says unpredictability alone is not the decisive variable and notes that foreseeability may bear on authorship in particular circumstances.

TEST:
Hold exact-output predictability low while progressively increasing control over the output distribution.

Then reverse the experiment: increase predictability without giving the user meaningful control.

Determine which condition better tracks the source’s concept of authorship.
