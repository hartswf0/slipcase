BGS-1884-10

TITLE:
Creative contribution is not authorship; authorship may be a position of control over the whole

SOURCE:
U.S. Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit — Aalmuhammed v. Lee — 2000 — 202 F.3d 1227.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
The Ninth Circuit observed that many people make genuinely creative contributions to a film, yet this cannot make all of them authors. It read Burrow-Giles as requiring something beyond minimal creative contribution and associated authorship with the person who superintends the work or exercises artistic control.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
Copyright contains two questions that our earlier forage was allowing to collapse:

1. Did this person originate expression?
2. Is this person an author of the larger work?

A person may satisfy the first without satisfying the second.

LOCAL MOVE:
The court uses the collaborative production of a film to pressure Burrow-Giles’s “master mind” language and distinguish creative contribution from authorship of the work as a whole.

SOURCE TERMS:
creative contribution
author
master mind
superintended
artistic control
origin

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
More creativity does not monotonically produce more authorship.

A lighting designer may make a visually profound difference.
A costumer may determine recognizable features.
An actor may improvise an immortal scene.

Yet the court refuses the inference:

CREATIVE DIFFERENCE
→ AUTHOR OF THE FILM.

QUESTION:
What additional relation converts “I made part of this different” into “this work owes its origin to me”?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Is authorship partly a topology of authority—where someone sits in a production system—rather than a quantity of creativity contributed?

MECHANISM:
Collaborative production distributes creative interventions across many agents.

If creativity alone establishes authorship:
many contributors
→ many putative authors
→ category loses discriminatory force.

The court therefore looks upward toward superintendence and control of the larger work.

FORMAL SHIFT:
<CREATIVE CONTRIBUTION>
→ <RELATION TO WHOLE WORK>
→ [ASK FOR SUPERINTENDENCE / CONTROL]
→ <AUTHOR OR CONTRIBUTOR>

SOURCE FORMALISM:

TENSION:
This sits uneasily beside Sarony’s case-specific list of small operations—costume, lighting, pose, expression—that collectively establish authorship.

Aalmuhammed suggests that the same operations, distributed among specialists, might establish creativity without establishing authorship of the resulting whole.

MISSING:
Why should aggregating many expressive decisions in one person transform those decisions from contributions into authorship?

Is the decisive variable:
quantity,
coordination,
final authority,
intent,
public attribution,
or power to decide what survives?

BOUNDARY:
Aalmuhammed concerns authorship of a collaborative motion picture and joint-authorship doctrine. Its control language cannot automatically be converted into a universal test for every medium.

TEST:
Take Sarony’s enumerated photographic decisions and distribute them among five people.

Keep the final photograph identical.

Ask whether authorship changes merely because the same creative operations now occupy different positions in a production hierarchy.
