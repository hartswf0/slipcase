TITLE:
Deliberate underspecification makes the missing instruction part of the creative technique

SOURCE:
Shambibble — interview with Watson Hartsoe — October 22, 2022 — 51:32–53:05. fileciteturn3file0L61-L69

PASSAGE:
[QUOTE]
“It’s very good at taking an underspecified—you know, which again directors are often prone to do, you know, ‘make it pop’ or whatever. It’s great at taking a vague notion and just like filling in some details that look cool.” fileciteturn3file0L65-L67

RESEARCH OBJECT:
Under-specification is not necessarily failed specification.

It can be intentional delegation.

LOCAL MOVE:
The source compares vague generative instructions to direction: the user intentionally supplies less than a complete description because the recipient is expected to complete it.

SOURCE TERMS:
underspecified
directors
make it pop
vague notion
filling in details

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The usual analysis treats missing instructions as evidence that the machine supplied the missing expression.

But what if leaving the detail unresolved was itself the human’s chosen operation?

QUESTION:
Can intentionally choosing not to determine an expressive feature contribute to authorship of the process that determines it?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Where is the boundary between authorship by delegation and abdication of authorship?

MECHANISM:
human selects high-level constraint
→ deliberately leaves variables open
→ responsive system fills variables
→ human evaluates completion

The gap is designed rather than accidental.

Variables of work:

Human determines subset H.

Question:

Does choosing
D = “delegate”
count as authorship of values eventually assigned to D?

TENSION:
[[BGS-1884-04]] showed that Sarony could “evoke” an expression rather than physically execute it.

But Wilde remained a human performer whose contribution exists within familiar structures of direction, performance, and possible authorship.

A generator makes the delegated detail without being legally available as a coauthor.

MISSING:
A theory of delegated creative discretion that does not collapse into either:

director owns everything

or

director authors only what was fully specified.

BOUNDARY:
Intentional under-specification can explain why a person chose a generative process. It does not by itself establish that the resulting unchosen particulars originated in that person.

TEST:
Create three versions of the same production:

A. human specifies every available expressive variable
B. human deliberately delegates named variables
C. human unknowingly omits those same variables

Hold outputs constant.

Ask whether intentional delegation changes the authorship analysis even though human causal control over the realized details is identical.
