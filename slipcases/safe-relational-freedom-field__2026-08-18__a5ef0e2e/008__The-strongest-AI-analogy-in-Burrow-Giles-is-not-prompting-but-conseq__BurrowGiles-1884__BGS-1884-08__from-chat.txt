TITLE:
The strongest AI analogy in Burrow-Giles is not prompting but consequential control

SOURCE:
U.S. Supreme Court — Burrow-Giles Lithographic Co. v. Sarony — 1884 — 111 U.S. 53, 59–61. fileciteturn0file1L71-L93

PASSAGE:
[OUR INFERENCE]
Burrow-Giles distinguishes an arguably mechanical image from Sarony’s authored image by recovering consequential acts of conception, selection, arrangement, direction, and production; its discussion of Nottage similarly asks who was the “effective cause” of the picture. fileciteturn0file1L71-L81 fileciteturn0file1L87-L93

RESEARCH OBJECT:
The useful comparison to generative systems is not “a prompt is like pressing a shutter” or “a prompt is like directing a sitter.” The case suggests a more discriminating variable: whether a person’s interventions demonstrably determine expressive features of the resulting artifact.

LOCAL MOVE:
[OUR INFERENCE]
Read together, the Sarony findings and Nottage discussion shift attention from the nominal interface used by a creator toward the causal efficacy of the creator’s operations.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
“Prompting” may be too coarse a unit for asking about authorship. Two prompts can be equally long, intentional, or elaborate while exercising radically different control over resulting expressive features.

QUESTION:
What is the smallest unit of human intervention that can be shown to have made a generated artifact specifically the artifact that resulted?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Should authorship analysis inspect commands, or transformations of the artifact’s possibility space?

MECHANISM:
A human intervention changes available or probable outputs.
The system responds.
The human inspects and intervenes again or accepts.
Some expressive alternatives disappear while others survive.
The eventual work accumulates a production history.

FORMAL SHIFT:

SOURCE FORMALISM:
NONE

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

For each intervention i:

S_i = available expressive states

human operation h_i:
S_i → S_i+1

Authorship question:
not merely “Was there a human prompt?”

but:
WHAT EXPRESSIVE DIFFERENCE DID h_i ACTUALLY MAKE?

TENSION:
Burrow-Giles involved a comparatively inspectable physical scene whose relevant variables Sarony could directly manipulate. Generative models can transform instructions through learned statistical machinery whose internal causal route is neither transparent nor equivalent to photographic optics.

MISSING:
Empirical and doctrinal measures of consequential control in systems where identical instructions may generate materially different outputs and small linguistic changes may have unpredictable effects.

BOUNDARY:
[OUR INFERENCE]
Burrow-Giles does not decide AI authorship, prompting, model outputs, or computational generation. Any transfer from the photographic case to generative AI must therefore be argued through a specific shared mechanism rather than asserted through technological resemblance.

CITATION TRAIL:
Burrow-Giles descendants concerning automated photography
Nottage v. Jackson
Cases concerning direction versus execution
Current administrative and judicial treatments of generative-AI authorship
Human-computer co-creation research measuring steerability and output variance

TEST:
For a generated image, preserve every prompt, seed, intermediate output, selection, edit, mask, crop, and regeneration. Then perform ablations: remove each human intervention and measure which expressive properties cease to persist. Compare this operational record against Sarony’s enumerated acts rather than comparing “AI” with “camera” as technologies.
