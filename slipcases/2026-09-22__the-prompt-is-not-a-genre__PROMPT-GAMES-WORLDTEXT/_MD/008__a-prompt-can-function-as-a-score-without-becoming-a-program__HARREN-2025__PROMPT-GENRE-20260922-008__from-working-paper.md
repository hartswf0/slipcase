TITLE:
A prompt can function as a score without becoming a program

SOURCE:
Natilee Harren, "George Brecht: Drip Music (Drip Event) (1959–62), from Water Yam (1963)," The Scores Project, Getty Research Institute, 2025.

SOURCE URL:
https://www.getty.edu/publications/scores/06/commentary/

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]\nBrecht's event scores preserve identity across materially different performances; open realization does not eliminate operational constraint.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
[OUR INFERENCE]\nOPERATIVE LANGUAGE CAN BE BINDING WITHOUT EXHAUSTIVELY DETERMINING THE RESULT.

LOCAL MOVE:
Use score-based art to block the reduction of prompts to programs.

SOURCE TERMS:
score; realization; invariance; latitude; performance

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
Variability can be intended rather than an execution error.

QUESTION:
What must remain invariant across admissible realizations of a prompt?

DEEPER QUESTION:
When does increased specificity improve control, and when does it destroy the productive latitude of the medium?

MECHANISM:
The score specifies constraints or relations while delegating other decisions to performer and situation.

FORMAL SHIFT:
VALID(y_i, S) can hold for many non-identical y_i.

SOURCE FORMALISM:
[PARAPHRASE]\nEvent scores support multiple realizations while retaining continuity as a work.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
PROGRAM asks for exact execution; SCORE asks for invariant relations across a family of executions.

TENSION:
Some prompt clauses are program-like while others are score-like inside the same request.

MISSING:
Clause-level classification of invariants versus deliberate latitude.

BOUNDARY:
Brecht is not writing about generative AI.

CITATION TRAIL:
Brecht -> open score -> prompt variability -> non-programmatic operation.

TEST:
Ask raters to identify intended invariants and latitude before generation, then evaluate outputs against both.

PLATFORM:
Fluxus; score; generative systems
