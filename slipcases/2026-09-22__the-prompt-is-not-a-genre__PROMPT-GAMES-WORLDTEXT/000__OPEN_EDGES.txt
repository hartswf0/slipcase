OPEN EDGES — THE PROMPT IS NOT A GENRE

1. GAME IDENTIFICATION
Can independent annotators identify instruction, score, program, plan, query, probe, gesture, commission, conversation, edit, constraint, and performance from full interaction traces?

2. TRACK CHANGE
What observable event marks a transition from one language game to another?

3. THICKNESS WITHOUT BLOAT
How can interfaces carry situational thickness through state, selection, gesture, schema, and persistence while preserving reconstructability?

4. SCORE / PROGRAM BOUNDARY
Which prompt clauses demand exact compliance and which intentionally preserve latitude?

5. WORLD RESISTANCE
Which substrates provide useful vetoes rather than merely another layer of model-generated plausibility?

6. WITNESS REPRESENTATIONS
When does an intermediate representation increase accountability, and when does it prematurely narrow discovery?

7. CORRESPONDENCE
How should partial, selective, score-like correspondence be evaluated without collapsing into one-to-one representation?

8. WORLDTEXT PERSISTENCE
What identity, relation, viewpoint, and history guarantees are minimally necessary for a generated environment to remain linguistically addressable?

9. AUTHORITY / EXECUTION
Who or what is entitled to execute a move when human, model, tool, schema, and environment each constrain different decisions?

10. RECONSTRUCTION
What must prompt archives preserve beyond the visible transcript to reproduce situated multimodal acts?
