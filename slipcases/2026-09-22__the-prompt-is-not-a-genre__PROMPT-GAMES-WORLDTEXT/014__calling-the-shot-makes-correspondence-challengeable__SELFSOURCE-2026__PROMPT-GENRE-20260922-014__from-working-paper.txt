TITLE:
Calling the shot makes correspondence challengeable

SOURCE:
Watson Hartsoe, The Prompt Is Not a Genre: Language Games, Operative Ekphrasis, and Worldtext, Working Paper, 22 September 2026.

SOURCE URL:
https://github.com/hartswf0/slipcase/blob/main/slipcases/2026-09-22__the-prompt-is-not-a-genre__PROMPT-GAMES-WORLDTEXT/the-prompt-is-not-a-genre__2026-09-22.pdf

PASSAGE:
[OUR SYNTHESIS]\nA prompt with no declared challengeable relations can always be accepted retrospectively after seeing the output.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
[OUR INFERENCE]\nCALLING THE SHOT TURNS GENERATION INTO A VISIBLE WAGER.

LOCAL MOVE:
Make pre-declared relations central to evaluation without demanding complete determinacy.

SOURCE TERMS:
called shot; correspondence; invariants; post hoc acceptance

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
Surprise and beauty can be useful while still providing almost no evidence that intended relations were controlled.

QUESTION:
Which relations were declared before generation and can therefore be falsified afterward?

DEEPER QUESTION:
How can open-ended scores preserve surprise while still making selected invariants testable?

MECHANISM:
Precommitment separates intended correspondence from retrospective interpretation.

FORMAL SHIFT:
DECLARE(R) before GENERATE(Y); then TEST(R,Y).

SOURCE FORMALISM:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

OUR FORMALIZATION:
Correspondence need not be one-to-one; only selected relations need be challengeable.

TENSION:
Over-specifying the called shot can turn exploratory generation into brittle drafting.

MISSING:
Metrics for partial correspondence that respect deliberate latitude.

BOUNDARY:
This is a methodological proposal from the working paper and project practice.

CITATION TRAIL:
score invariants + Operation Describe + WAG witness -> called shot.

TEST:
Record invariant declarations before generation and blind-score outputs against them.

PLATFORM:
Method; correspondence; evaluation
