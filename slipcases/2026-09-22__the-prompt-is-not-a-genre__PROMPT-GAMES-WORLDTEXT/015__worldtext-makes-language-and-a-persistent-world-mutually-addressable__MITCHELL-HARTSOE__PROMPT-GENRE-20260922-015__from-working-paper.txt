PROMPT-GENRE-20260922-015

TITLE:
Worldtext makes language and a persistent world mutually addressable

SOURCE:
W. J. T. Mitchell, Picture Theory: Essays on Verbal and Visual Representation, 1994. Extended by Hartsoe working paper.

SOURCE URL:
https://press.uchicago.edu/ucp/books/book/chicago/P/bo3683962.html

PASSAGE:
[OUR SYNTHESIS]\nImagetext names the entanglement of words and images; worldtext extends the problem to language coupled with a persistent, operable world that can be addressed again.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
[OUR INFERENCE]\nWORLDTEXT = LANGUAGE <-> PERSISTENT OPERABLE WORLD.

LOCAL MOVE:
Move beyond text-to-image and text-to-3D toward recursive address and revision.

SOURCE TERMS:
imagetext; worldtext; persistence; addressability; recursion

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
A generated scene becomes qualitatively different when later language can refer back to stable identities and relations inside it.

QUESTION:
What persistence is necessary for a generated environment to become a worldtext rather than a one-shot output?

DEEPER QUESTION:
How do identity, viewpoint, spatial relation, history, and state need to persist for language to remain referential?

MECHANISM:
The world preserves addressable entities and relations; later utterances modify them; resulting state feeds the next move.

FORMAL SHIFT:
TEXT_n -> WORLD_n -> TEXT_(n+1) -> WORLD_(n+1).

SOURCE FORMALISM:
[NO SOURCE FORMALISM]

OUR FORMALIZATION:
Worldtext is recursion across representational and operational states, not merely multimodality.

TENSION:
Persistence can constrain generative freedom and can harden accidental early choices.

MISSING:
Minimal persistence contract for worldtext systems.

BOUNDARY:
Worldtext is Hartsoe's proposed extension; Mitchell's imagetext does not imply this computational architecture.

CITATION TRAIL:
Mitchell imagetext -> operative ekphrasis -> persistent world -> worldtext.

TEST:
Attempt multi-turn references to entities, relations, and viewpoints after intervening edits; measure identity and relation preservation.

PLATFORM:
Worldtext; imagetext; spatial media
