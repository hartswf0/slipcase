TITLE:
PERSONALIZATION MAY BE THE ACTUATOR OF POPULATION POWER

SOURCE:
Moritz Hardt, Meena Jagadeesan, Celestine Mendler-Dünner — Performative Power — 2022 — §4.2, Proposition 5
TENSION SOURCE: Salomé Viljoen — A Relational Theory of Data Governance — 2021 — Introduction / Part II

SOURCE URL:
https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.17232
https://yalelawjournal.org/feature/a-relational-theory-of-data-governance

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
Hardt et al. show in their model that full personalization maximizes a firm's performative power.

Viljoen argues that commercial data collection derives much of its value from population-level relations that can subsequently be applied to individuals.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
Personalization and population-level computation are not opposites.

The individualized output may be the final actuator through which population knowledge acquires behavioral force.

LOCAL MOVE:
Hardt et al. move personalization from a service-quality feature into the machinery determining how strongly a firm can cause participants to adapt.

SOURCE TERMS:
personalization
performative power
population
relational insights
data relations
steering

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The interface can become more individual exactly as the underlying power becomes more relational.

QUESTION:
Is "personalization" misleading because it names the recipient of an intervention rather than the population machinery from which the intervention was derived?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Could the most intimate-looking computational systems also be the most population-dependent systems?

MECHANISM:
<many people's conduct>
→ population-level regularities
→ prediction about one person
→ personalized intervention
→ behavioral adaptation
→ new population data
→ revised regularities

FORMAL SHIFT:
<POPULATION DATA>
→ <PERSON-SPECIFIC DECISION FUNCTION>
→ [PERSONALIZED INTERVENTION]
→ <POPULATION DISPLACEMENT>

SOURCE FORMALISM:
Hardt et al.'s Proposition 5 establishes that, in the specified strategic-classification setting, full personalization reaches the upper bound on performative power.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

RELATIONAL MODEL
→ INDIVIDUAL ADDRESS
→ BEHAVIORAL RESPONSE
→ RELATIONAL MODEL'

The apparent individualization of the interface closes a population feedback loop.

TENSION:
Privacy discourse often treats personalization as an individualized transaction.

Hardt's formal result and Viljoen's relational account suggest another reading:
individual address can be the delivery mechanism of supraindividual power.

MISSING:
A distinction between:
personalization of output,
individualization of knowledge,
and individualization of power.

These are routinely collapsed.

BOUNDARY:
Hardt et al.'s maximization result belongs to a specified theoretical model and does not establish that every real-world personalization system maximizes platform power.

Viljoen does not formulate personalization as performative power.

CITATION TRAIL:
Strategic classification.
Performative prediction.
Behavioral advertising.
Population inference.
Data externalities and relational privacy.

TEST:
Hold the predictive model and available content fixed.

Compare:
A. one common decision policy for all users;
B. segmented policies;
C. fully personalized policies.

Measure not only predictive accuracy but how much participant behavior itself moves under each regime.
