TITLE:
CULTURAL TRANSMISSION CAN MAKE A LANGUAGE BETTER FOR THE MODEL AND WORSE AS LANGUAGE

SOURCE:
Tom Kouwenhoven, Max Peeperkorn, and Tessa Verhoef — Searching for Structure: Investigating Emergent Communication with Large Language Models — 2024 — §6 Iterated Learning

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
Across generational transmission chains, the artificial languages become substantially easier for LLM agents to learn.

But this improvement does not produce a corresponding increase in communicative success.

Signals become significantly longer and more ambiguous, and some vocabularies collapse toward repeated signals for different meanings.

The authors describe the resulting systems as optimized for LLM preferences but sometimes non-humanlike and degenerate.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
Transmission does not optimize culture in the abstract.

It optimizes culture for the transmission machinery.

LOCAL MOVE:
The source replaces human learners in an iterated-learning paradigm with LLM learners and observes which structures repeated machine transmission preferentially preserves and amplifies.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
A language can improve according to the learner's own loss landscape while becoming longer, more ambiguous, and less expressive by another standard.

QUESTION:
What kinds of language will become evolutionarily fit when human cultural material repeatedly passes through LLM summarization, generation, retrieval, rewriting, and imitation?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Are we beginning to produce cultural forms selected not primarily for human memory or human communication but for machine learnability?

MECHANISM:
<LANGUAGE GENERATION t>
→ sampled training subset
→ learned by LLM generation t+1
→ reproduction according to LLM inductive biases
→ selection of transmitted vocabulary
→ repeated generations
→ amplification of model-favored properties

FORMAL SHIFT:
<HUMAN-DESIGNED SIGNAL SYSTEM>

→ [REPEATED LEARNING + REPRODUCTION]
→ <MODEL-ADAPTED CULTURAL SYSTEM>

SOURCE FORMALISM:
The experiment runs six transmission chains of eight generations.

Each later generation learns a portion of the previous generation's produced signal-meaning mappings.

The vocabulary of the agent with the highest TopSim is transmitted forward.

The authors measure learnability, communicative success, TopSim, N-gram diversity, and uniqueness of signals.

TENSION:
[[FORAGE-WINFIELD-BLACKMORE-001]] made forgetting look potentially stabilizing.

[[FORAGE-KIRBY-001]] makes successful structure depend on a compression-expressivity trade-off.

Kouwenhoven et al. add a more disturbing possibility:

change the learner and the attractor changes.

An LLM transmission bottleneck need not reproduce the cultural pressures generated by human memory limitations.

MISSING:
Experiments involving mixed transmission chains:

human → human,
human → LLM,
LLM → human,
LLM → LLM.

Without them, it is unclear whether model-mediated cultural selection is amplified, corrected, or rejected when artifacts return to human use.

BOUNDARY:
The study uses artificial languages, a referential game, particular prompting procedures, and Llama 3 70B.

Its observed degeneracy is not evidence that natural languages or all LLM-mediated culture will undergo the same transformation.

Search specifically for traits that monotonically increase only in machine-dominated lineages.
