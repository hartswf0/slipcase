ZETTEL

ID:
FORAGE-KIRBY-001

TITLE:
FORGETTING DOES NOT PRODUCE STRUCTURE UNLESS SOMETHING ALSO PUNISHES THE LOSS OF DIFFERENCE

SOURCE:
Simon Kirby, Monica Tamariz, Hannah Cornish, and Kenny Smith — Compression and Communication in the Cultural Evolution of Linguistic Structure — 2015 — Abstract / experiments on learning and communication

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
Kirby and colleagues find that linguistic structure emerges from a trade-off between two pressures rather than from transmission bottlenecks alone:

compressibility imposed by learning
and
expressivity imposed by communication.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
A bottleneck can select for what is easy to transmit without selecting for what remains useful to distinguish.

Compression needs an opposing pressure.

LOCAL MOVE:
The source splits "cultural evolution toward structure" into two competing selection pressures that act on the transmitted system.

SOURCE TERMS:
compressibility
expressivity
learning
communication
cultural transmission
structured behaviour
iterated learning

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
Forgetting can stabilize the wrong thing.

A perfectly stable cultural system could preserve almost nothing by collapsing many meanings into the same reusable form.

QUESTION:
What distinguishes productive forgetting from degenerative forgetting?

DEEPER QUESTION:
When an artificial culture is compressed through bounded memory, what mechanism decides which differences must survive the compression?

MECHANISM:
learning pressure
→ favors compressible / learnable systems

communication pressure
→ penalizes systems that erase distinctions needed for successful interaction

their competition
→ can favor structured systems that are both learnable and expressive

FORMAL SHIFT:
<CULTURAL VARIATION>
→ <LEARNING BOTTLENECK + COMMUNICATIVE DEMAND>
→ [COMPETING SELECTION PRESSURES]
→ <COMPRESSIBLE BUT DISTINCTION-PRESERVING STRUCTURE>

SOURCE FORMALISM:
The source's central explanatory structure is a trade-off between:

compressibility,
imposed during learning,

and:

expressivity,
imposed during communication.

No source equation is required for this zettel.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

Let:

C = pressure toward compression
E = pressure toward preserving communicatively necessary distinctions

Then cultural stability alone cannot distinguish:

stable structure
from
stable collapse.

A useful evaluation requires at least:

STABILITY × EXPRESSIVITY.

TENSION:
[[FORAGE-WINFIELD-BLACKMORE-001]] found that limited memory produced the most stable behavioral populations in Copybots.

Kirby et al. make "stability" insufficient as a success criterion.

A system may become easier to transmit precisely by erasing distinctions.

The problem is therefore no longer:

How much should a culture forget?

It becomes:

Which distinctions are costly enough to remember anyway?

MISSING:
A measure of cultural persistence that separates:

retention of form,
retention of distinction,
retention of function,
and retention of historical lineage.

BOUNDARY:
Kirby et al. study the cultural evolution of artificial communication systems under specific experimental and simulated conditions.

Their results do not show that every finite-memory system requires the same compressibility-expressivity trade-off.

CITATION TRAIL:
[[FORAGE-WINFIELD-BLACKMORE-001]]

TEST:
Cross two experimental variables in an artificial agent culture:

MEMORY:
large / bounded

COMMUNICATIVE PRESSURE:
none / strong requirement to discriminate meanings

Measure separately:

behavioral stability,
number of preserved distinctions,
communication success,
compressibility,
lineage diversity.

Determine whether bounded memory still looks beneficial when "stable collapse" is counted as failure.
