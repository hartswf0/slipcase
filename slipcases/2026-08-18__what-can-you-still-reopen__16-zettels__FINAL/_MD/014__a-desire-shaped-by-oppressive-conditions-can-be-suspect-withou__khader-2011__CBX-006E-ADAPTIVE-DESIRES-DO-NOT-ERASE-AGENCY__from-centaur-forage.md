ID:
CBX-006E-ADAPTIVE-DESIRES-DO-NOT-ERASE-AGENCY

TITLE:
A desire shaped by oppressive conditions can be suspect without making its bearer cease to be an agent.

SOURCE:
Serene J. Khader — Adaptive Preferences and Women’s Empowerment — 2011 — Introduction, “Adaptive Preferences and Global Justice.”

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
Khader treats adaptive preferences as self-depriving desires shaped under unjust social conditions, but rejects the choice between simply accepting those preferences as authoritative and overriding the person who holds them. People with adaptive preferences can remain reflective agents and meaningful sources of decisions even while some of their preferences contribute to their deprivation.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
Preference deformation and agency are not mutually exclusive states.

LOCAL MOVE:
Khader separates criticism of a preference from erasure of the person whose preference is criticized.

SOURCE TERMS:
adaptive preferences
deprivation
flourishing
active choosers
reflective agents
intervention
deliberative perfectionism
empowerment

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
If autonomy becomes a provenance test, a system could classify a person whose preferences were shaped by domination as having “low authorship” and thereby justify overriding her.

The protection against manipulation could become another mechanism for denying agency.

QUESTION:
How can a freedom model represent deformation of preferences without converting questionable preference formation into diminished standing for the person?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Who is authorized to decide that another person’s desire is insufficiently theirs?

MECHANISM:
unjust conditions
→ constrained opportunity
→ adaptive preference formation
→ preference partly perpetuates deprivation.

But simultaneously:

person
→ reflection
→ deliberation
→ meaningful agency.

These processes coexist.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

Separate:

AUTONOMY_OF_AGENT ≠ QUALITY_OR_ORIGIN_OF_EACH_PREFERENCE

and:

SUSPECT(D) ≠ DISQUALIFY(AGENT)

A model therefore needs at least two axes:

agent deliberative standing
×
preference-formation conditions.

TENSION:
The parent wants to discount freedom when desires are engineered. Khader shows why a provenance penalty applied directly to the agent can reproduce paternalism.

MISSING:
A formal separation between:
the standing of the chooser,
the provenance of a particular desire,
the consequences of satisfying it,
and the legitimacy of intervention.

BOUNDARY:
Khader’s adaptive-preference analysis concerns deprivation and unjust social conditions, not AI-generated persuasion specifically. It supplies a counterexample to the assumption that compromised preference formation straightforwardly cancels agency.

TEST:

Test whether the proposed freedom model can criticize the formation process without automatically reducing the person’s authority to participate in changing it.
