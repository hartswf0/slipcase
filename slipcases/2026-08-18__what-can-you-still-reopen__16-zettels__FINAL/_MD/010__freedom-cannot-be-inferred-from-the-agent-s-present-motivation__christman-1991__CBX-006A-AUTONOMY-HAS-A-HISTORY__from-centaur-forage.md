ZETTEL

ID:
CBX-006A-AUTONOMY-HAS-A-HISTORY

TITLE:
Freedom cannot be inferred from the agent’s present motivational state.

SOURCE:
John Christman — “Autonomy and Personal History” — 1991 — Canadian Journal of Philosophy 21(1): 1–24.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
Christman argues that a coherent and integrated set of desires can itself be the result of extensive external manipulation. Extending coherence across an entire life does not solve the problem: an entire life can be coherently manipulated.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
Autonomy is diachronic. A present state containing desire, reflection, coherence, and successful action does not contain enough information to determine whether the state is autonomous.

LOCAL MOVE:
Christman attacks synchronic and integration accounts by constructing cases in which every presently observable sign of internal coherence survives manipulation.

SOURCE TERMS:
autonomy
personal history
self-government
preferences
values
integration
manipulation
conditioning
endogeneity of preferences

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The missing variable in SAFF may not be another property of the agent at time t. It may be a history.

QUESTION:
What minimum history must be represented before “acting according to one’s desires” can count as self-government?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Can autonomy be a state variable at all, or is it necessarily a property of a trajectory by which an agent became able to want, revise, reject, and act?

MECHANISM:
Influence history
→ preference formation
→ later reflection and integration
→ present coherent desire
→ action.

Two agents can therefore occupy the same present motivational state after different developmental trajectories.

FORMAL SHIFT:
<CURRENT AGENT STATE>
→ <TRAJECTORY OF PREFERENCE FORMATION>
→ [EVALUATE HISTORY, NOT ONLY END STATE]
→ <AUTONOMY JUDGMENT>

SOURCE FORMALISM:
NONE

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

Let X_t contain the present variables used by SAFF.

SAFF = F(X_t)

Christman’s problem requires:

AUTONOMY = G(X_0 → X_1 → ... → X_t)

such that:

X_t^A = X_t^B

does not imply:

AUTONOMY(A) = AUTONOMY(B)

when their formation histories differ.

TENSION:
A historical condition solves the parent’s immediate blindness to preference formation, but Christman also rejects the idea that merely being externally caused makes a preference non-autonomous. History matters without providing a simple contamination test.

MISSING:
The relevant feature of the history.

Is it:
awareness,
resistance,
revision capacity,
absence of coercion,
participation in formation,
counterfactual rejection,
or something else?

BOUNDARY:
Christman does not license the claim that externally influenced desires are therefore non-autonomous. His argument blocks a purely present-state account, not social influence itself.

CITATION TRAIL:
[[CBX-006-WHO-WROTE-THE-DESIRE]]
→ Christman, “Autonomy and Personal History”
→ preference endogeneity
→ historical theories of autonomy
→ what feature of a preference-formation history makes it self-governed?

TEST:
Construct two agents with identical present desires, second-order endorsements, reflective capacities, actions, and outcomes but different acquisition histories.

Then test candidate autonomy criteria one at a time to determine which can distinguish manipulation without classifying ordinary social formation as non-autonomous.
