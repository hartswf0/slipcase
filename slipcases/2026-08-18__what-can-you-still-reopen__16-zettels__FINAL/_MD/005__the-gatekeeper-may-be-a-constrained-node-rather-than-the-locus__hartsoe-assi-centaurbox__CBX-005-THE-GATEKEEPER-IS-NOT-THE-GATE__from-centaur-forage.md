ZETTEL

ID:
CBX-005-THE-GATEKEEPER-IS-NOT-THE-GATE

TITLE:
The gatekeeper may be a constrained node rather than the locus of the decision.

SOURCE:
Hartsoe & Assi — Thinking Outside the AI Box: The Centaur Box Experiment — supplied manuscript — “Level Check: Constraints and Affordances in Governance.”

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
The manuscript explicitly states that gatekeepers face organizational strategies, legal frameworks, market pressures, professional norms, different hierarchical ranks, and expert roles.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
Institutional feasible-action sets may dominate personal psychographic characteristics.

LOCAL MOVE:
The source begins with the worldview of the gatekeeper but then introduces forces capable of preventing that worldview from becoming action.

SOURCE TERMS:
gatekeepers
organizational policies
legal regulations
market pressures
professional norms
hierarchical ranks
maneuverability
leverage

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
Once those constraints are admitted, “gatekeeper” ceases to name an autonomous person. It may name an interface onto a larger approval machine.

QUESTION:
How much of a governance decision is attributable to the gatekeeper profile after institutional constraints are held constant?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Is targeting the psychology of a gatekeeper a category error when the effective decision-maker is a distributed organization?

MECHANISM:
Institutional constraints
+ role authority
+ veto structure
→ feasible action set
→ individual judgment inside that set
→ governance outcome.

FORMAL SHIFT:
<ORGANIZATION + ROLE + RULES>
→ <FEASIBLE ACTION SET>
→ [PERSON CHOOSES WITHIN SET]
→ <GOVERNANCE OUTCOME>

SOURCE FORMALISM:
NONE

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

Let F(C,R,V) be the actions feasible under constraints C, role authority R, and veto structure V.

Decision = argmax preference(x), x ∈ F(C,R,V)

If the desired action ∉ F, arbitrarily strong person-level persuasion has no direct effect unless it first changes C, R, or V.

TENSION:
The manuscript acknowledges system-level forces yet places substantial analytical weight on modeling individual worldviews, motivations, and biases.

MISSING:
A variance decomposition between person effects, role effects, organizational effects, and interaction effects.

BOUNDARY:
The source does not claim individual psychology is irrelevant. It provides enough institutional machinery to make individual sufficiency questionable.

CITATION TRAIL:
Organizational decision-making.
Street-level bureaucracy and bounded discretion.
Institutional veto-point research.
Sociotechnical accounts of AI governance.

TEST:
Hold one synthetic personality constant across several institutional constraint sets; then hold the institution constant while swapping personalities. Compare which manipulation changes simulated outcomes more.
