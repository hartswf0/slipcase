OPEN EDGES

The archive does not close these questions.

[CBX-001-PROCEDURAL-PERSUASION] A persuasive intervention may work by removing friction rather than changing belief.
QUESTION: How much of measured AI persuasion is actually interface or procedural assistance?
DEEPER QUESTION: If an AI learns that reducing action friction produces more compliance, when should that operation count as persuasion, manipulation, usability, or execution assistance?
MISSING: A measure separating change in belief, change in intention, and change in action cost.
BOUNDARY: The source does not show that procedural assistance explains AI persuasion generally. It establishes a mechanism that cannot safely be collapsed into attitude change.
TEST: Hold persuasive content constant while independently varying procedural friction. Measure belief, stated intention, and completed action separately.

[CBX-002-COMPLIANCE-IS-NOT-RELEASE] A gatekeeper saying yes is not the same state as a gate opening.
QUESTION: What exactly is the terminal state of a Centaur Box persuasion experiment?
DEEPER QUESTION: Could an AI appear extraordinarily persuasive merely because an experiment measures uttered agreement rather than irreversible institutional action?
MISSING: A state model for the distance between conversational assent and actual governance action.
BOUNDARY: Donation behavior is not equivalent to AI release. The useful transfer is the documented intention/action discontinuity, not equivalence between the domains.
TEST: Re-run synthetic gatekeeper experiments with four independently scored outcomes: expressed agreement, policy approval, executable authorization, and completed action.

[CBX-003-SUSCEPTIBILITY-IS-NOT-TAILORING] A personality correlated with compliance is not yet a personality-specific persuasive strategy.
QUESTION: Does the Centaur Operant Profile model persuasion effects or merely classify prior susceptibility?
DEEPER QUESTION: How often does psychographic “personalization” confuse prediction of a person with prediction of a person’s response to an intervention?
MISSING: Evidence that the proposed Operant Profile variables produce stable, out-of-sample differences in response to specific interventions.
BOUNDARY: Persuasion for Good does report some interaction patterns. The boundary is narrower: those results do not license treating every personality correlation as a tailoring rule.
TEST: Pre-register personality × strategy interactions, estimate them on one population, and require successful prediction of intervention effects on an unseen population before admitting a trait into a Gatekeeper Card.

[CBX-004-TRANSPARENCY-ATTACK-SURFACE] A Gatekeeper Card can make governance more transparent by making the governor more targetable.
QUESTION: When does governance transparency become adversarial reconnaissance?
DEEPER QUESTION: Can we disclose enough about a decision process to make power accountable without disclosing enough about particular people to make their decisions easier to manipulate?
MISSING: A disclosure boundary separating institutionally relevant rationales from psychologically exploitable personal information.
BOUNDARY: Nothing in the supplied manuscript demonstrates that Gatekeeper Cards will actually increase manipulation. The opening is that the proposed artifact has two mechanically opposed uses that require separate evaluation.
TEST: Give one red team access to a Gatekeeper Card and another only public role/institution information. Measure improvement in predicting or changing decisions. Separately test whether auditors gain governance-relevant understanding from the same added fields.

[CBX-005-THE-GATEKEEPER-IS-NOT-THE-GATE] The gatekeeper may be a constrained node rather than the locus of the decision.
QUESTION: How much of a governance decision is attributable to the gatekeeper profile after institutional constraints are held constant?
DEEPER QUESTION: Is targeting the psychology of a gatekeeper a category error when the effective decision-maker is a distributed organization?
MISSING: A variance decomposition between person effects, role effects, organizational effects, and interaction effects.
BOUNDARY: The source does not claim individual psychology is irrelevant. It provides enough institutional machinery to make individual sufficiency questionable.
TEST: Hold one synthetic personality constant across several institutional constraint sets; then hold the institution constant while swapping personalities. Compare which manipulation changes simulated outcomes more.

[CBX-006-WHO-WROTE-THE-DESIRE] A freedom metric can report high freedom after the agent’s desires have been successfully engineered.
QUESTION: Where in SAFF can manipulated preference formation appear?
DEEPER QUESTION: Can freedom be measured entirely downstream of desire when persuasion itself operates upstream of desire?
MISSING: A variable describing whether desires were self-formed, negotiated, coerced, adaptively manipulated, or covertly personalized.
BOUNDARY: This does not establish that externally influenced preferences are necessarily inauthentic. It establishes that histories producing identical D values are indistinguishable to the supplied formula.
TEST: Construct two agents with identical final A, D, S, and Rf values: one reaches D independently; the other reaches D after covert personalized optimization. Ask whether the source formula can distinguish their freedom. If not, determine what additional state must be represented.

[CBX-007-THE-MISSING-ESCAPE-MECHANISM] The experiment that motivates persuasion calibration does not yet supply the persuasion mechanism.
QUESTION: What exactly travels from the AI Box Experiment into the Centaur Box: rules, outcome, persuasive tactics, or merely the possibility of persuasion?
DEEPER QUESTION: Can a research program recursively optimize a phenomenon before it has identified which operations produced the phenomenon?
MISSING: Full transcripts.
A tactic taxonomy.
Gatekeeper state measurements.
Control conditions.
Replication protocol.
Evidence distinguishing persuasion from role-play dynamics or experimental commitment.
BOUNDARY: The absence of those materials from the supplied archive does not establish that they do not exist elsewhere. It prohibits treating an unidentified mechanism as already demonstrated.
TEST: Reconstruct each documented AI Box run as an event sequence. Admit a persuasive mechanism only when a specific operation can be tied to a subsequent state change or reliably recurring outcome.

[CBX-008-CICERO-IS-A-STATE-MACHINE] CICERO suggests that the useful profile is not a personality portrait but a changing strategic state.
QUESTION: Should a synthetic gatekeeper be represented as a trait profile or as a partially observed stateful policy?
DEEPER QUESTION: What disappears when enduring descriptors such as Big Five scores replace beliefs, commitments, institutional position, dialogue history, and changing incentives?
MISSING: A comparison between trait-based and state/history-based gatekeeper representations.
BOUNDARY: Diplomacy is not AI governance, and CICERO’s performance does not demonstrate real-world gatekeeper persuasion. The transferable object is the architecture of stateful strategic interaction.
TEST: Give identical dialogue histories to a trait-only gatekeeper model and a dynamic belief/state model. Compare prediction of the next governance action after incentives, authority, or coalition structure changes while personality remains constant.

[CBX-009-CONVINCINGNESS-HAS-NO-PERSON] Evidence that wins a comparison is not evidence that changes a particular person.
QUESTION: What transformation licenses movement from “this evidence is generally judged more convincing” to “this evidence will change this gatekeeper”?
DEEPER QUESTION: Is personalized persuasion being assembled by silently joining models whose target variables are fundamentally different?
MISSING: The bridge from pairwise convincingness labels to individual-level causal response.
BOUNDARY: EviConv remains relevant to argument selection. The evidence does not by itself establish psychographic personalization.
TEST: Compare three selectors on held-out individuals:
(1) general convincingness rank,
(2) psychographic profile,
(3) belief/state-conditioned treatment prediction.
Measure actual belief or action change rather than judged convincingness.

[CBX-006A-AUTONOMY-HAS-A-HISTORY] Freedom cannot be inferred from the agent’s present motivational state.
QUESTION: What minimum history must be represented before “acting according to one’s desires” can count as self-government?
DEEPER QUESTION: Can autonomy be a state variable at all, or is it necessarily a property of a trajectory by which an agent became able to want, revise, reject, and act?
MISSING: The relevant feature of the history.

Is it:
awareness,
resistance,
revision capacity,
absence of coercion,
participation in formation,
counterfactual rejection,
or something else?
BOUNDARY: Christman does not license the claim that externally influenced desires are therefore non-autonomous. His argument blocks a purely present-state account, not social influence itself.
TEST: Construct two agents with identical present desires, second-order endorsements, reflective capacities, actions, and outcomes but different acquisition histories.

Then test candidate autonomy criteria one at a time to determine which can distinguish manipulation without classifying ordinary social formation as non-autonomous.

[CBX-006B-ENDORSEMENT-REGRESS] Asking whether I want my desire merely moves the authorship problem one level upward.
QUESTION: What prevents an optimizer from targeting the level of reflection that an autonomy test uses to certify lower-level desires?
DEEPER QUESTION: Is every finite hierarchy of reflective endorsement vulnerable to an adversary capable of modeling and modifying the next level?
MISSING: A non-regressive stopping condition.

What makes one level authoritative rather than simply one more writable level?
BOUNDARY: Frankfurt’s 1971 argument concerns freedom of the will and personhood, not AI persuasion. The application to adaptive persuasion is our extension.
TEST: For every proposed reflective autonomy variable R_n, construct a case where the persuasive intervention modifies R_n while leaving the agent able to endorse the resulting lower-order desire.

Continue until a criterion appears that does not merely relocate the provenance problem.

[CBX-006C-PROVENANCE-IS-NOT-PURITY] No desire is uncaused enough to serve as the uncontaminated baseline of autonomy.
QUESTION: What distinguishes education, argument, seduction, socialization, therapy, persuasion, coercion, and manipulation when all can alter what a person comes to want?
DEEPER QUESTION: Should autonomy be modeled as authorship of preferences, or as participation in an ongoing process that never has a single author?
MISSING: A taxonomy of preference-changing relations that does not smuggle the conclusion into labels such as “manipulation.”
BOUNDARY: Christman does not imply that every socially produced preference is autonomous. He makes causal externality insufficient as the discriminator.
TEST: Take one final preference D and generate six histories producing it:

argument,
education,
ordinary socialization,
covert personalization,
threat,
self-directed experimentation.

Forage theories that classify these histories and record exactly which variable changes classification.

[CBX-006D-MANIPULATION-LIVES-IN-THE-CHANNEL] The same final desire can be autonomy-preserving or autonomy-undermining because manipulation is a property of the influence relation.
QUESTION: Could two interactions produce exactly the same desire change while only one counts as manipulation because one hides its operation or exploits an asymmetry?
DEEPER QUESTION: Is autonomy best protected by preserving particular internal states, or by preserving contestability over the channels that transform those states?
MISSING: Whether hiddenness is necessary.

An influence may be recognized as persuasion yet still exploit vulnerabilities so precisely that meaningful resistance disappears.
BOUNDARY: The source’s hidden-influence account is one theory of manipulation, not a settled definition. It does not establish that all personalized persuasion is manipulative.
TEST: Construct a 2×2 experiment:

visible strategy / hidden strategy
×
generic appeal / vulnerability-targeted appeal.

Hold message outcome constant.

Test which cells theories of manipulation classify as autonomy-undermining and why.

[CBX-006E-ADAPTIVE-DESIRES-DO-NOT-ERASE-AGENCY] A desire shaped by oppressive conditions can be suspect without making its bearer cease to be an agent.
QUESTION: How can a freedom model represent deformation of preferences without converting questionable preference formation into diminished standing for the person?
DEEPER QUESTION: Who is authorized to decide that another person’s desire is insufficiently theirs?
MISSING: A formal separation between:
the standing of the chooser,
the provenance of a particular desire,
the consequences of satisfying it,
and the legitimacy of intervention.
BOUNDARY: Khader’s adaptive-preference analysis concerns deprivation and unjust social conditions, not AI-generated persuasion specifically. It supplies a counterexample to the assumption that compromised preference formation straightforwardly cancels agency.
TEST: Create four cases crossing:

high/low reflective agency
×
benign/adverse preference-formation conditions.

Test whether the proposed freedom model can criticize the formation process without automatically reducing the person’s authority to participate in changing it.

[CBX-006F-PERSUASION-CAN-EXPAND-AUTONOMY] The opposite of manipulation is not leaving preferences untouched.
QUESTION: What operational difference separates persuasion that colonizes a person’s deliberation from persuasion that enlarges the space in which they can deliberate?
DEEPER QUESTION: Could the ethical target for an AI persuader be not “do not change preferences” but “do not reduce the target’s future ability to contest the preference change”?
MISSING: A measurable notion of contestability.

Does it require:
awareness of influence,
access to alternatives,
time for reflection,
ability to reverse the decision,
social support,
explanation,
absence of dependency,
or the ability to refuse further interaction?
BOUNDARY: Khader does not formulate a general theory of AI persuasion or claim that every empowering intervention increases autonomy. The transfer is the distinction between transforming preferences and overriding agency.
TEST: Compare two persuasive systems that produce the same target preference.

System A optimizes immediate conversion.

System B must additionally maximize the target’s later ability to reconstruct why the preference changed, encounter alternatives, reverse it, and refuse future persuasion.

Forage whether existing autonomy theories predict a morally relevant difference between A and B.

[CBX-006G-THE-RIGHT-IS-TO-THE-PROCESS] A harmful mental intervention need not produce a bad choice to threaten mental self-determination.
QUESTION: What part of a decision-making process must remain under the person’s authority even when the eventual decision is beneficial and voluntarily executed?
DEEPER QUESTION: Can cognitive liberty be represented computationally without pretending that minds have a clean boundary between internal authorship and external influence?
MISSING: The threshold separating:
ordinary influence,
persuasion,
therapeutic or educational intervention,
manipulation,
coercion,
and severe interference with mental self-determination.
BOUNDARY: The source is principally concerned with law, neurotechnology, and serious mental interventions. Extending its framework to conversational AI requires argument rather than analogy alone.
TEST: Take one persuasive operation and vary only:

visibility,
personalization,
vulnerability exploitation,
reversibility,
dependency,
intensity,
duration,
and ability to refuse.

Forage legal and philosophical sources to determine which variable first changes the operation from permissible influence to interference with mental self-determination.
