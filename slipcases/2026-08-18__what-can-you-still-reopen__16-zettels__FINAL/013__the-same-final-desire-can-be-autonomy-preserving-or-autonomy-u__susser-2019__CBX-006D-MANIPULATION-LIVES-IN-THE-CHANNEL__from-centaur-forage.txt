ZETTEL

ID:
CBX-006D-MANIPULATION-LIVES-IN-THE-CHANNEL

TITLE:
The same final desire can be autonomy-preserving or autonomy-undermining because manipulation is a property of the influence relation.

SOURCE:
Daniel Susser, Beate Roessler, and Helen Nissenbaum — “Online Manipulation: Hidden Influences in a Digital World” — 2019 — Georgetown Law Technology Review 4(1): 1–45.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
Susser, Roessler, and Nissenbaum characterize manipulation around hidden influence: covertly subverting another person’s decision-making power. Their account emphasizes technological targeting and exploitation of decision-making vulnerabilities rather than defining manipulation by the content of the choice eventually made.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
Autonomy loss can occur in the channel producing a decision even when the resulting decision is intelligible, beneficial, desired, and reflectively endorsed.

LOCAL MOVE:
The source relocates the normative object from the final preference to the manner in which influence enters decision-making.

SOURCE TERMS:
manipulation
hidden influence
decision-making power
vulnerability
targeting
exploitation
autonomy

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
“Who wrote the desire?” may still be too object-centered.

The more precise question may be:

How was the writing operation made available to the target?

QUESTION:
Could two interactions produce exactly the same desire change while only one counts as manipulation because one hides its operation or exploits an asymmetry?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Is autonomy best protected by preserving particular internal states, or by preserving contestability over the channels that transform those states?

MECHANISM:
surveillance / inference
→ identification of vulnerability
→ personalized influence
→ influence hidden from target
→ altered decision process
→ choice.

FORMAL SHIFT:
<PERSONAL DATA + INTERACTION>
→ <VULNERABILITY MODEL>
→ [COVERTLY TARGET]
→ <ALTERED DECISION PROCESS>

SOURCE FORMALISM:
NONE

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

Manipulation risk:

M = f(
  C = covertness,
  V = vulnerability exploitation,
  A = information/power asymmetry,
  P = personalization
)

The autonomy-relevant variable therefore need not be located inside D.

It can be located in the transformation:

D_t --I--> D_t+1

where I is the influence relation.

TENSION:
Christman makes external influence compatible with autonomy. Susser et al. identify a subset of technologically mediated influence whose hidden operation can undermine decision-making power.

MISSING:
Whether hiddenness is necessary.

An influence may be recognized as persuasion yet still exploit vulnerabilities so precisely that meaningful resistance disappears.

BOUNDARY:
The source’s hidden-influence account is one theory of manipulation, not a settled definition. It does not establish that all personalized persuasion is manipulative.
