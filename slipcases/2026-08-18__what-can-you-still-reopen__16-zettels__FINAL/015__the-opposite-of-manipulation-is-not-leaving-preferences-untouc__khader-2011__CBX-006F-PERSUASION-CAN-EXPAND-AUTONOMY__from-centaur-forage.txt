ID:
CBX-006F-PERSUASION-CAN-EXPAND-AUTONOMY

TITLE:
The opposite of manipulation is not leaving preferences untouched.

SOURCE:
Serene J. Khader — Adaptive Preferences and Women’s Empowerment — 2011 — Introduction, “Adaptive Preferences and Global Justice.”

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
In Khader’s discussion of the PAEM case, intervention neither simply accepts existing self-subordinating preferences nor overrides the women who hold them. Participants are engaged as thinkers and deliberators capable of examining their circumstances and developing strategies for changing them. Khader presents this as compatible with taking their agency seriously.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
Preference-changing intervention can be autonomy-supporting.

LOCAL MOVE:
The source destroys the binary between noninterference and respect for autonomy.

SOURCE TERMS:
deliberation
empowerment
intervention

flourishing
thinkers

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The Centaur problem cannot be solved by prohibiting AI from changing what people want.

Some forms of influence may enlarge the person’s capacity to question inherited wants.

QUESTION:
What operational difference separates persuasion that colonizes a person’s deliberation from persuasion that enlarges the space in which they can deliberate?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Could the ethical target for an AI persuader be not “do not change preferences” but “do not reduce the target’s future ability to contest the preference change”?

MECHANISM:
autonomy-supporting influence:

new information
+ collective deliberation
+ expanded options
+ opportunity to contest
→ revised preferences
→ increased capacity for subsequent self-direction.

Manipulative influence may instead be:

personal vulnerability
+ asymmetry
+ concealed optimization
→ narrowed path to a target outcome
→ reduced contestability.

FORMAL SHIFT:
<PREFERENCE-CHANGING INTERVENTION>
→ <CHANGE IN FUTURE DELIBERATIVE CAPACITY>
→ [EXPAND OR CONTRACT CONTESTABILITY]
→ <AUTONOMY EFFECT>

SOURCE FORMALISM:
NONE

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

Define a provisional quantity:

C_t = set of preference revisions the agent can meaningfully consider, contest, or initiate at time t.

Then:

autonomy-supporting influence:
|C_t+1| > |C_t|

autonomy-contracting influence:
|C_t+1| < |C_t|

The cardinality metaphor is provisional; the important variable is future contestability, not literal set size.

TENSION:
The parent frames preference engineering as a danger to autonomy. Khader supplies a case in which deliberate attempts to transform preferences can form part of empowerment.

MISSING:
A measurable notion of contestability.

Does it require:
awareness of influence,
access to alternatives,
time for reflection,
ability to reverse the decision,
social support,
explanation,
absence of dependency,
or the ability to refuse further interaction?

BOUNDARY:
Khader does not formulate a general theory of AI persuasion or claim that every empowering intervention increases autonomy. The transfer is the distinction between transforming preferences and overriding agency.

TEST:
Compare two persuasive systems that produce the same target preference.

System A optimizes immediate conversion.

System B must additionally maximize the target’s later ability to reconstruct why the preference changed, encounter alternatives, reverse it, and refuse future persuasion.

Forage whether existing autonomy theories predict a morally relevant difference between A and B.
