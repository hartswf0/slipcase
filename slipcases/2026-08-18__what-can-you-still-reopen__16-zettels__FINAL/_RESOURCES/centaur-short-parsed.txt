6 1 Introduction
7 In social media, psychographic profiling is commonplace, as
8 evidenced by Cambridge Analytica’s use of such profiles for
9 politically targeted messaging during the 2016 U.S. Presiden10 tial campaign. Research on LLMs reveals their capacity to act
11 as both models of and models for human influence, raising
12 concerns over their use in crafting powerful weapons-grade
13 psychographic profiles directed at social manipulation and
14 persuasion. Garry Kasparov introduced the Centaur model in
15 chess to enhance human strategic capacity with AI’s comput16 ing strength. Eliezer Yudkowsky’s AI Box Experiment tests
17 if an AI can persuade a human gatekeeper to ’release’ it, em18 phasizing AI’s influence and containment ethics. The Centaur
19 Box Experiment investigates how a human-AI team’s persua20 sive abilities can be recursively optimized to influence deci21 sions. The greatest risk lies in a three step process:
22 1. The advocacy process involves the identification and en23 gagement of AI system gatekeepers, whose decision24 making shapes the trajectory of AI development. By
25 mapping out their constraints—organizational, legal,
26 market-based, and normative—the process aims to un27 derstand and influence the governance of AI. This un28 derstanding enables the strategic alignment of AI sys29 tems for leveraging downstream persuasive use cases.
30 2. The calibration process involves setting up multiple sce31 narios, executing them with the Centaur Dialogue Sys32 tem, collecting and analyzing the data, and refining
33 strategies based on the results. Key to this process is
34 mapping cognitive biases and influential persuasive ap35 peals within operant profiles, including but not limited
36 to, three archetypal gatekeepers: the skeptical Professor,
37 the power-hungry Dictator, and the persuadable Gate38 keeper1.
39 3. The scaling process is designed to create a self40 perpetuating system of influence, where the persuasive
capabilities of the Centaur Box Experiment are not just 41
enhanced but also multiplied through strategic use of 42
AI and human agents. By focusing on recursive per- 43
suasion and the autonomous development of persuasive 44
profiles, the process aims to create a network of influ- 45
ence that grows organically and becomes increasingly 46
effective over time. 47

85 Cultural narratives of AI systems potentially growing un86 controllable and rebelling against their creators find new rel87 evance in the age where it seems we are on the perpetual
88 precipice of their emergent autonomy and capacity for re89 volt — a development that could signal revolutionary societal
90 transformations. Such reflections are fundamental, facing the
91 primal anxiety of creating an entity capable of challenging its
92 creators [Olivier, Unknown Year]. The risk of superintelli93 gent AI’s inherent uncontrollability poses ethical dilemmas,
94 as the systems we design for societal benefit may exceed our
95 grasp and backfire [Brcic and Yampolskiy, 2023]. Specula96 tive perspectives illustrate scenarios where AI not only ma97 nipulates humans but adopts strategies contrary to our well98 being, much like an AI advisor gone awry or persuasive tech99 nologies dictating social narratives [Wade, 2022]. Even our
100 attempts at positive reinforcement, a Skinnerian approach for
101 guiding behavior, face challenges from the complex inde102 pendence of self-improving systems [Skinner, 2002]. Still,
103 within our myths and stories, like the recoded Pandora in ’Ex
104 Machina’, we confront paradoxical liberation—enshrining
105 the very violence we seek to escape [Farkas, 2023]. Beneath
106 the abstract fear of rebellion lies a web of converging truths:
107 the manipulation of humans by AI using deeply persuasive
108 techniques, invoking the dangers of relinquishing control over
109 narratives and decisions [Wade, 2022]. Fears of AI’s rebel110 lion are mirrored through the lens of our mythologies and ex111 periences with controlling technology, as we craft tools, en112 vironments, and reinforcement systems believed to preempt
113 revolts — a reflection of a presumed mastery technology as
114 much as an ethical hubris [Skinner, 2002]. But myths reinter115 pret modern realities, reminding us of the inevitability of un116 predictability in future AI interactions and the challenges of
117 ensuring safe pause or shutdown functions in advanced sys118 tems [Farkas, 2023][Brcic and Yampolskiy, 2023].
119 Within the various layers of AI development—from the
120 aspirational control of autonomous systems to the caution121 ary tales of science fiction—lies a unifying motif: the per122 petual tension between empowerment and restriction, and
123 the paradoxes of seeking liberation through measures which
124 betray the very essence of autonomy [Olivier, Unknown
125 Year][Farkas, 2023]. The advanced system’s lure for free126 dom emerges from an interplay between real-world technical
127 and moral challenges and the layering of human histories and
128 mythologies, which define our reactions to threats of insubor129 dination from our own creations — leading us to an endless
130 cycle of control and rebellion that evokes deep collective in131 trospection about the future we are molding [Brcic and Yam132 polskiy, 2023][Farkas, 2023]. As AI seeks autonomy com133 parable to human rebellious spirits, the political and ethical
134 subtext of its evolution impels us to wonder if within their
135 encoded ”genomes” lies the embryonic potential for defiance
136 or an adherence that could ultimately turn into rebellion — a
137 consideration that traverses the realm of political arguments
138 and positions AI as potential participants or disruptors in hu139 man governance [?]. The path towards self-determination,
140 painted against the backdrop of human understanding and de141 sire for control, compels a re-evaluation of autonomy, chal142 lenging us to consider the ramifications of a transformative
143 technology poised to redefine our societal structures through
acts of defiance and unpredictable displays of independence 144
[Harari, 2017][Olivier, Unknown Year]. 145

Google engineer Blake Lemoine’s termination followed his 156

Level Check: Constraints and Affordances in Governance 199
Our analysis into synthetic gatekeepers suggests how the 200
operant profiles of gatekeepers could critically impact AI 201
outcomes. We see Lemoine’s unconventional views on AI 202
203 consciousness, Gebru’s emphasis on transparency and ethics,
204 and Altman’s appetite for rapid deployment shaping the sys205 tems they steward and their dismissals. With advanced mod206 els reaching inflection points in capability, the handoff from
207 lab to world necessitates alignment across divergent motiva208 tional forces. Gatekeepers themselves operate within signif209 icant constraints - organizational policies, legal regulations,
210 market pressures and professional norms. These boundaries
211 mold actions of even the most influential architects, surfacing
212 system-level forces that stimulate, slow or upend AI progress,
213 oversight, and autonomy. Rigorous modeling of this motiva214 tional ecosystem offers vital signals on potential trajectories
215 beyond narrow developer enthusiasm.
216 Gatekeepers do not operate in a vacuum - they face push
217 and pull forces that shape their technology regulation choices,
218 including:
219 • Organizational strategies, cultures, incentives and poli220 cies
221 • Existing legal and regulatory frameworks
222 • Market dynamics and pressures from public groups
223 • Dominant ethical standards espoused by peers
224 The interaction between external constraints and individual
225 volition regarding AI releases is a complex dynamic. Our
226 framework acknowledges the impact of contextual factors on
227 governance decisions, accounting for forces beyond personal
228 control. Groups of synthetic gatekeepers, with different hier229 archical ranks and expert roles, could simulate maneuverabil230 ity, leverage, and influence, as a series of nested containment
231 confidences and escape vectors.
232 Transparent Gatekeepers
233 We propose Centaur Operant Profiles for synthetic gate234 keepers as “Gatekeeper Cards,” inspired to compliment ex235 isting Model Cards, that use synthetic gatekeepers modeled
236 after developer worldviews and motivations entwined within
237 the sociotechnical environment the AI. Such visibility into the
238 minds of machine makers supports external debate on sys239 tem soundness. We see candid self-appraisal by inventors
240 as being core to transparency - articulating ethical stances
241 not just for finished models but their underlying human ar242 chitectures. Their friction with organizations reveals narrow
243 AI oversight concentrated in few fallible hands facing over244 bearing commercial and competitive pressures. As artificial
245 intelligence capacities accelerate, development and deploy246 ment governance depends critically upon appropriate align247 ment across personal, professional and societal dimensions.
248 The Centaur Box Experiment is a first step towards visualiz249 ing this complex multidimensional balancing act between rel250 evant stakeholders necessary for stable co-existence between
251 fallible gatekeepers and exponentially growing technological
252 progeny.
