ZETTEL

ID:
CBX-006C-PROVENANCE-IS-NOT-PURITY

TITLE:
No desire is uncaused enough to serve as the uncontaminated baseline of autonomy.

SOURCE:
John Christman — “Autonomy and Personal History” — 1991 — Canadian Journal of Philosophy 21(1): 1–24.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
Christman begins from the fact that people are not self-made: values and preferences emerge through many influences distributed across personal history. His problem is therefore not to locate preferences untouched by external causes, but to determine when the preferences people acquire can nevertheless count as expressions of self-government.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
Preference provenance cannot be represented as a scalar measuring how much external influence occurred.

LOCAL MOVE:
The source turns the search away from causal purity and toward the agent’s relation to the processes through which preferences were formed.

SOURCE TERMS:
self-government
personal history
influences
preferences
values
self-determination
autonomy

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The parent’s proposed ΠD risks imagining an impossible contrast between “self-formed” and “externally influenced” desire.

Every desire has provenance.

The research object is not whether someone else participated in writing it.

It is what kinds of participation preserve or destroy authorship.

QUESTION:
What distinguishes education, argument, seduction, socialization, therapy, persuasion, coercion, and manipulation when all can alter what a person comes to want?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Should autonomy be modeled as authorship of preferences, or as participation in an ongoing process that never has a single author?

MECHANISM:
environment
+ relationships
+ institutions
+ discourse
+ reflection
→ preference formation
→ revision
→ later action.

External causation is ubiquitous; autonomy must discriminate within it.

FORMAL SHIFT:
<EXTERNAL INFLUENCE?>
→ <MODE OF PREFERENCE FORMATION>
→ [DISTINGUISH FORMS OF PARTICIPATION]
→ <AUTONOMY-RELEVANT HISTORY>

SOURCE FORMALISM:
NONE

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

Replace:

ΠD ∈ [SELF-FORMED, EXTERNALLY-FORMED]

with a process description:

H_D = {
  influences,
  visibility,
  agent participation,
  opportunities for contestation,
  opportunities for revision,
  dependency,
  coercive constraints
}

Autonomy is not inversely proportional to the number of influences.

TENSION:
The parent correctly detects missing preference provenance but initially formalizes provenance too much like a purity term. Christman makes external influence ordinary rather than exceptional.

MISSING:
A taxonomy of preference-changing relations that does not smuggle the conclusion into labels such as “manipulation.”

BOUNDARY:
Christman does not imply that every socially produced preference is autonomous. He makes causal externality insufficient as the discriminator.

Forage theories that classify these histories and record exactly which variable changes classification.
