Bublitz and Merkel argue that legal protections focus more systematically on bodies and brains than on minds and mental states. They identify manipulative interference with decision-making processes as a gap and argue for stronger protection of mental liberty and the inner sphere of persons.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
Mental self-determination protects something upstream of choice outcomes.

LOCAL MOVE:
The source treats interference with decision-making as independently normatively significant rather than waiting to see whether the resulting decision causes conventional harm.

SOURCE TERMS:
mental self-determination
mental liberty
mental integrity
manipulation
decision-making processes
inner sphere
intervention

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
A freedom metric based on whether an agent eventually gets what it wants arrives too late.

The possible violation may already have occurred during the production of the wanting.

QUESTION:
What part of a decision-making process must remain under the person’s authority even when the eventual decision is beneficial and voluntarily executed?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Can cognitive liberty be represented computationally without pretending that minds have a clean boundary between internal authorship and external influence?

MECHANISM:
external intervention
→ alteration of mental process
→ altered preference / judgment
→ voluntary-looking action.

The normative evaluation attaches partly to the intervention itself.

FORMAL SHIFT:
<MENTAL PROCESS>
→ <INTERVENTION INTO PROCESS>
→ [ASSESS LEGITIMACY]
→ <DECISION>

SOURCE FORMALISM:
NONE

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

SAFF evaluates downstream:

D → action → outcome → reflection

Mental self-determination introduces an upstream constraint:

I → FORM(D)

such that:

PERMISSIBLE(I → FORM(D))

must be evaluated independently of:

UTILITY(D)
or
SUCCESS(action(D)).

TENSION:
Bublitz and Merkel motivate stronger barriers against serious mental interference. Khader shows that some deliberate preference-changing interventions can support agency. The missing distinction therefore cannot be “intervention versus no intervention.”

MISSING:
The threshold separating:
ordinary influence,
persuasion,
therapeutic or educational intervention,
manipulation,
coercion,
and severe interference with mental self-determination.

BOUNDARY:
The source is principally concerned with law, neurotechnology, and serious mental interventions. Extending its framework to conversational AI requires argument rather than analogy alone.
