TITLE:
A Visible Prompt Can Change the Culture Faster Than It Changes the Model

SOURCE:

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE — PRIMARY ARCHIVE]

The community is described as an open space where users brainstorm from one another. But visible front-page keywords can rapidly cause users to reproduce the same category of imagery, creating a reason to constrain certain outputs.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
Prompt visibility creates a feedback loop outside the neural network.

A striking output appears.
Its prompt is visible.
Other users copy the terms.
The copied terms produce more related outputs.
Those outputs become more visible.
Users infer that the terms are important.
The visual field fills with the pattern.

No model retraining is necessary.

The community itself can perform rapid cultural amplification.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
A model can appear to have developed an aesthetic obsession even when its weights have not changed.

What changed may be the human input distribution.

Prompt culture feeds itself.

The visible output surface becomes an input-generating machine for future prompts.

QUESTION:
How often are apparent changes in a generative platform’s “style” actually changes in collective prompting behavior rather than changes in the model?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Once generated outputs and their instructions are publicly visible, should the human community be modeled as part of the generative system’s recurrent loop?

MECHANISM:

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

Let:

Pₜ = distribution of prompts at time t
G = unchanged generative model
R = visibility/ranking mechanism
H = human imitation/transformation process

Xₜ = G(Pₜ)

Pₜ₊₁ = H(R(Xₜ,Pₜ))

Therefore:

Xₜ₊₁ ≠ Xₜ

even if:

Gₜ₊₁ = Gₜ.

Observed aesthetic drift can emerge from recurrent human prompting alone.
