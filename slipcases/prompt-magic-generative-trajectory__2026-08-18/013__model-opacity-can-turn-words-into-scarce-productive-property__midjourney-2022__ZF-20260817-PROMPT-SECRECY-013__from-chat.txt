RESEARCH OBJECT:
The “magic prompt” becomes economically strange once practitioners believe a small linguistic sequence grants access to a valuable model behavior.

The phrase becomes:

knowledge
technique
recipe
secret
style
shortcut
competitive advantage.

Opacity can therefore create artificial scarcity around otherwise trivially copyable text.

The scarce object is not the string itself.

It is knowing that this string does something valuable.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The easier a prompt is to copy, the stronger the incentive may become to conceal it.

A valuable prompt has a peculiar structure:

near-zero reproduction cost
+
high discovery cost
+
uncertain durability.

That resembles neither a conventional artwork nor an ordinary physical tool.

QUESTION:
What exactly do practitioners believe they own when they refuse to disclose a successful prompt?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Does model opacity produce a new economy in which causal knowledge about proprietary systems becomes privately accumulated productive capital?

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

Let:

c(p) = cost of copying prompt p ≈ 0
d(p) = cost of discovering that p has useful effect
v(p,M) = value of effect under model M

Then:

INFORMATION RENT ≈ v(p,M) - c(p)

provided knowledge of p remains scarce.

But:

model update M → M′

may cause:

v(p,M′) → 0.

The prompt secret is therefore a rapidly depreciating information asset.

TENSION:
Prompt communities depend on sharing to build collective expertise.

Prompt professionals may depend on withholding to preserve advantage.

The same technical opacity generates:

COMMUNITY COMMONS
and
PRIVATE KNOW-HOW.
