The Generative Artist May Be Hunting an Event, Not Specifying an Image

“You’re going in to steal the moment.”

RESEARCH OBJECT:
This is a much stranger theory of AI art than “the artist describes what they imagine.”

The model becomes an event-producing environment.

The artist enters it not necessarily as architect but as witness.

Their job is to notice the moment that should be taken.

This moves generative practice toward photography, documentary, field observation, improvisation, and hunting.

The machine does not simply execute the artist’s prior image.

It produces situations in which artistic decisions become possible.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
A person can exercise artistic control by refusing to control the event they hope to capture.

That sounds contradictory only if authorship requires prior specification.

Photography has long made room for another possibility:

the artist authors a relation to contingency.

Generative systems may radicalize that condition because the contingency itself is computationally produced.

QUESTION:
What is the generative equivalent of the photographer’s “decisive moment”?

DEEPER QUESTION:
If the artist’s primary act becomes recognizing something they could not have specified before seeing it, is prompting still the right place to locate authorship?

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

Let:

G = generative process
xₜ = candidate encountered at time t
J = artist’s situated judgment

The artist need not possess target x* beforehand.

Instead:

x₁, x₂, ... xₙ ~ G

until:

J(xₖ | encountered context) → “this.”

The creative event is partially:

RECOGNITION OF xₖ

rather than prior specification of xₖ.

TENSION:
Millière defends AI art partly through intentionality.

The documentary model weakens one conception of intention while strengthening another.

WEAKENS:
the artist need not intend the detailed outcome.

STRENGTHENS:
the artist intentionally constructs and inhabits a situation in which unforeseen material can be recognized.

MISSING:
A vocabulary separating:

INTENTION-TO-PRODUCE-X

from:

INTENTION-TO-ENTER-A-PROCESS-WHERE-X-MAY-BE-DISCOVERED.

BOUNDARY:
Not every act of choosing among generated images constitutes meaningful artistic seeing.

Without evidence of discriminating judgment, “documentary” can become an elegant description of random selection.

TEST:
Compare two generative workflows.

TARGET CONDITION:
artist writes a detailed target description before generating.

DOCUMENTARY CONDITION:
artist states only a field of interest and must discover the final work through observation and selection.

Afterward ask artists to provide:
decision histories
rejected candidates
reasons for selection
moments when goals changed.

Test whether expert judgment appears primarily before execution or emerges through encounter.
