RESEARCH OBJECT:
The early Midjourney interface cannot be reduced to:

TEXT BOX
→ MODEL.

The operating environment was:

PUBLIC CHAT
+ VISIBLE PROMPTS
+ VISIBLE OUTPUTS
+ CONSTANT STRANGERS
+ REMIX
+ STATUS DIFFERENCES
+ RAPID COPY
+ MODEL.

Discord was not merely where the tool happened to live.

It changed what could be learned.

Every nearby generation became an accidental tutorial.
Every prompt became potentially observable technique.
Every strange result became a possible communal discovery.
Every newcomer entered a torrent of other people's experiments before possessing a theory of what counted as signal.

The social room was part of the instrument.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
Prompt literacy may have been partly manufactured by forced peripheral vision.

Users did not learn only by asking:

WHAT SHOULD I TYPE?

They learned by continuously seeing:

WHAT EVERYONE ELSE JUST TYPED
AND WHAT HAPPENED.

A crowded Discord channel therefore functions like an enormous involuntary laboratory notebook.

QUESTION:
How much early Midjourney expertise depended on the public visibility of other users’ failed and successful experiments?

DEEPER QUESTION:
If the same model had launched as a solitary blank text box rather than inside a teeming public Discord, would “prompt engineering” have developed into the same practice at all?

MECHANISM:
[OUR INFERENCE]

PUBLIC GENERATION
→ OBSERVABLE PROMPT/OUTPUT PAIRS
→ COPYING
→ MUTATION
→ COMPARISON
→ LOCAL THEORY
→ REPOSTING
→ COMMUNITY VOCABULARY.

Knowledge production occurs beside the model rather than solely inside the individual practitioner.

FORMAL SHIFT:
PROMPTING =
individual model interaction

becomes:

PROMPTING =
socially coupled parallel experimentation.

SOURCE FORMALISM:
NONE

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

For users u₁...uₙ:

each execution:

eᵢ = (promptᵢ, outputᵢ)

is potentially visible to nearby users.

Thus learning input for uⱼ is not:

history(uⱼ)

but:

history(uⱼ)
∪
visible(e₁...eₙ).

The effective training rate of the human practitioner therefore scales with socially observable experimentation.

TENSION:
Discord is simultaneously:

PEDAGOGY
and
OVERLOAD.

The same teeming stream that lets users learn from thousands of experiments also makes causal knowledge difficult to stabilize.

The archive contains both the complaint that newcomers are dumped into chaos and evidence that this chaos produces prompt culture.

MISSING:
A comparison between users who learned Midjourney publicly in Discord and users who learned equivalent models privately.

We do not know whether public exposure creates:
faster expertise
more superstition
more convergence
more stylistic imitation
more exploration
or all of these simultaneously.

BOUNDARY:
Not every observed prompt-output pair teaches anything.

Public visibility can amplify cargo cults just as easily as valid techniques.
