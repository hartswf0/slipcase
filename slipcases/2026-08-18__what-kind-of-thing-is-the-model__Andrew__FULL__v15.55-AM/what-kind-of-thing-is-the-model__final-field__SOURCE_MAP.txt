CLAIM ID: C1
CLAIM: Andrew describes early Midjourney prompting as exploratory intervention: users probe capability boundaries and may follow unexpected outputs rather than correct them back toward the original intention.
TYPE: SOURCE-SUPPORTED
ZETTEL: MJ-GC-004 · MJ-GC-005 · MJ-GC-006 · GC-DEEP-20260817-01
SOURCE: October 23, 2022 Midjourney interview with Andrew
CITEKEY: andrew2022interview
SUPPORT: Direct interview passages include “I wonder if,” questions about what the system can do, and “that’s not what I asked for … Let’s investigate that more.”
INFERENCE: The paper groups these moves under the term epistemic prompting; that label is the paper’s synthesis, not Andrew’s vocabulary.

CLAIM ID: C2
CLAIM: Some prompting actions are valuable because they reveal information about the system rather than because they directly advance a finished artifact.
TYPE: COMPILER-SYNTHESIS
ZETTEL: GC-DEEP-20260817-02 · MJ-GC-004 · MJ-GC-006
SOURCE: Kirsh & Maglio 1994 + Andrew interview
CITEKEY: kirsh1994epistemic · andrew2022interview
SUPPORT: Kirsh and Maglio distinguish epistemic from pragmatic actions; Andrew describes capability probes whose immediate product is knowledge about model behavior.
INFERENCE: The paper applies the functional distinction to prompting without claiming a historical influence or identity between Tetris action and generative-AI practice.

CLAIM ID: C3
CLAIM: Unexpected generated artifacts can reorganize the next design move when their deviation remains interpretable enough to become new material for intention.
TYPE: INTERPRETATION
ZETTEL: MJ-GC-005 · MJ-GC-029 · MJ-GC-029-A · GC-DEEP-20260817-03
SOURCE: Schön 1992 + Andrew interview + Epstein et al. 2022
CITEKEY: schon1992reflective · andrew2022interview · epstein2022happy
SUPPORT: Schön analyzes reflective conversation with materials; Andrew describes pursuing unexpected images; Epstein et al. report useful lateral or implementation ideas from discrepant generated imagery, with overly abstract outputs sometimes becoming less useful.
INFERENCE: The paper treats useful deviation as interpretable discrepancy rather than maximum surprise.

CLAIM ID: C7
CLAIM: A generated regularity can change status inside practice: first noticed as a phenomenon, then named/theorized, and later recruited as a repeatable test object.
TYPE: COMPILER-SYNTHESIS
ZETTEL: MJ-GC-017 · MJ-GC-024 · GC-DEEP-20260817-09
SOURCE: Andrew interview + Rheinberger 2004
CITEKEY: andrew2022interview · rheinberger2004experimental
SUPPORT: Andrew describes a recurring default face named “Midge” and a deliberately odd recurring character used as a “test subject.” Rheinberger distinguishes epistemic things from technical objects within experimental systems.
INFERENCE: The paper reads the movement from recurring output to reusable probe as a conversion from phenomenon into local apparatus. It does not claim a formal scientific instrument or Rheinbergerian genealogy.

CLAIM ID: C8
CLAIM: Andrew’s distinction between Midjourney as a “game” and as a “tool” is best understood not as capability versus incapability, but as exploratory generativity versus enough stabilized control to sustain consequential work across generations.
TYPE: COMPILER-SYNTHESIS
ZETTEL: MJ-GC-016 · MJ-GC-025 · MJ-GC-026 · MJ-GC-027 · GC-DEEP-20260817-07
SOURCE: Andrew interview + Kirsh & Maglio 1994
CITEKEY: andrew2022interview · kirsh1994epistemic
SUPPORT: Andrew explicitly says Midjourney can feel like a game because there is “not quite enough control,” links control to bringing work into the real world and meaning something, and calls isolated images too ephemeral for sustained stories.
INFERENCE: Toolhood is formulated as stabilization of selected epistemic gains into reusable invariants.
