TITLE:
SUPERSTITION IS A SYMPTOM OF EXPLORATORY EXPERIMENTATION WITHOUT STABLE CONTROLS — folk knowledge grows where causal discrimination is expensive.

SOURCE:
Max Planck Institute for the History of Science — “Generating Experimental Knowledge: Experimental Systems, Concept Formation and the Pivotal Role of Error” — umbrella research theme, 2004–2007. SOURCE URL: https://www.mpiwg-berlin.mpg.de/research/projects/GeneratingExperimentalKnowledge

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
The MPIWG project describes explorative experimentation as inquiry that is not simply designed to test a prior theory, and stresses that experimental objects, technical arrangements, concepts, and error criteria are fine-tuned together as research unfolds.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
PROMPT-FOLKLORE-AS-UNDERCONTROLLED-EXPLORATION.

LOCAL MOVE:
[[MJ-GC-010]] and [[MJ-GC-011]] can be split more carefully. The community’s anecdotes are not automatically useless because they are not hypothesis tests; some are exploratory probes that generate candidate phenomena. The epistemic failure occurs when exploratory observations are promoted to causal claims without the controls needed to discriminate alternatives.

SOURCE TERMS:
“explorative”
“knowledge-generating”
“error”
“experimental systems”
“concept formation”
“fine-tuned”

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The boundary is not SCIENCE versus SUPERSTITION. It is OBSERVATION versus CAUSAL CLAIM. A weakly controlled observation may be exactly what supplies the next phenomenon worth testing.

QUESTION:
Which pieces of prompt folklore are best treated as hypotheses generated by exploration rather than beliefs to accept or ridicule?

DEEPER QUESTION:
What minimal experimental infrastructure would let practitioner communities promote a prompt regularity from anecdote to warranted operational knowledge?

MECHANISM:
EXPLORATORY GENERATION
→ candidate regularity
→ social repetition
→ folk explanation.

Without controlled discrimination, explanation can harden prematurely.
With controlled discrimination, candidate becomes supported or rejected.

FORMAL SHIFT:
FROM: anecdote OR experiment.
TO: anecdote can be an exploratory observation whose epistemic status changes only after discriminating tests.

SOURCE FORMALISM:
The MPIWG project description distinguishes explorative experimentation from simple hypothesis testing and emphasizes the epistemology of error within broader experimental systems.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

OBSERVATION → HYPOTHESIS_CANDIDATE.
HYPOTHESIS_CANDIDATE + CONTROLLED_TEST → {SUPPORTED, REJECTED, UNRESOLVED}.

Skipping the second transition creates folklore with inflated causal status.

TENSION:
Formal controls can be impossible or unstable in proprietary, version-changing systems. Practical communities may have to act on weaker evidence than laboratory ideals permit.

MISSING:
A graded evidentiary standard for black-box generative systems whose models and interfaces change during investigation.

BOUNDARY:
This does not certify informal prompt communities as scientific communities; it uses experimental epistemology to separate kinds of inquiry occurring inside them.

TEST:
Take five historical “magic prompt” beliefs. For each, reconstruct the first observation, the first causal explanation, attempts at controlled replication, and the model version. Classify where status inflation occurred.
