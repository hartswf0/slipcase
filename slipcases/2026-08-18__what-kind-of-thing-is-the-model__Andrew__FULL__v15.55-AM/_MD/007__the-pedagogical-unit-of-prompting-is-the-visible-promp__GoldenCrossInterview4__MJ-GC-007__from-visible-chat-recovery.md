TITLE:
The pedagogical unit of prompting is the visible prompt-output pair.

SOURCE:
GOLD_MJ_Interview_4.wh.GoldenCross_otter_ai 2.pages — Otter.ai transcript — 11:49.

PASSAGE:
[QUOTE]
“as you see how other people write, you know, the best way to learn is by looking at how other people's prompts look. And then what they turn out to be.”

[QUOTE]
“If someone is willing to share what they did, that's really the best way to learn.”

RESEARCH OBJECT:
PROMPT-OUTPUT-PAIR-AS-LEARNING-OBJECT.

LOCAL MOVE:
The speaker does not say that reading prompts alone is sufficient. Learning requires seeing prompt language beside what that language produced.

SOURCE TERMS:
“how other people write”
“how other people's prompts look”
“what they turn out to be”
“share what they did”
“best way to learn”

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
A prompt has little pedagogical meaning without evidence of its execution.

QUESTION:
Is the basic transmissible unit of prompt knowledge a phrase, a complete prompt, or a prompt-output relation?

DEEPER QUESTION:
What forms of literacy emerge when linguistic meaning is learned through repeated observation of machine consequences?

MECHANISM:
OBSERVE(prompt)
+ OBSERVE(output)
→ infer relation
→ reuse/modify prompt practice.

FORMAL SHIFT:
FROM prompt text as knowledge
TO prompt-execution pair as knowledge.

SOURCE FORMALISM:
NONE

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
LEARNING_OBJECT := ⟨PROMPT, OUTPUT⟩
not PROMPT alone.

TENSION:
One visible output cannot establish what a prompt reliably causes because generation is stochastic and prompts contain multiple interacting variables.

MISSING:
Whether users compare seeds, repeated generations, or controlled prompt variants when learning from others.

BOUNDARY:
Visible prompt-output pairs provide examples, not necessarily causal proof.

CITATION TRAIL:
Transcript 11:49
→ prompt visibility
→ corresponding output
→ social acquisition of prompt practice.

TEST:
Compare this claim with the speaker's later criticism that prompting anecdotes do not constitute scientific evidence.
