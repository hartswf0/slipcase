TITLE:
MIDGE BECOMES YAMO — a generated phenomenon can move from anomaly to named object to reusable experimental instrument.

[PARAPHRASE]
Rheinberger notes that epistemic things can become technical things within an experimental process, while technical elements can in turn become research objects.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
PHENOMENON-TO-INSTRUMENT-CONVERSION.

LOCAL MOVE:
[[MJ-GC-017]] and [[MJ-GC-024]] appear separate: one names a recurring default face (“Midge”), the other reuses an impossible character as a “test subject.” In relation they reveal a more general operation. Communities notice recurrence, name it, stabilize an identity around it, and sometimes reuse a generated object as a probe of future model behavior.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
An output is not condemned to remain an output. It can become part of the apparatus used to investigate the generator that produced it.

QUESTION:
What transformations are required for a generated artifact to become a benchmark or probe?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Can generative systems manufacture their own future test instruments by producing salient objects that users then stabilize and reuse?

MECHANISM:
RECURRENT / STRANGE OUTPUT
→ recognition
→ naming
→ identity stabilization
→ repeated use across conditions
→ diagnostic comparison.

FORMAL SHIFT:
FROM: MODEL → ARTIFACT.
TO: MODEL → ARTIFACT → COMMUNITY INSTRUMENT → TEST MODEL.

SOURCE FORMALISM:
Rheinberger describes movement between underdetermined epistemic things and stabilized technical objects inside experimental systems.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

GENERATE(P) → phenomenon E.
NAME(E) → reusable reference R_E.
TEST(model_t, R_E) across contexts/versions → diagnostic trace.

The generated thing becomes part of the measurement practice.

TENSION:
Midge and Yamo are different cases: one is an emergent default discovered in outputs; the other is a deliberately cultivated character. The proposed conversion is a family resemblance that requires case-by-case reconstruction.

MISSING:
A corpus showing whether named community characters/defaults were systematically reused across versions for comparison.

BOUNDARY:
The Rheinberger relation is an analytic analogy, not evidence that practitioners consciously adopted experimental-systems theory.

TEST:
Identify five named recurring artifacts or community characters. Trace first appearance, naming, repeated use, and whether later outputs were judged relative to them. Distinguish meme, character, benchmark, and diagnostic probe.
