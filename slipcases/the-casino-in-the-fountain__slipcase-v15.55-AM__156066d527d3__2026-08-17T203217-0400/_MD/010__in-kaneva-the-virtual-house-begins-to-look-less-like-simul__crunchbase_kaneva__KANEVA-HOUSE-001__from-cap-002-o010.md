ID: KANEVA-HOUSE-001

TITLE:
In Kaneva, the virtual house begins to look less like simulated shelter than a spatial operating system for media and identity.

SOURCE:
Crunchbase — Kaneva company profile; MediaPost — “Kaneva Brings YouTube Into Virtual World” — 2007.

SOURCE URL:
https://www.crunchbase.com/organization/kaneva

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE] Users personalized homes and worlds, met through avatars, and could bring shared media such as YouTube video into the 3D environment.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
The Kaneva home combines three ordinarily separate representational forms:

PROFILE → WHO I AM
PLAYLIST/MEDIA LIBRARY → WHAT I LIKE
ROOM/HOME → WHERE I AM

Kaneva makes them mutually spatial.

LOCAL MOVE:
A user's social profile becomes associated with an avatar, possessions, a customizable home, and media encountered by visitors.

SOURCE TERMS:
profile
avatar
home
personalize
media sharing
virtual TV
friends

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The digital “home” may not primarily imitate domestic architecture. Its walls and furniture provide an interface for arranging social identity, access, media, and attention.

QUESTION:
When does a profile become a house?

DEEPER QUESTION:
What does architecture do to information once files, preferences, relationships, and media must acquire locations in order to be encountered?

TENSION:
A contemporary critic dismissed Kaneva as something like a glorified television set. But that criticism accidentally identifies the stranger possibility: Kaneva made the television set, room, avatar, and media library into one social interface.

MISSING:
We still need primary documentation showing exactly how home permissions, media ownership, furniture, profiles, and visitor access were represented in Kaneva's system.

BOUNDARY:
The sources establish customizable homes and spatial media use. The interpretation of the home as an “operating system for identity” is ours.
