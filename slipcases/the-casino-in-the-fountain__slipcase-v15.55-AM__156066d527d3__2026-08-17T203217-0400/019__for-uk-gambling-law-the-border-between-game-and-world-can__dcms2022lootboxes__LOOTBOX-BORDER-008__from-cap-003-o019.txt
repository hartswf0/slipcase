LOOTBOX-BORDER-008

TITLE:
For UK gambling law, the border between game and world can determine whether a loot box counts as gambling.

SOURCE:
UK Department for Digital, Culture, Media & Sport — Government Response to the Call for Evidence on Loot Boxes in Video Games — 2022.

SOURCE URL:
https://www.gov.uk/government/calls-for-evidence/loot-boxes-in-video-games-call-for-evidence/outcome/government-response-to-the-call-for-evidence-on-loot-boxes-in-video-games

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
The UK government observed that most loot boxes did not meet the Gambling Act definition because prizes remained confined to the game and lacked real-money convertibility.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
The legal boundary can turn on whether value crosses the membrane of the game.

RANDOMNESS is insufficient.

PAYMENT is insufficient.

PRIZE is insufficient.

CONVERTIBILITY becomes load-bearing.

LOCAL MOVE:
The government's analysis distinguishes gambling-like similarities from the statutory classification of gambling.

SOURCE TERMS:
randomised items
gambling

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The ontology of the WORLD has legal consequences.

The same random digital object may change regulatory category if it acquires an exit into a real-money market.

QUESTION:
Can interoperability convert play into gambling?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Does adding a bridge between virtual and external value systems transform the legal identity of an otherwise unchanged mechanic?

TENSION:
Psychologically, a non-cashable randomized purchase can still resemble gambling.

Legally, resemblance and statutory qualification are not identical.

MISSING:

BOUNDARY:
This describes the UK government's 2022 analysis. Gambling classifications differ by jurisdiction and can change.
