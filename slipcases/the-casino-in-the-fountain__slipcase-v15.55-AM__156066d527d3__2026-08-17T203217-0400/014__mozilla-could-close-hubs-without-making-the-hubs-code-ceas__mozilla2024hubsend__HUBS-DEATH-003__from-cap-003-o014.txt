ZETTEL

ID:
HUBS-DEATH-003

TITLE:
Mozilla could close Hubs without making the Hubs code cease to exist.

SOURCE:
Mozilla Support — “End of support for Mozilla Hubs” — 2024.

SOURCE URL:
https://support.mozilla.org/en-US/kb/end-support-mozilla-hubs

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
Mozilla ended Hubs support on May 31, 2024; the codebase subsequently moved under management of the Hubs Foundation.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
SERVICE DEATH and SYSTEM DEATH become separable.

Mozilla could terminate:

billing
demo infrastructure
Hubs Cloud provisioning
institutional support

without necessarily terminating:

source
protocols
client architecture
the possibility of independently operated instances.

LOCAL MOVE:
Open source creates a strange afterlife in which the institution and the computational object can diverge.

SOURCE TERMS:
ended support
demo server
Hubs Cloud
codebase
Hubs Foundation

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
Who owns the continuity of a world after its maker leaves?

QUESTION:
What exactly dies when a virtual world shuts down?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Can a world possess institutional mortality while retaining technical reproducibility?

MECHANISM:
<MOZILLA HUBS>
→ [MOZILLA WITHDRAWS OPERATION]
→ <SERVICE ENDS>

while:

<SOURCE CODE>
→ [TRANSFER COMMUNITY STEWARDSHIP]

FORMAL SHIFT:

SOURCE FORMALISM:
NONE

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

DEATH(WORLD) is not Boolean.

DEATH :=
{
    hosted_service: true,
    original_operator: true,
    source_code: false,
    recreatability: partial,
    historical_instance_state: ?
}

TENSION:
A proprietary virtual world can disappear when its operator shuts down its servers.

An open-source world may lose its inhabited instance while its machinery remains reproducible.

MISSING:
Which Hubs rooms, assets, identities, histories, and social relations actually survived the transition?

BOUNDARY:
Code survival is not world survival. Source code cannot by itself reproduce lost social history or instance state.
