ID: CALVINO-013

TITLE:
The supposedly objective narrative filters already encode judgments about believable people.

SOURCE:
Paul Braffort — "L'ordre dans le crime" — 1997, section 2, objective and subjective constraints.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
Calvino's proposed filters exclude or deprioritize actions according to assumptions about physical capability, reciprocity, motive, and the economy of repeated acts. Braffort distinguishes these restrictions from constraints based on particular character personalities.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
The classification of narrative plausibility judgments as objective computational constraints.

LOCAL MOVE:
The project must translate informal expectations about what people can or would do into explicit compatibility rules.

SOURCE TERMS:
"contraintes objectives";
"contraintes subjectives";
"compatibilité"; "économie narrative."

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
A machine may appear to eliminate objectively impossible stories while implementing culturally situated judgments that its programmer has formalized as impossibilities.

QUESTION:
Who decides that a fictional action is impossible rather than merely improbable, unconventional, or aesthetically undesirable?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Does anticombinatorics risk eliminating precisely the anomalous characters and events that creative literature can make intelligible?

MECHANISM:
The designer encodes an expectation as a rule. The rule removes candidates before a reader or writer can assess their narrative value.

FORMAL SHIFT:
Background assumption
→ compatibility rule
→ [candidate exclusion]
→ narrowed narrative possibility space.

SOURCE FORMALISM:
Calvino distinguishes classes of actions and constraints relating to their compatibility, order, and character-specific plausibility.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

Hard impossibility:
C(a, S) = false

Contextual plausibility:
P(a | S, character, genre) varies

A narrative system that converts low plausibility into impossibility loses the distinction between these judgments.

TENSION:
Some restrictions are indispensable for coherent physical causality. Others silently enforce genre conventions or assumptions about social behavior.

MISSING:
An explicit provenance record for each constraint and a method for contesting or revising its justification.

BOUNDARY:
The source demonstrates the presence of plausibility judgments in the specification. It does not establish that every constraint is arbitrary or biased.

CITATION TRAIL:
Braffort's description of the original synopsis.
Calvino, "Prose et anticombinatoire."
The history of formal character representations in computational storytelling.

TEST:
Audit each original filter. Classify it as logical necessity, physical assumption, social expectation, genre convention, or aesthetic preference. Reverse one assumption at a time and inspect the resulting narratives.

QUESTION:
Which of these artifacts should be treated as the primary evidence for the historical experiment's capabilities?

How does a literary machine acquire retrospective technical coherence when its records combine fiction, demonstration, theoretical argument, and unpublished specifications?

Literary problem

TITLE:
Contemporary models may relocate selection into training rather than eliminate it.

SOURCE:
Long Ouyang et al. — "Training Language Models to Follow Instructions with Human Feedback" — NeurIPS (2022).

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
The researchers collect human-written demonstrations and rankings of model outputs, then use them to train and refine a language model's behavior.

Human evaluation incorporated into a model's learned generative behavior before an individual user requests an artifact.

LOCAL MOVE:
The paper distinguishes language modeling from following human instructions and demonstrates a procedure for modifying a model through preference feedback.

SOURCE TERMS:
"demonstrations"; "rankings";
"reward model"; "reinforcement learning from human feedback."

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The research note's division of labor between a generative model and a human who selects its outputs neglects the human judgments already embedded in some trained models.

QUESTION:
How much of contemporary computational selection occurs before generation, through data selection, annotation, preference modeling, and optimization?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Where should authorship be located when an artifact depends on accumulated evaluations from people who never encounter the eventual user or work?

MECHANISM:
People evaluate candidate behaviors. Their assessments become training data. An optimization procedure changes the model's output distribution.

FORMAL SHIFT:
Human judgments
→ labeled demonstrations and rankings
→ [model optimization]
→ revised generative behavior.

SOURCE FORMALISM:
Supervised fine-tuning.
Reward modeling.
Reinforcement learning from human feedback.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

Candidate outputs
→ human preference data
→ learned preference signal
→ modified model parameters
→ subsequent output distribution.

This differs from a human choosing among outputs after generation.

TENSION:
Preference alignment is not equivalent to artistic judgment. A model optimized to follow instructions is not thereby optimized to generate aesthetically consequential exceptions.

MISSING:
A provenance system connecting particular training judgments to particular output characteristics; a defensible account of artistic evaluation across different communities.

This paper establishes one training methodology, not the architecture or training history of all contemporary generative models.

Research on preference learning and reward modeling.

ID: CALVINO-016

TITLE:
An admissible event sequence still needs an explanation for why its characters act.

SOURCE:
Mark O. Riedl and R. Michael Young — "Narrative Planning: Balancing Plot and Character" — Journal of Artificial Intelligence Research 39 (2010), pp. 217–267.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
The authors develop a narrative planner that searches for causally coherent action sequences while also representing goals that explain characters' participation in those sequences.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
A distinction between causal validity and perceived character intentionality.

LOCAL MOVE:
The paper treats narrative generation as a planning problem in which actions must contribute both to plot progression and to intelligible character behavior.

SOURCE TERMS:
"narrative planning";
"character intentionality";
"causal links";
"Intent-based Partial Order Causal Link."

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
Calvino's basic anticombinatorial problem asks whether actions can coexist. A stronger narrative problem asks why those actions belong to this story, performed by these characters.

QUESTION:
Can a narrative satisfy every logical compatibility constraint while remaining unintelligible as character-driven fiction?

DEEPER QUESTION:
What is the smallest representation that distinguishes an admissible sequence of events from a motivated narrative?

MECHANISM:
A planning system links actions through preconditions and effects while associating character actions with explanatory goals.

FORMAL SHIFT:
Initial world state
→ candidate actions
→ [causal and intentional planning]
→ coherent action sequence with goal explanations.

SOURCE FORMALISM:
Intent-based Partial Order Causal Link planning, abbreviated IPOCL.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

For each narrative action a:

1. Its required preconditions must hold.
2. Its effects must update the world consistently.
3. A relevant character goal should explain its performance.

Logical validity alone checks only part of the problem.

TENSION:
An intentionally motivated character can still participate in an aesthetically unsuccessful story. Conversely, literature can deliberately present unexplained or contradictory motives.

MISSING:
A general account of how genre, unreliable narration, thematic ambiguity, and literary surprise modify the desirability of explicit intention.

BOUNDARY:
IPOCL is one approach to narrative planning. It is not a universal model of literary creativity or a direct continuation of Calvino's experiment.

CITATION TRAIL:
Riedl and Young (2010).
Meehan (1977).
Dehn (1981).
Calvino's subjective narrative constraints, as documented by Braffort.

TEST:
Generate narratives with identical event inventories but different planning conditions: causal-only, causal-plus-intentional, and intentional with permitted ambiguity. Compare consistency and reader interpretations of character motives.

TITLE:
The unity of Invisible Cities may be a recurring referent, not a recoverable geography.

SOURCE:
Natalie Berkman — "Italo Calvino's Oulipian Clinamen" — MLN 135.1 (2020), pp. 267–273.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
Berkman examines the structured arrangement of the fifty-five city descriptions and the significance of Venice in the central portion of the book. She interprets Venice as a recurring referent that destabilizes the otherwise geometrical organization.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
The difference between many descriptions sharing an imagined referent and many observations describing one spatially determinate location.

LOCAL MOVE:
Berkman identifies tension between the book's geometric organization and its semantic treatment of cities, memory, and Venice.

SOURCE TERMS:
"Le città invisibili"; "Venice";
"geometric structure"; "clinamen";
"crystal"; "flame."

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
A collection of fictional cities may possess deep narrative unity without possessing a single reconstructable topography.

QUESTION:
When descriptions suggest one imagined place, what entitles us to infer a common physical world rather than a common symbolic, autobiographical, or thematic referent?

DEEPER QUESTION:
How can an inferred world preserve ambiguity instead of forcing every image into one geometrically consistent reconstruction?

MECHANISM:
Repeated motifs, memories, and narrative relations connect descriptions whose spatial properties need not be mutually compatible.

FORMAL SHIFT:
Multiple city descriptions
→ recurring motifs and referents
→ [interpretive association]
→ narrative unity without necessary geographic identity.

SOURCE FORMALISM:
Eleven categories of five cities, arranged across nine chapters.

This is a documented literary organization, not an algorithm for reconstructing a three-dimensional city.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

Given fictional observations O1 ... On:

H1: All observations describe one physical place.
H2: Observations describe different places sharing a thematic referent.
H3: Observations describe incompatible versions of one imagined place.

Do not assume H1 before testing the alternatives.

TENSION:
Spatial reconstruction favors continuity and consistency. Calvino's literary method can make contradiction and variation essential to a place's significance.

MISSING:
A representation that preserves conflicting spatial hypotheses while maintaining a usable narrative world.

BOUNDARY:
Berkman's analysis does not establish that all fifty-five cities are literally locations within one physically consistent Venice.

ID: CALVINO-018

TITLE:
Calvino suspected that combinatorial order might be a defense against the very uncertainty literature should confront.

SOURCE:
Italo Calvino — "Cybernetics and Ghosts" — 1967, pp. 14–16.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
Calvino describes the reassurance he derives from reducing seemingly unlimited complexity to finite arrangements. He then questions whether this attraction to formal order expresses a defensive wish to protect himself from the unknown.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
The possibility that computational formalization is motivated partly by a desire to make uncertainty manageable.

LOCAL MOVE:
Calvino interrupts his argument for the literary machine by questioning the motives underlying his own attraction to combinatorial explanation.

SOURCE TERMS:
"discrete"; "finite"; "security";
"intellectual agoraphobia"; "unknown."

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
Calvino does not simply alternate between confidence in machines and nostalgia for human creativity. He explicitly questions whether the desire to formalize literature is itself an evasion of literary experience.

QUESTION:
Does anticombinatorics enlarge creative possibility, or does it produce a controllable domain by excluding forms of uncertainty that resist representation?

DEEPER QUESTION:
What would a generative system look like if its objective were not to resolve uncertainty as quickly as possible, but to preserve productive ambiguity?

MECHANISM:
An indeterminate situation is decomposed into categories and admissible relations. The resulting model makes a subset of the situation manageable while leaving other aspects unrepresented.

FORMAL SHIFT:
Unclassifiable experience
→ discrete representation
→ [formal restriction]
→ manageable possibility space.

SOURCE FORMALISM:
NONE. Calvino offers a philosophical and psychological self-critique.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

Represented possibility ≠ total possibility.

A model M maps an experience E into a tractable representation R.

M(E) = R

The operations available inside R do not exhaust everything that could matter in E.

TENSION:
Without formalization, large possibility spaces may be impossible to explore systematically. With excessive formalization, important uncertainties can disappear before exploration begins.

MISSING:
A practical criterion for distinguishing productive formal restriction from premature closure.

BOUNDARY:
Calvino's self-critique does not amount to a rejection of mathematics, formal constraint, or computational literature.

CITATION TRAIL:
Calvino, "Cybernetics and Ghosts," sections III–IV.
Calvino, "The Challenge to the Labyrinth."
Calvino's later writings on multiplicity and exactitude.

TEST:
Give an author and a generative system an unresolved narrative situation. Compare workflows that require early formal specification with workflows that permit competing interpretations to remain open. Record when ambiguity is resolved and whether useful alternatives disappear.
