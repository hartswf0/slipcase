ID: CALVINO-001

TITLE:
The 1967 literary machine was supposed to rebel against its own rules.

SOURCE:
Italo Calvino — "Cybernetics and Ghosts" — 1967.
English translation: The Uses of Literature (1986), pp. 10–11.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
Calvino distinguishes machines that produce formally correct works from a more ambitious literary machine capable of revising its own conventions. Such a machine might turn against its established literary procedures and invent new ones.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
A distinction between generating unusual outputs within an established system and transforming the system that determines which outputs are possible.

LOCAL MOVE:
Calvino rejects mechanical classicism as the ultimate achievement of computational literature. He imagines machines capable of learning and transforming their own programs.

SOURCE TERMS:
"literature-machine"; "classicism"; "disorder";
"learning"; "changing their own programs."

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The research note treats creative deviation primarily as a later complication of Calvino's mechanical combinatorics. But the possibility of machine-initiated formal innovation was already present in 1967.

QUESTION:
Does contemporary generative AI fulfill Calvino's prediction if it produces novel artifacts without independently changing its generative conventions?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Is creativity a property of an output, a generative procedure, or the capacity to challenge and replace that procedure?

MECHANISM:
The distinction is between variation under fixed rules and changes to the rules governing variation.

FORMAL SHIFT:
Literary convention
→ executable generative rules
→ [machine revises its own rules]
→ changed space of possible literature.

SOURCE FORMALISM:
NONE. Calvino describes a hypothetical capability, not a working algorithm.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

Level 1: y = G(theta, x, z)
Level 2: theta' = U(theta, history, criterion)

The second level changes generative behavior rather than merely selecting another sample.

TENSION:
A stochastic model can produce radical variation without ever revising its learned parameters. Conversely, a self-modifying machine might become more conservative.

MISSING:
What triggers dissatisfaction? What evaluates formal innovation? Who establishes the criterion for changing the generator?

BOUNDARY:
The passage does not demonstrate machine autonomy, aesthetic judgment, consciousness, or a historical influence on modern AI.

CITATION TRAIL:
Calvino, "Cybernetics and Ghosts," pp. 10–13.
Calvino's earlier writings on literary experimentation.
Oulipian distinctions between constraint and exception.

TEST:
Compare fixed-weight sampling, adaptive decoding, and systems that revise their own generative procedures. Determine which operation, if any, corresponds to Calvino's proposed machine.

ID: CALVINO-002

TITLE:
The reader was the decisive literary machine before the clinamen returned to the author.

SOURCE:
Italo Calvino — "Cybernetics and Ghosts" — 1967,
pp. 13–14, 19–20.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
Calvino suggests that mechanizing composition would make reading the decisive event in literary life. A permutation acquires literary significance through its effect on a historically situated person and society.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
A displacement of aesthetic significance from the mechanism of production to the event of reception.

LOCAL MOVE:
Calvino removes the privileged author without reducing literature to formal syntax. Meaning arises when a combination acquires significance beyond the operation that produced it.

SOURCE TERMS:
"reading"; "permutations"; "poetic result";
"consciousness"; "unconscious"; "ghosts."

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The question "Can a machine create literature?" may already be badly formed. Calvino's earlier essay places literary significance in the encounter between an artifact and its readers.

QUESTION:
What would it mean to evaluate an AI-generated work without treating either its human prompter or its computational producer as the unique location of creativity?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Can a literary event be computationally produced without its significance being computationally determined?

MECHANISM:
Generation makes combinations available. Interpretation activates meanings that may not have been specified in the procedure.

FORMAL SHIFT:
Generated artifact
→ historically situated encounter
→ [interpretation]
→ literary significance.

SOURCE FORMALISM:
NONE.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

Meaning = R(work, reader, historical_context)

R is not a metric supplied by Calvino. It designates a relation that cannot be reduced to the work's generation probability.

TENSION:
The later "Prose and Anticombinatorics" assigns the clinamen an exceptional place in the artist's creative work. Dennis Duncan interprets this as a change in Calvino's conception of authorship.

MISSING:
An account of how reception feeds back into the construction, circulation, and revision of a work.

BOUNDARY:
This passage does not establish that readers alone create literary value or that the original author is irrelevant.

CITATION TRAIL:
Calvino, "Cybernetics and Ghosts."
Calvino, "Prose and Anticombinatorics."
Dennis Duncan, "Calvino, Llull, Lucretius" (2012).

TEST:
Read the 1967 and 1977/1981 formulations together. Track where each locates the operation that transforms a formally produced text into literature: generator, author, reader, or social context.

ID: CALVINO-003

TITLE:
Anticombinatorics meant explicit constraint construction, not simply choosing from many possibilities.

SOURCE:
Paul Braffort — "L'ordre dans le crime: Une expérience cybernétique avec Italo Calvino" — Europe, no. 815, March 1997, sections 2–3.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
Braffort describes writing programs that eliminated incompatible narrative combinations through logical and semantic compatibility matrices. The resulting filters did not adequately control combinatorial explosion.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
The difference between constraints that define admissible narrative states and probabilities that favor particular generated sequences.

LOCAL MOVE:
Braffort identifies anticombinatorial work with the explicit specification of structures, rules, and constraints. His account gives the term a narrower operational meaning than general selection from possibility.

SOURCE TERMS:
"filtres anti-combinatoires";
"matrices de compatibilité";
"contraintes objectives";
"contraintes subjectives."

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The apparent equivalence between anticombinatorics and probabilistic generation disappears when the systems' operations are compared.

QUESTION:
What would a modern generative model need to represent before it could perform Calvino's anticombinatorial operations rather than merely resemble their outcomes?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Can narrative admissibility be learned statistically, or does it require explicit representations of causality, action, time, and changing world states?

MECHANISM:
Calvino–Braffort:
Define candidate relations, then test their compatibility.

Autoregressive language modeling:
Estimate a conditional distribution over subsequent tokens.

FORMAL SHIFT:
Narrative actions
→ character/action relations
→ [compatibility tests]
→ admissible sequences.

SOURCE FORMALISM:
Four characters; twelve specified actions; pairwise relations; logical and semantic filters.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

Constraint system:
C(sequence, world_state) ∈ {0,1}

Language model:
P(token_t | token_<t, context)

A highly probable token sequence is not necessarily an admissible event sequence.

TENSION:
A learned model can approximate constraints without implementing an explicit logical verifier. A constraint engine can enforce consistency without producing interesting prose.

MISSING:
A shared representation that permits both learned generation and verification of narrative state transitions.

BOUNDARY:
Braffort's surviving account does not supply a complete executable implementation or evidence that the intended novel was successfully generated.

CITATION TRAIL:
Braffort, section 2, objective and subjective constraints.
Calvino, "Prose et anticombinatoire," pp. 319–331.
Meehan, TALE-SPIN (1977).

TEST:
Construct a small four-character narrative corpus. Compare free generation, probabilistic reranking, and explicit state-based constraint checking. Measure violations separately from aesthetic preferences.

ID: CALVINO-004

TITLE:
The foundational combinatorial calculation contains a numerical error.

SOURCE:
Paolo Alessandrini — "La matematica di Italo Calvino" — Archimede 3 (2023), p. 160.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
Alessandrini identifies an arithmetic mistake in Calvino's calculation of the possible crime configurations. The combinatorial expression is correct; the printed numerical result is not.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
A discrepancy between the formal specification of a possibility space and its stated cardinality.

LOCAL MOVE:
Alessandrini separates the mathematical structure of the crime problem from the numerical result attributed to it.

SOURCE TERMS:
"disposizioni con ripetizione"; "coppie ordinate";
"schemi"; "12 alla dodicesima potenza."

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
A story concerned with the computational mastery of possibility contains an error in its most conspicuous calculation.

QUESTION:
Does the error matter only as arithmetic, or does it expose a broader ambiguity between the mathematical possibility space and the actual narrative problem?

DEEPER QUESTION:
How much of the apparent rigor of computational literature survives when we audit its representations instead of accepting its stated numbers?

MECHANISM:
Four distinct people produce twelve possible ordered, nonreflexive pairs. Twelve independently assigned actions therefore produce 12^12 assignments.

FORMAL SHIFT:
4 characters
→ 4 × 3 ordered pairs
→ 12 choices for each of 12 actions
→ 12^12 assignments.

SOURCE FORMALISM:
12^12 = 8,916,100,448,256

Printed in Calvino:
8,874,296,672,256

Difference:
41,803,776,000

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

The initial search space assumes:
1. Exactly twelve action occurrences.
2. One ordered character pair per action.
3. No self-directed actions.
4. Independent assignment before compatibility constraints.

The counting rule does not establish that all these assignments represent narratable stories.

TENSION:
The equation counts assignments, not distinct plots under interpretation, causal equivalence, or narrative redundancy.

MISSING:
Evidence identifying the origin of the arithmetic mistake; a specification of when two assignments should count as the same narrative.

BOUNDARY:
The error does not invalidate the combinatorial construction itself or establish a general failure of computational approaches to literature.

CITATION TRAIL:
Alessandrini (2023), p. 160.
Calvino, "The Burning of the Abominable House" (1995 translation), p. 157.
Braffort (1997), section 2.

TEST:
Reproduce the count, then calculate how it changes under explicit restrictions on reciprocal relations, character mortality, duplicated motives, and permutation equivalence. Compare the different meanings of "possible story."

ID: CALVINO-005

TITLE:
Calvino identified character transformation and then excluded it from the computation.

SOURCE:
Paul Braffort — "L'ordre dans le crime" — 1997, section 2, discussion of subjective constraints.

PASSAGE:
[QUOTE]
"Pour le moment on renonce à explorer ce domaine."

[PARAPHRASE]
Calvino allows that characters could acquire or lose incompatibilities through actions they perform or undergo, but sets that possibility aside.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
Dynamic narrative constraints that change as a consequence of the narrative itself.

LOCAL MOVE:
The project confronts the possibility that characters cannot be modeled adequately through fixed attributes and compatibility relations.

SOURCE TERMS:
"contraintes subjectives";
"changer dans le déroulement de l'histoire";
"incompatibilités."

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The most promising computational problem may be the one Calvino explicitly declined to explore.

QUESTION:
What if narrative possibility is not a fixed search space, but a space whose constraints are continually rewritten by the story's events?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Can the possibility space change during narrative generation without making the model's consistency criteria arbitrary?

MECHANISM:
An event changes character attributes, relationships, obligations, or capabilities. Those changes alter which subsequent events are possible or plausible.

FORMAL SHIFT:
Character state at t
→ event
→ [update attributes and relations]
→ revised possibilities at t+1.

SOURCE FORMALISM:
Braffort reports the possibility of changing incompatibilities, but supplies no completed procedure for implementing this domain.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

S(t+1) = T(S(t), action(t))

C(t+1) = Constraints(S(t+1))

AvailableActions(t+1) =
  {a : C(t+1, a) = true}

An action changes not only the story but also its future possibility space.

TENSION:
Strict filtering presupposes stable constraints. Character development can invalidate assumptions that were previously useful for excluding combinations.

MISSING:
A representation for belief revision, learning, changing motives, transformations of relationships, and conflicting character perspectives.

BOUNDARY:
Calvino anticipated the problem. The source does not demonstrate that he solved it.

CITATION TRAIL:
Braffort's unpublished synopsis, quoted in the 1997 essay.
Narrative planning and character-state modeling.
Meehan, TALE-SPIN (1977).
Riedl and Young (2010).

TEST:
Run the same twelve-action narrative under two conditions: fixed character compatibility and compatibility updated after every event. Compare which plots become newly possible and which contradictions disappear.

ID: CALVINO-006

TITLE:
The machine becomes a character in a story whose algorithm never included it.

SOURCE:
Paul Braffort — "L'ordre dans le crime" — 1997, section 2, description of the additional plot.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
Waldemar's suspicion introduces Skiller and Waldemar himself into the original four-person mystery. Braffort goes further, counting two programs among the resulting eight actants, one of which may be imaginary.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
An expanding narrative ontology that incorporates its own computational machinery.

LOCAL MOVE:
Braffort distinguishes the basic combinatorial plot from an additional plot in which the investigator, his employer, and the programs become implicated in the crime.

SOURCE TERMS:
"intrigue de base"; "intrigue surajoutée";
"actants"; "programmes"; "machination."

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The computational model is designed for four people, but the fiction becomes interesting precisely when the machinery constructing the model enters the modeled world.

QUESTION:
Is the failure to include the investigator and programs a limitation of the algorithm, or the device that makes Calvino's story literary?

DEEPER QUESTION:
How can a narrative system represent an observer whose investigation changes the events being investigated?

MECHANISM:
The original crime generates an investigation. The investigation creates new suspicions and actions. Those actions make the investigator a participant in another possible crime.

FORMAL SHIFT:
Four-person crime model
→ investigation
→ [investigator and programs become actants]
→ expanded and recursive narrative model.

SOURCE FORMALISM:
Initial combinatorial specification:
4 characters, 12 actions.

The expanded eight-actant structure is described by Braffort, not implemented as a completed computational model.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

World W0 contains characters A–D.

Model M observes W0.
Observer O executes M.
Events implicate O and M.

W1 = W0 ∪ {O, M, new_events}

The system's representation becomes part of the world it must represent.

TENSION:
Natalie Dehn's later AUTHOR model distinguishes the author from the storyworld. Calvino's fiction destabilizes that distinction by making the investigator's modeling operations part of the story.

MISSING:
An operational account of recursive representation, unreliable observation, and the causal effects of investigation.

BOUNDARY:
Braffort's count of eight actants is a retrospective analysis of the fiction, not evidence of an eight-actant program.

CITATION TRAIL:
Calvino, "The Burning of the Abominable House."
Braffort's working synopsis.
Dehn, "Story Generation After TALE-SPIN" (1981).
Borges, "The Garden of Forking Paths."

TEST:
Build the four-person model, then introduce the investigator's actions as events. Determine whether the original constraints can still describe the expanded narrative without adding a higher-order representational layer.
