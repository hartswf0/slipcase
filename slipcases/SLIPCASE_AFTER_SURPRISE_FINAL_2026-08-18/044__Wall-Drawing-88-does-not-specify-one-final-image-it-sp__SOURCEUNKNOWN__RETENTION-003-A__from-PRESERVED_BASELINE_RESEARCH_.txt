ZETTEL

ID:
RETENTION-003-A

TITLE:
Wall Drawing 88 does not specify one final image; it specifies a family of admissible realizations.

SOURCE:
Sol LeWitt — Wall Drawing 88 — June 1971 — primary instruction reproduced in the MASS MoCA Sol LeWitt Wall Drawing Retrospective record.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
LeWitt specifies a six-inch grid and requires each square to contain at least one not-straight line oriented in one of four directions, but leaves the direction, number, and form of those lines open to the draftsman.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
AN INSTRUCTION CAN DETERMINE A POSSIBILITY SPACE WITHOUT DETERMINING A UNIQUE OUTPUT.

LOCAL MOVE:
RETENTION-003 says consequential decisions may be stored upstream in instructions.

Wall Drawing 88 makes the stronger case:

the upstream decision can deliberately store CONSTRAINTS while leaving downstream branches unresolved.

SOURCE TERMS:
grid
not straight lines

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
If the work survives radically different executions, the work cannot be identified with one trajectory through the instruction.

The instruction seems to specify an equivalence class of acceptable paths.

QUESTION:
What makes two visibly different realizations instances of the SAME instruction-based work?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Could the identity of an operative description reside in invariants across realizations rather than in the realization itself?

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

Let:

    C = constraints specified by LeWitt.

Then define the work extensionally as:

    W_C = {x | C(x)=true}

rather than as one privileged output:

    W = x*.

Execution chooses:

    x ∈ W_C.

TENSION:
Not every technically constraint-satisfying realization would necessarily be accepted as a legitimate LeWitt execution.

Institutional practice, drafting conventions, materials, authorization, and tacit knowledge may further restrict W_C.

MISSING:
The full acceptance criteria used by LeWitt, his studio, and later installers to decide whether an execution counts as correct.

BOUNDARY:
Textual constraints alone may underdetermine the actual identity conditions of the work.

TEST:
Produce twenty realizations satisfying the literal instruction for Wall Drawing 88.

Ask experienced LeWitt installers which they would accept.

Any rejected literal-compliant realization reveals additional unwritten constraints.
