TITLE:
Plural coordination requires common reductions without requiring a neutral common world.

SOURCE:
User-provided manuscript / conversation synthesis — 2026-08-16.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
Coordination among heterogeneous actors requires protocols, fields, interfaces, and translations. Successful exchange does not prove neutrality because each common representation preserves some differences better than others.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
PLURAL INTEROPERABILITY.

LOCAL MOVE:
Separate INTEROPERABILITY from ONTOLOGICAL NEUTRALITY.

SOURCE TERMS:
plural
common interface
protocol
translation
interoperability
difference
error

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
A system can coordinate actors successfully while structurally preventing one actor from expressing what matters.

QUESTION:
What status should a plural system assign to differences it cannot presently represent?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Can interoperability preserve disagreement rather than forcing every difference into a common ontology?

MECHANISM:
world A→τA→common C; world B→τB→C; coordination through C while losses L_A≠L_B.

FORMAL SHIFT:
<COMMON REPRESENTATION> → <SHARED OPERABILITY + ASYMMETRIC LOSS>

SOURCE FORMALISM:
NONE

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
Plural interface should distinguish REPRESENTED, TRANSLATED, CONTESTED, CURRENTLY UNREPRESENTABLE rather than only VALID/ERROR.

TENSION:
Too much tolerance for unrepresentability can prevent coordination.

MISSING:
A protocol for acting under unresolved ontological disagreement.

BOUNDARY:
Pluralism requires common structures plus revisability and explicit status for what those structures cannot carry.

CITATION TRAIL:
[[CIRCULATION-001]] → translation → preservation authority → plural coordination.

TEST:
Add explicit UNREPRESENTABLE/CONTESTED states to a shared form and test whether coordination survives without coercive recoding.
