RETENTION-005-R

TITLE:
The prompt history may be the most informative authored artifact even when neither individual outputs nor the final prompt reveal the artist’s intervention.

SOURCE:
U.S. Copyright Office Review Board — Théâtre D’opéra Spatial — 2023; U.S. Copyright Office Part 2 — 2025; Beijing Internet Court — Li v. Liu — 2023.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
The legal sources disagree about iteration, but together expose a missing evidentiary object: not prompt count or final prompt, but the sequence of intended modifications and machine responses through which a practitioner learns and alters a trajectory.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
THE ARTISTIC SITE MAY BE THE PROMPT TRAJECTORY.

LOCAL MOVE:
Locate intervention in repeated PERCEIVE → DECIDE → INTERVENE → TEST rather than one prompt string.

SOURCE TERMS:
revision
prompt words
parameters

output

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
Two people may end with identical final prompt; one copied it, another learned through experiments what its components do. The text is identical; operative knowledge differs.

QUESTION:
Can artistic authorship depend partly on acquired control knowledge that is not visible in the final prompt or final image?

DEEPER QUESTION:
What evidence would distinguish KNOWING HOW TO STEER from merely possessing the same command string?

TENSION:
Copyright protects original expression, not expertise, labor, or method; trajectory can prove craft while adding little protectable expression.

MISSING:
A theory separating CRAFT EVIDENCE, CONTROL EVIDENCE, AUTHORSHIP EVIDENCE, and COPYRIGHTABLE EXPRESSION.

BOUNDARY:
A production path can matter aesthetically and causally even where law declines to protect the knowledge revealed by the path.
