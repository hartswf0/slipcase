RETENTION-005-M-B-A

TITLE:
Andrien creates an expressive-surplus problem for generative AI: an intermediary may contribute non-rote expression while still being legally incapable of authorship.

SOURCE:
Andrien v. Southern Ocean County Chamber of Commerce — 1991; Thaler v. Perlmutter — 2025.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
Andrien preserves delegator authorship where intermediary realization is rote/mechanical; Thaler requires statutory authorship to be human. Together they leave a middle state where machine contribution can be causally substantial but legally nonauthorial.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
NONHUMAN EXPRESSIVE CONTRIBUTION CAN FALL BETWEEN LEGAL CATEGORIES.

LOCAL MOVE:
The law need not choose USER AUTHORED IT or MACHINE AUTHORED IT; some expressive surplus may have no human author.

SOURCE TERMS:
rote
mechanical transcription
intellectual modification

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
A machine may contribute too much to be analogous to rote transcription but cannot occupy the author slot.

QUESTION:
How should a work be analyzed when human-authored constraints coexist with expressive elements whose immediate causal source is a legally nonauthorial machine process?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Does copyright require element-level partition rather than one authorship verdict for the whole output?

MECHANISM:
human supplies C_h; machine resolves open V; y=Expression(C_h,M(V)); Thaler blocks Author(M); Andrien analogy may block attributing all M(V) to H.

BOUNDARY:
