TITLE:
Wall Drawing 289 separates the persistent work from every temporary material realization.

SOURCE:
Sol LeWitt — Wall Drawing #289 — 1976 — instruction/certificate record preserved by the Whitney Museum of American Art.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
The Whitney describes the permanent concrete form of Wall Drawing #289 as its typewritten guidelines and signed certificate of authenticity. Its wall manifestation can be executed anew, with exact angles, line lengths, scale, and configuration varying with draftsmen and architectural setting.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
THE DURABLE ARTIFACT IS THE AUTHORIZED GENERATIVE SPECIFICATION, NOT THE CURRENT OUTPUT.

LOCAL MOVE:
RETENTION-003 says instructions defer realization.

Wall Drawing #289 pushes further:

the realized image can disappear while the work persists as the authority to generate another legitimate realization.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The thing owned and the thing seen are not materially identical.

A wall manifestation can be destroyed without destroying the work’s capacity to be instantiated again.

QUESTION:
What exactly persists when every physical realization is temporary?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Does an instruction-based work require not only a generative description but an authorization mechanism that distinguishes legitimate execution from unauthorized imitation?

MECHANISM:
persistent:

instruction

certificate / ownership authorization

→ installation event

→ temporary physical wall drawing

→ erasure

while:

instruction/certificate persist

→ possible future reinstallation.

TENSION:
Reducing the work to its certificate would be too strong.

The instruction, LeWitt’s authorship, institutional conventions, provenance, materials, and execution practices jointly sustain identity.

MISSING:
The exact legal and studio rules governing transfer, destruction, reinstallation, and unauthorized copies of LeWitt wall drawings.

BOUNDARY:
GENERATIVE DESCRIPTION alone may not be sufficient for work identity.

AUTHORIZED GENERATIVE DESCRIPTION may be the operative object.
