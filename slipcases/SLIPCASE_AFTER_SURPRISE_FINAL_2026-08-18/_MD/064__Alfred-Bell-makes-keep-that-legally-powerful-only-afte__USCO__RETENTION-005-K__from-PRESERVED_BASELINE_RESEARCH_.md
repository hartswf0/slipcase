RETENTION-005-K

TITLE:
Alfred Bell makes “keep that” legally powerful only after the accident has arisen inside a human act of making.

SOURCE:
Alfred Bell & Co. v. Catalda Fine Arts, Inc. — 191 F.2d 99 (2d Cir. 1951); U.S. Copyright Office — 2025.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
Alfred Bell permits copyright in an inadvertent variation arising through a human copyist’s own reproduction and later adopted by the author. The accident occurs within the human act of making.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
ACCIDENTAL ORIGIN and NONHUMAN ORIGIN ARE DIFFERENT QUESTIONS.

LOCAL MOVE:
SURPRISE DOES NOT AUTOMATICALLY DEFEAT AUTHORSHIP, but adoption need not convert every externally produced surprise into one’s authorship.

SOURCE TERMS:
inadvertent variation
author
adopt

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The key variable may be not intention but where the generative deviation arose relative to the claimant’s own creative act.

QUESTION:
How much causal participation before an accidental variation occurs is required for later adoption to count as authorship?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Can AI prompting place the user INSIDE the generative act strongly enough for Alfred Bell’s adoption principle to matter?

MECHANISM:
Alfred Bell: human copying → unintended variation x → recognize/adopt x. GenAI: prompt/setup → model generation → unintended x → recognize/adopt x.

TENSION:
The line between the human’s own process and an external machine process is unstable in highly interactive tools.

MISSING:
A principled account of causal proximity that does not simply privilege bodily manipulation.

BOUNDARY:
Alfred Bell undermines a prospective-intention requirement; it does not make bare selection of arbitrary machine output authorship.
