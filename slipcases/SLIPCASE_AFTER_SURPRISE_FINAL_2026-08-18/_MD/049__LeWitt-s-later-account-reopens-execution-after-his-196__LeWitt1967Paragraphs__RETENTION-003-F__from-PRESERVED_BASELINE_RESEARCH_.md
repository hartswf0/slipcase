ZETTEL

ID:
RETENTION-003-F

TITLE:
LeWitt’s later account reopens execution after his 1967 essay had rhetorically closed it.

SOURCE:
Sol LeWitt — “Paragraphs on Conceptual Art” — 1967.
Sol LeWitt — later statement on wall-drawing realization reproduced by the Museum of Modern Art in its record for Wall Drawing #1187, Scribbles: Curves — 2005.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
In 1967 LeWitt emphasizes that planning and decisions are made beforehand and that execution becomes comparatively perfunctory. In a later account of wall-drawing realization, he compares the relation to a composer and pianist and allows that both parties have room to make a statement of their own.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
EXECUTION RETURNS AS PERFORMANCE.

LOCAL MOVE:
RETENTION-003 inherits the strong 1967 model:

CONCEPTION
→ EXECUTION.

Later LeWitt complicates it:

CONCEPTION
→ PERFORMANCE WITH EXECUTOR CONTRIBUTION.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The artist most useful for proving that decisions can precede execution also becomes evidence that execution need not be mechanically empty.

QUESTION:
Did LeWitt’s understanding of executor agency change, or were performative contributions compatible with his conceptual framework from the beginning?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Can an instruction simultaneously determine the identity of a work and authorize another person to contribute expressive difference during realization?

MECHANISM:
artist establishes concept/instructions

→ executor interprets and realizes them

→ physical/material decisions occur

→ result remains a realization of LeWitt’s work while also containing executor-specific contribution.

FORMAL SHIFT:
<EXECUTION AS PERFUNCTORY>
→ <EXECUTION AS CONSTRAINED PERFORMANCE>

TENSION:
The 1967 and later statements may describe different bodies of work or different explanatory emphases rather than a theoretical reversal.

MISSING:
The date and original context of LeWitt’s composer/pianist statement and its relation to his earlier conceptual-art writings.

BOUNDARY:
DEFERRED EXECUTION does not entail MECHANICALLY EMPTY EXECUTION.
