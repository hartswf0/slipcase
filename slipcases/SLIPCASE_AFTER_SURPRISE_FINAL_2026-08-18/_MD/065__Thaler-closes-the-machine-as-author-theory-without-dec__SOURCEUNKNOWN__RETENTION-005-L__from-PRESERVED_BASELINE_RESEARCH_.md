ZETTEL

ID:
RETENTION-005-L

TITLE:
Thaler closes the machine-as-author theory without deciding the prompt-user-as-author theory.

SOURCE:
Thaler v. Perlmutter — 130 F.4th 1039 (D.C. Cir. 2025).

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
Thaler holds that copyrightable work must initially be authored by a human where the application named the Creativity Machine as sole author. The court did not reach Thaler’s separate claim that he himself should count as author through making and using the machine because that theory was not preserved before the agency.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
THE MOST IMPORTANT PROMPT-AUTHORSHIP QUESTION SURVIVES THALER.

LOCAL MOVE:
Thaler answers WHO CANNOT OCCUPY THE AUTHOR SLOT; it does not resolve which human-directed generative workflows contain human authorship.

SOURCE TERMS:
human authorship
machine
sole author
made and used
waiver
Copyright Act

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
“Thaler says AI art is not copyrightable” is broader than the holding.

QUESTION:
What federal appellate doctrine will eventually govern the amount and type of human control necessary for prompt-mediated authorship?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Could a court accept human authorship without deciding that the machine is a coauthor, by treating the machine as causal machinery inside the human process?

MECHANISM:
machine = sole author → rejected. Separate architecture human → prompt/configure/select/edit → machine → output remains element-specific.

FORMAL SHIFT:
<WHO MAY BE AN AUTHOR?> → <WHICH EXPRESSIVE ELEMENTS DID THE HUMAN AUTHOR?>

SOURCE FORMALISM:
NONE

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
Thaler supports Author(machine)=false. It does not establish that every y generated from human prompt p is nonauthored by h.

TENSION:
Whatever later theory succeeds must still locate copyrightable expression in human creative activity.

MISSING:
Controlling appellate precedent evaluating a rich human prompting and iterative-control record on the merits.

BOUNDARY:
Do not cite Thaler as direct authority that prompts cannot constitute authorship.
