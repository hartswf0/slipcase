RETENTION-005-N-C

TITLE:
Approval can convert machine-added prompt content from hidden intervention into an explicit human commitment.

SOURCE:
COLLISION — Prompt Expansion — 2024; U.S. Copyright Office — Part 2 — 2025.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
Automatic prompt systems can add details not in the initial query. Human review can then accept, edit, or reject those commitments before generation.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
HUMAN ADOPTION CAN OCCUR BEFORE GENERATION.

LOCAL MOVE:
The boundary between PROMPTING and SELECTION can occur inside the prompt pipeline.

SOURCE TERMS:
prompt expansion
selection
control
modification
human authorship
expanded prompt

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The machine can propose an expressive commitment and the human can ratify it before execution.

QUESTION:
Does reviewing and approving a machine-expanded prompt change the authorship analysis compared with invisible automatic expansion?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Is VETO POWER over machine-added expressive commitments itself a consequential creative operation?

MECHANISM:
q → machine p_eff → human ACCEPT/EDIT/REJECT → approved p′ → generator → y.

FORMAL SHIFT:
<HIDDEN REWRITE> → <PROPOSED EXPRESSIVE MATERIAL> → [HUMAN RATIFICATION] → <AUTHORIZED OPERATIVE PROMPT>

SOURCE FORMALISM:
NONE

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
C_added generated by machine; HumanApprove(C_added) converts it into an adopted constraint, not necessarily human-originated expression.

TENSION:
Mere approval of another creator’s expression does not ordinarily make one its author.

MISSING:
A doctrine distinguishing approval, selection, editing, and authorship at the pre-generation prompt stage.

BOUNDARY:
Pre-generation adoption strengthens causal-control evidence without automatically transferring authorship of machine-originated wording.

TEST:
Compare hidden rewrite, visible read-only rewrite, and editable required-approval rewrite; measure alignment and unwanted additions.
