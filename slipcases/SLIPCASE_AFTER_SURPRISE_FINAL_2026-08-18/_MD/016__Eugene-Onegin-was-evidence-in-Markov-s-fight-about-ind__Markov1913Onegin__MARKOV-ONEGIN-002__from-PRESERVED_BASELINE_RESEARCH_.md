TITLE:
Eugene Onegin was evidence in Markov’s fight about independence, not the object his mathematics was designed to explain.

SOURCE:
A. A. Markov — work on chains of dependent trials, 1906–1913.
Brian Hayes — “First Links in the Markov Chain” — 2013 — historical reconstruction of Markov’s dispute with Pavel Nekrasov.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
Nekrasov had connected the law of large numbers to statistical independence and extended that connection into arguments about voluntary action and free will. Markov developed limit results for dependent sequences, undermining the necessity of independence. His later Onegin calculation provided a concrete sequence in which adjacent events were dependent.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
PUSHKIN IS AN EXPERIMENTAL WEAPON IN A PROBABILITY DISPUTE.

LOCAL MOVE:
The essay’s line:

“Markov’s stripped poem is aimed at no one. Markov is.”

can be hardened historically.

The target of the reduction is an argument about independence.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The most famous early mathematical encounter between probability and literature was not primarily an attempt to understand language.

Literature supplied a convenient observable chain.

QUESTION:
Why did Markov choose literary text as his empirical demonstration rather than another dependent sequence?

DEEPER QUESTION:
How often do later “models of culture” originate as demonstrations of mathematical machinery whose original explanatory target lay elsewhere?
