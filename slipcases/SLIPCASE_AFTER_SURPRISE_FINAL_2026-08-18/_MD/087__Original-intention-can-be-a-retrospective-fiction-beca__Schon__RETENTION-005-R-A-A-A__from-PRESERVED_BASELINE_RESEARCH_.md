RETENTION-005-R-A-A-A

TITLE:
“Original intention” can be a retrospective fiction because intentions themselves are outputs of making.

SOURCE:
Donald A. Schön — “Designing as Reflective Conversation with the Materials of a Design Situation” — 1992.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
In Schön’s move experiments, a designer makes a move, evaluates unintended consequences, and develops a more specific intention through the intermediate artifact. Intention itself develops during designing.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
INTENTION HAS A TRAJECTORY.

LOCAL MOVE:
A complete pre-generation intention is not required, but retrospective “I always wanted that” also cannot be taken at face value.

SOURCE TERMS:
move experiment
evolving intention
unintended consequences
appreciation
seeing-moving-seeing
conversation

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
A legal test demanding complete prior intent can misdescribe exploratory making; unrestricted retrospective intent can launder accidents.

QUESTION:
How can we preserve intention formation without permitting intention laundering?

DEEPER QUESTION:
What evidence distinguishes THE RESULT TAUGHT ME WHAT I WANTED from I DECIDED I HAD WANTED THE RESULT AFTER IT APPEARED?

MECHANISM:
I0→move→S1→surprise u→appreciation→I1→later interventions preserve/develop u.

FORMAL SHIFT:
<INTENTION BEFORE MAKING> → <INTENTION AS STATE VARIABLE EVOLVING DURING MAKING>

SOURCE FORMALISM:
Schön describes intentions developing through appreciation of intermediate products/unintended consequences.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
Legitimate discovered intention: Surprise(u,t1) then Commit(u,t2>t1) then later intervention t3 preserves/modifies u.

TENSION:
Later commitment can still be narrated falsely unless production trace records behavior.

MISSING:
A nonverbal criterion for commitment when artists do not narrate intentions during work.

BOUNDARY:
Prior intention can be strong evidence but is not a universal requirement for creative authorship.

CITATION TRAIL:

TEST:
After an unplanned feature appears, ignore verbal explanation and observe subsequent sacrifices/choices made to preserve or elaborate it.
