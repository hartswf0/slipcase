RETENTION-005-S-B

TITLE:
Stern Electronics suggests a narrower replacement for distributional authorship: authorship of invariants across variable realizations.

SOURCE:
Stern Electronics v. Kaufman — 1982; Williams Electronics v. Artic International — 1982.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
Both game cases emphasize substantial recurring images, sounds, and sequences across player-variable performances.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
COPYRIGHT ALREADY HANDLES VARIABLE OUTPUTS BY LOOKING FOR STABLE EXPRESSIVE INVARIANTS.

LOCAL MOVE:
Replace abstract distributional authorship with a legally more familiar question: what expressive form remains invariant across realizations?

SOURCE TERMS:
repetitive sequence
constant
variation
player
fixation
audiovisual work
memory

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
Law need not choose between one exact token and an abstract probability distribution; it can locate recurring structure.

QUESTION:
Could prompt-mediated authorship be analyzed by identifying expressive invariants deliberately imposed across multiple generations?

DEEPER QUESTION:
When does a recurring invariant come from the user’s constraint rather than the pretrained generator’s default?

MECHANISM:
generate y1...yn → extract Inv({y_i}) → ask which invariant is attributable to human constraint versus model.

FORMAL SHIFT:
<DISTRIBUTIONAL AUTHORSHIP> → <AUTHORSHIP OF CROSS-REALIZATION INVARIANTS>

SOURCE FORMALISM:
Game cases reason from recurring audiovisual elements, not probability distributions.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
H_invariant=Inv(Y|human control c)-Inv(Y|baseline).

TENSION:
Game invariants were authored and stored; prompt-derived invariants may be statistical effects rather than fixed expression.

MISSING:
A fixation theory for expressive constraints persisting statistically rather than deterministically.

BOUNDARY:
The cases are analogies for variable works, not authority that prompt-induced invariants are copyrightable.

CITATION TRAIL:
[[RETENTION-005-S]] → Stern/Williams → variable sequence → recurring invariant → test prompt-authored invariants.

TEST:
Generate 100 outputs under artist setup and baseline; find stable properties significantly more persistent under artist condition and verify they were conceived.
