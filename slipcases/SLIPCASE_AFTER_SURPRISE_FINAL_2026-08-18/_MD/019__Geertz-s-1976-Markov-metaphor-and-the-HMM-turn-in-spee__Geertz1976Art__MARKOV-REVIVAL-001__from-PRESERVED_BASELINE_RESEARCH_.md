MARKOV-REVIVAL-001

TITLE:
Geertz’s 1976 Markov metaphor and the HMM turn in speech recognition are a real chronological collision with no demonstrated genealogy between them.

SOURCE:
Clifford Geertz — “Art as a Cultural System” — 1976.
James K. Baker — “Stochastic Modeling as a Means of Automatic Speech Recognition” — Carnegie Mellon University doctoral work — 1975.
Carnegie Mellon historical accounts of speech recognition.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
Geertz publishes the phrase “artistic Markov process” in 1976. At Carnegie Mellon, James Baker’s mid-1970s work applies hidden Markov modeling to speech recognition, part of an emerging statistical approach that later became highly consequential in automatic speech recognition.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
THE COINCIDENCE IS STRONGER THAN THE GENEALOGY.

LOCAL MOVE:
The essay’s timing joke survives if stated as parallel chronology rather than intellectual transmission.

SOURCE TERMS:
Markov process
hidden Markov model
speech recognition
stochastic modeling
1975
1976

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
At almost the same historical moment:

one field could treat Markov as an old reductive model of language,

another could be discovering that Markovian latent-state machinery was extraordinarily productive for recognizing speech,

and Geertz could deploy “Markov” casually inside anthropology.

QUESTION:
Was Geertz aware of contemporary statistical speech-recognition research?

DEEPER QUESTION:
How can one mathematical form be simultaneously declining as a THEORY OF LANGUAGE and ascending as an ENGINEERING MODEL OF SIGNAL-TO-LANGUAGE INFERENCE?

OUR FORMALIZATION:

TENSION:
Chronological simultaneity is intellectually suggestive but proves no influence.

“Markov looked old” also requires disciplinary reception evidence rather than inference from Chomsky alone.

MISSING:
Citation/reception studies of Markov models in linguistics between 1957 and 1976.

Any evidence that Geertz encountered Baker, speech-recognition research, or information-processing discussions of HMMs.

BOUNDARY:
The juxtaposition is historically defensible.

The claim that Geertz’s metaphor was “safe because Markov had lost” remains [OUR INFERENCE] until reception evidence is found.
