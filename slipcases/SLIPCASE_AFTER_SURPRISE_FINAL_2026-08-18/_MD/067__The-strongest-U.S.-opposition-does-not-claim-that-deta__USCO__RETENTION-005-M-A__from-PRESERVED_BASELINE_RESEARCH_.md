RETENTION-005-M-A

TITLE:
The strongest U.S. opposition does not claim that detailed prompts are automatically authorship; it makes reflected conception the hinge.

SOURCE:
Pamela Samuelson, Christopher Jon Sprigman, and Matthew Sag — Comment to U.S. Copyright Office AI Notice of Inquiry — 2023; U.S. Copyright Office — Part 2 — 2025.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
Samuelson, Sprigman, and Sag agree simple prompting is ordinarily insufficient but argue no reason exists in principle that a prompt could never be detailed enough if the result reflects the human original conception.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
PROMPT DETAIL is evidentiary; REFLECTED CONCEPTION is the opposing criterion.

LOCAL MOVE:
Both sides can agree that effort and simple prompts are weak; the dispute begins over when mediation severs conception from fixed expression.

SOURCE TERMS:
original intellectual conception
detailed prompts
human authorship
control
final form
prompting

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The opposition is much narrower than PROMPTS ARE AUTHORSHIP.

QUESTION:
How much of a human conception must remain perceptible in the output before machine interpretation becomes realization rather than independent expressive contribution?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Does copyright require control of the causal process, or sufficiently specific correspondence between prior conception and final expressive form?

MECHANISM:
human conception C → prompt p → model M → y; evaluate correspondence(C,y), not prompt length.
