ZETTEL

ID:
FRONTIER-001

TITLE:
Later stabilization of a surprise does not travel backward and make the maker the originator of the surprise.

SOURCE:
U.S. Copyright Office — Copyright and Artificial Intelligence, Part 2: Copyrightability — 2025; United States’ 2026 summary-judgment brief in Allen v. Perlmutter.

SOURCE URL:
https://www.copyright.gov/ai/docs/us-reply-brief-and-cross-motion-for-summary-judgment.pdf

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
The Copyright Office distinguishes AI-generated expressive material from later human-authored contributions such as selection, arrangement, and modification. In the Allen litigation, the government argues that Allen did not author the underlying Midjourney visual expression while noting that directly human-authored additions could be registered if the AI-generated material were disclaimed.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
TEMPORAL FRONTIER OF AUTHORSHIP.

LOCAL MOVE:
Keep downstream human contribution strong without retroactively reallocating the origin of an unplanned expressive event.

SOURCE TERMS:
human authorship
AI-generated material
selection
arrangement
modification
disclaimer
temporal attribution

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The same feature can have two histories: an initial occurrence whose expressive form was not human-authored and a later career in which a human deliberately preserves, relates, or transforms it.

QUESTION:
Where does human authorship begin in the history of an initially unplanned generated feature?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Can attribution attach to the subsequent organization built around a surprise without attaching to the surprise’s first occurrence?

MECHANISM:
At t1 system produces x unexpectedly → at t2 human recognizes x → at t3 human commits to preserve/develop x → later interventions make surrounding structure depend on x.

FORMAL SHIFT:
<RETROSPECTIVE ADOPTION> → <FORWARD-LOOKING ATTRIBUTION FRONTIER>

SOURCE FORMALISM:
U.S. copyright doctrine requires human authorship and recognizes protectable human selection, arrangement, and modification; it does not state the temporal-frontier theory proposed here.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
If Origin(x,t1)=machine-mediated/unattributed and Commit_h(x,t2), then later human contribution may begin at t2/t3 in relations and transformations governed by x. Commit_h(x,t2) does not imply Origin_h(x,t1).

TENSION:
Later human work can become extensive enough that separating underlying generated material from human-authored organization is practically difficult.

MISSING:
Case law squarely addressing a temporally layered feature whose initial generated form is retained but whose later relational role is heavily human-shaped.

BOUNDARY:
This is a research criterion and attribution model, not a statement of current U.S. copyright law.

CITATION TRAIL:
[[RETENTION-005]] → post-generation contribution → Allen/USCO → surprise can become a future constraint without retroactive origin.

TEST:
For one unexpected feature, compare the state before its appearance, the first retention decision, and every later intervention. Attribute only what becomes prospectively constrained after recognition.
