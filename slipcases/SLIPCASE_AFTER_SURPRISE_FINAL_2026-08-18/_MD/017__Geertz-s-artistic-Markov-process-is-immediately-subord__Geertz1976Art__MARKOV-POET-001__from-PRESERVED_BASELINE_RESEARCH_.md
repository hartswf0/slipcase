ZETTEL

ID:
MARKOV-POET-001

TITLE:
Geertz’s “artistic Markov process” is immediately subordinated to composition-in-performance.

SOURCE:
Clifford Geertz — “Art as a Cultural System” — 1976 — Moroccan oral-poetry discussion.
Albert B. Lord — The Singer of Tales — 1960 — composition-in-performance lineage explicitly invoked by Geertz.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
Geertz says the Moroccan poet builds the poem incrementally from a bounded repertoire of thematic, figurative, and formal formulae, comparing this local assembly process to an “artistic Markov process.” But he immediately embeds that molecular construction inside singing, percussion, dance, genre demands, approval, and censure, insisting that the poem cannot be separated from the performance and is continually remade as an act.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
The Markov analogy applies to one layer of production:

FORMULA SELECTION / SEQUENTIAL ASSEMBLY.

It does not describe the whole ontology of the poem.

LOCAL MOVE:
The essay’s strongest Geertz passage contains its own warning against reducing the cultural act to its generative mechanism.

SOURCE TERMS:
artistic Markov process
formulae

singing

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
Geertz simultaneously says:

THE POEM IS MARKOV-LIKE

and:

THE POEM CANNOT BE ABSTRACTED FROM THE PERFORMANCE.

The apparent contradiction disappears if the Markov description is only one projection of a larger event.

QUESTION:
What exactly is the object to which Geertz applies the Markov analogy:
the sequence of verses,
formula selection,
poetic competence,
or the entire performance?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Can one process possess a perfectly useful low-dimensional generative description while that description remains radically insufficient as an account of the act?

SOURCE FORMALISM:
NONE.

Geertz offers a comparison, not a transition matrix, state definition, Markov property, or estimated stochastic model.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

Let:

    P = full performance process

and:

    π(P) = sequence of formula selections.

A Markov approximation might describe:

    π(P)

without implying:

    P = π(P).

TENSION:
The metaphor may still contain more technical content than casual ornament because Geertz stresses bounded formula stock and piecewise sequential construction.

But nothing in the passage establishes the Markov property formally.

MISSING:
Evidence concerning what Geertz knew about Markov models and whether “Markov process” was intended technically or colloquially.

BOUNDARY:
The passage supports:

FORMULAIC GENERATION IS PART OF THE POEM’S MAKING.

It does not support:

THE POEM IS EXHAUSTIVELY EXPLAINED BY A MARKOV PROCESS.

TEST:
Take Geertz’s performance description and assign every described element to one of:

SEQUENTIAL GENERATION
FORMAL CONSTRAINT
FEEDBACK
SOCIAL AUTHORITY
ECONOMIC INCENTIVE
TARGET SELECTION
BODILY PERFORMANCE
AUDIENCE RESPONSE.

Then ask which columns survive if the event is represented only as a first-order Markov chain over formulae.
