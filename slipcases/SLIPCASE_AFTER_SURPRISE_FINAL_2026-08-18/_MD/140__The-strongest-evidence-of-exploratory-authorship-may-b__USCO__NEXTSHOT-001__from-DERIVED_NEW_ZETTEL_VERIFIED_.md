TITLE:
The strongest evidence of exploratory authorship may be neither predicting the first result nor liking it afterward, but making a discovered difference govern what happens next.

PASSAGE:
[OUR INFERENCE]
Schön shows that intentions and relevant distinctions can emerge through intermediate results. The Copyright Office rejects iteration that merely resamples. Samuelson, Sprigman, and Sag defend exploratory development. Midway prevents causal trajectory control from becoming sufficient for authorship, while Stern directs attention to recurring authored structure. Together these sources support a narrower discriminator: a surprise becomes evidence of authorship-like agency when it is converted into a prospective constraint that materially structures later realizations.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
PROSPECTIVE STABILIZATION AFTER SURPRISE.

LOCAL MOVE:
Replace “call the shot” with a temporally more demanding test: CALL THE NEXT SHOT.

SOURCE TERMS:
evolving intention

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
Accident and control are not opposites. Accident can be the event that creates a new controllable distinction.

QUESTION:
Can a production trace identify the moment when an attractive accident stops being merely adopted and becomes a constraint governing later action?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Could this provide a non-binary evidentiary bridge between prior-specification theories of authorship and pure post-hoc selection?

MECHANISM:
1. Surprise u appears at t1. 2. Maker perceives u as consequential at t2. 3. Maker performs intervention a at t3 intended to preserve/alter u. 4. Alternative trajectories where u would disappear are rejected or corrected. 5. u or its transformed successor persists into later state y_n because of those interventions.

FORMAL SHIFT:
<PREDICT RESULT?> / <SELECT RESULT?> → <DISCOVER → COMMIT → INTERVENE → STABILIZE>

SOURCE FORMALISM:
NONE combining these sources.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
ProspectiveStabilization(u)=Surprise(u,t1) ∧ Commit(u,t2) ∧ ∃a_{t>t2}[do(a) causally increases preservation or controlled transformation of u in later states].

TENSION:
A consumer can learn a preference and continue searching for it. The test must therefore establish causal intervention that changes future production, not repeated filtering alone.

MISSING:
Empirical validation against painters, photographers, procedural artists, game players, and generative-system users.

BOUNDARY:
This is a research hypothesis and evidentiary test. It is not current copyright doctrine and does not make all iterative use authorial.

TEST:
Run matched studies in which an unforeseen feature first appears. In one condition participants may only select among subsequent outputs. In another they can intervene to preserve or transform the feature. Measure whether later states retain the feature because of intervention rather than filtering, and compare attribution judgments.
