RETENTION-005-J

TITLE:
The same prompting act can be classified as insufficient authorship, personalized expression, or a statutory “arrangement necessary for creation,” depending on jurisdiction.

SOURCE:
U.S. Copyright Office — 2025; Beijing Internet Court — Li v. Liu — 2023; UK Copyright, Designs and Patents Act 1988 §9(3); UK Government copyright-and-AI report — 2026.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
U.S. doctrine asks whether human expressive elements are sufficiently authored; the Beijing judgment treated a specific prompt-and-parameter workflow as personalized expression; UK §9(3) contains a special rule deeming the person making necessary arrangements to be author of a computer-generated work.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
THE LAW DOES NOT DISCOVER ONE NATURAL LOCATION OF AUTHORSHIP.

LOCAL MOVE:
Comparative law reveals at least three attribution logics: EXPRESSION, CREATIVE CHOICE, and ARRANGEMENT FOR PRODUCTION.

SOURCE TERMS:
human authorship
personalized expression
arrangements necessary
computer-generated work
prompt
originality

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The same technical workflow can receive a different legal author without any change to the person or machine.

QUESTION:
What normative purpose is each authorship rule trying to serve: reward expression, recognize creative choice, assign ownership efficiently, or incentivize investment?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Should operative-description theory distinguish CAUSAL AUTHORSHIP from LEGAL AUTHORSHIP rather than seeking one boundary for both?

MECHANISM:
same chain human → prompt → AI system → output; attribution rule A protects perceptible human expression; B infers personalized expression from choices; C deems arranger author.

FORMAL SHIFT:
<ONE CAUSAL PATH> → [DIFFERENT NORMATIVE ATTRIBUTION FUNCTIONS] → <DIFFERENT LEGAL AUTHORS>

SOURCE FORMALISM:
UK §9(3) uses a statutory deeming rule for computer-generated works.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
For one history H: Author_US(H), Author_CN(H), Author_UK(H) need not be equal.

TENSION:
The doctrines are not equivalent: the UK rule is a statutory fiction, Beijing found personalized expression, and U.S. law insists on human authorship.

MISSING:
Cases applying UK §9(3) directly to modern generative-AI prompting.

BOUNDARY:
Comparative law reveals normative allocation layered onto causal history; it cannot tell us who “really” authored the artifact.
