ZETTEL

ID:
RETENTION-004

TITLE:
Duchamp makes selection itself intentional while attempting to remove aesthetic preference from the selection criterion.

SOURCE:
Marcel Duchamp — “Apropos of ‘Readymades’” — talk delivered at the Museum of Modern Art, New York — October 19, 1961; published 1966.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
Duchamp insists that his readymade choices were not dictated by aesthetic pleasure but sought what he describes as visual indifference and an absence of judgments of good or bad taste.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
SELECTION is not equivalent to:

THAT OUTCOME LOOKED BEST.

LOCAL MOVE:
The parent’s unresolved RETENTION problem imagines:

ACCIDENT
→ RECOGNITION OF ATTRACTIVE DIFFERENCE
→ KEEP THAT.

Duchamp supplies a deliberately antagonistic case:

OBJECT
→ CHOOSE IT
while attempting NOT to prefer it aesthetically.

SOURCE TERMS:
choice
readymade
visual indifference
taste
aesthetic delectation
anesthesia

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
Selection can be highly intentional while suppressing the very discriminatory judgment that “call the shot” treats as evidence of control.

QUESTION:
What kind of agency is exercised when the maker controls WHICH OBJECT IS ADMITTED while refusing to control its aesthetic properties?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Must agency involve preference over expressive features, or can it consist in changing an object’s status, frame, or career?

MECHANISM:
existing manufactured object
→ artist encounters/selects object
→ naming/inscription/presentation
→ object enters new institutional/artistic relation.

FORMAL SHIFT:
<SELECT AMONG AESTHETIC OUTCOMES>
→ <SELECT WHILE SUSPENDING AESTHETIC OUTCOME PREFERENCE>
→ <STATUS / FRAME TRANSFORMATION>

SOURCE FORMALISM:
NONE

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

Separate:

    Selection(x)

from:

    Preference_feature(x,f).

It is possible that:

    Selection(x)=true

while:

    ∂Preference / ∂f ≈ 0

for the aesthetic dimensions the chooser is attempting to neutralize.

TENSION:
Perfect indifference may be impossible.

Choosing this object rather than another inevitably introduces some discrimination, even if the criterion is anti-aesthetic or conceptual.

MISSING:
What positive criterion governed Duchamp’s individual selections once aesthetic pleasure was excluded?

BOUNDARY:
Retention is evidence that a choice occurred.

It is not sufficient evidence about WHAT PROPERTY the chooser controlled or valued.

CITATION TRAIL:
[[PATH-001]]

TEST:
For any retained generative output ask independently:

1. WHY was this output retained?
2. WHICH feature caused retention?
3. Was the feature prospectively targeted?
4. Was retention aesthetic, conceptual, functional, evidentiary, accidental, or arbitrary?

If these cannot be answered, KEEP THAT remains causally underspecified.
