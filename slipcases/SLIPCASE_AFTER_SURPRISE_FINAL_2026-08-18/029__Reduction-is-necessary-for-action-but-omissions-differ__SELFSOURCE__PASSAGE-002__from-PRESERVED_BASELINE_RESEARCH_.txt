TITLE:
Reduction is necessary for action, but omissions differ by recoverability.

SOURCE:
User-provided manuscript / conversation synthesis — 2026-08-16.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
Every representation reduces because an unreduced world is unusable. Omissions differ: some are benign, some recoverable, some expressible elsewhere, some presently inexpressible, and some destroy reconstruction conditions.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
A TAXONOMY OF REPRESENTATIONAL ABSENCE.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
Two representations can omit the same quantity of information while differing radically in whether the missing relation can later be reconstructed.

QUESTION:
What are the minimal types of representational loss?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Should adequacy depend less on retained information quantity than on whether consequentially relevant information remains recoverable before action becomes irreversible?

MECHANISM:

OUR FORMALIZATION:

Recoverability_R(x,t) depends on provenance, alternate representations, time remaining, and reconstruction operations. Critical loss occurs when consequence precedes feasible recovery.

TENSION:
Recoverability is actor- and resource-relative. Theoretically recoverable information may be practically inaccessible.

MISSING:

Loss is not automatically injustice or inadequacy; consequence and recovery conditions matter.
