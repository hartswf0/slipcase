ID:
RETENTION-006

TITLE:
“Keep that” is not one operation; it contains at least four different causal structures.

SOURCE:
COLLISION — John Cage on chance operations; Marcel Duchamp on readymade choice; Sol LeWitt on decisions prior to execution; Philip Galanter on autonomous generative systems; U.S. Copyright Office on selection, arrangement, and modification.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
The sources distribute artistic decision across radically different stages: Cage designs a process while refusing outcome preference; Duchamp selects while resisting aesthetic preference; LeWitt front-loads decisions into instructions; Galanter defines generative practice through autonomous systems; current copyright analysis recognizes creative downstream arrangement and modification.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
RETENTION must split.

LOCAL MOVE:
The parent treated the accidental result followed by KEEP THAT as one unresolved case.

The collision reveals at least four.

SOURCE TERMS:
choice

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The identical surface gesture:

KEEP THIS

may perform radically different operations.

QUESTION:
Which forms of retention alter the causal path strongly enough to count as a consequential branch?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Can agency be located by identifying WHAT FUTURE POSSIBILITY SPACE the retention act changes?

MECHANISM:
CASE 1 — ACCEPTANCE:

system produces x
→ maker permits x to stand.

CASE 2 — SELECTION:

{x₁,...,xₙ}
→ maker chooses x_k.

CASE 3 — COMMITMENT:

x_k
→ maker freezes property f
→ subsequent production must preserve f.

CASE 4 — RECOMPOSITION:

{x_i}
→ maker selects/arranges/modifies
→ new structure y.

FORMAL SHIFT:
<KEEP THAT>
→ {
    <ACCEPT>,
    <SELECT>,
    <COMMIT>,
    <RECOMPOSE>
  }

SOURCE FORMALISM:
NONE

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

Let Ω_t be the possibility space before retention.

Define retention operation R by its effect on future possibilities:

    R : Ω_t → Ω_{t+1}.

ACCEPT:
    little or no further production follows.

SELECT:
    Ω shrinks to chosen candidate.

COMMIT:
    Ω shrinks to candidates preserving feature f.

RECOMPOSE:
    selected material becomes input to a new transformation space.

TENSION:
These operations frequently occur together.

Selecting one image may simultaneously commit its composition, trigger further edits, and reject thousands of alternatives.

MISSING:
An empirical annotation method that can distinguish these operations in real creative histories.

BOUNDARY:
“Selection” is too coarse to serve as evidence of control until its downstream causal effect is specified.
