RETENTION-005-O-B

TITLE:
CONTU’s influential computer-as-tool model depended on a technical premise that modern generative systems no longer straightforwardly satisfy.

SOURCE:
National Commission on New Technological Uses of Copyrighted Works — Final Report — 1978; discussed in Thaler v. Perlmutter — 2025.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
CONTU treated computers as instruments activated by humans that performed what humans directed them to perform, not as contributing authors.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
A DURABLE LEGAL CLASSIFICATION WAS BUILT AROUND A HISTORICALLY SPECIFIC MACHINE MODEL.

LOCAL MOVE:
Expose the hidden technical ontology: COMPUTER = INERT EXECUTIVE INSTRUMENT.

SOURCE TERMS:
computer
inert instrument
activated
directed
author
CONTU

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
Generative systems can resolve unspecified variables, rewrite prompts, sample alternatives, and introduce detail not explicitly directed.

QUESTION:
Does the human-authorship rule logically depend on CONTU’s inert-instrument account, or is that account only historical evidence?

DEEPER QUESTION:
What happens when a legal category survives the technical ontology that once made it intuitively obvious?

MECHANISM:
CONTU: H→explicit d→C→mechanical realization. Generative: H→partial p→M→resolves open variables→y.

FORMAL SHIFT:
<INSTRUMENT EXECUTES DIRECTION> → <SYSTEM COMPLETES UNDERDETERMINED DIRECTION>

SOURCE FORMALISM:
NONE

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
Let U(p) be unresolved expressive variables after user input; generative systems can leave U large before machine realization.

TENSION:
Thaler’s human-authorship holding does not require AI to be technically inert.

MISSING:
How courts will characterize systems that are neither authors nor inert instruments.

BOUNDARY:
Erosion of CONTU’s analogy does not itself overturn the human-authorship rule.
