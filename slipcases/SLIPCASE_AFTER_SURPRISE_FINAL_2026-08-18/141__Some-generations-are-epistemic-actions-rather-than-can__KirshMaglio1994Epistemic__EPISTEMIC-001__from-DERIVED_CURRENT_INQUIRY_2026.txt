ZETTEL

ID:
EPISTEMIC-001

TITLE:
Some generations are epistemic actions rather than candidate artworks.

SOURCE:
David Kirsh and Paul Maglio — “On Distinguishing Epistemic from Pragmatic Action” — Cognitive Science 18(4) — 1994.

SOURCE URL:
https://doi.org/10.1207/s15516709cog1804_1

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
Kirsh and Maglio distinguish pragmatic actions that advance an external task from epistemic actions performed to improve the agent’s information or computation. In their Tetris analysis, an action can return the physical world to its prior state while leaving the player better informed.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
EPISTEMIC GENERATION.

LOCAL MOVE:
Split GENERATE into at least GENERATE-TO-MAKE, GENERATE-TO-SELECT, and GENERATE-TO-LEARN.

SOURCE TERMS:
epistemic action
pragmatic action
information
external computation
generation
learning

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
A discarded output can be causally central to making even when nobody ever considered keeping it. It can change the maker’s knowledge of the system or problem.

QUESTION:
When a generative output is produced primarily to learn a model’s behavior, what evidence does it provide about creative agency?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Does copyright analysis risk conflating sampling for possession with sampling for cognition when both appear as generate-inspect-revise logs?

MECHANISM:
Question or uncertainty → generation → perceptible response → updated human model of system/problem → later intervention.

FORMAL SHIFT:
<GENERATION AS CANDIDATE PRODUCTION> → <GENERATION AS EXTERNALIZED INQUIRY>

SOURCE FORMALISM:
Kirsh and Maglio formalize epistemic versus pragmatic action conceptually; they do not discuss generative AI or copyright.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
Let G_t be a generation event. Classify its immediate purpose as MAKE, SELECT, or LEARN. LEARN events update an internal model Ĝ_h even when their outputs are discarded.

TENSION:
One event can be both epistemic and pragmatic: an output can teach the maker something and later enter the work.

MISSING:
A behavioral discriminator that does not rely entirely on retrospective self-report of why an output was generated.

BOUNDARY:
Epistemic generation is evidence of inquiry and learning, not by itself authorship of any generated expressive element.

CITATION TRAIL:
[[RETENTION-005-R]] → prompt trajectory → Kirsh/Maglio → generation can be an information-seeking action → distinguish experimental probing from shopping.

TEST:
For a recorded workflow, label generations prospectively when possible as MAKE, SELECT, or LEARN. Test whether LEARN events change later interventions more than they change candidate preference.
