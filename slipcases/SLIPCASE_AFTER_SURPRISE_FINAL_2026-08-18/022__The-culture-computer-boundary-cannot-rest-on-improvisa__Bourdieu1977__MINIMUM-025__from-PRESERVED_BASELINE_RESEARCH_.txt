ZETTEL

ID:
MINIMUM-025

TITLE:
The culture/computer boundary cannot rest on improvisation versus exact semantics because formally exact semantics can describe distributions of improvisatory outcomes.

SOURCE:
COLLISION — Bourdieu 1977; Dal Lago and Zorzi 2012. Bourdieu characterizes practice through regulated improvisation; probabilistic operational semantics formally assigns distributions over computational outcomes. 22

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
One source rejects social practice as conscious obedience to explicit rules; the other shows that operational semantics need not determine one unique run.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
UNDERDETERMINED BEHAVIOR and FORMAL SEMANTICS are compatible.

LOCAL MOVE:
The collision removes a false opposition between improvisation and mathematical operational description.

SOURCE TERMS:
regulated improvisation
disposition
probabilistic operational semantics
distribution
evaluation

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The conclusion treats underdetermination as if it mathematically prevents operational semantics.

Operational semantics can represent underdetermination.

QUESTION:
What kind of underdetermination cannot be captured by a formal relation or probability kernel?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Is the real cultural resistance to computation unpredictability, or the instability of the state variables and transition ontology themselves?

MECHANISM:
possible cultural formalization:

social configuration γ
→ socially conditioned relation/kernel K
→ multiple plausible practices.

The difficult part becomes:
defining γ and K faithfully.

FORMAL SHIFT:
<IMPROVISATION>
≠
<ABSENCE OF FORMAL CONSEQUENCE>

SOURCE FORMALISM:
Dal Lago and Zorzi provide formal distributions of outcomes.
Bourdieu provides no formal transition machinery.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

Potential social kernel:

    K : S → Δ(S)

may be formally well-defined in a model even when observed practice varies.

The empirical question is then:

    Does K preserve the mechanisms that matter?

not:

    Is K deterministic?

TENSION:
A probabilistic model can badly misrepresent social action by treating historical interpretation, reflexivity, norm contestation, and category change as fixed stochastic variables.

MISSING:
ONTOLOGICAL STABILITY.

Can S and K remain fixed while actors reinterpret the categories composing S and K?

BOUNDARY:
Probability rescues formalizability, not reductionism.

CITATION TRAIL:
practice theory.
probabilistic transition systems.
reflexive agents.
endogenous state spaces.

TEST:
Formalize one cultural practice probabilistically.

Then let actors alter the meaning of one state category itself.

If the model cannot express that without redesigning its state space, the deeper boundary lies in ONTOLOGY CHANGE rather than nondeterminism.
