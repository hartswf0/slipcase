ZETTEL

ID:
SEARCH-001

TITLE:
Exploration differs from shopping when an intermediate artifact changes the operations the maker can subsequently perform.

SOURCE:
COLLISION — Kirsh and Maglio on epistemic action; Donald Schön on reflective designing; U.S. Copyright Office treatment of repeated prompting.

SOURCE URL:
https://doi.org/10.1207/s15516709cog1804_1

PASSAGE:
[OUR INFERENCE]
Browsing candidates and exploratory making can share the surface sequence generate-inspect-reject. The stronger distinction is whether an intermediate result becomes an experimental surface that changes later operative knowledge and constraints rather than merely changing which candidate is preferred.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
EXPLORATION VERSUS SHOPPING.

LOCAL MOVE:
Replace iteration count and rejection count with a test of whether observation changes the structure of subsequent interventions.

SOURCE TERMS:
exploration
selection
shopping
epistemic action
reflection-in-action
operation

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
A shopper can learn what they like while browsing. Therefore preference refinement alone does not distinguish creative exploration from search through supplied alternatives.

QUESTION:
What must change after an intermediate output for a process to count as exploratory making rather than candidate shopping?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Does the artifact teach the maker a new operation, variable, or constraint that causally structures subsequent production?

MECHANISM:
candidate x appears → either (A) preference updates and x is selected/rejected, or (B) maker extracts relation/operation r → later actions are reorganized around r.

FORMAL SHIFT:
<ITERATION COUNT> → <STRUCTURAL CHANGE IN SUBSEQUENT ACTION>

SOURCE FORMALISM:
Kirsh/Maglio and Schön provide the underlying epistemic and reflective mechanisms; the exploration-versus-shopping discriminator is our synthesis.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
Exploratory event e_t has operational residue if it changes the set or policy of later interventions: A_{t+1} or π_{t+1} differs because of knowledge acquired from e_t.

TENSION:
Exploration can still be trivial, and shopping can involve sophisticated judgment. This distinction concerns process structure, not artistic merit.

MISSING:
A robust method for inferring operational residue from behavior without requiring the maker to narrate intentions.

BOUNDARY:
Exploration does not by itself settle copyright authorship.

CITATION TRAIL:
[[EPISTEMIC-001]] + [[DISCOVERY-002]] → generation can teach → distinguish learning-to-act from merely selecting among outputs.

TEST:
Blind analysts to final selected output. Give them the action trace and ask whether each discarded generation altered later control choices. Compare to random-resampling and shopping baselines.
