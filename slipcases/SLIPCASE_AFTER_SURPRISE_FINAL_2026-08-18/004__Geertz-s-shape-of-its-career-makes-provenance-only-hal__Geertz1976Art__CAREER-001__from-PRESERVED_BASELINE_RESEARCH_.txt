ZETTEL

ID:
CAREER-001

TITLE:
Geertz’s “shape of its career” makes provenance only half of the path.

SOURCE:
Clifford Geertz — “Art as a Cultural System” — 1976; collected in Local Knowledge — 1983 — Bamileke chief-statue discussion, drawing on Jacques Maquet.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
Geertz describes a Bamileke chief’s statue made at accession and subsequently altered in significance as both the physical object and the memory of the chief undergo change. He asks whether the relevant form lies in the statue’s shape or in the shape of its career and answers that both matter.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
ARTIFACT HISTORY HAS AT LEAST TWO DIRECTIONS:

BACKWARD INTO PRODUCTION
and
FORWARD INTO CAREER.

LOCAL MOVE:
The essay’s demand for production paths cannot become another version of origin determinism.

SOURCE TERMS:
statue
chief
memory
weathering
form
career
fate
meaning
force

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
Perfect provenance would still fail Geertz’s test.

Knowing every production intervention does not tell us what the object later becomes in collective use.

QUESTION:
How should an account of operative description join production trace to post-production career without reducing one to the other?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Can the same artifact acquire new operative roles after leaving the interpretive machinery that produced it?

MECHANISM:
PRODUCTION:

intentions/interventions
→ artifact A_0.

CAREER:

A_0
+
successive audiences/institutions/events
→ uses and interpretations
→ altered status A_t
→ consequences not controlled by maker.

FORMAL SHIFT:
<PROVENANCE TRACE>
→ <ARTIFACT>
→ <CAREER TRACE>

SOURCE FORMALISM:
NONE.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

Two paths:

    τ_backward :
    causes → artifact

    τ_forward :
    artifact → consequences.

A complete causal history around artifact A requires both:

    Causes(A)
    and
    Effects(A).

TENSION:
The forward career can feed back into provenance judgments:
later fame, controversy, copying, or institutional classification can change which aspects of production historians consider salient.

MISSING:
A formalism for artifacts whose meanings, classifications, and causal roles change across receiving assemblages.

BOUNDARY:
Production history matters without exhausting meaning.

Reception history matters without retroactively becoming the production mechanism.

TEST:
Choose one artifact with excellent production provenance.

Construct two independent timelines:

A. PRODUCTION DECISIONS
B. POST-RELEASE USES / RECLASSIFICATIONS / CONSEQUENCES.

Identify claims explainable only by B.
