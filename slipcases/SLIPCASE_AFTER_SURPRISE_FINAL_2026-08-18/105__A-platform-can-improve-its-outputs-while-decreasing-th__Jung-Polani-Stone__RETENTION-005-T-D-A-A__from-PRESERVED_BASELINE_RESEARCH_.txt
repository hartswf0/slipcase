ID:
RETENTION-005-T-D-A-A

TITLE:
A platform can improve its outputs while decreasing the artist’s empowerment.

SOURCE:
COLLISION — Tobias Jung, Daniel Polani, and Peter Stone — “Empowerment for Continuous Agent-Environment Systems” — 2011; RETENTION-005-T-D-A on platformization.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
Empowerment evaluates effective influence through action channels rather than output reward. Therefore a system can produce “better” outputs while exposing fewer reliably distinguishable ways for the user to alter them.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
QUALITY–EMPOWERMENT DIVERGENCE.

LOCAL MOVE:
Make affordance-loss measurable: output quality and creative-instrument quality can move in opposite directions.

SOURCE TERMS:
empowerment
actions
successor states
control
influence
options

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
AUTOMATION CAN IMPROVE THE ARTIFACT AND DEGRADE THE INSTRUMENT SIMULTANEOUSLY.

QUESTION:
Should creative-tool evaluation separately report output quality and user empowerment?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Could apparent progress of generative interfaces conceal systematic contraction of the human-controllable state space?

MECHANISM:
M1 moderate baseline quality + many intervention channels; M2 higher quality + automated defaults + fewer distinguishable channels.

FORMAL SHIFT:
<BETTER GENERATOR> ≠ <BETTER CREATIVE INSTRUMENT>

SOURCE FORMALISM:
Empowerment depends on action-to-future-state transition dynamics and is independent of external reward quality.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
Evaluate Q=output quality, E=user empowerment, P=user plasticity separately. Q2>Q1 can coexist with E2<E1.

TENSION:
Automation can also increase empowerment by making previously unreachable states reachable.

MISSING:
Longitudinal empowerment measurements across real interface/model versions.

BOUNDARY:
Reduced difficulty is not necessarily reduced agency; the question is whether reliably distinguishable reachable futures expand or contract.

CITATION TRAIL:
[[RETENTION-005-T-D-A]] → affordance loss → empowerment → quality/control divergence.

TEST:
Run identical tasks across versions; measure output preference, target success, reachable target states, local edit reliability, and recoverability; plot Q vs E.
