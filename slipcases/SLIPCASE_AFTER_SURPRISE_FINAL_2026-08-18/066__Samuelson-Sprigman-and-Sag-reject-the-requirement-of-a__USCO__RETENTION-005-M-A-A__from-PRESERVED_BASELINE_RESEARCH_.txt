TITLE:
Samuelson, Sprigman, and Sag reject the requirement of a complete preexisting conception and explicitly defend exploratory iterative authorship.

SOURCE:
Pamela Samuelson, Christopher Jon Sprigman, and Matthew Sag — Comments to U.S. Copyright Office AI Notice of Inquiry — October 30, 2023.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
The commenters argue authorship can potentially emerge through iterative refinement and selection even when the maker begins with only a loose conception and discovers the work through interaction with a medium.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
THE STRONGEST OPPOSITION IS NOT MASTER-PLAN AUTHORSHIP; IT IS EXPLORATORY AUTHORSHIP.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The opposition contains two theories: detailed conception realized by machine and exploratory authorship emerging through process.

QUESTION:
Are detailed-prior-conception and exploratory-authorship theories legally compatible?

DEEPER QUESTION:
What makes exploratory selection by an AI user closer to a painter responding to thrown paint than to a shopper searching until an acceptable image appears?

BOUNDARY:
The position is not “long prompts count”; its radical claim is that authorship can emerge through iterative medium engagement.
