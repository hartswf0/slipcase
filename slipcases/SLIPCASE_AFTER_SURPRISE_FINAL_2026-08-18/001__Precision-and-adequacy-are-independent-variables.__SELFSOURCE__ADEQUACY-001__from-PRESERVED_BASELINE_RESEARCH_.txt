TITLE:
Precision and adequacy are independent variables.

SOURCE:
User-provided manuscript / conversation synthesis — 2026-08-16.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
A representation can become more detailed, accurate, or benchmark-successful while becoming less adequate to the consequential situation in which it operates.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
RELATIONAL ADEQUACY.

LOCAL MOVE:
Separate ACCURACY OF REPRESENTATION from FIT FOR CONSEQUENCE.

SOURCE TERMS:
precision
adequacy
benchmark
correction
consequence
relational

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
Improving measured dimensions can increase institutional confidence while leaving the consequentially relevant missing variable untouched.

QUESTION:
What is the correct relation between statistical accuracy and operational adequacy?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Can a representation be Pareto-better on standard metrics while worse for actors bearing correction costs?

MECHANISM:
metric M improves → reliance rises → consequential omitted variable persists → contestation becomes harder → practical adequacy can fall.

FORMAL SHIFT:
<PRECISION ↑> ↛ <ADEQUACY ↑>

SOURCE FORMALISM:

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
Adequate(R | consequence c, actor a, time t, correction channel k), not Adequate(R) simpliciter.

TENSION:
Precision can improve adequacy when the measured variable genuinely matters and correction remains available.

MISSING:
A multidimensional adequacy function that does not merely encode arbitrary stakeholder preferences.

BOUNDARY:
This is not anti-measurement; measurement quality is insufficient evidence of consequential fitness.

CITATION TRAIL:
[[PASSAGE-002]] → loss → adequacy → consequential validity.

TEST:
For one model improvement, measure benchmark accuracy separately from appeal success, error burden, latency, harm severity, and recovery time.
