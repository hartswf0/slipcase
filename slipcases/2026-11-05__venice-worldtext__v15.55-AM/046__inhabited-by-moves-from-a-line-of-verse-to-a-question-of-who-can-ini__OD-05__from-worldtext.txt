ZETTEL

ID: OD-05

TITLE: “Inhabited by” moves from a line of verse to a question of who can initiate an event

SOURCE: Alison Knowles — interview with Janet Sarbanes — conducted 2011 — CalArts account. Newman — Art as ‘Operation’ — 2026 — discussion of the open structure and conviviality. Bolter and Hartsoe — Operative Ekphrasis — §§6–7.

PASSAGE: [QUOTE] Knowles says the structures “had to be activated.” [PARAPHRASE] She describes classes and readings, a rail for sound lines, food events, early-morning use of the light entering through a hole in the roof, and students proposing activities.

RESEARCH OBJECT: The capacity of occupants to initiate and alter the activities of a described place.

LOCAL MOVE: Knowles’s account takes the inquiry beyond the stanza’s population field and beyond the construction of a shell. It shows how time, access, equipment, and permission entered the work.

SOURCE TERMS: activated, inhabitants, readings, Gift Event II, conviviality.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE: The program can name inhabitants before any exist. Once people arrive, they can become operators who choose what happens there. Which activities can occur depends partly on conditions no inhabitant label specifies.

QUESTION: What can inhabitants do in a worldtext that the original describer did not already decide for them?

DEEPER QUESTION: Who can change the conditions of participation when occupants want to use the world differently?

MECHANISM: The opening admits changing light. Sound lines make certain gatherings possible. Knowles and participants organize activities. Access, funding, and institutional tolerance help determine which activities can continue. The computer generates the stanza. The activation depends on other operators.

FORMAL SHIFT: <inhabitants as a phrase in a quatrain> → <people with situated access and equipment> → [initiate or adapt an activity] → <a place with a use history>

SOURCE FORMALISM: The sources document activities and infrastructure. No fixed program of participant roles or complete access protocol is supplied.

OUR FORMALIZATION: [OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX] Try “A room lit by a moving opening, inhabited by two people who remember the event differently. Either person may start a replay. Each can leave a visible annotation for the other, and each can hide their own annotation without deleting it.” Trace which actor can do what and what persists after each action.

TENSION: The house may have actively elicited uses through its geometry and setting. It may also have served as a container for an unusually permissive teaching practice. Knowles’s light and sound examples make both readings testable.

MISSING: An activation history detailed enough to separate what depended on the built features from what depended on Knowles, the participants, or CalArts.

BOUNDARY: The interview establishes that activation involved people and infrastructure. It does not establish that the selected stanza caused any one use.

CITATION TRAIL: [[AP-06]] → the interview’s roof opening, sound lines, and student initiatives → an event flyer, photograph, or participant account that documents one use.

TEST: Reconstruct one activation from three traces: the relevant line in the stanza, a physical feature of the installation, and a record of what someone did. Ask which link in the proposed chain is evidenced. In the digital analogue, see whether a second participant can initiate an unplanned use and leave a state another participant can encounter.
