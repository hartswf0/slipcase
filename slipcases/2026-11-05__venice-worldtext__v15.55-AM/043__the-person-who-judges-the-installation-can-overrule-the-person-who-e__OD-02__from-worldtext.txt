ZETTEL

ID: OD-02

TITLE: The person who judges the installation can overrule the person who executed it

SOURCE: Sol LeWitt — Doing Wall Drawings — 1971. MASS MoCA — Wall Drawing 1180 — installation account with testimony from senior drafter John Hogan.

PASSAGE: [QUOTE] LeWitt writes, “All wall drawings contain errors, they are part of the work.” [PARAPHRASE] Hogan identifies the clipping of the circle and unequal distribution of lines in Beirut as installation errors. MASS MoCA describes its later realization as an opportunity to update the Estate’s record.

RESEARCH OBJECT: The authority to distinguish an admissible difference from a violation of the plan.

LOCAL MOVE: LeWitt’s essay allows unforeseen decisions and errors within a realization. The installation record gives the parent zettel a concrete case where variation prompted a new realization.

SOURCE TERMS: plan, draftsman, violation, randomly spaced, equally distributed.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE: The same account leaves marker-tip width open, describes the drafter judging density by eye and hand, and treats the clipped circle as an error. Each decision belongs to a different part of the work.

QUESTION: In thick prompting, who gets to say “keep that accident” and who can require the scene to be run again?

DEEPER QUESTION: Does operator name the actor that changes the artifact or the actor whose judgment determines whether that change stands?

MECHANISM: A plan sets conditions. A drafter chooses how to realize them in the available space. A later evaluator compares the installation with the plan, and an institution can commission another realization or update the record. These roles may be held by different people.

FORMAL SHIFT: <instruction and site> → <candidate realization> → [judge differences against the plan] → <accepted realization or reason to remake>

SOURCE FORMALISM: The instruction sets a four-meter circle, two line classes of 10,000 each, random spacing, and equal distribution. Hogan describes increasing density over successive passes. No quantitative tolerance for evenness appears in the public account.

OUR FORMALIZATION: [OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX] In an orb-editing session, declare “the entire orbit remains traversable” as a condition of acceptance. Delegate surface wear to the renderer and the judgment of whether an unexpected trace belongs to the scene to the director. Log who made each decision.

TENSION: Hogan’s judgment could be explained by an invariant already in the score, by a studio convention learned through practice, or by both. The circle’s clipping and the density judgment may belong to different categories.

MISSING: How Hogan and other drafters distinguish small departures that belong to a realization from violations that require correction.

BOUNDARY: The documented case establishes that a named drafter judged the earlier installation incorrect. It leaves open how that judgment was shared, contested, or formally authorized.

CITATION TRAIL: [[AP-05]] → LeWitt’s distinction between error and violation → Hogan’s Beirut and North Adams comparison → Estate installation records.

TEST: Show independent reviewers three versions of the same score realization: intact boundary with variable marks, intact boundary with visibly uneven density, and clipped boundary. Ask which they would accept and which source of authority they used to decide.
