ID: AP-04
TITLE: A score can specify the boundary of an event and leave its contents to the room

SOURCE: Michael Newman — Art as ‘Operation’ — 2026 — Cage discussion in the supplied transcript. John Cage — 4′33″ (In Proportional Notation) — MoMA collection record.

PASSAGE: [PARAPHRASE] Newman treats Tudor’s piano-lid movements as temporal markers that draw attention to sounds occurring within the marked interval. MoMA distinguishes the premiere from the surviving proportional score, where spatial distances encode durations.

RESEARCH OBJECT: The relation between a score’s duration, the performer’s boundary cues, and the listener’s attention.

LOCAL MOVE: Newman treats strict timing and environmental contingency as mutually enabling.

SOURCE TERMS: measures, contingency, graphic score, interpretation.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE: The score, the gestures that announce its sections, and the audible event can vary independently. A system can reproduce the duration and leave listeners unable to tell when the work begins.

QUESTION: Which part of a performance makes incidental sound available as something an audience can hear as the work?

MECHANISM: A score specifies temporal divisions. A performer makes divisions perceptible. Listeners may then attend to sounds they would otherwise treat as interruptions or background.

FORMAL SHIFT: <duration> → <spatial intervals on the surviving score> → [mark an interval in performance] → <occasion for listening>

SOURCE FORMALISM: The surviving score maps spacing to duration. Tudor’s lid gestures are documented performance actions.

OUR FORMALIZATION: [OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX] Separate the timer, the boundary cue, the acoustic field, and the listener’s uptake.

TENSION: Newman contrasts graphic scores with player-piano instructions. This particular graphic score encodes timing precisely. The openness under examination lies in the room and in how performers and listeners take up the frame.

MISSING: A way to distinguish what changes because a cue is perceived from what changes because people already know they are at a performance of Cage.

BOUNDARY: The piano lid documents one realization. Its necessity for the work is an open question.

CITATION TRAIL: Compare the MoMA manuscript and performance account with Irwin Kremen’s discussion of proportional notation at the John Cage Trust.

TEST: Present the same recording with and without perceptible movement boundaries. Ask listeners to mark when a given ambient sound became part of the event, then compare their accounts with their prior knowledge of the piece.
