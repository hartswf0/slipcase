ZETTEL

ID: WTW-02
TITLE: Chance acts inside a cabinet someone has already designed
SOURCE: Michael Newman, “Art as ‘Operation’: from Lovelace and Mallarmé to Adversarial Poetry,” Adversarial Poetry symposium, Ca’ Foscari, October 1, 2026. Recording description and conference programme. The spoken transcript was unavailable.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE — recording description] In Mallarmé’s notes for Le Livre, an operator places pages in a lacquer cabinet by chance within a designated numerical constraint. Newman reads the arrangement alongside Lovelace’s account of operations that alter relations among things.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
The division between selecting the possible arrangements and selecting a particular arrangement.

LOCAL MOVE:
Newman places the making of the work in the execution of a procedure. The page, cabinet, numerical constraint, and operator all participate.

SOURCE TERMS:
Operator, operating notation, contingency, calculation, Le Livre.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The author’s withdrawal from choosing each arrangement can make earlier choices about the procedure more consequential. Someone still decides what can move and under what conditions.

QUESTION:
When a prompt delegates a decision to chance, where does the user specify the boundary of that delegation?

MECHANISM:
A rule sets the range of allowable moves. An operator makes a contingent choice within that range. The material arrangement determines how that choice becomes available to a reader or audience.

FORMAL SHIFT:
<pages and cabinet> → <allowable placements> → [select and execute one arrangement] → <performed book>.

SOURCE FORMALISM:
The description specifies page placement and a numerical constraint. It leaves the exact selection procedure and distribution open.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX] Separate the units that may change, the constraints that persist, and the action that selects one realized outcome.

TENSION:
A. An operative description can carry the work across executors.
B. The cabinet and the performer’s handling are part of what makes the result this work.

MISSING:
Which constraints are explicit in Mallarmé’s notes and which belong to Newman’s reconstruction.

BOUNDARY:
Newman’s comparison puts Lovelace and Mallarmé in relation. It supplies no evidence of direct influence. The Le Livre procedure concerns a projected performance described in notes.

CITATION TRAIL:
Mallarmé’s Le Livre notes, Lovelace’s 1843 Notes on the Analytical Engine, and Sol LeWitt’s wall-drawing instructions as a comparison for how much discretion a score gives its executor.

TEST:
Have two operators follow the same page arrangement rule. Repeat with a paper cabinet and a digital one. Record which decisions transfer with the rule and which have to be made again because the medium changed.
