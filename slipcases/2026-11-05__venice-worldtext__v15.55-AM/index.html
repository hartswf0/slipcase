IMG-JEPA-01 · The positive-pair graph defines what counts as the same scene

TXT · Markdown

ZETTEL

ID: IMG-JEPA-01

TITLE:
The positive-pair graph defines what counts as the same scene

SOURCE:
Randall Balestriero and Yann LeCun — Proving and Improving the Platonic Representation Hypothesis with JEPAs — 2026 — §§2.1–2.3, Figure 3, §7

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE] In the spectral setting, the learned directions come from the graph that positive pairs draw on world states. Exact agreement requires shared positives and uniqueness up to rotation. When the graphs differ, the bound depends on their mismatch and on the spectral gap.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
The rule for pairing observations as a prior about which differences matter.

LOCAL MOVE:
The proof rewrites each modality’s learning objective on the same state space.

SOURCE TERMS:
Positive-pair distribution, world kernel, injective rendering, spectral gap.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
[OUR INFERENCE] On Flickr, descriptions are grouped by photograph. The photograph supplies an address for the scene before the text encoder learns anything. Grouping by photographed event instead would change the learning problem.

QUESTION:
What does an imagetext experiment take as its unit of sameness: the image, the depicted event, or the relation a reader is asked to notice?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Can two descriptions of different images form a better pair for studying composition than two descriptions of the same image?

MECHANISM:
Changing the pair rule changes the graph. Under the paper’s spectral objective, this can change which distinctions survive in the leading embedding directions.

FORMAL SHIFT:
<Observations> → <weighted edges between states> → [spectral embedding] → <geometry of learned similarities>

SOURCE FORMALISM:
Kπ = D^(-1/2) Π D^(-1/2), where Π represents the positive-pair distribution and D = diag(p). With second-order anti-collapse and a spectral gap, the solution is determined by the leading nonconstant eigenspace, up to rotation.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
NONE

TENSION:
The proof requires a specified relation among states. In imagetext analysis, deciding whether two views depict the same event can itself be an interpretive judgment.

MISSING:
A test that varies the grouping rule while holding the actual images and texts fixed.

BOUNDARY:
The spectral result supplies a sufficient condition for alignment under its assumptions. The paper leaves uniqueness under LeJEPA’s full-distribution constraint open.

CITATION TRAIL:
Laplacian eigenmaps and spectral contrastive learning, both cited in §2.1.

TEST:
Take one staged dispute photographed from several angles and a second dispute staged with the same blocking. Compare grouping by image ID, event ID, and blocking. Hold out one camera angle. Ask which grouping retrieves the same event and which retrieves the same composition.

[[Imagetext as a Unit of Analysis]]

IMG-JEPA-02 · A second description can inherit the first description’s omissions

TXT · Markdown

ZETTEL

ID: IMG-JEPA-02

TITLE:
A second description can inherit the first description’s omissions

SOURCE:
Randall Balestriero and Yann LeCun — Proving and Improving the Platonic Representation Hypothesis with JEPAs — 2026 — Appendix B, Table 7, §7

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE] At the 2.24M-image scale, alt-text paired with a text-only paraphrase scores .060 on WIT, against .055 for corrupted views of alt-text alone and .107 for the set using alt-text and two LLaVA recaptions. The recaptions carry information from prior image–text training.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
Whether a second text comes from looking at the image again or from rewriting the first text.

LOCAL MOVE:
Table 7 varies both the available descriptions and the rule for drawing positive views.

SOURCE TERMS:
Alt-text, recaption, paraphrase, good views, bad views, scene-explained fraction.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
[OUR INFERENCE] Thick prompting can accumulate many sentences while remaining dependent on a single act of seeing. Conversely, two captions written by the same image model may repeat a learned convention even when both are produced from pixels.

QUESTION:
When a caption introduces a detail absent from the first description, how do we tell a fresh observation from a shared captioning convention?

DEEPER QUESTION:
How much of measured alignment comes from access to visible evidence, and how much from learning what caption writers tend to mention?

MECHANISM:
A paraphraser receives the first description. An image captioner receives visual evidence and brings its own training priors. Those are different routes for a detail to enter the second text.

FORMAL SHIFT:
<Caption or photograph> → <input to a second describer> → [paraphrase or recaption] → <different evidence available for positive pairing>

SOURCE FORMALISM:
NONE

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
Log each description’s parent evidence: pixels, earlier text, or both. Independently mark whether each newly introduced claim is visible, contradicted, or undecidable from the image.

TENSION:
The caption source matters even under the paper’s bad-view rule. At the 50M-image scale, the two LLaVA recaptions with bad views score .092 on WIT, against .056 for alt-text with bad views.

MISSING:
A control that separates independent access to pixels from captioner conventions and differences in literalness.

BOUNDARY:
Table 7 supports effects of both data and view construction. Its rows do not isolate a causal effect of independent looking.

CITATION TRAIL:
Recap-DataComp, cited as [34], and the LLaVA caption provenance discussed in §7.

TEST:
For the same Shield of Achilles stills, collect one text-only paraphrase, one image-only description by a fresh observer, and one image-only description from a second captioner. Blindly verify every detail absent from the first text against the image. Count supported discoveries and repeated unsupported additions separately.

IMG-JEPA-03 · Viewpoint can be nuisance in a caption and instruction in a prompt

ZETTEL

ID: IMG-JEPA-03

TITLE:
Viewpoint can be nuisance in a caption and instruction in a prompt

SOURCE:
Randall Balestriero and Yann LeCun — Proving and Improving the Platonic Representation Hypothesis with JEPAs — 2026 — §2.3

PASSAGE:
[QUOTE] “It is a nuisance only for alignment”

RESEARCH OBJECT:
A variable whose status changes when language moves from describing an image to directing its production.

LOCAL MOVE:
The authors distinguish the state a modality shares with others from observation-specific variation. They explicitly acknowledge that a camera-acting world model would need its viewpoint.

SOURCE TERMS:
State, nuisance, viewpoint, independent nuisances, alignment.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
[OUR INFERENCE] An image encoder can benefit from treating two camera angles as equivalent for scene recognition. A prompt critic applying that same equivalence could reward a generator for ignoring an explicit camera instruction.

QUESTION:
Can an imagetext critic recognize the same depicted event across views while remaining sensitive to the requested view?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Who decides which details should survive alignment when the language itself selects what matters visually?

MECHANISM:
The positive-pair rule resamples viewpoint in the source model. A prompt such as “seen directly overhead” instead makes viewpoint a target whose value must affect the rendered image.

FORMAL SHIFT:
<Camera angle> → <nuisance for scene recognition or constraint for generation> → [ignore or discriminate] → <different judgments of the same image pair>

SOURCE FORMALISM:
In §2.3, observations are g_m(z, ν). Good views draw independent nuisance variables ν and ν′; corruptions of one observation share its nuisance.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
Keep separate labels for event identity and prompt-constraint satisfaction. The same image pair may match on one and differ on the other.

TENSION:
A critic that strongly preserves camera geometry may mistake a viewpoint change for an event change. A critic that suppresses it may miss a visible prompt violation.

MISSING:
Instruction-conditioned evidence about which differences the critic should ignore.

BOUNDARY:
The paper’s nuisance analysis describes an alignment objective. Applying it to prompt compliance is an inference about a different task.

CITATION TRAIL:
§2.3’s explicit camera-acting example and §3.3’s tests of video frames as image views.

TEST:
Render the same dispute overhead and at eye level. Use both versions with “a dispute before elders,” then score both against “a dispute before elders seen directly overhead.” A critic passes if it preserves event identity in the first comparison and detects the wrong view in the second.

IMG-JEPA-04 · A prompt can change an image without making its meaning recoverable

TXT · Markdown

ZETTEL

ID: IMG-JEPA-04

TITLE:
A prompt can change an image without making its meaning recoverable

SOURCE:
Randall Balestriero and Yann LeCun — Proving and Improving the Platonic Representation Hypothesis with JEPAs — 2026 — §2.1, Appendix A.2, §7

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE] The proof uses the inverse of each injective rendering on its image. This lets the authors rewrite the objective over observations as an objective over world states. The limitations section identifies lossy real modalities as a departure from that setup.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
The difference between a prompt detail having a visual effect and a viewer being able to infer that detail.

LOCAL MOVE:
Injectivity supplies recoverability in the formal model before learning begins.

SOURCE TERMS:
Injective rendering, left inverse, world state, population objective.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
[OUR INFERENCE] Changing “blood-price” to “unpaid debt” could systematically alter a generator’s lighting or blocking while leaving the dispute’s stakes undecidable to viewers. There could be a causal effect without a readable sign of its cause.

QUESTION:
Which parts of an imagetext can be reconstructed from the image, and which only become determinate when the text is present?

DEEPER QUESTION:
If a classifier recovers the prompt change from lighting but human readers cannot identify the stakes, what exactly has crossed modalities?

MECHANISM:
A prompt intervention can shift the distribution of outputs. Recovering the intervention from a particular image requires evidence that distinguishes it from competing prompts. The text can supply the distinction directly in the combined imagetext.

FORMAL SHIFT:
<Prompt contrast> → <distributions of rendered images> → [blind discrimination] → <estimate of what the image makes recoverable>

SOURCE FORMALISM:
The source uses x = g_m(z), with a left inverse on the range of g_m. It gives no model of prompt interventions.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
Record three results separately: whether outputs change under a prompt edit, whether viewers infer the edited detail from pixels, and whether adding the text changes their reading.

TENSION:
A failed inference can indicate that the generator ignored the phrase, that it encoded the difference through an incidental visual cue, or that the stakes properly remain in the verbal part of the imagetext. Those readings require different evidence.

MISSING:
The viewer and the prompt intervention as explicit variables in the paper’s setup.

BOUNDARY:
The theorem does not imply that a generated image is invertible to its prompt, or that an apparent prompt match identifies what caused the image.

CITATION TRAIL:
The proof’s change-of-variables step in Appendix A.2 and the lossy-modality limitation in §7.

TEST:
Generate matched-seed pairs for “blood-price” and “unpaid debt,” holding staging and style instructions fixed. Test blind image-only judgments, then repeat with the captions swapped. Include a visible inside/outside edit to check that the same viewers can detect a renderable distinction.

IMG-JEPA-05 · The caption closest to the image embedding did not improve decoding

TXT · Markdown

ZETTEL

ID: IMG-JEPA-05

TITLE:
The caption closest to the image embedding did not improve decoding

SOURCE:
Randall Balestriero and Yann LeCun — Proving and Improving the Platonic Representation Hypothesis with JEPAs — 2026 — §5.1 and appendix passage “Scaling the captioner”

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE] The authors generate several captions and select one by its agreement with the others under an n-gram similarity rule. Selecting the beam candidate closest to the rotated image embedding in text-JEPA space does not help. Additional decoder pretraining improves decoding of true caption embeddings without improving Flickr or COCO transfer.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
A split between geometry that makes captioning possible and a selection rule that produces a good caption.

LOCAL MOVE:
The paper fits a map between frozen embedding spaces, then uses a separately trained language decoder and an explicit candidate selection procedure.

SOURCE TERMS:
Rotated image embedding, soft prompt, consensus decoding, minimum Bayes risk, CIDEr.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
[OUR INFERENCE] The caption that most resembles other plausible captions can win even when a rarer caption preserves the detail that makes this image singular.

QUESTION:
Does caption consensus remove hallucinated detail, or does it suppress accurate details that the decoder seldom names?

DEEPER QUESTION:
What would count as a good caption when the purpose is to direct attention toward a difficult feature rather than to agree with typical descriptions?

MECHANISM:
The pipeline maps vision features into text-embedding space, projects the resulting vector into soft-prompt tokens, and decodes language. The consensus rule rewards lexical agreement among candidates, so a detail’s chance of surviving selection depends partly on how often other samples mention it.

FORMAL SHIFT:
<Image features> → <mapped text embedding> → [soft-prompt decoding and candidate selection] → <one caption>

SOURCE FORMALISM:
The scaled decoder projects an embedding to ten Qwen2.5 input embeddings through a two-layer MLP. Consensus compares 32 sampled captions and one greedy caption using CIDEr-style n-gram similarity without document frequencies.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
NONE

TENSION:
The paper reports a useful decoding choice under its evaluation. Whether that choice favors generic descriptions at the expense of rare visible details remains an open empirical question.

MISSING:
Feature-level judgments of the winning caption and the candidates it displaced.

BOUNDARY:
The result does not establish that consensus systematically erases details or that embedding similarity generally fails as a caption critic.

CITATION TRAIL:
ClipCap for the soft-prompt projection and reference [27] for minimum Bayes risk decoding.

TEST:
Use one candidate pool per image and apply both selectors to that same pool. Choose images with a rare but visible detail. Have readers mark supported rare details and unsupported additions independently of caption fluency. Check whether the selectors trade one error for the other.

[[Imagetext and the Captioning Apparatus]]

[[Consensus Captioning]]

The learned constant supplied alongside the image embedding in ClipCap’s frozen-decoder variant.

FORMAL SHIFT:
<CLIP image embedding plus learned constant> → <transformer-generated prefix> → [frozen language-model decoding] → <caption>

NONE

[OUR INFERENCE] The constant may mostly steer caption style, or it may also change which visible distinctions reliably reach the output. A fluent caption can result in either case.

MISSING:
A controlled intervention on the constant that distinguishes a change in phrasing from a change in which visible propositions appear.
