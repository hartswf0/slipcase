ID: BAJOHR-08
TITLE: A world can defeat its inhabitants’ experiments
SOURCE: Hannes Bajohr — “The Latent Space of Meaning and the Novel,” Venice, 2025, closing comparison with Quentin Meillassoux’s Science Fiction and Extro-Science Fiction.

PASSAGE: [PARAPHRASE] Bajohr compares partly intelligible AI fiction to Meillassoux’s Type 2 worlds. The decisive distinction in Meillassoux is whether stable natural laws can be investigated from within the fiction.

RESEARCH OBJECT: A failure of causal inquiry inside the story world versus a failure of continuity in the telling.
LOCAL MOVE: The talk moves from generated discontinuities to a literary category in which the conditions of scientific inquiry can break down.
SOURCE TERMS: Extro-science fiction, Type 2, regularity, causal order.
WHAT BECAME STRANGE: A reader can explain a disappearing object as a narration error while a character, faced with the same disappearance, might have to revise what they think can be known.
QUESTION: When does a generated discontinuity become an event that the fictional world has to live with?
MECHANISM: The text establishes an expectation, stages a breach, and lets later action or inquiry depend on the breach. Repeated failed attempts to predict it can make the limits of inquiry part of the fiction.
FORMAL SHIFT: <apparent discontinuity> → <event acknowledged within the fiction> → [attempt explanation or replication] → <consequences for what characters can know>.
SOURCE FORMALISM: A philosophical typology. The sources supply no classification procedure for generated passages.
OUR FORMALIZATION: [OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX] Distinguish the reader’s attribution of a glitch from the characters’ ability to observe, revisit, or act on its effects.
TENSION: A. The passage makes the failure of prediction part of the world. B. An ordinary fictional world can be recovered by repairing a gap in the narration.
MISSING: A passage-level demonstration that later scenes take an apparent rupture as a premise.
BOUNDARY: A single inexplicable event is compatible with omitted information, unreliable narration, or a stable law the characters have yet to discover.
CITATION TRAIL: Meillassoux’s distinction between a world with unusual laws and a world in which science becomes impossible.
TEST: Take one discontinuity and keep the rupture fixed across two versions. In one, later scenes silently restore continuity. In the other, characters observe its effects and fail to reproduce it under matched conditions. Ask readers where they locate the failure and what evidence could change that reading.
