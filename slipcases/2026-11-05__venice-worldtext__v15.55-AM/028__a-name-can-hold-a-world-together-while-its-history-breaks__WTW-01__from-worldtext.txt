ID: WTW-01
TITLE: A name can hold a world together while its history breaks
SOURCE: Hannes Bajohr, “The Latent Space of Meaning and the Novel: World Models in AI and Literature,” Chat Token Vector, Ca’ Foscari, June 12, 2025. Supplied transcript and recording description.

PASSAGE:
[QUOTE — supplied transcript] “The text hangs together somehow, just not strongly.”
[PARAPHRASE] In (Berlin, Miami), recurring people, places, and things give the reader a sense of the world even as motives, timelines, and consequences become difficult to reconcile.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
The recurrence of a fictional entity without a stable account of what happened to it.

LOCAL MOVE:
Bajohr uses his novel to ask how much coherence a text needs before a reader can experience it as a world. He treats “worldness without a world” as a tentative limit and entertains degrees of worldness.

SOURCE TERMS:
Weak force, worldness, interpretive labor, realized context.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
Readers might agree that two appearances belong to the same character while disagreeing about what that character knows, remembers, or has done.

QUESTION:
What is the smallest relation that words must carry across passages for readers to experience those passages as one world?

MECHANISM:
A recurring name cues identification. Readers connect scenes through that identity and supply some missing causal links. In the production account, Bajohr himself carries an earlier detail forward by typing “Jawling pregnancies” to steer another completion.

FORMAL SHIFT:
<earlier passage> → <remembered entity and attribute> → [reader links or author reintroduces] → <continuity across passages>.

SOURCE FORMALISM:
Iterative sentence completion is the reported writing procedure. The talk supplies no operational measure of worldness.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX] Score agreement about entity identity separately from agreement about event order and causal consequences.

TENSION:
A. The text supplies enough recurrence to establish a shared world.
B. Readers supply different reconstructions that each feel coherent on their own.

MISSING:
Independent reader reconstructions and a record of which continuities Bajohr seeded during composition.

BOUNDARY:
The experiment concerns literary worldness. Whether the model can preserve state across interventions is a separate question. The supplied transcript contains no direct discussion of Ha and Schmidhuber or Wong and Tenenbaum’s framework.

CITATION TRAIL:
Bajohr’s production note in Ensemble Park no. 1 (2024). Compare Wong et al., From Word Models to World Models (2023), for a system that makes some consequences explicit and queryable.

TEST:
Give readers the same set of passages with the recurring character names either preserved or replaced by scene-specific aliases. Ask who is the same across scenes and what happened to them. Check whether the two judgments move together.
