ZETTEL

ID: WTW-03
TITLE: The alien needed something the actors could respond to
SOURCE: Denis Villeneuve and Nina Beguš, Arrival anniversary conversation, Academy Museum, November 2, 2026. Recording, approximately 15:25–17:30.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE — recording transcript] Beguš asks how Villeneuve brought the aliens to life. Villeneuve describes puppeteers casting shadows on the screen during shooting so Amy Adams and Jeremy Renner had a presence to act against.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
An on-set cue that gives an actor a counterpart before the audience sees the finished creature.

LOCAL MOVE:
Asked how the film made the aliens believable, Villeneuve gives a production account centered on the actors’ capacity to enter a relationship with a presence.

SOURCE TERMS:
Shadows, screen, presence, belief, relationship.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
A shadow can be visually underspecified and still tell an actor something precise about when and where to attend.

QUESTION:
What does a scene description have to turn into so a performer can respond as if another being is there?

MECHANISM:
Puppeteers produce a perceptible event on the set. The actors can use its location, timing, and movement in their performance. The audience later encounters the creature partly through those recorded responses.

FORMAL SHIFT:
<fictional encounter> → <shadow moving on the screen> → [actor attends and responds] → <recorded encounter>.

SOURCE FORMALISM:
NONE. The conversation supplies a production procedure and Villeneuve’s interpretation of its effect.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX] Separate the cue available to the performer from the image available to the audience. Track which cues the performance visibly depends on.

TENSION:
A. The shadow shaped the actors’ timing and attention.
B. The performers could have produced a comparably convincing encounter from a static mark and verbal direction.

MISSING:
Rehearsal footage or matched takes showing which actions responded to the puppetry.

BOUNDARY:
Villeneuve’s account establishes what was done on set. Its causal contribution to the finished performance remains a testable inference.

CITATION TRAIL:
The Academy Museum event page and the film’s production material. Compare the filmed performances with the physical cues available during shooting.

TEST:
Stage the same first-contact beat twice with identical dialogue, blocking, and camera placement. Give one performer a live moving shadow and the other a fixed eyeline mark. Compare the timing of gaze, pauses, and reciprocal gestures.
