ID: BAJOHR-03
TITLE: The box fits the pen only under ordinary circumstances
SOURCE: Yehoshua Bar-Hillel — “The Present Status of Automatic Translation of Languages,” 1960, Appendix III. Revisited by Bajohr in the Venice talk, 2025.

PASSAGE: [QUOTE — Bar-Hillel] “The box was in the pen.”
[PARAPHRASE] The paragraph supplies a child and a toy box. Bar-Hillel explicitly allows that the same paragraph might occur inside a fairy tale or dream.

RESEARCH OBJECT: The defeasible assumption that ordinary relative sizes govern this particular story.
LOCAL MOVE: The talk pairs Bar-Hillel’s containment ambiguity with Winograd’s car and street example. Bar-Hillel’s own qualification brings genre into the inference.
SOURCE TERMS: “ordinary circumstances,” relative sizes, semantic ambiguity.
WHAT BECAME STRANGE: Knowing the usual dimensions of a pen and a toy box still leaves open whether this passage licenses those dimensions.
QUESTION: Can a system use ordinary containment as a default and reverse it when the story supplies contrary evidence?
MECHANISM: Infer the kind of situation, provisionally apply its size and containment expectations, then revise the selected sense if the passage defeats those expectations.
FORMAL SHIFT: <ambiguous occurrence> → <candidate senses and story premises> → [apply or override a containment default] → <contextual sense>.
SOURCE FORMALISM: The example stipulates two senses of “pen.” It supplies no executable rule for overriding the default.
OUR FORMALIZATION: [OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX] Cross an ordinary or fantastic setting with the presence or absence of an explicit miniature box and hollow writing pen.
TENSION: A. A model uses the actual containment relation. B. It follows lexical cues such as “toy,” “child,” or “fairy tale.”
MISSING: A comparison where genre cues and containment evidence point in opposite directions.
BOUNDARY: Getting the familiar sentence right establishes performance on that item. Transfer to altered story premises is a separate result.
CITATION TRAIL: Bar-Hillel’s discussion of Warren Weaver’s window of context, then Terry Winograd’s Understanding Natural Language.
TEST: Hold “The box was in the pen” fixed. In one version establish a play enclosure. In another establish a toy box small enough to fit inside a hollow fountain pen. Include fantastic versions where the play enclosure is still the answer.
