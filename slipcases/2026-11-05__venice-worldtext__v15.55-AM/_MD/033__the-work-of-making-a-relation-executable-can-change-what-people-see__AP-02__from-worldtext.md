ID: AP-02
TITLE: The work of making a relation executable can change what people see in it

SOURCE: Ada Lovelace — Notes to Menabrea’s Sketch of the Analytical Engine — 1843 — Note G. Newman quotes the opening claim about origination in his 2026 talk.

PASSAGE: [QUOTE] “it is likely to exert an indirect and reciprocal influence on science itself in another manner.”

RESEARCH OBJECT: The feedback from preparing a formalism for execution to investigating the subject being formalized.

LOCAL MOVE: After assigning executive powers to the engine, Lovelace considers what reorganizing mathematical relations for the engine might do to mathematical inquiry.

SOURCE TERMS: “executive faculties,” “mechanical combinations,” “reciprocal influence.”

WHAT BECAME STRANGE: The discovery Lovelace imagines can begin while people are deciding how to express the relations for the machine. It need never appear in an output.

QUESTION: Where should a history of computational origination look for a new idea: in the machine’s result, in the representation built for it, or in the subsequent work of interpreting either?

MECHANISM: People reorganize formulae for mechanical combination. The reorganization makes different relations available for inspection. Investigators can pursue a relation that was harder to see in its previous form.

FORMAL SHIFT: <known mathematical relations> → <arrangement suitable for the engine> → [redistribute and combine] → <relations newly exposed to inquiry>

SOURCE FORMALISM: Note G gives a conceptual account of this feedback and separately works through the Bernoulli-number example. It presents the scientific feedback as a speculative consequence.

OUR FORMALIZATION: [OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX] Record what becomes visible at three points: writing the representation, executing it, and examining the result.

TENSION: Newman invokes origination to ask whether a machine can discover or adopt a new frame. Note G also locates changes of frame in the work people do to make calculation possible.

MISSING: A documented case where the preparation for execution produced an identifiable mathematical insight.

BOUNDARY: The passage proposes a mechanism for discovery across people and machinery. Its operation in any particular case requires evidence.

CITATION TRAIL: Read the opening of Note G with its paragraphs on reciprocal influence. Compare Turing’s treatment of the Lovelace objection and his turn toward surprise in §6(6).

TEST: Reconstruct one dependency in the Bernoulli calculation twice, first from the algebra and then from Lovelace’s table. Identify a relation visible in one representation and see whether the other already makes it explicit.
