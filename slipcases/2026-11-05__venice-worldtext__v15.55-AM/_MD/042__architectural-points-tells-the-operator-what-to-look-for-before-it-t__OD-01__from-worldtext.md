ZETTEL

ID: OD-01

TITLE: “Architectural points” tells the operator what to look for before it tells anyone what to draw

SOURCE: Sol LeWitt — Wall Drawing 51 — 1970 — instruction and installation account at MASS MoCA. Michael Newman — Art as ‘Operation’ — 2026 — LeWitt discussion. Jay David Bolter and Watson Hartsoe — Operative Ekphrasis: From Imagetext to Worldtext — §6.

PASSAGE: [QUOTE] “All architectural points connected by straight lines.” The museum documents lines involving features such as door frames, fire alarms, and electrical sockets.

RESEARCH OBJECT: The operation that makes an existing feature available as an endpoint.

LOCAL MOVE: LeWitt specifies a relation to draw and names a class of things to which that relation applies. The installation supplies the instances.

SOURCE TERMS: architectural points, straight lines, blue snap lines, pre-existing space.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE: “All” sounds exhaustive. Its scope becomes determinate only after someone decides whether a socket, a seam, or a repair counts. A thick description of the wall could change the drawing before the first line is snapped.

QUESTION: In word-to-world work, who decides which things in the current scene the words refer to before the system changes anything?

DEEPER QUESTION: When a scene offers two plausible referents, should the operator choose one, preserve both readings, or return the decision to the person holding the controller?

MECHANISM: The draftspeople inspect a site, recognize candidate features as architectural points, and use the snap line to connect them. In a proposed digital analogue, a scene interpreter would identify candidate features and an editor would apply the operation to the selected ones.

FORMAL SHIFT: <wall with architectural features> → <registered endpoints> → [connect eligible points] → <site-specific drawing>

SOURCE FORMALISM: LeWitt gives a connection rule. The museum describes chalk-line execution and site-dependent endpoints. The public instruction gives no exhaustive test for endpoint eligibility.

OUR FORMALIZATION: [OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX] For a Memory Weaver scene, try “Connect every place where the damaged frame meets a repair. Leave decorative seams alone.” Record the candidate object IDs and the evidence for classifying each before drawing any connections.

TENSION: A procedural reading treats selection as setup and line drawing as the main operation. A descriptive reading locates the consequential decision in what the operator notices and counts. Once the points are fixed, both readings can produce the same lines.

MISSING: A record of how two LeWitt installation teams handled a marginal feature and whether their choices were considered equally valid.

BOUNDARY: Site dependence is documented. The claim that the draftspeople explicitly negotiated ambiguous features needs installation evidence.

CITATION TRAIL: [[AP-05]] → Wall Drawing 51’s two simultaneous Turin installations → surviving endpoint marks, photographs, or installation notes.

TEST: Give two operators the same room containing a doorframe, a socket, a patched hole, and a painted fake vent. Compare their endpoint sets before either makes a mark. Then give both the same endpoint set and compare the remaining differences.
