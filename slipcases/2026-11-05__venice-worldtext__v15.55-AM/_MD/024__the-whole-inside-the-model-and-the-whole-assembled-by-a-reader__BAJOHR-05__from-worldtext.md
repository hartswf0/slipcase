TITLE: The whole inside the model and the whole assembled by a reader
SOURCE: M. Beatrice Fazi — “The Computational Search for Unity,” online first 2024, discussion of the model’s representational world. Read alongside Bajohr’s Venice talk, 2025.

PASSAGE: [QUOTE — Fazi] “a togetherness of distributed representations.”
[PARAPHRASE] Fazi locates this unity inside computation. Bajohr brings it alongside Blumenberg’s novel, whose context becomes perceptible in time and across perspectives.

RESEARCH OBJECT: The change in what “a world” denotes when the phrase travels from a model to a novel.
LOCAL MOVE: Bajohr uses Fazi’s computational whole to make comparison with the literary whole possible.
SOURCE TERMS: Synthesis, representational reality, realized context, worldness.
WHAT BECAME STRANGE: A single trained model can support many mutually incompatible stories. A reader normally encounters one unfolding configuration at a time.
QUESTION: Which relation has to survive the move from the computational whole to the literary whole for the analogy to explain anything?
MECHANISM: The model structures possibilities for continuation. A realized sequence exposes some of those possibilities to a reader, who can link passages and revise earlier inferences.
FORMAL SHIFT: <relational possibilities> → <selected sequence> → [interpret across passages] → <provisional fictional context>.
SOURCE FORMALISM: NONE. The sources offer accounts of synthesis and context, with no shared metric.
OUR FORMALIZATION: [OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX] Track three objects separately: the distribution of continuations, the dependencies in one realized text, and the dependencies readers reconstruct.
TENSION: A. A specific dependency inside the model can explain a dependency readers recover. B. “World” connects forms of unity that can vary independently.
MISSING: A traced relation connecting a model-side dependency to a textual dependency and a reader inference.
BOUNDARY: The shared vocabulary licenses a comparison. It leaves the correspondence between the three objects to be shown.
CITATION TRAIL: Hans Blumenberg’s “The Concept of Reality and the Possibility of the Novel” and Fazi’s account of the model as its own world model.
TEST: Generate several continuations from the same checkpoint and opening. Locate a relation that varies across outputs, such as which character knows a secret. Ask what evidence readers use to recover the relation in each version, then test whether changing that evidence changes their inference.
