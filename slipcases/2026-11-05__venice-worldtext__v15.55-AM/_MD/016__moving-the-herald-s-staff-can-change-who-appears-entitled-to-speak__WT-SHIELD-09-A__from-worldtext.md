WT-SHIELD-09-A

TITLE:
Moving the herald’s staff can change who appears entitled to speak

SOURCE:
Homer — Iliad 18.502–506 — translated by A. T. Murray
https://www.theoi.com/Text/HomerIliad18.html

PASSAGE:
[QUOTE] “give judgment, each in turn”
[PARAPHRASE] The heralds restrain the crowd. The elders sit in a sacred circle, hold the heralds’ staves, and rise to give judgment.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
An interactive staff that can be picked up by an elder, a spectator, or the reader.

LOCAL MOVE:
The passage places objects, positions, and turns of speech in the same description of the hearing.

SOURCE TERMS:
Heralds, elders, sacred circle, staves, judgment, turn.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
[OUR INFERENCE] The parent asks what happens when the reader crosses the circle. Picking up a staff poses a sharper question. An interactive world must decide whether the reader can handle a prop, interrupt a proceeding, or acquire a recognized role. Those consequences can look identical in the first frame after pickup.

QUESTION:
When a medium lets the reader move an object, what relation to that object is the movement allowed to change?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Can the reader possess an object while the world still refuses the authority they take it to signify?

MECHANISM:
The physical state changes when the staff changes hands. A separate response by the heralds and elders determines whether the assembly treats that change as relevant to who can speak.

FORMAL SHIFT:
<Staff depicted in a hearing> → <Object with a holder and a procedural context> → [Reader takes the staff] → <Physical possession with possible recognition, interruption, or refusal>

SOURCE FORMALISM:
NONE

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
Track staff possession, recognized speaking turn, and audience response separately. Specify in the scene packet which of those states a pickup can change.

TENSION:
READING A: The staff helps make an already authorized speaker’s turn visible.
READING B: In the adaptation, acquiring the staff can itself confer or contest a turn.
The same image of a person holding the staff can fit both.

MISSING:
Evidence for how audiences infer authority from the relation among the staff, the holder, and the assembly’s response.

BOUNDARY:
Homer’s description associates staves with elders giving judgment. It supplies no rule saying that any holder acquires authority or that an outsider who takes a staff becomes a judge.

CITATION TRAIL:
[[WT-SHIELD-09]] → Iliad 18.502–506 → [[WT-ROLE-09-A-A]] on what remains possible after an action.

TEST:
Build two branches with the same pickup animation. In one, the herald recognizes the reader and yields the floor. In the other, the herald asks for the staff back and the elders continue. Ask when the reader believed they acquired a turn: at pickup, at recognition, or only when the assembly listened.
