ID: AP-03
TITLE: Chance supplies a curve that someone must authorize as a standard

SOURCE: Michael Newman — Art as ‘Operation’ — 2026 — discussion of Duchamp’s 3 Standard Stoppages in the supplied transcript. MoMA’s object record identifies the threads and wooden templates.

PASSAGE: [QUOTE, supplied transcript] “what constitutes a standard is a result of a performative that is ultimately a social act.”

RESEARCH OBJECT: The adoption of a contingent trace as an instrument of measure.

LOCAL MOVE: Newman separates the fall of the thread from the act of taking its shape as a standard.

SOURCE TERMS: chance, measure, standard, performative, contingency.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE: The procedure begins with the meter already in force. The fixed unit supplies the material for making a rival instrument that looks like it could authorize a unit of its own.

QUESTION: Where does the work’s claim to authority reside: in the drop protocol, the transfer to a template, the presentation of the object, or someone’s willingness to measure with it?

MECHANISM: A one-meter thread takes a shape. The shape is preserved and transferred to wood. The new template makes the trace available for repeated use and invites a comparison with a conventional standard.

FORMAL SHIFT: <unit-length thread> → <preserved curve> → [transfer to a template and designate] → <candidate standard>

SOURCE FORMALISM: The museum account specifies the thread length and drop height and describes the transfer of the curve. The process for authorizing a new standard is Newman's interpretive question.

OUR FORMALIZATION: [OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX] Distinguish generating a trace, preserving it, and authorizing reuse. Any of those steps could vary independently.

TENSION: The strongest rival reading is that the authority is deliberately theatrical. The templates can expose the contingency of standards even if nobody could or would use them as standards.

MISSING: Evidence about which features of the preserved traces resulted from the drop and which resulted from mounting or selection.

BOUNDARY: The finished objects show preservation and transfer. The fidelity of each trace to an unaided fall requires separate evidence.

CITATION TRAIL: Compare MoMA’s object record with Duchamp’s questionnaire and Herbert Molderings’s reconstruction of the work.

TEST: Take one feature visible in the surviving assembly and ask whether it could distinguish a free-fall trace from a mounted or adjusted one. Then ask which reading of the work, if either, actually depends on that distinction.
