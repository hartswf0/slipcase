ID: OD-07

TITLE: Cohen’s sheep test who can make a description take effect

SOURCE: Clifford Geertz — Thick Description: Toward an Interpretive Theory of Culture — 1973 — Cohen episode. Operation Describe — The Thick Prompt and The Cohen Case. Newman — Art as ‘Operation’ — 2026 — discussion of code switching and context.

PASSAGE: [PARAPHRASE] Cohen describes the sheep as his indemnity. French authorities treat him as a suspected spy, imprison him, and confiscate the flock. The Thick Prompt synthesis proposes that Cohen could have anticipated reclassification and framed the claim in terms legible to both orders.

RESEARCH OBJECT: The difference between describing an act in another code and gaining standing for that description.

LOCAL MOVE: Operation Describe makes reclassification a layer of thick prompting. Geertz’s account puts pressure on where the difficulty sits. The colonial authorities can impose the consequences of their reading.

SOURCE TERMS: ‘ar, indemnity, mezrag, reclassification, stakes.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE: Better phrasing might let the authorities understand Cohen’s claim perfectly while leaving them free to reject it. What travels across the boundary is a flock and a claim. The authority that made the claim effective may fail to travel with them.

QUESTION: When a thick prompt specifies two incompatible readings, who gets to decide which reading governs the next action?

DEEPER QUESTION: Can a worldtext hold incompatible accounts of the same event while preserving the unequal power of their interpreters?

MECHANISM: A claim has standing in one social order. At a boundary, another authority can reclassify the act and enforce a new outcome. The authority that controls custody changes the world even when the parties can paraphrase each other accurately.

FORMAL SHIFT: <flock and indemnity claim> → <case under another jurisdiction> → [accept, reject, or reclassify under unequal authority] → <changed custody and recourse>

SOURCE FORMALISM: Geertz supplies the narrative and the conflicting interpretations. Operation Describe supplies a six-layer design heuristic. Neither supplies a jurisdiction engine.

OUR FORMALIZATION: [OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX] Keep one flock and one history. Give the claimant and the authorities separate accounts of its status. Encode who can seize it, who can contest the seizure, and where a refusal is recorded. Treat those permissions as additional to the descriptive prompt.

TENSION: The synthesis reads the encounter as a problem of anticipating rival codes. Geertz’s account supports a rival hypothesis in which accurate translation leaves the imbalance of power intact. Both mechanisms could be present.

MISSING: Evidence that a differently presented claim could have changed the colonial decision in that setting.

BOUNDARY: The episode warrants questioning the phrase “thin prompting” as an explanation of Cohen’s predicament. It leaves the counterfactual outcome unresolved.

CITATION TRAIL: [[AP-10]] → Newman’s code-switching example → Geertz’s sequence of award, seizure, and appeal → Operation Describe’s sixth layer.

TEST: Compare two versions of an interactive scene. In one, improve the explanation of the claim while keeping authority fixed. In the other, keep the wording fixed and add an enforceable appeal. Track comprehension and custody separately. The difference identifies what belongs to description and what belongs to the world’s distribution of power.
