ID: BAJOHR-02
TITLE: A shared cast can survive incompatible readings of what happened
SOURCE: Hannes Bajohr — “The Latent Space of Meaning and the Novel,” Venice, 2025, discussion of (Berlin, Miami).

PASSAGE: [QUOTE — supplied transcript] “The text hangs together somehow, just not strongly.”
[PARAPHRASE] Recurring people, places, and things remain recognizable even as the reader supplies motives and smooths over temporal discontinuities.

RESEARCH OBJECT: Agreement about who and what belongs to a world alongside disagreement about what caused its events.
LOCAL MOVE: Bajohr uses the book’s partial legibility to separate worldness from the strength of its plot.
SOURCE TERMS: Weak force, worldness, interpretive labor.
WHAT BECAME STRANGE: Readers may agree they entered the same world while constructing mutually incompatible stories about it.
QUESTION: Does weak coherence name a property readers agree on, or the feeling that their individually repaired stories make sense?
MECHANISM: Recurring names and motifs cue readers to identify entities across passages. Readers can then supply different links among those entities and events.
FORMAL SHIFT: <passages> → <reader-identified entities and events> → [link recurrences and infer causes] → <reconstructed story>.
SOURCE FORMALISM: NONE.
OUR FORMALIZATION: [OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX] Elicit entity matches and causal links as separate annotations. Measure agreement on each.
TENSION: A. Entity agreement survives causal disagreement. B. Readers infer a private plot first and identify entities through it.
MISSING: Independent reader accounts elicited before exposure to Bajohr’s synopsis.
BOUNDARY: The lecturer’s reading supplies the distinction. Its distribution across readers is unmeasured.
CITATION TRAIL: Angus Fletcher’s 2021 essay “Why Computers Will Never Write Good Novels” and Bajohr’s account of readers repairing inconsistencies in Ensemble Park.
TEST: Give readers either an original sequence or one with selected recurring names consistently replaced by scene-specific aliases. Keep event order fixed. Compare shared entity maps, causal maps, and perceived world coherence.
