ID: OD-09

TITLE: The trial on Achilles’ shield gives the operator a proceeding and leaves a verdict to be made

SOURCE: Homer — Iliad 18.497–508 — trial scene, read in Samuel Butler’s translation. Bolter and Hartsoe — Operative Ekphrasis — §6 — the shield as worldtext. Geertz — Thick Description — Cohen episode.

PASSAGE: [PARAPHRASE] Two men dispute a blood-price, spectators take sides, heralds restrain the crowd, and elders take turns offering judgments. The passage depicts a proceeding without reporting its final verdict.

RESEARCH OBJECT: A consequential transition whose participants and partial procedure are described while its outcome remains open.

LOCAL MOVE: Operation Describe reads the shield as a world that exceeds its visible surface. The trial makes it possible to ask exactly what its description gives someone enough information to enact.

SOURCE TERMS: blood-price, assembly, heralds, elders, judgment, worldtext.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE: A rendering can visibly include the men, the crowd, the circle, and the gold while silently deciding whose account is true. A playable version can preserve the dispute by tracking what each person claims, who has heard it, and whether anyone authorized a judgment.

QUESTION: What should a world-making operator do when the source describes an institution in action and leaves a consequential decision underdetermined?

DEEPER QUESTION: Who gets to complete the world where the source is silent: the compiler, the author, the people inhabiting it, or the authority represented inside it?

MECHANISM: Describe actors and their positions. Bind speech and action to roles. Record competing claims without resolving them by default. A hearing can then advance through publicly observable turns. Any verdict requires an additional rule, a delegated judgment, or a deliberate decision to leave the case open.

FORMAL SHIFT: <described dispute> → <roles, claims, turns, and unresolved status> → [hear, contest, and possibly adjudicate] → <persistent case history>

SOURCE FORMALISM: The passage gives an order of activity, distinct roles, and the presence of gold. It does not supply a full adjudication procedure. Translations differ on the force of one party’s claim about payment.

OUR FORMALIZATION: [OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX] “The claimant says the blood-price remains unsettled. The other party makes a competing claim. The crowd may react but cannot settle the case. Give each elder a turn. Record the claims and reactions. Leave the outcome pending unless an authorized participant supplies a decision.” The last two rules are design choices to make the passage playable.

TENSION: Filling the gap can create a usable simulation and quietly resolve a contested reading. Leaving the gap open can preserve the text’s uncertainty and produce a world whose inhabitants need some other means of acting.

MISSING: A criterion for when a completion counts as interpretation that should be visible to the user, versus a routine implementation decision.

BOUNDARY: The procedural reconstruction can test worldtext’s claim about rules, memory, and consequence. It cannot establish a definitive account of Homeric legal procedure.

CITATION TRAIL: [[AP-10]] and [[AP-06]] → Iliad 18.497–508 → compare Butler with Walter Leaf’s “The Trial Scene in Iliad XVIII” → test what each reading changes in the initial state.

TEST: Build two paper walkthroughs from the same passage. In one, treat payment as alleged to have occurred. In the other, treat payment as an offer whose acceptance is disputed. Give participants the same roles and turns. Compare which questions they ask and what decisions become possible before any visual differences are introduced.
