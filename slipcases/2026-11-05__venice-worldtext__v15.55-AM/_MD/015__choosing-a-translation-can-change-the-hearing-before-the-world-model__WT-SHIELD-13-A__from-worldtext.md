TITLE:
Choosing a translation can change the hearing before the world model renders it

SOURCE:
Homer — Iliad 18.497–501 — translated by A. T. Murray
https://www.theoi.com/Text/HomerIliad18.html
Homer — Iliad 18.497–501 — translated by Samuel Butler
https://beta.perseus.tufts.edu/urn%3Acts%3AgreekLit%3Atlg0012.tlg001.perseus-eng4%3A18.497/

PASSAGE:
[QUOTE — Murray] “he had paid all”
[QUOTE — Butler] “he had the right to pay off the damages in full”

RESEARCH OBJECT:
A single bible entry describing what the first disputant claims.

LOCAL MOVE:
Murray gives the speaker a claim about a completed payment. Butler gives him a claim about his right to make a payment.

SOURCE TERMS:
Blood-price, payment, right to pay, assembly.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
[OUR INFERENCE] The parent keeps the verdict open while treating the case as settled enough to stage. These translations differ earlier. In one adaptation, a witness who saw money change hands could matter. In the other, even an undisputed record of payment would leave the right to settle in question.

QUESTION:
Can a Worldtext bible preserve rival accounts of what the hearing is about before it branches on the verdict?

DEEPER QUESTION:
At what point does a production summary become a ruling on the source?

MECHANISM:
A translation supplies the dispute’s operative proposition. A bible entry carries that proposition into the scene packet. The packet then shapes which witnesses, props, and actions a writer or model introduces as relevant.

FORMAL SHIFT:
<Passage with divergent translations> → <Competing descriptions of the dispute> → [Select or retain a reading in the bible] → <Different evidence becomes relevant in the hearing>

SOURCE FORMALISM:
NONE

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
Give each adaptation premise a translation reference and scope. Record completed payment and right to settle as different propositions. Preserve the speaker attribution in both.

TENSION:
A production can choose one translation to give the scene a clear dramatic question. A comparative Worldtext can retain both and let the reader discover that changing the translation changes what would settle the case.

MISSING:
A reading of the Greek and its interpretive history that evaluates the two translations.

BOUNDARY:
The excerpts establish a consequential difference between English renderings. They do not settle the Greek or establish that the first speaker’s claim is true within the scene.

CITATION TRAIL:
[[WT-SHIELD-13]] → Murray and Butler on Iliad 18.497–501 → [[WT-ROLE-03]] on how translation changes the variables an inquiry needs.

TEST:
Give two writers the same blocking and ending constraint. Give one Murray’s formulation and the other Butler’s. Ask each to write one question an elder would ask and name one piece of evidence that could answer it. Then introduce the same receipt in both scenes and see what it actually resolves.
