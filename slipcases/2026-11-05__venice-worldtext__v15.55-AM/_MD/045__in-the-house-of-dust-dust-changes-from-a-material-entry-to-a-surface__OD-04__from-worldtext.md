TITLE: In The House of Dust, “dust” changes from a material entry to a surface someone has to make

SOURCE: Alison Knowles — interview with Janet Sarbanes — conducted 2011 — account of fabricating and using The House of Dust. Museum of Modern Art — Thinking Machines extended labels — account of the four lists and Tenney’s FORTRAN implementation. Michael Newman — Art as ‘Operation’ — 2026 — Knowles discussion.

PASSAGE: [QUOTE] Knowles recalls using finely ground material on the exterior so it had “this sort of pavementlike quality.” [PARAPHRASE] She describes a fabricated shell, struts, wood blocks, poured cement, and stone or gravel chips.

RESEARCH OBJECT: The conversion of a material word into a decision about what a building can be made of and how its surface reads.

LOCAL MOVE: Newman follows computer-generated verse into built form. Knowles’s account identifies a material negotiation inside that passage.

SOURCE TERMS: material, shell, cement, gravel, foundry, pavementlike.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE: In the poem generator, dust is a selectable material entry. In the fabricated work, it can become an account of a surface rather than the load-bearing substance. Fidelity can involve changing what kind of constraint the word expresses.

QUESTION: Who decides that a word has changed jobs when a description moves from one medium to another?

MECHANISM: Knowles supplies the vocabulary. Tenney implements a combinatorial procedure. A run produces a stanza. Knowles selects and works through the stanza in a fabrication setting. The material response is produced through decisions involving construction and perceptual effect.

FORMAL SHIFT: <material word in a list> → <selected line in a stanza> → [interpret under fabrication conditions] → <surface and structure with distinct roles>

SOURCE FORMALISM: Four lists and a computer-generated quatrain are documented. The construction account supplies materials and fabrication steps. It does not provide a rule mapping a stanza entry to a building specification.

OUR FORMALIZATION: [OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX] Use “an orb made of memory ash.” Ask for a working scene that specifies separately what holds the orb together, what its surface looks like, and what happens when it is touched. Require the operator to record which meaning of “ash” each decision preserves.

TENSION: The gritty surface can be read as a material translation of dust. It could also be a practical or aesthetic choice reached for independent reasons. The interview verifies the materials but leaves the decision’s genealogy open.

MISSING: A dated fabrication sketch, letter, or interview passage explicitly connecting the poem’s material entry to a particular construction decision.

BOUNDARY: The word-to-material shift is our reconstruction. Knowles’s account supports the difference between the stanza’s material description and the documented build.
