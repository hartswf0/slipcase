ID: AP-05
TITLE: A line can obey the instruction and fail the installation

SOURCE: Michael Newman — Art as ‘Operation’ — 2026 — LeWitt discussion in the supplied transcript. MASS MoCA — Wall Drawing 159 — installation account.

PASSAGE: [QUOTE, supplied transcript] “the concept is material and the execution by hand involves embodied intellect.”

RESEARCH OBJECT: A decision the draftsman makes on site that a written instruction delegates.

LOCAL MOVE: Newman complicates the division between conception and execution. MASS MoCA’s account supplies an instance where earlier solutions can serve as templates while later draftspeople still interpret the instruction.

SOURCE TERMS: conception, execution, embodied intellect, architectural context, draftsman.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE: Two installations might satisfy a geometric reading of the instruction and differ in whether they qualify as realizations of the same work. Exact geometric compliance and institutional acceptance are separate questions.

QUESTION: Which choices are left to the draftsman, and who decides whether a choice exceeds that discretion?

MECHANISM: The instruction refers to geometric features. A draftsman identifies those features in a material setting, resolves ambiguities, and draws. The installation is then taken up as a realization of the work.

FORMAL SHIFT: <written geometric instruction> → <references located in a wall drawing> → [interpret and draw] → <site-specific realization>

SOURCE FORMALISM: MASS MoCA describes constructions using corners, side midpoints, and the center. Its public account does not provide a complete acceptance procedure.

OUR FORMALIZATION: [OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX] Give one implementation an explicit rule for every geometric ambiguity. Ask an experienced draftsman to mark the cases in which following that rule would produce a doubtful realization.

TENSION: Newman calls the LeWitt instruction algorithm-like and the Fluxus score prompt-like. A LeWitt instruction can already delegate interpretation. Degree and location of delegation may distinguish particular works better than those two labels.

MISSING: An example of an installation decision that was rejected, revised, or explicitly accepted after disagreement.

BOUNDARY: Physical variation alone cannot tell us which choices were understood as interpretation, error, or authorized adaptation.

CITATION TRAIL: MASS MoCA’s account of Wall Drawing 159. Follow the first-installation documentation and later installation records for the same work.

TEST: Compare two documented realizations. Find one visible difference and trace it back to the instruction, the local surface, or an installer’s recorded decision.
