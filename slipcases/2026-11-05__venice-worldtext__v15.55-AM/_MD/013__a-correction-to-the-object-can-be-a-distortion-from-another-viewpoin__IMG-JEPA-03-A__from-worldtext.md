TITLE:
A correction to the object can be a distortion from another viewpoint

SOURCE:
Plato — Sophist — 235d–236c — translated by Benjamin Jowett
Randall Balestriero and Yann LeCun — Proving and Improving the Platonic Representation Hypothesis with JEPAs — 2026 — §2.3

PASSAGE:
[QUOTE — Plato, Jowett translation] “make only the proportions which appear to be beautiful, disregarding the real ones.”

RESEARCH OBJECT:
The large sculpture whose proportions are altered for the spectator’s position.

LOCAL MOVE:
The Stranger distinguishes likeness-making from appearance-making. A maker can enlarge distant parts so they look proportionate from below. The passage presents this as abandoning true proportions, even though the alteration is what gives the intended spectator a proportionate appearance.

SOURCE TERMS:
Likeness-making, appearances, proportions, position of the spectator, viewpoint, nuisance.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
[OUR INFERENCE] In JEPA §2.3, viewpoint can vary across observations of a fixed object. In the sculpture example, the maker alters the object in anticipation of the viewpoint. What the spectator can see helps determine what gets made.

QUESTION:
When a prompt says “make the figure look proportionate from below,” what should a caption critic check: the dimensions of the figure or the appearance from below?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Could two images agree on a visible relation precisely because the objects they depict differ?

MECHANISM:
Geometry and viewing position jointly determine apparent proportions. A maker can change one to compensate for the other. A verbal description can specify either the construction or the desired appearance, and those instructions can call for different objects.

FORMAL SHIFT:
<Object proportions and spectator position> → <projected proportions> → [alter the object for the intended view] → <appearance that may cease to match from another position>

SOURCE FORMALISM:
NONE

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
Label a description’s target as object geometry, projected appearance from a specified view, or appearance across views.

TENSION:
The Stranger’s classification gives priority to actual proportions. A description written for the person standing below the sculpture could be correct about appearance while being wrong if read as a statement about dimensions.

MISSING:
A way for the critic to tell which level the words specify before it scores a match.

BOUNDARY:
Plato gives an example of making appearances, with no claim about learned invariance. The connection to the JEPA nuisance variable is an analytical comparison.

CITATION TRAIL:
[[IMG-JEPA-03]] → JEPA §2.3 on viewpoint → Sophist 235d–236c → Republic X 598a–b on the couch seen from different positions.

TEST:
Make two renders from below: one of a proportionate figure and one of a figure whose upper body has been enlarged to compensate for perspective. Render each again from eye level. Ask critics to evaluate separately whether the head is oversized and whether it looks oversized from below. Record whether their judgments follow the object or the view.
