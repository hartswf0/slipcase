TITLE: The same image can guide, document, and expose an operation

SOURCE: Michael Newman — Art as ‘Operation’ — 2026 — Farocki discussion in the supplied transcript. Citation trail verified to Harun Farocki’s “Phantom Images.”

PASSAGE: [QUOTE, supplied transcript] “what an image does or activates within a technical circuit.”

RESEARCH OBJECT: The role a particular copy or feed plays in an operation.

LOCAL MOVE: Newman follows the move from an image’s depicted content to its causal function, then invokes broadcast and gallery contexts that give the footage new audiences.

SOURCE TERMS: operational image, feedback, technical circuit, machines for machines.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE: A visually unchanged sequence can participate in guidance, be released as evidence, and become material for a critical montage. Its operative status can change at each handoff.

QUESTION: Which copy of the image entered the guidance circuit, and what can the televised copy establish about that role?

MECHANISM: A feed can inform an action when it reaches an operator or component able to change a state. A recording or copy can later be routed to viewers and used to support a different claim or action.

FORMAL SHIFT: <sensor image> → <signal, record, or broadcast copy> → [route to a recipient able to act] → <guidance, public narration, or critical scrutiny>

SOURCE FORMALISM: The supplied talk describes the guidance function. It gives no system diagram identifying the consumers of the signal or the point where a recording was made.

OUR FORMALIZATION: [OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX] Treat “operational” as a relation among an image, a recipient, and an action at a specified stage.

TENSION: The phrase “machines for machines” directs attention to automation. Depending on the system, human operators, telemetry, autonomous guidance, and public-affairs personnel can occupy different points in the image’s path.

MISSING: Provenance for one specific sequence and technical evidence identifying the actors or components that used its live feed.

BOUNDARY: A broadcast clip establishes that footage reached a public circuit. Its role in steering or targeting requires evidence from the original system.

CITATION TRAIL: Farocki’s “Phantom Images,” p. 17, then the provenance and documentation of a single sequence reused in Eye/Machine.

TEST: Trace one clip from acquisition through release. At each documented handoff, ask what decision or physical action would have changed if the feed or copy had been withheld.
