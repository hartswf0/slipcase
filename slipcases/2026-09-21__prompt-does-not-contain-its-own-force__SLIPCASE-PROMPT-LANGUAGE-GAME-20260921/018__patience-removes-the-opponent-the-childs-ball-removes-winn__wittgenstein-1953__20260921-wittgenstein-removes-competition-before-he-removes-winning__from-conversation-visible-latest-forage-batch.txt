TITLE:
Patience Removes the Opponent; the Child’s Ball Removes Winning and Losing Too

SOURCE:
Ludwig Wittgenstein — Philosophical Investigations — 1953 — §66. ([wab.uib.no](https://wab.uib.no/ojs/index.php/agora-wab/article/view/2946/3613?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE] Wittgenstein asks whether games always involve winning and losing or competition between players, then directs attention to patience. He next moves to ball games and gives the case of a child throwing a ball at a wall and catching it, where winning and losing disappear as well. ([wab.uib.no](https://wab.uib.no/ojs/index.php/agora-wab/article/view/2946/3613?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

RESEARCH OBJECT:
A sequence of neighboring cases removing apparently central properties one at a time rather than jumping directly to a radically different example.

LOCAL MOVE:
The argument pressures definitions incrementally: competition can disappear while something still comfortably counts as a game; then winning/losing can disappear too.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The useful method is not merely collecting weird edge cases. It is constructing a controlled path where each neighboring case removes one candidate essential property while preserving enough continuity to keep comparison intelligible.

QUESTION:
What is the prompt equivalent of moving from competitive game → patience → child-with-ball: a sequence that removes one supposedly essential prompt property at each step?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Can the five-term definition MOVE + GAME + SITUATION + EXECUTOR + TEST be pressure-tested through minimal pairs rather than through unrelated counterexamples?
