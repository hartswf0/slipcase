002. Under Time Pressure, Engelbart’s Brick-Pencil Did Not Merely Slow Writing: It Made the Writing Larger
    ID: 20260921-brick-pencil-changes-the-size-of-thoughts-on-paper
    WHAT BECAME STRANGE: The medium’s limitation becomes visible in the morphology of the resulting language artifact. Large writing is not a “preference” for large writing; it is evidence of the control problem produced by the tool.
    DEEPER QUESTION: Could prompt corpora be read archaeologically, with verbosity, headings, repetition, capitalization, delimiters, and defensive constraints treated as traces of the failures users were compensating for?
