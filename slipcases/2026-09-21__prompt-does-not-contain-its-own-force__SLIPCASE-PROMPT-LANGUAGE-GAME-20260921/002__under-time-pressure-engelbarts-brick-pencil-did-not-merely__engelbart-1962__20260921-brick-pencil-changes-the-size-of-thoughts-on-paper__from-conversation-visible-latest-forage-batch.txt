TITLE:
Under Time Pressure, Engelbart’s Brick-Pencil Did Not Merely Slow Writing: It Made the Writing Larger

SOURCE:
Douglas C. Engelbart — Augmenting Human Intellect: A Conceptual Framework — 1962 — §2c4l–m. ([dougengelbart.org](https://dougengelbart.org/content/view/138/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE] Engelbart reports that writing with a pencil fixed to a brick was slower and less precise. When experimenters tried to hurry, they had to enlarge the writing. Repeating the passage also tired the hand and arm.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
A change in tool resistance altering the spatial form of symbolic output, not merely its production speed.

LOCAL MOVE:
Engelbart observes a compensatory deformation: increased physical difficulty produces larger marks when speed is demanded.

SOURCE TERMS:

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The medium’s limitation becomes visible in the morphology of the resulting language artifact. Large writing is not a “preference” for large writing; it is evidence of the control problem produced by the tool.

QUESTION:
Which recognizable prompt forms are morphological traces of friction in current model interfaces rather than stable strategies for describing tasks?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Could prompt corpora be read archaeologically, with verbosity, headings, repetition, capitalization, delimiters, and defensive constraints treated as traces of the failures users were compensating for?

MECHANISM:
tool makes fine control costly
→ user attempts same temporal performance
→ user increases gesture scale
→ symbol morphology changes
→ changed morphology becomes normal under that tool.

BOUNDARY:
The experiment directly demonstrates deformation of handwriting under physical resistance. Reading prompt syntax as analogous evidence requires historical testing.
