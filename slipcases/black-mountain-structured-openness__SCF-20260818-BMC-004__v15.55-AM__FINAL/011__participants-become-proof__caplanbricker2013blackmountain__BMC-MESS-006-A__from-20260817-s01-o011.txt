BMC-MESS-006-A

TITLE:
Belief becomes dangerous when participants must make the experiment succeed in order to prove that the experiment works.

SOURCE:
Nora Caplan-Bricker — “This North Carolina Campus Was Meant to Show Off the Future of Online Education” — The New Republic — 2013 — https://newrepublic.com/article/116014/black-mountain-sole-mooc-campus-online-learners-has-rough-start

PASSAGE:
[QUOTE] “if we don’t, that disproves the process and says it doesn’t work.”

RESEARCH OBJECT:
Participant commitment can cease to be merely a resource for sustaining an experiment and become part of the experiment’s evidentiary apparatus: participants feel responsible not only for participating but for producing the success that will validate the method.

LOCAL MOVE:
The SOLE case reverses the optimistic version of “belief as infrastructure.” Commitment can keep an unstable organization running, but it can also make participants responsible for protecting the theory from falsification.

SOURCE TERMS:
“responsible”
“obligation”
“process”
“disproves”
“proof of concept”
“community”

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
An experimental institution may ask the same people to be subjects of the experiment, operators of the experiment, and evidence that the experiment succeeds.

QUESTION:
When does belief stop functioning as coordination and begin contaminating the experiment’s ability to learn from failure?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Can a community genuinely test its own institutional model when participant commitment makes failure personally or morally costly to acknowledge?

MECHANISM:
participants commit to experimental model
→ model’s survival depends on participant labor
→ participant outcomes become evidence about model
→ failure threatens both institution and participants’ prior commitment
→ participants acquire incentive to repair, reinterpret, or personally absorb failure
→ falsification becomes socially costly.

FORMAL SHIFT:
<belief in shared endeavor>
→ <obligation to make endeavor succeed>
→ [PARTICIPATE + REPAIR + DEMONSTRATE]
→ <participant-produced evidence of viability>

SOURCE FORMALISM:
SOLE participants were involved in sustaining the institution itself; one described feeling responsible for the organization because failure would “disprove the process.” The article also reports participant involvement in crowdfunding the institution.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

Ordinary experimental separation:

system → outcome → evaluation.

SOLE-like entanglement:

participant belief
→ participant labor
→ system outcome
→ evidence for system
↘_____________________↑

The evaluator is partially inside the causal loop.

TENSION:
Exactly this recursive commitment may have characterized historical Black Mountain College and helped it persist for decades. The problem is therefore not simply that belief enters the loop, but whether the institution possesses mechanisms allowing committed participants to register failure without experiencing it as betrayal.

MISSING:
Practices by which either Black Mountain institution distinguished “the experiment has encountered evidence against itself” from “participants have failed to believe or work hard enough.”

BOUNDARY:
One participant’s statement cannot establish that all SOLEmates experienced ideological pressure or that leadership deliberately imposed such a burden.

CITATION TRAIL:
[[BMC-MESS-006]]
→ belief proposed as infrastructure capable of absorbing weak formal structure
→ SOLE participants explicitly connect their own performance to proof of the process
→ commitment becomes an epistemic variable rather than merely a coordination variable.

TEST:
Find episodes in both Black Mountain institutions where participants argued that a failure reflected the model itself versus insufficient effort, commitment, or implementation. Code who was permitted to attribute failure to the system and what happened to those who did.
