TITLE:
The flagship success case never used the flagship technology.

SOURCE:
Nora Caplan-Bricker — “This North Carolina Campus Was Meant to Show Off the Future of Online Education” — The New Republic — 2013.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE] Black Mountain SOLE called Chris Coleman its “first major case study” after his existing startup won seed funding and he left for Philadelphia. He had not taken a single MOOC.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
A proof-of-concept whose success subtracts the concept supposedly being proved. The institution organized itself around the proposition that MOOCs could supply curriculum while a campus supplied community, yet one of its clearest success stories succeeded without consuming the curricular technology at all.

LOCAL MOVE:
Caplan-Bricker lets SOLE’s preferred success case become evidence against the most obvious description of what SOLE was.

SOURCE TERMS:
“major case study”
“seed funding”
“MOOC”
“desired result”
“proof of concept”

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The apparent failure of the MOOC premise may be the more valuable result. Something in the surrounding apparatus—permission, peers, time, residential proximity, entrepreneurial legitimacy, mentors, or simply freedom from another institution—may have been doing the consequential work.

QUESTION:
What was the actual active ingredient of Black Mountain SOLE if a paradigmatic successful participant could bypass its defining instructional technology?

DEEPER QUESTION:
How often does an experimental institution discover what it actually built only when its most successful users misuse, ignore, or discard the component its founders considered essential?

MECHANISM:
institution proposes technology T as core
→ participant enters surrounding environment E
→ participant exploits E while bypassing T
→ participant produces highly legible success
→ institution treats outcome as validation
→ success case becomes evidence that E, not T, may contain the operative mechanism.

FORMAL SHIFT:
<MOOC-centered educational experiment>
→ <participant who does not use MOOCs>
→ [EXPLOIT ADJACENT AFFORDANCES]
→ <institutionally celebrated success>

SOURCE FORMALISM:
NONE

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

Proposed model:

SUCCESS = f(MOOC + COMMUNITY)

Aberrant case:

MOOC = 0
SUCCESS = 1

Therefore the case does not identify the full function, but it destroys any simple necessity claim:

MOOC ≠ necessary condition for observed success.

The “failure” of the core technology becomes a stepping stone toward identifying the hidden system.

TENSION:
The case may still validate SOLE’s broader proposition that a residential community can support self-directed projects. Alternatively, calling Coleman a success may simply reveal that entrepreneurial outcomes were already privileged as the institution’s preferred metric.

MISSING:
What Coleman actually used at SOLE: mentors, peers, space, routines, introductions, legitimacy, staff advice, funding networks, or merely uninterrupted project time.

BOUNDARY:
One case cannot establish that MOOCs were useless to all participants. It establishes something stranger and narrower: SOLE’s own showcase case did not demonstrate the necessity of SOLE’s nominal curricular technology.

CITATION TRAIL:
[[BMC-MESS-003-A]]

→ SOLE’s “first major case study”
→ the case succeeds without the institution’s defining technology
→ investigate success-by-bypass as a diagnostic for hidden institutional machinery.

TEST:
Reconstruct every participant whom SOLE publicly treated as a success. For each, record MOOC use, staff interaction, peer interaction, project state on arrival, resource use, and outcome. Remove the nominal program components one by one and ask which components remain common across successful cases.
