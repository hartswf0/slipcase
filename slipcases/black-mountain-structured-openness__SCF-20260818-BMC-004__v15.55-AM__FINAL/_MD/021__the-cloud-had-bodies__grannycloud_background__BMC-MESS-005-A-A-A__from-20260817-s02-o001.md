TITLE:
The cloud stopped when bodies could no longer reach it.

SOURCE:
The Granny Cloud — “Background & History” — n.d.

SOURCE URL:
https://thegrannycloud.org/about/background/

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE] The Granny Cloud’s official history reports that during COVID-19 many children could no longer reach the centres from which they connected, often had minimal or no home internet, and that ageing volunteers and health challenges also contributed to the initiative’s 2022 wind-down. The project resumed in 2026 at one location with a fresh volunteer team.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
Embodied infrastructure inside an apparently disembodied educational “cloud.” Online mediation depended on children reaching physical centres, network access, and a continuing population of available human volunteers.

LOCAL MOVE:
The earlier zettel found the teacher returning as a remote grandmother. The project history pushes the mechanism one level lower: the remote grandmother herself depends on terminals, rooms, connectivity, health, age, schedules, and bodies capable of arriving on both ends of the connection.

SOURCE TERMS:
“encouragement”

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The “cloud” failed through terrestrial conditions. Children could not reach the places where the cloud became available, and the people supplying its affective infrastructure aged.

QUESTION:
What counts as the infrastructure of self-organized learning when the interaction appears online but its continuity depends on bodies, places, connectivity, care, and volunteer time?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Do theories of digital self-organization systematically misclassify embodied maintenance as background because the visible interaction occurs through a screen?

MECHANISM:
children can reach a shared access point
→ connectivity makes remote contact possible
→ volunteers supply language, encouragement, continuity, and attention
→ repeated interaction supports self-directed inquiry
→ physical access or volunteer availability breaks
→ the nominally online relation becomes unavailable.

FORMAL SHIFT:
<remote pedagogical cloud>
→ <centres + connectivity + available volunteers>
→ [MAINTAIN ACCESS ON BOTH ENDS]
→ <continuable mediated learning>

SOURCE FORMALISM:
NONE

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

Let:
A = learner access to a usable centre/device
N = network availability
V = available mediator population
C = continuity of coordination

Then a remote learning relation R requires more than an online service:

R ≈ f(A, N, V, C)

If any indispensable term approaches zero, the “cloud” may disappear locally even while the internet itself remains available elsewhere.

TENSION:
The official history is an organizational self-account, not an independent causal evaluation. The same closures may also reflect funding, school participation, project design, or changing institutional priorities.

MISSING:
Session-level records connecting closure or continuity to centre access, connectivity, volunteer availability, funding, and local coordination; demographic and workload data for volunteers; evidence from children and centre coordinators.

BOUNDARY:
The source supports the existence of embodied and infrastructural dependencies. It does not establish their relative causal weight or imply that all online self-organized learning requires the Granny Cloud model.

TEST:
Construct a timeline of Granny Cloud centres and interruptions. For each location and period, record learner access, internet access, volunteer availability, local coordinator capacity, funding, session frequency, and closure/continuity. Test which combinations predict whether the supposedly online relation remains usable.
