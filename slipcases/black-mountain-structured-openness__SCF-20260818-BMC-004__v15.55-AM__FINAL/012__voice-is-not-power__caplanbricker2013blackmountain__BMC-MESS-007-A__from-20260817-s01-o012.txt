ZETTEL

ID:
BMC-MESS-007-A

TITLE:
“Everything is horizontal” survived rhetorically even when control over material survival remained vertical.

SOURCE:
Nora Caplan-Bricker — “This North Carolina Campus Was Meant to Show Off the Future of Online Education” — The New Republic — 2013 — https://newrepublic.com/article/116014/black-mountain-sole-mooc-campus-online-learners-has-rough-start

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE] SOLE described itself as horizontal and invited even a visiting reporter to participate in decisions. Yet when scholarship funding was shortened, leadership described the change as a community decision while affected participants characterized it as leadership’s call; one learned of it through a one-month notice at her bedroom door.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
Participatory voice and material decision power can separate. An institution can distribute conversational participation broadly while retaining concentrated control over money, housing, contracts, and continued membership.

LOCAL MOVE:
Caplan-Bricker supplies the concrete mechanism missing from the previous Black Mountain governance zettel: informal hierarchy becomes visible when decisions cross from discussion into control of scarce resources.

SOURCE TERMS:
“horizontal”
“community meeting”
“leadership’s call”
“Pay It Forward”
“one month’s notice”
“have a say”

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The crucial test of horizontality may not be who is invited to speak but who controls the state transitions that determine whether another participant can remain in the institution.

QUESTION:
Which decisions reveal the actual topology of power inside an organization that describes itself as horizontal?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Should democratic governance be modeled as distribution of voice, distribution of decision rights, distribution of resources, or distribution of the capacity to impose consequences?

MECHANISM:
participatory discourse is broadly distributed
→ scarce resource decision arises
→ actors controlling finance possess asymmetric information and execution authority
→ decision is narrated through communal language
→ material consequence is imposed asymmetrically
→ formal horizontality and operational hierarchy diverge.

FORMAL SHIFT:
<everyone has a say>
→ <unequal control of consequential resources>
→ [ALLOCATE / WITHDRAW / NOTIFY]
→ <effective hierarchy>

SOURCE FORMALISM:
The source contrasts the organization’s claim that “Everything is horizontal” with conflicting accounts of the decision to limit scholarship support and the way affected participants received notice.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

For actor i, distinguish:

V_i = voice rights
D_i = formal decision rights
R_i = resource control
E_i = execution authority

Horizontality in V does not entail:

V_i ≈ V_j
⇒ D_i ≈ D_j
⇒ R_i ≈ R_j
⇒ E_i ≈ E_j.

The consequential topology may be encoded in R and E rather than V.

TENSION:
Financial scarcity can require decisions that no horizontal deliberative procedure can make painless. The existence of unequal consequences does not itself demonstrate bad faith; it makes the location of final authority empirically traceable.

MISSING:
SOLE’s formal legal authority, control over bank accounts, scholarship agreements, employment roles, voting rules, and procedures for binding community decisions.

BOUNDARY:
Caplan-Bricker reports conflicting participant and leadership accounts. The article establishes discrepancy but cannot by itself determine the complete governance structure or intentions of those involved.

CITATION TRAIL:

TEST:
For every major SOLE decision documented in available records, identify who could propose, deliberate, veto, authorize expenditure, sign contracts, change funding, and execute the result. Reconstruct governance as an actual permission graph rather than from organizational rhetoric.
