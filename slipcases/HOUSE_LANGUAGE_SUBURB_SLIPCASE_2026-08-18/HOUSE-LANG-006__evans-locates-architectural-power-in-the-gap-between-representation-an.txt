ID:
HOUSE-LANG-006

TITLE:
EVANS LOCATES ARCHITECTURAL POWER IN THE GAP BETWEEN REPRESENTATION AND BUILDING, NOT IN THEIR IDENTITY.

SOURCE:
Robin Evans — “Translations from Drawing to Building” — in Translations from Drawing to Building and Other Essays — 1997 — pp. 154–155.

PASSAGE:
[QUOTE]
“Recognition of the drawing’s power as a medium turns out…to be recognition of the drawing’s distinctness from…what is represented.”

RESEARCH OBJECT:
Representation-to-building difference as productive rather than defective.

LOCAL MOVE:
Evans compares linguistic translation with the architectural passage from drawing to building but rejects the fantasy of a neutral medium through which meaning travels unchanged.

SOURCE TERMS:
translation; drawing; building; medium; distinctness; likeness; architecture; language; communication

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The interesting fact may be not that a house can pass through words but that the house cannot pass unchanged through any representation.

QUESTION:
What if “The House That Words Built” should become “The House Translation Built”?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Which deviations between prompt, model, drawing, fabrication instruction, construction, and inhabited building are noise—and which are where architectural invention occurs?

MECHANISM:
A representational medium transforms what can be conceived, communicated, measured, and built.

TENSION:
“The House That Words Built” makes the first medium sovereign; Evans makes intermediate media unavoidable.

MISSING:
Transformations introduced by projection, scale, notation, software, file format, convention, contract, tolerance, builder interpretation, site.

BOUNDARY:
Evans concerns drawing and building; he does not establish that natural-language prompting behaves like architectural drawing.

CITATION TRAIL:
Robin Evans; Mario Carpo; Alberti; architectural specification studies; BIM/CAD interoperability.

TEST:
Recover every representation of one completed component and ask which representation most strongly determined the final thing.
