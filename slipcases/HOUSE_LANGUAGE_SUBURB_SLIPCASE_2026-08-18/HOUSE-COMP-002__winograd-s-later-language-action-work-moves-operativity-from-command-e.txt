HOUSE-COMP-002

TITLE:
WINOGRAD’S LATER LANGUAGE/ACTION WORK MOVES OPERATIVITY FROM COMMAND EXECUTION TO THE SOCIAL ORGANIZATION OF COMMITMENT.

SOURCE:
Terry Winograd and Fernando Flores — Understanding Computers and Cognition: A New Foundation for Design — 1986; Terry Winograd — “A Language/Action Perspective on the Design of Cooperative Work” — 1986.

PASSAGE:
[QUOTE]
“This paper introduces a perspective based on language as action, and explores its consequences for practical system design.”

RESEARCH OBJECT:
THE DIFFERENCE BETWEEN EXECUTABLE COMMAND AND SOCIALLY OPERATIVE UTTERANCE.

LOCAL MOVE:
The later Winograd/Flores trajectory changes the theoretical object: language matters because people accomplish coordinated activity through communicative acts, commitments, requests, responses, and breakdowns.

SOURCE TERMS:
language; action; conversation; commitment; cooperative work; design; breakdown

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
There are at least two ways for words to do something computationally: SHRDLU gives WORDS → MACHINE INTERPRETATION → EXECUTION; language/action gives WORDS → HUMAN COMMITMENTS → COORDINATED ACTIVITY.

QUESTION:
Does “The House That Words Built” concern words that machines execute or words through which people become obligated to act?

DEEPER QUESTION:
What happens when both mechanisms coexist in one construction stack?

MECHANISM:
Communicative acts reorganize relations among actors; designed computer systems can make commitments explicit, persistent, and actionable within cooperative work.

FORMAL SHIFT:
<COORDINATION PROBLEM>
→ <LANGUAGE ACT>
→ [CREATE / MODIFY COMMITMENT]
→ <COORDINATED HUMAN ACTION>

SOURCE FORMALISM:
The language/action perspective structures cooperative work around recognizable communicative actions and consequences; it is not a machine-control language.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
REQUEST → COMMITMENT → AUTHORIZED TASK → WORK → DECLARATION OF COMPLETION → ACCEPTANCE. Compare COMMAND → PARSE → EXECUTE with REQUEST → PROMISE → COORDINATE → ACT.

TENSION:
A theory of executable description can erase people, institutions, contracts, permissions, and promises that make construction happen.

MISSING:
The connection between commitment structures and physical representations: who translates promise into schedule, drawing, procurement, machine command, inspection?

BOUNDARY:
The language/action perspective does not imply utterances directly produce material changes; its primary object is cooperative human activity mediated by language and technology.

CITATION TRAIL:
J. L. Austin; John Searle; Fernando Flores; Lucy Suchman; CSCW critiques; construction contracts and change orders.

TEST:
Trace one architectural change request through REQUEST → ACCEPTANCE → MODEL REVISION → DRAWING → PROCUREMENT → SITE WORK → INSPECTION; locate where social performativity ends and computational execution begins.
