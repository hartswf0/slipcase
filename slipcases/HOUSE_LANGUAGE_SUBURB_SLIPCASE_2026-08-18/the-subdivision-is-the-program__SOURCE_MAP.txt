SOURCE MAP — THE SUBDIVISION IS THE PROGRAM

CLAIM S01
CLAIM: Architectural operativity should include rule systems that constrain possibilities, not only descriptions that specify form.
TYPE: COMPILER-SYNTHESIS
ZETTEL: HOUSE-LANG-005; HOUSE-LANG-007; HOUSE-LANG-007-SUB-01; HOUSE-LANG-007-SUB-05
SOURCE: Austin; Goodman; Village of Euclid; Reston Association
SUPPORT: Words act inside institutional arrangements; zoning and design review alter what physical states are permitted.
INFERENCE: The paper names this broader class OPERATIVE CONSTRAINT.

CLAIM S02
CLAIM: The suburban house is partly constituted by a possibility-space beyond its lot.
TYPE: INTERPRETATION
ZETTEL: HOUSE-LANG-007-SUB-01
SOURCE: Village of Euclid v. Ambler Realty Co.
SUPPORT: The case treats zoning as spatial segregation of uses and explicitly discusses protecting detached-residence districts from apartment houses.
INFERENCE: The paper treats forbidden neighboring types as part of the house's operative environment.

CLAIM S03
CLAIM: A built environment can be transformed without geometric change when occupancy rules change who may inhabit it.
TYPE: SOURCE-SUPPORTED + INTERPRETATION
ZETTEL: HOUSE-LANG-007-SUB-02
SOURCE: Levittown lease covenant; Shelley v. Kraemer context
SUPPORT: The covenant constrained occupancy by race.
INFERENCE: Occupancy is modeled as a state variable of the architectural world.

CLAIM S04
CLAIM: Catalog and production systems discretize architectural choice before any user “freely” specifies a house.
TYPE: SOURCE-SUPPORTED + INTERPRETATION
ZETTEL: HOUSE-LANG-007-SUB-03; HOUSE-COMP-007
SOURCE: Sears 1918; Alexander pattern language
SUPPORT: Sears presents named plans, prices, and options; pattern languages organize reusable design choices.
INFERENCE: The paper treats repertoire construction as an upstream authority over desire.

CLAIM S05
CLAIM: Levittown's scaling mechanism was operational decomposition and serial repetition, but the exact number of steps is unresolved.
TYPE: SOURCE-SUPPORTED
ZETTEL: HOUSE-LANG-007-SUB-04; HOUSE-LANG-007-SUB-08
SOURCE: Ames & McClelland 2002; Berenson 2025
SUPPORT: Both describe specialized crews and serial operations; they disagree between 27 and 26 steps.
INFERENCE: The paper refuses an exact step count and treats the discrepancy as a historiographic warning.

CLAIM S06
CLAIM: A suburban house can remain subject to a textual validation regime after initial construction.
TYPE: SOURCE-SUPPORTED + INTERPRETATION
ZETTEL: HOUSE-LANG-007-SUB-05
SOURCE: Reston Association Design Review Board
SUPPORT: The DRB interprets covenants, reviews alterations, renders decisions, hears appeals, and bars construction before final written decisions.
INFERENCE: The paper calls this continuing compilation.

CLAIM S07
CLAIM: McMansion incoherence may be analytically useful as a record of socially legible feature selection rather than merely stylistic failure.
TYPE: INTERPRETATION
ZETTEL: HOUSE-LANG-007-SUB-06
SOURCE: Kate Wagner 2026
SUPPORT: Wagner describes idiosyncratic fantasies, imitation detached from how originals work, and a developing design language.
INFERENCE: “Tokenization” is a heuristic, not a source term or demonstrated genealogy.

CLAIM S08
CLAIM: The marketed representation of a house can mutate independently from its material state and feed into economic decisions.
TYPE: SOURCE-SUPPORTED + OPEN-QUESTION
ZETTEL: HOUSE-LANG-007-SUB-07; HOUSE-LANG-007-SUB-09
SOURCE: Kate Wagner 2026
SUPPORT: Wagner documents virtual staging's power to alter apparent spatial scale and characterizes houses as made to scroll.
INFERENCE: Whether platform circulation causally selects future built form remains unproven and is retained as a research program.
