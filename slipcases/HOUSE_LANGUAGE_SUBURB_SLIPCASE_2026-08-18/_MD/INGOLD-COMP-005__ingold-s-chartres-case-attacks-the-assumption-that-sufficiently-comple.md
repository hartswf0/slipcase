INGOLD-COMP-005

TITLE:
INGOLD’S CHARTRES CASE ATTACKS THE ASSUMPTION THAT SUFFICIENTLY COMPLETE RULES CAN SUBSTITUTE FOR WORKMANSHIP.

SOURCE:
Tim Ingold — Making: Anthropology, Archaeology, Art and Architecture — 2013 — Chapter 4, “On Building a House,” pp. 56–57.

PASSAGE:
[QUOTE]
“designs do not magically transmute into the forms they specify”

RESEARCH OBJECT:
THE NON-CODIFIABLE RESIDUE BETWEEN RULE AND RESULT.

LOCAL MOVE:
Through David Turnbull’s Chartres case, Ingold preserves two failures of prior-design explanation: design does not explain workmanship; replacing workmanship with exhaustive rules attributes too much causal power to codification.

SOURCE TERMS:
design specification; workmanship; rules; algorithms; skilled practice; situation; fine-tuned action

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
Programming languages appear powerful because formal semantics allow rules to determine operations. Construction asks what happens when necessary actions cannot be pre-specified at required granularity.

QUESTION:
Can a sufficiently expressive architectural programming language eliminate workmanship, or does workmanship name precisely what remains when rule specification ends?

DEEPER QUESTION:
When an AI system fills gaps not explicitly represented in a construction program, has workmanship been codified—or relocated into a statistical model whose inherited judgements are harder to inspect?

MECHANISM:
Rules constrain action; situations introduce particulars not exhausted by rules; skilled practitioners discriminate among possibilities and fine-tune action.

FORMAL SHIFT:
<PRIOR DESIGN SPECIFICATION>
→ <RULE-GOVERNED PRACTICE>
→ [SITUATED FINE-TUNING]
→ <BUILT FORM>

SOURCE FORMALISM:
No executable formalism; the source distinguishes codified rules from situated practice necessary to complete a cathedral.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
Let R = explicit rules, S = situation, J = situated judgement, A = action. Strong-program thesis: A = R(S). Ingold/Turnbull pressure: R constrains A, but J(S, history, material variation, skill) is required.

TENSION:
[[HOUSE-COMP-005]] and [[HOUSE-COMP-006]] show formal rules can generate form or machine operations; neither proves formal rules are sufficient for situated building practice.

MISSING:
Inventory of decisions in contemporary fabrication that are explicitly programmed, algorithmically derived, statistically learned, sensor-resolved, operator-resolved, or improvised.

BOUNDARY:
Ingold does not prove skilled practice is in principle non-computable; he attacks unwarranted explanatory substitution of rules for workmanship.

CITATION TRAIL:
[[HOUSE-COMP-005]] → Ingold → David Turnbull → Michael Polanyi → Lucy Suchman → Hubert Dreyfus → tacit knowledge in automation → robotic skill learning.

TEST:
Take a rule-produced assembly, alter one material/tool/site/tolerance variable, rerun, and inventory residual adjustments that become candidates for WORKMANSHIP.
