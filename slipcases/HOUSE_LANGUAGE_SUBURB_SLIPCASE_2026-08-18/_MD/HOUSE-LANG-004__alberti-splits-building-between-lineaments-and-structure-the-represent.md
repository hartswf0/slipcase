ID:
HOUSE-LANG-004

TITLE:
ALBERTI SPLITS BUILDING BETWEEN LINEAMENTS AND STRUCTURE: THE REPRESENTATION CAN PRESCRIBE FORM WITHOUT BEING THE BUILDING.

SOURCE:
Leon Battista Alberti — On the Art of Building in Ten Books — c. 1452; English trans. 1988 — Book I.

PASSAGE:
[QUOTE]
“The whole matter of building is composed of lineaments and structure.”

RESEARCH OBJECT:
The representational/material split internal to architectural production.

LOCAL MOVE:
Alberti separates lineaments from structure, permitting architectural form to be conceived separately from material realization.

SOURCE TERMS:
lineaments; structure; lines; angles; surfaces; place; number; scale; order

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
A house can precede its construction representationally without the representation itself becoming lumber, stone, labor, fastening, weatherproofing, or ground.

QUESTION:
Is “The House That Words Built” actually about language becoming material—or about language gaining control over the lineament layer from which material work is subsequently organized?

DEEPER QUESTION:
What is the minimum representational object from which a sufficiently organized building process can proceed?

MECHANISM:
Lineaments determine relational form; structure realizes a building materially. The architectural object crosses a discontinuity between immaterial ordering and material execution.

FORMAL SHIFT:
<INTENDED BUILDING>
→ <LINEAMENTS>
→ [PRESCRIBE RELATIONS]
→ <STRUCTURED MATERIAL BUILDING>

SOURCE FORMALISM:
Alberti explicitly distinguishes LINEAMENTS from STRUCTURE.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
DESIGN INTENTION → LINEAMENTS → MATERIAL OPERATIONS → BUILDING. The research problem is the middle arrow: what makes a representation actionable?

TENSION:
If words produce lineaments but labor produces structure, “words built it” may suppress a translation chain.

MISSING:
Who converts lineaments into operations? What tolerances are added? What happens when matter resists? Which decisions cannot be fixed at the lineament stage?

BOUNDARY:
Alberti’s lineaments are not equivalent to natural-language descriptions, prompts, source code, BIM files, or construction documents without further evidence.

CITATION TRAIL:
Alberti Book I; Mario Carpo; Robin Evans; studies of lineamenta / materia.

TEST:
Compare verbal description → sketch → measured drawing → BIM object → shop drawing → layout marks → constructed element for one stair, roof edge, window, or wall.
