TITLE:
GOODMAN’S WORLDS ARE NOT MADE FROM NOTHING: WORLDMAKING BEGINS WITH OTHER WORLDS AND OPERATES AS REMAKING.

SOURCE:
Nelson Goodman — Ways of Worldmaking — 1978 — “Words, Works, Worlds,” p. 6.

PASSAGE:
[QUOTE]
“Worldmaking as we know it always starts from worlds already on hand.”

RESEARCH OBJECT:
Prior worlds as the input to making.

LOCAL MOVE:
Goodman refuses a foundational raw material beneath world versions and asks how worlds are made from other worlds, characterizing making as remaking.

SOURCE TERMS:
worlds; worldmaking; worlds already on hand; remaking; versions; composition; decomposition; labels

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
“The House That Worlds Built” becomes mechanically interesting: a made thing may begin not with isolated words but with already-organized worlds.

QUESTION:
What pre-existing worlds must be present before a particular house can become thinkable, specifiable, legal, financeable, constructible, and recognizable as a house?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Can the causal history of a building be reconstructed as collisions among world-versions rather than the intention of a single designer expressed through representations?

MECHANISM:
Worldmaking proceeds by operations on worlds already available, including composition/decomposition, weighting, ordering, deletion/supplementation, and deformation.
