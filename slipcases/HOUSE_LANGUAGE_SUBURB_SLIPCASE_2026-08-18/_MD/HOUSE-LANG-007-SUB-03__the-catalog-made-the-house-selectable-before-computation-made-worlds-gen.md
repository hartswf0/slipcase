TITLE:
THE CATALOG MADE THE HOUSE SELECTABLE BEFORE COMPUTATION MADE WORLDS GENERATABLE

SOURCE:
Sears, Roebuck and Co., Honor Bilt Modern Homes, 1918 trade catalog, digitized by Internet Archive. SOURCE URL: https://archive.org/details/honorbilt.modernhomes.sears.1918

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
The catalog presents prefabricated houses through named offerings containing plans, prices, drawings, and associated finishing products.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
THE HOUSE CATALOG AS AN EARLY PARAMETER INTERFACE BETWEEN DESIRE AND CONSTRUCTION.

LOCAL MOVE:
The genealogy of operative description should not jump directly from blueprint to prompt.

The house catalog inserts an intermediate object:

a bounded vocabulary of possible houses presented to a non-architect as selectable descriptions.

The customer does not begin with unrestricted form-making.

The customer encounters an already discretized field of possibilities.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
Before a house could be “generated,” it could already be browsed.

Architecture had become a menu.

This matters because prompting may inherit not only architectural description but the older commercial operation:

SELECT FROM A LATENT CATALOG OF RECOGNIZABLE TYPES.

QUESTION:
Is the prompt-generated house historically closer to the catalog house than to the architect's blueprint?

DEEPER QUESTION:
When users believe they are describing a desired house freely, are they actually navigating a pre-existing repertoire in the same way catalog customers navigated named models?

MECHANISM:
catalog
→ discrete represented house options
→ comparison by image / plan / price
→ selection
→ prefabricated material system
→ construction

Representation does not merely depict the eventual house.

It organizes the chooser's space of possible desires.

FORMAL SHIFT:
HOUSE DESCRIPTION AS SPECIFICATION

→ HOUSE DESCRIPTION AS OPTION IN A SEARCH SPACE

The important operation becomes not only BUILD(description).

It becomes CHOOSE(repertoire).

SOURCE FORMALISM:

CATALOG

OUR FORMALIZATION:

Prompt generation may mutate this into:

USER_DESCRIPTION
→ SEARCH / SAMPLE(LATENT_REPERTOIRE)
→ MATERIALIZE(candidate)

TENSION:
The catalog expands access to architectural choice.

But it also pre-structures what counts as something one might choose.

Choice increases while the ontology of possible houses is quietly supplied in advance.

MISSING:
Evidence about how buyers actually modified Sears designs.

How often did purchasers depart from catalog models?

Were named styles used as fixed products, starting points, or social signals?

BOUNDARY:
A Sears catalog is not a generative model.

The analogy becomes useful only at the level of:

represented repertoire
→ user selection
→ downstream materialization.

It must not erase the enormous differences between inventory selection and statistical generation.
