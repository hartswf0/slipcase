ZETTEL

ID:
HOUSE-LANG-005

TITLE:
AUSTIN MAKES WORDS OPERATIVE ONLY INSIDE FELICITOUS ARRANGEMENTS OF PEOPLE, PROCEDURES, CIRCUMSTANCES, AND OTHER ACTIONS.

SOURCE:
J. L. Austin — How to Do Things with Words — lectures 1955; published 1962 — pp. 5–9.

PASSAGE:
[QUOTE]
“The uttering of the sentence is, or is a part of, the doing of an action.”

RESEARCH OBJECT:
Distributed conditions of linguistic efficacy.

LOCAL MOVE:
Austin isolates utterances whose issuance performs rather than reports an action, then refuses a magical interpretation: words work in appropriate circumstances and often require additional actions by the speaker or others.

SOURCE TERMS:
performative; utterance; action; appropriate circumstances; operative; procedure; speaker; other actions; infelicity

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
“Words do things” is not yet an explanation of how words do things. The difficult object is the arrangement that lets an utterance count as an action.

QUESTION:
What are the architectural felicity conditions under which a description can cease to be merely descriptive and become an instruction with consequences for a building?

DEEPER QUESTION:
When a prompt changes a model, a model changes a drawing, a drawing releases fabrication, and fabrication changes matter, where exactly is the operative force located?

MECHANISM:
UTTERANCE + recognized procedure + authorized participants + appropriate circumstances + required accompanying actions → successful act.

FORMAL SHIFT:
<WORDS>
→ <CONVENTIONALLY RECOGNIZED ACT>
→ [SATISFY CONDITIONS / COMPLETE PROCEDURE]
→ <SOCIAL OR INSTITUTIONAL CHANGE>

SOURCE FORMALISM:
Austin supplies classes of performative success and failure across the lectures, but no executable syntax for architectural action.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]
DESCRIPTION D becomes operative for building only if D + AUTHORITY + INTERPRETER + PROCEDURE + MATERIAL CAPACITY + FOLLOW-THROUGH → BUILDING CONSEQUENCE.

TENSION:
The thesis “language becomes construction” is sharpened: language does not acquire efficacy alone; an arrangement makes linguistic acts efficacious.

MISSING:
Who recognizes the instruction? Who has authority? What representation receives it? What tolerance governs interpretation? Who bears responsibility?

BOUNDARY:
Austin does not argue that linguistic acts directly produce arbitrary physical effects; his paradigm cases depend on conventional procedures and surrounding action.

CITATION TRAIL:
Austin Lectures II–IV and VIII–X; Searle; construction contracts, specifications, RFIs, change orders.

TEST:
Keep “Move this wall 300 mm east” constant across casual conversation, sketch note, signed change order, BIM revision, and robotic command; observe which surrounding conditions determine whether anything happens.
