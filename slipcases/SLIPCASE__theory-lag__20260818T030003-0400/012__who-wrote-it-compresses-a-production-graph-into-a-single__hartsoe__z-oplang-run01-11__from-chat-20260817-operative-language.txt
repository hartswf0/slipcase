PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
The manuscripts repeatedly decompose writing into intention, specification, generation, selection, revision, placement, responsibility, and other operations. They argue that “human wrote it” and “AI wrote it” become inadequate when asked to describe the whole process.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
The <author label> as a compression function applied to a distributed production graph.

LOCAL MOVE:
The source stops trying merely to decide who deserves the title “author” and asks why authorship is expected to remain indivisible.

SOURCE TERMS:
“distributed”
“operations”
“selection”
“specification”
“revision”
“responsibility”
“single person”
“whole event”
“praise and blame”

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The problem may not originate in generative systems.

The problem may originate in demanding that a heterogeneous causal and intellectual process terminate in a Boolean or single-name attribution.

“Who wrote it?” asks for:

<distributed process>
→ <one actor>.

This is a lossy operation.

QUESTION:
Which distinctions are destroyed when a production graph is compressed into an author label?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Did the modern author category become stable partly because institutions require compact locations for credit, ownership, responsibility, credentialing, praise, and blame?

MECHANISM:
Production contains nodes:

Institutional attribution:
[compresses] this graph into <author-name>.

The compressed representation enables administrative operations while concealing process structure.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

Let G = (V,E) be the production graph.

V includes actors, artifacts, judgments, transformations, and decisions.

An authorship regime applies:

A(G) → {person, persons, organization, model, hybrid label}

The philosophical question concerns information lost under A.

TENSION:
All concepts compress.

An attribution label can be useful without claiming to describe the entire causal process.

“I wrote it” may function pragmatically as responsibility-taking rather than metaphysical causal exhaustiveness.

The existence of lost information therefore does not invalidate authorship.

MISSING:
Historical evidence for why authorship institutions privilege singular attribution.

Legal, scholarly, literary, and commercial authorship may compress different aspects for different purposes.

These must not be treated as one institution.

BOUNDARY:
The evidence does not establish that authorship is merely bureaucratic fiction.

It establishes that singular attribution is a coarser representation than the production process it names.

TEST:
Take one complex artifact and represent its production in:
A. conventional author credit,
B. contributor taxonomy,
C. detailed production graph,
D. prompt receipts.

Ask which decisions, responsibilities, dependencies, and contributions disappear in each compression.
