Description does not become operation; machinery makes description consequence-bearing.

PASSAGE:
[QUOTE]
“A description does not become an operation.”

[PARAPHRASE]
The sharper formulation is that someone constructs machinery in which describing has effects; the machinery can then disappear rhetorically from the sentence “description becomes operation.”

RESEARCH OBJECT:
<consequence-bearing description> as a replacement for the stronger ontological claim <executable description>.

LOCAL MOVE:
The source first develops “operative ekphrasis,” then subjects its own slogan to criticism by restoring the machine that mediates between words and consequences.

SOURCE TERMS:
“description”
“operation”
“effects”
“machine”
“operative”
“ekphrasis”
“instrument”

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
Nothing intrinsic to the sentence “Make the house remember” guarantees that anything happens.

Its operative quality is relational.

The same sentence can be:
- a poem,
- a note,
- an instruction to a person,
- input to a generative system.

The words remain materially similar while the surrounding coupling changes.

QUESTION:
What machinery must exist for a description to acquire reliable consequences?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Should the ontology of operative language be located in expressions at all, or in couplings among representations, interpreters, permissions, and action systems?

MECHANISM:
<description>
[enters] <interpretive machinery>.

<interpreter>
[maps] <description> into <possible operations>.

<action affordance>
[permits] <operation to modify state>.

Therefore:

<description> alone
does not [execute].

<description + interpreter + affordance>
can [produce consequence].

FORMAL SHIFT:
<DESCRIPTION>
→ <DESCRIPTION-IN-INTERPRETIVE-SYSTEM>
→ [INTERPRET + ACT]
→ <STATE CHANGE>

SOURCE FORMALISM:
NONE.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

Consequence-bearing description event:

D_c := (D, I, A, S)

where:
D = description,
I = interpreter,
A = available action repertoire,
S = current state.

Consequences arise from the coupling, not D alone.

TENSION:
Formal programs are also inert without interpreters, hardware, operating systems, and environments.

Therefore restoring machinery does not by itself distinguish prompts from programs.

The distinction must concern what kinds of machinery bridge representation and consequence and what they require the representation to specify.

MISSING:
The intermediary chain:
language → model representation → interpretation → tool/action selection → permission → state transition.

The source currently compresses these stages.

BOUNDARY:
The evidence does not justify saying that description literally acquires execution semantics in itself.

It supports the narrower claim that contemporary systems can couple descriptive expressions to consequential processes with unusually little explicit formalization.

CITATION TRAIL:
Speech-act theory.
Actor-network accounts of mediation.
Programming-language interpreters.
Human-computer action semantics.
Contracts, commands, plans, and institutional texts as consequence-bearing representations.

TEST:
Hold one sentence constant and place it into:
A. printed prose,
B. message to a human builder,
C. generative image system,
D. tool-using autonomous agent,
E. world simulation.

Trace every intermediary required before a state changes.

Compare where operative force resides in each case.
