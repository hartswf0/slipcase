TITLE:
Negative specification requires tacit judgment, but tacit knowledge is not automatically machine-addressable.

SOURCE:
Michael Polanyi — The Tacit Dimension — 1966 — opening formulation as reproduced by University of Chicago Press.

SOURCE URL:
https://press.uchicago.edu/ucp/books/book/chicago/T/bo6035368.html

PASSAGE:
[QUOTE]
“we can know more than we can tell”

RESEARCH OBJECT:
The gap between <tacit discrimination> and <machine-addressable feedback>.

Polanyi gives a strong precedent for knowledge that exceeds explicit statement. But prompting adds a second problem: tacit judgment must somehow be converted into a signal a generative system can use.

LOCAL MOVE:
Polanyi refuses the equation of knowledge with fully explicit propositions.

SOURCE TERMS:
“tacit”
“know”
“tell”
“personal”
“knowledge”

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
Negative specification is not solved by saying that the user “knows tacitly.”

A model cannot act directly on an unexpressed discrimination. The interaction must produce an interface between tacit recognition and machine-usable revision.

QUESTION:
What representational transformations allow tacit human discrimination to become operational feedback without pretending that the tacit knowledge has been fully verbalized?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Is prompting important partly because it builds a low-bandwidth but iterative bridge from tacit judgment to machine-addressable constraints?

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

Tacit criterion T is not directly observed.
Feedback F is a partial projection:

F = g(T, candidate, interface).

The model updates behavior from F, not from T itself.

TENSION:
Invoking tacit knowledge can become an excuse for criteria that are inconsistent, post-hoc, or indefensible.

Not every unexplained preference is deep tacit knowledge.

MISSING:
Methods distinguishing stable tacit discrimination from noise, preference drift, or retrospective rationalization.

BOUNDARY:
Polanyi’s work does not concern language models or prompting.

It licenses only the weaker point that explicit verbal statement need not exhaust knowledgeable discrimination.

CITATION TRAIL:

Embodied interaction.

TEST:
Compare feedback modalities for the same generative task: verbal explanation, binary reject, ranking, direct edit, gesture, and example selection. Measure which allow convergence while requiring the least explicit articulation of criteria.
