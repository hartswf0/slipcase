TITLE:
Prompt receipts record causal lineage, not authorship; their stronger object is decision leverage.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
The dissertation notes propose retaining prompts, failures, branches, candidates, selections, and transformations to make contribution inspectable. The later aphoristic text complicates this: a receipt establishes that a transaction occurred but does not establish who desired the object.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
<decision leverage>: the degree to which an intervention restructures the downstream possibility space.

Prompt receipts may be more valuable for locating high-leverage decisions than for proving authorship.

LOCAL MOVE:
The original source introduces provenance as an institutional answer to “Did AI write this?” The later text turns against any assumption that a sufficiently complete archive can settle the metaphysical question.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
Counting prompts, edits, or keystrokes has almost no necessary relation to intellectual consequence.

A two-word rejection can eliminate an entire conceptual direction.

A thousand-word initial prompt can prove almost irrelevant if the decisive structure emerges later.

The relevant topology may concern changes to future possibility rather than quantity of textual labor.

QUESTION:
Where in a generative lineage do human interventions actually change what can happen downstream?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Could intellectual contribution be represented through the topology of consequential decisions without pretending that causal influence is identical to authorship?

MECHANISM:
<generation process>
[branches into] {candidate futures}.

<decision>
[retains/eliminates/transforms] subsets of those futures.

The larger the downstream restructuring, the greater the decision’s leverage.

A receipt provides the evidence needed to reconstruct such branch points.

SOURCE FORMALISM:
The source supplies a lineage sequence and an archival list, but no measure of contribution.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

For decision d at state s:

L(d) ≈ change in reachable downstream possibility space attributable to d.

Possible operationalizations:
- candidates eliminated,
- constraints introduced,
- conceptual dependencies changed,
- later text requiring revision,
- branches made unreachable.

This measures leverage, not moral ownership.

TENSION:
A seemingly small intervention may be high leverage only because of extensive prior tacit knowledge.

Conversely, automated actions can have enormous causal leverage without intellectual contribution.

Decision leverage therefore cannot become a disguised authorship score.

MISSING:
A measure separating:
<causal leverage>
<intellectual judgment>
<effort>
<originality>
<responsibility>.

Also missing is access to unrealized alternatives; a receipt records generated branches, not the total counterfactual space.

BOUNDARY:
Prompt receipts cannot prove who “really wrote” an artifact.

Decision leverage cannot by itself determine authorship.

The evidence licenses a narrower use: provenance can reveal where the production trajectory materially changed.

CITATION TRAIL:
Version-control historiography.

Studies of creative selection.

TEST:
Take a complete prompt lineage.

For each human intervention, remove or replace it and regenerate downstream.

Estimate which interventions cause major structural divergence.

Compare those high-leverage points with the author’s own retrospective account of where intellectual contribution occurred.
