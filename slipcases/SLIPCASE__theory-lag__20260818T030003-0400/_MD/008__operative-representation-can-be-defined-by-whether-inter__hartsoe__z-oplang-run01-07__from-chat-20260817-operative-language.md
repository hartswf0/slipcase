TITLE:
Operative representation can be defined by whether interpretation changes a system’s reachable futures.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE]
The manuscript asks what happens when thick descriptions do more than establish meaning for a reader: they can become inputs to machinery that constructs artifacts or continues worlds.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
<reachability change> as an operational criterion for operative representation.

A representation becomes consequential when its interpretation modifies which future states of a system are reachable.

LOCAL MOVE:
The source moves from literary description to world specification but does not yet supply a state-based account of what “operative” means.

SOURCE TERMS:
“constructs an artifact”
“operative”
“different worlds”
“world specification”
“instantiates”
“operate the described world”
“world can continue”

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
“Operation” need not mean a deterministic instruction.

A representation might instead modify constraints, affordances, probabilities, or available actions.

What matters is that after interpretation the future-state structure differs.

QUESTION:
Can operative representation be defined by its ability to alter the reachable state-space of a system?

DEEPER QUESTION:
How strong must that alteration be before we distinguish operative representation from ordinary representation that merely influences a human who later acts?

MECHANISM:
System begins in state:

<S0>

with reachable futures:

R(S0).

<representation>
[is interpreted].

Interpretation:
- adds constraints,
- introduces entities,
- changes affordances,
- alters transition probabilities,
- enables operations,
- blocks operations.

Therefore:

R_before(S0) ≠ R_after(S0).

FORMAL SHIFT:
<REPRESENTATION>
→ <INTERPRETED CONSTRAINT / AFFORDANCE>
→ [MODIFY TRANSITION STRUCTURE]
→ <CHANGED REACHABLE FUTURES>

SOURCE FORMALISM:
NONE.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

Let a system have states S and transition relation T.

Before interpretation:
T = T0.

After representation r is interpreted:
T = T_r.

r is operationally consequential when:

T_r ≠ T0

or equivalently when some state becomes newly reachable, unreachable, or differently probable.

This does not require deterministic execution.

TENSION:
Human language has always changed reachable futures.

A command, law, promise, map, architectural plan, or threat can reorganize subsequent action.

Therefore reachability does not make generative prompting unprecedented.

The comparative question becomes the mediation distance, speed, scale, reversibility, and formalization required to connect representation to state change.

MISSING:
Measures of:
- mediation distance,
- time to consequence,
- reversibility,
- scope,
- authority,
- probability,
- interpretive variation.

Also missing is a distinction between changing modeled possibility and changing material possibility.

BOUNDARY:
This formalization does not establish that a represented world is equivalent to the material world.

It does not establish that all influence counts as execution.

It offers a testable interpretation of “operative” at the level of state transitions.

TEST:
Use the same representation in multiple systems and enumerate available transitions before and after interpretation.

Example:
“Make the house remember previous occupants.”

Compare:
A. static text,
B. interactive fiction engine,
C. generative world model,
D. human architectural workshop.

Record which transitions become newly possible and through how many mediating actors.
