The dissertation notes initially present prompt receipts as a way to make the distribution of agency legible. The aphoristic manuscript then warns that even a complete archive cannot necessarily settle “Did you write it?”

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The evidentiary failure of prompt receipts may be their theoretical success.

If a receipt cannot answer:

“Who wrote it?”

because it instead shows:

<formulation>
→ <generation>
→ <surprise>
→ <rejection>
→ <revision>
→ <selection>
→ <responsibility>,

then it exposes why the binary question was impoverished.

The receipt preserves the process against premature attributional compression.

QUESTION:
Should prompt receipts be designed to document distributed production rather than adjudicate singular authorship?

DEEPER QUESTION:
What scholarly practices become possible if provenance records are treated as first-class intellectual artifacts rather than defensive evidence attached to a finished work?

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

Conventional attribution:

G → A

where complex graph G is compressed into author label A.

Prompt receipt:

G → R(G)

where R attempts to preserve selected nodes, edges, alternatives, and judgments from G.

A receipt is successful when R(G) retains distinctions relevant to intellectual inspection rather than merely accumulating exhaustive logs.

TENSION:
A complete record can itself become a bureaucratic fantasy.

More provenance does not necessarily produce more understanding.

An enormous interaction log may hide decisive acts beneath thousands of trivial ones.

Selection is therefore unavoidable even in the representation of provenance.

Prompt receipts themselves become authored representations of authorship.

MISSING:
A theory of what a receipt must preserve.

Possible candidates:
- branching points,
- rejected alternatives,
- rationale,
- source dependencies,
- model/version,
- human modifications,
- high-leverage decisions,
- unresolved uncertainties.

Also missing: privacy and practical limits on complete archival capture.

BOUNDARY:
Prompt receipts cannot establish intention, understanding, or authorship merely through completeness.

They cannot make tacit judgment fully explicit.

Their defensible function is narrower: preserve enough production structure that claims about contribution can be examined rather than simply asserted.

TEST:
Represent one generative writing process in three forms:

A. final artifact + “AI-assisted” label,
B. complete chronological transcript,
C. curated prompt receipt preserving decisions, branches, rejected alternatives, transformations, and rationale.

Give each representation to independent readers.

Test which allows them to reconstruct:
- intellectual decisions,
- delegation,
- uncertainty,
- responsibility,
- turning points.

If the complete transcript performs worse than the structured receipt, provenance requires theory rather than mere logging.
