ZETTEL

Z-OPLANG-FORAGE-20260817-04

TITLE:
The author is constructed by operations on texts, not merely compressed from a production graph.

SOURCE:
Michel Foucault — What Is an Author? / “The Author Function” — 1969; English translation in Language, Counter-Memory, Practice — 1977 — excerpt.

SOURCE URL:
https://foucault.info/documents/foucault.authorFunction.en/

PASSAGE:
[QUOTE]
“It results from a complex operation whose purpose is to construct the rational entity we call an author.”

RESEARCH OBJECT:
<author construction> as a pressure on <author as compression>.

The production-graph model treats an author label as a lossy summary of causal contribution. Foucault shifts the mechanism: an author is also produced by historically variable operations of classification, comparison, exclusion, ownership, and textual handling.

LOCAL MOVE:
Foucault argues that the author-function is neither universal nor a spontaneous attribution of a discourse to an individual.

SOURCE TERMS:
“author-function”
“complex operation”
“construct”
“appropriation”
“not universal or constant”
“comparisons”
“traits”
“continuities”
“exclusions”

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The “author as compression” zettel still begins with a supposedly pre-existing production graph and asks what gets lost when it is summarized.

Foucault asks a prior question: what textual and institutional operations make an “author” appear as the relevant rational entity in the first place?

QUESTION:
Does AI-mediated writing challenge authorship because production becomes distributed, or because existing author-functions cease to organize the resulting discourse cleanly?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Which new author-functions might institutions construct for artifacts whose production includes model generation, human selection, iterative specification, and responsibility?

SOURCE FORMALISM:

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

Instead of only:
A(G) → author label,

model:
A(G, D, I, H) → author-function,

where D = discourse type,
I = institutional operations,
H = historical regime.

TENSION:
Foucault’s author-function is not a quantitative account of contribution and should not be used as a direct empirical model of generative production.

A production graph and an author-function answer different questions and may both be needed.

MISSING:
Institution-specific evidence from graduate education, publishing, copyright, and creative industries showing how AI-mediated contribution is actually classified.

BOUNDARY:
The excerpt does not address AI or prove that contemporary institutions will abandon singular authorship.

It defeats only the assumption that author attribution is a natural, universal reading-off of causal production.

CITATION TRAIL:
Copyright and AI authorship policy.
Contributor taxonomies.
Scientific credit systems.
Film and collaborative authorship.
Institutional dissertation policies.

TEST:
Compare how the same generative production graph is attributed under four regimes: dissertation authorship, copyright registration, film credits, and scientific contributor taxonomies. Identify which operations each institution treats as author-making.
