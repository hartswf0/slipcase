TITLE: YELMO MAY BELONG TO A SURREALIST GENEALOGY RATHER THAN AN AI-NATIVE ONE

SOURCE: Scott Roberts — “Surrealist Storytelling: Artistic Experimentation and Spontaneity through Improvisation,” in The Rose Metal Press Field Guide to Graphic Literature, edited by Kelcey Ervick and Tom Hart, Rose Metal Press — publisher table of contents: https://rosemetalpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/FGtoGraphicLiteratureTableofContents.pdf ; Scott Roberts — Midjourney exquisite-corpse post — https://www.instagram.com/p/Ch6jYdhOw0j/

PASSAGE: [QUOTE] “Surrealist Storytelling: Artistic Experimentation and Spontaneity through Improvisation.” [QUOTE] Roberts separately describes an “on-going exquisite corpse game” conducted with Midjourney.

RESEARCH OBJECT: Roberts’s published artistic research and his early Midjourney practice converge on a vocabulary that predates generative AI: Surrealism, improvisation, spontaneity, experimentation, and exquisite corpse. This complicates the idea that Midjourney itself created the “multiplayer imagination game” described in the parent. A stronger possibility is that an existing artistic practice recognized a machine unusually compatible with its own method: submit something incomplete, accept an uncontrolled transformation, respond to what arrives, and discover the work through continuation.

LOCAL MOVE: Instead of asking only what Midjourney taught Roberts and his collaborators, reverse the genealogy: ask what artistic traditions Roberts may have brought to Midjourney that made particular affordances legible as valuable. The parent’s pleasure in being “led by MJ” and not “trying to control everything” becomes especially significant beside Roberts’s explicit scholarly interest in spontaneity through improvisation.

SOURCE TERMS: Surrealist storytelling; artistic experimentation; spontaneity; improvisation; exquisite corpse

WHAT BECAME STRANGE: [OUR INFERENCE] Generative AI’s famous “surprise” may not constitute a new creative method at all. For an improvisational artist, model unpredictability can function as an unusually fast partner in an older practice of deliberately surrendering partial control.

QUESTION: Did Roberts and his collaborators discover improvisation through Midjourney, or did prior improvisational practice teach them how to use Midjourney differently from users seeking deterministic control?

DEEPER QUESTION: Which supposedly novel practices of generative AI are actually older artistic techniques whose cost, speed, scale, or partner structure has changed?

MECHANISM: Existing practice values incompletion and surprise → opaque generator produces material the artist did not fully specify → unexpected result is treated as a proposition rather than an error → artist answers that proposition with another move → narrative/aesthetic form is discovered through consecutive response.

FORMAL SHIFT: FROM: GENERATIVE-AI CREATIVITY = BETTER CONTROL OF A NOVEL MACHINE. TO: GENERATIVE-AI CREATIVITY MAY ALSO = OLD IMPROVISATIONAL PRACTICE + A NEW KIND OF UNPREDICTABLE PARTNER.

TENSION: The correspondence is strong but genealogy remains unresolved. Roberts’s chapter and his Midjourney practice could arise from the same long-standing improvisational method; alternatively, Roberts may have theorized improvisation more explicitly after encountering generative systems. Publication chronology alone cannot establish conceptual chronology.

BOUNDARY: Similarity is not influence. The title of Roberts’s chapter proves a published concern with Surrealist storytelling and improvisation, not that the Yelmo project consciously implemented the chapter’s method.
