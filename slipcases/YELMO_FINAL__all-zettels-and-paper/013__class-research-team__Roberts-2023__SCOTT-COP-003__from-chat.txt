TITLE: WHEN THE TOOL MOVES FASTER THAN THE SYLLABUS, THE CLASS BECOMES A RESEARCH TEAM

SOURCE: Scott Roberts — Instagram, AI Art Tools research team / Move.ai — https://www.instagram.com/reel/CoXh-ZQD4mk/ ; Scott Roberts / DePaul Center for Teaching and Learning — “The Cyborg Muse: The Potential of AI Tools to Enhance and Accelerate Creativity” — https://www.linkedin.com/posts/scott-roberts-3a63523_28th-annual-teaching-and-learning-conference-activity-7059776060971769856-V8fR ; DePaul faculty biography — https://www.depaul.edu/faculty/scott-roberts

PASSAGE: [QUOTE] Roberts: “our brilliant AI Art Tools research team successfully figured out Move.ai.” [QUOTE] The course presentation calls the students “my student research collaborators” and says “we researched and discovered” bleeding-edge AI tools.

RESEARCH OBJECT: Roberts’s language destabilizes the conventional relation between teacher, student, and curriculum. Students are not merely learners receiving established expertise. They are a “research team” and “research collaborators” collectively figuring out tools whose usable practices have not yet settled. The course itself becomes an apparatus for producing the knowledge that a conventional course would normally presume already exists.

LOCAL MOVE: [[PROMPT-FORAGE-001]] treated prompt expertise as socially accumulated knowledge rather than simple understanding of model internals. Roberts’s teaching practice provides an institutional implementation of that possibility: a classroom organized as a community that discovers operational knowledge collectively.

SOURCE TERMS: research team; student research collaborators; experimental course; researched; discovered; bleeding-edge AI tools; Move.ai; AI Art Tools for Animation, Cinema, and Theatre

WHAT BECAME STRANGE: [OUR INFERENCE] In a sufficiently unstable technical field, the distinction between novice and expert becomes multidimensional. Roberts may possess far greater animation and pedagogy expertise while a student may discover the crucial affordance of yesterday’s newly released tool first. Expertise becomes locally transferable rather than permanently ranked.

QUESTION: What becomes of apprenticeship when the master does not possess a stable body of tool knowledge that precedes the apprentice?

DEEPER QUESTION: Can a university course become a genuine community of practice precisely because no participant—not even the instructor—can fully occupy the position of settled expert?

MECHANISM: Rapidly changing tools appear → instructor creates an experimental environment rather than fixed canonical workflow → students and instructor investigate tools → someone discovers a working operation → result becomes shared group knowledge → demonstrations/projects expose further unknowns → community iterates → temporary repertoire becomes pedagogical material.

FORMAL SHIFT: FROM: TEACHER_KNOWS → STUDENT_LEARNS → STUDENT_EXECUTES. TO: {TEACHER + STUDENTS} ENCOUNTER UNKNOWN TOOL → DISTRIBUTED EXPERIMENT → LOCAL DISCOVERY → SHARED REPERTOIRE → TEMPORARY EXPERTISE.

SOURCE FORMALISM: The sources explicitly describe an “experimental course,” “student research collaborators,” “bleeding-edge AI tools we researched and discovered,” and an “AI Art Tools research team” that “figured out” a motion-capture system.

OUR FORMALIZATION: [OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX] UNKNOWN_CAPABILITY → MANY PRACTITIONERS TRY OPERATIONS → ONE WORKS → SHOW GROUP → GROUP REPRODUCES → PROCEDURE ENTERS LOCAL REPERTOIRE → NEW TOOL / FAILURE DESTABILIZES PROCEDURE

TENSION: Calling students “research collaborators” could be pedagogical rhetoric rather than evidence of genuinely distributed epistemic authority. Roberts may still have selected tools, framed research questions, evaluated success, and controlled what counted as curricular knowledge. Conversely, those framing functions may themselves be the relevant expert role once operational knowledge becomes unstable.

MISSING: Course syllabus; assignment sheets; Slack/Discord exchanges; student reflections; records showing who introduced particular tools and techniques; evidence of a student-originated discovery becoming course-wide practice.

BOUNDARY: “Research team” and “research collaborators” are Roberts/DePaul’s own descriptions of this course context. They do not establish that every participant possessed equal authority or that the course formally operated as a research laboratory.

CITATION TRAIL: [[PARENT-ZETTEL-ID]] → parent community learns prompting by inspecting and sharing others’ experiments → Roberts moves generative experimentation into an “experimental course” → students become “research collaborators” who collectively “figure out” tools → next edge: trace one concrete technique from first discovery by an individual participant to adoption by the entire class

TEST: Obtain one semester’s chronological course archive. Select five techniques ultimately used by multiple students. For each, locate FIRST APPEARANCE, FIRST SUCCESS, PERSON WHO INTRODUCED IT, FIRST PEER REUSE, FIRST INSTRUCTOR REUSE, and FORMALIZATION INTO ASSIGNMENT/DEMO. If knowledge repeatedly travels student→teacher and peer→peer rather than exclusively teacher→student, the “research team” description gains operational support.
