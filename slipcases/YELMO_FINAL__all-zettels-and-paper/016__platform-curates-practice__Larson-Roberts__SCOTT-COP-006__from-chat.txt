TITLE: THE PLATFORM BEGAN INTERVIEWING PRACTITIONERS WHILE THE PRACTICE WAS STILL BEING INVENTED

SOURCE: Joshua Larson — LinkedIn post concerning his feature in Midjourney Magazine Issue #1, with public comment by Scott Roberts — https://www.linkedin.com/posts/torncanvas_im-honored-to-have-received-the-first-full-activity-7087502452266057728-DgS_

PASSAGE: [QUOTE] Roberts: “They interviewed me last month and as a result decided to stop doing them that way 😄”

RESEARCH OBJECT: A public comment by Roberts indicates that Midjourney itself interviewed him during the early period in which its practitioner culture was being publicly documented through Midjourney Magazine. This introduces another actor into the community-of-practice genealogy: the platform does not merely provide the tool around which practitioners gather. It can select practitioners, interview them, publish exemplars, and thereby circulate particular ways of understanding what competent or interesting practice looks like.

LOCAL MOVE: [[PROMPT-FORAGE-001]] treated prompt expertise as socially transmitted among users. Add a missing transmission path: USER PRACTICE → PLATFORM EDITORIAL SELECTION → PUBLISHED PRACTITIONER → OTHER USERS. Community knowledge may loop through the institution operating the machine.

SOURCE TERMS: interview; Midjourney Magazine; Issue #1; feature; practitioner

WHAT BECAME STRANGE: [OUR INFERENCE] A platform can begin writing the history of its community before the community has finished inventing its methods. The archive is therefore not neutral after-the-fact documentation. Editorial selection can help decide which practitioners, images, workflows, and vocabularies become remembered as representative of the early medium.

QUESTION: What did Midjourney ask Roberts about, and which parts of his practice did the platform consider worth recording?

DEEPER QUESTION: When a platform curates exemplary users while its affordances are still evolving, does documentation merely record a community of practice or participate in selecting the practices that will become canonical?

MECHANISM: Users develop heterogeneous practices → platform notices/selects some practitioners → interviews/editorial features translate practice into stories and exemplars → magazine circulates those exemplars → other users encounter a curated version of competent/interesting practice → attention and imitation potentially feed back into community norms.

FORMAL SHIFT: FROM: PLATFORM PROVIDES TOOL; COMMUNITY PRODUCES CULTURE. TO: PLATFORM PROVIDES TOOL + OBSERVES COMMUNITY + CURATES COMMUNITY BACK TO ITSELF.

SOURCE FORMALISM: The source establishes that Joshua Larson publicly discussed receiving a feature in Midjourney Magazine Issue #1 and that Roberts publicly responded that Midjourney had interviewed him the previous month. The actual Roberts interview has not been recovered in this forage.

TENSION: Being interviewed does not prove influence, publication, endorsement, or canonization. Roberts’s joking comment also gives no evidence about the interview’s content. The strong claim remains a hypothesis until the interview or editorial archive is located.

MISSING: Roberts’s Midjourney interview; publication status; questions asked; interviewer identity; date; images discussed; whether Yelmo or collaborative prompting appeared; Midjourney Magazine editorial criteria; other early interviewees and the practices they represented.

BOUNDARY: The available public source supports only that Roberts says Midjourney interviewed him. It does not establish that his interview appeared in the magazine or that his work shaped Midjourney policy or community norms.
