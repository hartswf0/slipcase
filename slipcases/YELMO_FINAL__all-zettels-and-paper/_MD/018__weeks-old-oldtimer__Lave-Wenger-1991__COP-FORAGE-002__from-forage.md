TITLE: AN OLD-TIMER CAN BE ONLY WEEKS OLD

SOURCE: Jean Lave and Etienne Wenger — Situated Learning: Legitimate Peripheral Participation — Cambridge University Press, 1991 — https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511815355

SOURCE URL: https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511815355

PASSAGE: [PARAPHRASE] Lave and Wenger reconceive learning as increasing participation in communities of practitioners, with newcomers moving through forms of legitimate peripheral participation toward fuller participation.

RESEARCH OBJECT: [[SCOTT-COP-003]] asks what happens to apprenticeship when the instructor cannot possess a settled body of tool knowledge before the student. Early generative-AI communities intensify the problem because the entire practice may be younger than its practitioners’ relationships to one another. Someone with six weeks of daily Midjourney experimentation can become an “old-timer” relative to a person arriving today, even though both are historically new to the medium.

LOCAL MOVE: Pressure legitimate peripheral participation under conditions of technical birth rather than established craft. Replace the stable master/apprentice chronology with rapidly produced local asymmetries of exposure, experimentation, access, and reputation.

SOURCE TERMS: legitimate peripheral participation; newcomer; old-timer; community of practitioners; full participation; situated learning

WHAT BECAME STRANGE: [OUR INFERENCE] Seniority can be generated faster than canon. The community can possess experts before it possesses stable explanations, textbooks, standards, or even a durable version of the tool.

QUESTION: What confers expert status when nobody has long-term experience of the practice itself?

DEEPER QUESTION: Can communities reliably distinguish accumulated competence from first-mover advantage when the historical depth of the field is measured in weeks?

MECHANISM: New platform appears → heterogeneous participants enter at different times/intensities → high-frequency experimenters accumulate local procedural knowledge → visibility and successful artifacts attract attention → newcomers imitate or request prompts → relative status differentiates rapidly → subsequent platform changes can abruptly redistribute expertise.

FORMAL SHIFT: FROM: NOVICE → YEARS OF PARTICIPATION → OLD-TIMER. TO: NOVEL PLATFORM + UNEQUAL EXPOSURE RATE → RAPID RELATIVE EXPERTISE → FRAGILE OLD-TIMER STATUS.

SOURCE FORMALISM: Lave and Wenger’s framework centers legitimate peripheral participation and changing participation relations between newcomers and established practitioners. “Weeks-old old-timer” is our transformation of that framework for a newly born technical practice.

OUR FORMALIZATION: [OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX] EXPERTISE_RANK(t) ≈ f(ENTRY_TIME, EXPERIMENT_RATE, SOCIAL_VISIBILITY, TRANSFERABLE_PRIOR_CRAFT, MODEL_VERSION) rather than simply YEARS_IN_FIELD.

TENSION: Roberts complicates any purely platform-native hierarchy because he entered with substantial preexisting expertise in animation, visual development, improvisational storytelling, and teaching. What looks like fast Midjourney expertise may partly be old craft knowledge finding a new instrument.

MISSING: Entry dates and activity levels for members of the specific early Midjourney group; evidence showing how participants themselves identified advanced users; whether status persisted across major model-version changes.

BOUNDARY: Lave and Wenger studied established practices, not communities forming around newly released generative systems. The accelerated-seniority claim is an inference requiring historical data from the community.

CITATION TRAIL: [[SCOTT-COP-003]] → teacher and students collectively discover unstable tools → Lave/Wenger newcomer/old-timer relations → newly born platform compresses expertise chronology → next edge: reconstruct actual status formation in the 2022 Midjourney channels and distinguish platform tenure from transferable artistic expertise

TEST: Reconstruct one early Midjourney channel month by month. For each participant record first appearance, posting frequency, prompt-sharing behavior, peer requests for help, remix frequency, featured images, and references indicating reputation. Compare these measures with prior artistic/technical experience. Then test how rankings change after a major model version transition.
