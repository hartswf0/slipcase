TITLE:
Human–robot research proposed a new ontological category fifteen years before LLM encounter ontology.

SOURCE:
Peter H. Kahn Jr., Aimee L. Reichert, Heather E. Gary, Takayuki Kanda, Hiroshi Ishiguro, Solace Shen, Jennifer H. Ruckert, and Brian Gill — The New Ontological Category Hypothesis in Human-Robot Interaction — 2011 — HRI ’11, pp. 159–160. ([ieeexplore.ieee.org](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/6281274/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

PASSAGE:
[QUOTE] The authors ask whether personified robots are animate or inanimate and answer that the possibilities may be “yes, no, and both.” ([researchgate.net](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/221473304_The_new_ontological_category_hypothesis_in_human-robot_interaction?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

RESEARCH OBJECT:
Encounter with personified computation destabilizing basic being-categories is not an LLM-era discovery.

LOCAL MOVE:
Kahn and colleagues synthesize evidence for the hypothesis that personified robots and related computational systems can constitute a new ontological category rather than fitting ordinary animate/inanimate classifications.

SOURCE TERMS:
new ontological category
personified robots
animate
inanimate
computational systems
human-robot interaction

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The recent claim that generative AI forces fundamentally new ontological thinking has an empirical HRI prehistory extending at least to the early 2000s.

QUESTION:
Did LLMs create a new human-machine ontology, or did they relocate an older HRI category problem from embodied robots into language?

DEEPER QUESTION:
What changed when ontological ambiguity moved from visible artificial bodies to fluent text?

MECHANISM:
artifact exhibits animate/social behaviors
→ existing binary categories conflict
→ users attribute mixed properties
→ hybrid classification stabilizes
→ candidate new category emerges.

SOURCE FORMALISM:
The hypothesis treats basic categories of being as empirically investigable through human responses to personified robots. ([ieeexplore.ieee.org](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/6281274/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

TENSION:
Encounter Ontology 2026 presents the encounter itself as ontologically primary. Kahn et al. locate ontological novelty in the perceived entity/category, not the relation.

MISSING:
A direct historical citation path from HRI’s new ontological category literature into current LLM encounter scholarship.

BOUNDARY:
Chronological priority does not establish influence. The HRI hypothesis concerns categorization of personified robots rather than generative conversational trajectories.
