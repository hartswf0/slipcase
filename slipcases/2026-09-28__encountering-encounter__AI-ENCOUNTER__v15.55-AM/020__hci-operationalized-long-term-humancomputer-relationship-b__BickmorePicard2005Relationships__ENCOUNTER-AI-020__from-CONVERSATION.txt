ID:
ENCOUNTER-AI-020

TITLE:
HCI operationalized long-term human–computer relationship before generative AI made relation appear spontaneous.

SOURCE:
Timothy W. Bickmore and Rosalind W. Picard — Establishing and Maintaining Long-Term Human-Computer Relationships — 2005 — ACM Transactions on Computer-Human Interaction 12(2), 293–327. ([media.mit.edu](https://www.media.mit.edu/publications/establishing-and-maintaining-long-term-human-computer-relationships/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE] Bickmore and Picard define relational agents as computational artifacts specifically designed to establish and maintain long-term social-emotional relationships with users and experimentally compare such an agent with a task-oriented alternative. ([media.mit.edu](https://www.media.mit.edu/publications/establishing-and-maintaining-long-term-human-computer-relationships/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

RESEARCH OBJECT:
A substantial part of what now gets called AI encounter already existed as deliberately engineered relational continuity.

LOCAL MOVE:
The authors borrow from social psychology, sociolinguistics, and communication research to turn “relationship” into a design target and measurable HCI outcome.

SOURCE TERMS:
relational agents
long-term interaction
social-emotional relationship
trust
liking
respect
relationship quality

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
LLMs did not introduce computers that users could form relationships with. They changed the generative bandwidth, linguistic flexibility, and apparent openness of a much older design program.

QUESTION:
Which properties of present LLM encounters are genuinely new relative to relational-agent HCI?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Is “encounter” new because the machine changed, because interaction became linguistically open-ended, or because users can no longer easily perceive the relationship script?

MECHANISM:
encode relationship-building behaviors
→ repeated interaction
→ maintain social history/continuity
→ user attributes relational qualities
→ trust/liking/continuation intention changes.

FORMAL SHIFT:
<TASK-ORIENTED AGENT>
→ <RELATIONSHIP-BUILDING DESIGN>
→ [REPEATED SOCIAL-EMOTIONAL INTERACTION]
→ <MEASURABLE HUMAN-COMPUTER RELATIONSHIP>

SOURCE FORMALISM:
The reported controlled experiment involved 101 users interacting daily with an exercise-adoption system for a month; the relational-agent condition produced greater reported respect, liking, trust, and desire to continue interaction than an equivalent task-oriented agent. ([media.mit.edu](https://www.media.mit.edu/publications/establishing-and-maintaining-long-term-human-computer-relationships/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

RELATIONAL CONTINUITY =
MEMORY
+ SOCIAL CUES
+ REPEATED CONTACT
+ RELATIONSHIP MAINTENANCE OPERATIONS.

GENERATIVITY is not logically required.

TENSION:
Encounter Ontology treats generative open-endedness as one admission condition. Bickmore and Picard show that persistent social relationship effects can be engineered without contemporary generative models.

MISSING:
A direct experimental comparison between scripted relational agents and generative LLM agents holding memory, embodiment, and interaction duration constant.

BOUNDARY:
Measured liking, trust, and continuation desire demonstrate effects on users. They do not demonstrate reciprocal social relationship in the strong philosophical sense.

TEST:
Compare scripted and generative agents across one month while matching relational cues and memory. Test whether generativity predicts conceptual change, perceived autonomy, surprise, attachment, or only conversational breadth.
