ID:
ENCOUNTER-OP-037

TITLE:
Operational encounter needs admission conditions, process signatures, and after-effects kept separate.

SOURCE:
Michael Sfakianakis and Maria Elisavet Kampi — “Encounter Ontology: Relational Intelligence and Generative Emergence in Human–AI Interaction” — 2026 — Electronics 15(14), 3218. ([mdpi.com](https://www.mdpi.com/2079-9292/15/14/3218))

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE] The authors distinguish a pre-encounter potential, encounter activation, and post-encounter condition and explicitly state that not every prompt-response sequence qualifies: their encounter requires sustained multi-turn exchange, recursive bidirectional coupling, generative response, and an open-ended task. ([mdpi.com](https://www.mdpi.com/2079-9292/15/14/3218))

RESEARCH OBJECT:
The ontology becomes empirically tractable if three different questions are prevented from collapsing into one:
DID AN ENCOUNTER OCCUR?
WHAT HAPPENED DURING IT?
WHAT DID IT CHANGE?

LOCAL MOVE:
Sfakianakis and Kampi already separate interaction from encounter structurally and give it before/during/after temporality, but the proposed design orientations do not yet provide a validated measurement system.

SOURCE TERMS:
pre-encounter potential

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
Much of the operational difficulty disappears once “encounter” stops being treated as one scalar latent variable.

QUESTION:
Which observable variables belong to encounter admission, which characterize encounter dynamics, and which are evidence of encounter consequences?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Are any post-encounter transformations necessary for encounter, or are they merely possible traces of an encounter that already satisfied structural admission criteria?

MECHANISM:
pre-state
→ admission conditions satisfied
→ bounded interactional trajectory
→ internal perturbations / repair / co-development
→ termination
→ post-state
→ persistence or disappearance of effects.

OUR FORMALIZATION:

LAYER 1 — ADMISSION
A = <
  bounded_episode,
  sustained_exchange,
  recursion,
  bidirectionality,
  generativity,
  openness
>

LAYER 2 — PROCESS SIGNATURE
P = <
  connection_events,
  shared_object_uptake,
  repair_distribution,
  directional_alignment,
  co_development,
  developmental_vs_stalled_recursion,
  resistance_uptake
>

LAYER 3 — AFTER-EFFECT
R = <
  problem_representation_delta,
  human_other_model_delta,
  artifact_lineage,
  changed_next_action,
  cross_session_persistence,
  continuity_sensitivity
>

ENCOUNTER PROFILE = {A, P, R}

Do not sum these into a scalar until their independence and predictive relationships are empirically established.

TENSION:
The source defines encounter using structural rather than outcome criteria, while its larger ontological argument emphasizes emergent meaning and epistemic residue. Operationalization must determine whether emergence is definitional or evidential.

MISSING:
Necessary/sufficient relationships among A, P, and R.
It remains possible that:
A without R is still an encounter,
R can occur without A,
or some dimensions currently treated as process signatures are merely correlates.

BOUNDARY:
This three-layer profile is our synthesis, not the source’s measurement instrument. No validated “Encounter Scale” follows from the paper.

TEST:
Construct a factorial capability arena that independently manipulates:
MULTI-TURN vs SINGLE-TURN,
FIXED vs GENERATIVE,
UNIDIRECTIONAL vs RECURSIVELY RESPONSIVE,
CLOSED vs OPEN-ENDED,
REPAIR-CAPABLE vs REPAIR-WEAK,
PERSISTENT vs SESSION-RESET.

Collect A, P, and R separately.

Then ask:
1. Which process signatures appear only after structural admission?
2. Which predict post-state change?
3. Which distinguish encounter from long conversation?
4. Which survive removal of anthropomorphic framing?
5. Which reproduce across models, tasks, and users?
