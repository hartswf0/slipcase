TITLE:
“Interface” converts meeting into a boundary that can be engineered.

SOURCE:
American Heritage Dictionary — “interface,” usage history; historical computing usage from the 1960s. ([ahdictionary.com](https://ahdictionary.com/word/search.html?q=interfaces&utm_source=chatgpt.com))

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE] Before its computing use, interface named a common boundary surface; computing repurposed it for the point connecting systems. ([ahdictionary.com](https://ahdictionary.com/word/search.html?q=interfaces&utm_source=chatgpt.com))

RESEARCH OBJECT:
The conceptual move from encounter to interface is not merely terminological: relation becomes locatable at a designed boundary.

LOCAL MOVE:
The technical term begins with the common boundary between bodies or regions and becomes a computing term for a connection point between systems or devices.

SOURCE TERMS:
interface
boundary
surface
systems
connection
interaction

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
An interface presupposes that the participating systems can be bounded enough for a common boundary to be specified. Encounter need not.

QUESTION:
Did computing’s interface concept make heterogeneity manageable by relocating relation into a standardized boundary?

DEEPER QUESTION:
What must be hidden on both sides for an interface to appear as a clean surface?

MECHANISM:
heterogeneous systems
→ boundary specification
→ compatible representations/protocols
→ exchange becomes engineerable.

TENSION:
Suchman demonstrates that even a technically engineered interface does not guarantee shared intelligibility. Barad pressures the apparently prior existence of A and B themselves.

MISSING:
The actual historical pathway by which the physical-science term “interface” entered specific computer engineering communities in the 1960s.

BOUNDARY:
Dictionary chronology demonstrates semantic history, not causal intellectual transmission into HCI.

TEST:
Corpus-search ACM, IEEE, ARPA, and manufacturer documentation from 1955–1975 for “interface.” Classify what sits on each side of the interface: device/device, program/program, human/machine, organization/organization. Identify when “human” becomes one of the bounded systems.
