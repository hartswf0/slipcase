ENCOUNTER-AI-021

TITLE:
Before theorizing AI encounter, philosophy must decide whether communication occurred at all.

SOURCE:
Herman Cappelen and Rachel Sterken — Introduction — Communicating with AI: Philosophical Perspectives — 2026 — pp. xi–xviii. ([academic.oup.com](https://academic.oup.com/book/63302/chapter/573018117?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

PASSAGE:
[QUOTE] The editors ask whether “the entire framework of communication is a mistake” and whether interaction with LLMs requires a new conceptual framework. ([academic.oup.com](https://academic.oup.com/book/63302/chapter/573018117?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

RESEARCH OBJECT:
“Encounter” enters a field in which even the apparently weaker category “communication” remains philosophically unsettled.

LOCAL MOVE:
The volume suspends the assumption that fluent linguistic exchange proves shared language, understanding, meaningful speech acts, or ordinary communicative relation.

SOURCE TERMS:
communication
shared language
understanding
alien
meaning
speech acts
trust
responsibility
de-anthropomorphizing

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
Calling something an encounter may smuggle in more relational structure than calling it communication, while philosophers are still disputing whether communication is present.

QUESTION:
What does “encounter” allow us to analyze without first settling whether the LLM understands or means anything?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Could encounter be deliberately thinner than communication at the semantic level while thicker than interaction at the consequential level?

MECHANISM:
human enters words

→ human interprets output

POSSIBLE DISTINCTION:

INTERACTION:
causal exchange.

COMMUNICATION:
meaning-bearing exchange.

ENCOUNTER:
situated consequential event that may remain agnostic about machine-side meaning.

TENSION:
Demuro and Gurney call ChatGPT a nonhuman interlocutor without requiring sentience. Machidon reserves genuine encounter for reciprocal vulnerable relation. The Oxford volume shows that the intermediate notion of communication itself cannot be taken for granted.

MISSING:
A theory specifying which properties encounter requires while remaining neutral on contested claims about AI semantics and consciousness.

BOUNDARY:
The editors pose an open methodological problem. They do not endorse “encounter” as its solution.

TEST:

Identify the weakest definition that still distinguishes encounter from arbitrary causal interaction.
