TITLE:
Language may construct the artificial Other before the machine earns that status ontologically.

SOURCE:
Mark Coeckelbergh — You, Robot: On the Linguistic Construction of Artificial Others — 2011 — AI & Society 26(1), 61–69. ([doi.org](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00146-010-0289-z?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE] Coeckelbergh argues that talk about and talk to robots does not merely report an independently fixed human-robot reality; linguistic practice interprets and co-shapes relations with artificial “quasi-others.” ([researchgate.net](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/220414565_You_Robot_On_the_linguistic_construction_of_artificial_others?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

RESEARCH OBJECT:
Artificial otherness can be produced through address.

LOCAL MOVE:
The paper moves the analysis of human-robot relations away from intrinsic machine properties toward phenomenological and hermeneutic mediation by language.

SOURCE TERMS:
artificial others
quasi-others
language

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The pronoun “you” may be an operation. Addressing a system as a second person can help constitute the social relation subsequently cited as evidence that it is an other.

QUESTION:
Does prompting create an artificial second person through grammar before any philosophical determination of agency or personhood occurs?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Could second-person address be one of the smallest operative mechanisms by which interaction becomes encounter-like?

MECHANISM:
artifact produces socially legible behavior
→ human addresses/describes artifact linguistically
→ linguistic categories mediate experience
→ expectations and practices shift
→ artifact is encountered as quasi-other.

TENSION:
Fernandez-Borsot argues that AI requires a carefully differentiated “analytical other.” Coeckelbergh suggests that linguistic practice is already actively making artificial others before such classification is stabilized.

MISSING:
Controlled evidence isolating grammatical address from embodiment, social cues, model behavior, and interface personification.

BOUNDARY:
Linguistic construction does not imply that ontological properties are arbitrary or that language alone determines what machines are.
