OPEN EDGES

## ENCOUNTER-001 — The adversarial root of “encounter” does not establish an adversarial theory of encounter.
QUESTION: When twentieth-century thinkers make encounter philosophically consequential, are they recovering an older adversarial semantic residue, independently constructing a new technical concept, or simply exploiting an ordinary word already transformed by centuries of use?
DEEPER QUESTION: Can “encounter” have a conceptual genealogy at all, or do several unrelated technicalizations merely share one lexical carrier?
MISSING: Citation receipts showing which twentieth-century theorists actually consulted, invoked, or depended upon the etymology.
TEST: Build a dated corpus of technical uses of encounter/rencontre/Begegnung and search for explicit etymological discussion or citation. Separate independent technicalizations from demonstrable transmission.

## ENCOUNTER-002 — Goffman technicalizes encounter by removing surprise and trouble from it.
QUESTION: If Goffman’s encounter requires mutual monitoring and a jointly sustained focus, can human–AI interaction qualify when only the human possesses access to the interaction as a social situation?
DEEPER QUESTION: Is mutual orientation more fundamental to encounter than contingency?
MISSING: An account of whether artificial systems can participate in rather than merely simulate the ratification of a shared situation.
TEST: Annotate human–LLM sessions for Goffman’s encounter conditions: initiation, ratification, mutual focus, monitoring, repair, termination. Determine which conditions are interactional effects produced solely by the human.

## ENCOUNTER-003 — Turing produces the machine encounter by preventing an ordinary encounter.
QUESTION: Is mediation merely the channel of a human–AI encounter, or can selective deprivation of information actively manufacture the alterity attributed to the system?
DEEPER QUESTION: How much of machine otherness is an interface effect?
MISSING: An account of what the communication medium contributes independently of machine intelligence.
TEST: Compare judgments of the same AI across text-only, voice, avatar, visible-process, and model-internals conditions. If perceived otherness changes substantially, the “encounter” is partly generated by mediation architecture.

## ENCOUNTER-004 — Licklider’s “symbiosis” preserves a division of agency inside the partnership.
QUESTION: At what point does complementary division of labor become an encounter rather than an instrumentally organized partnership?
DEEPER QUESTION: Does contemporary agentic AI actually undo Licklider’s allocation of goals and evaluation to the human, or merely obscure that allocation?
MISSING: A criterion distinguishing delegated operation, initiative, negotiation, and genuine revision of goals.
TEST: Code modern AI products for where goals, hypotheses, criteria, action selection, and evaluation are actually assigned. Compare the resulting agency map with Licklider’s 1960 partition.

## ENCOUNTER-005 — Direct manipulation succeeds by making the computer disappear as an encountered entity.
QUESTION: Did graphical HCI domesticate human-computer encounter by designing away the computer’s alterity?
DEEPER QUESTION: Is conversational AI historically remarkable because the computational substrate returns from transparency and reappears as an apparent interlocutor?
MISSING: Evidence that conversational systems actually reverse this trajectory rather than simply producing a new transparency around much larger infrastructure.
TEST: Compare design manifestos from command-line, direct-manipulation, ubiquitous-computing, and conversational-AI eras for whether the machine is framed as object, medium, environment, servant, partner, or interlocutor.

## ENCOUNTER-006 — Suchman makes “human-machine interaction” itself an empirical question.
QUESTION: Which apparent reciprocities of contemporary AI are accomplishments of the model, which are accomplishments of the human, and which arise only from the coupled situation?
DEEPER QUESTION: Can interaction be operationalized without smuggling in symmetry?
MISSING: A contemporary replication with probabilistic language models capable of generating context-sensitive repair behavior.
TEST: Run controlled breakdowns in human-human and human-LLM dialogue. Compare who detects trouble, who initiates repair, what contextual resources are used, and whether apparent repair tracks actual shared understanding.

## ENCOUNTER-007 — Pratt changes the unit from two encountering subjects to a historically produced contact space.
QUESTION: What disappears analytically when human–AI encounter is represented as a conversational dyad?
DEEPER QUESTION: Can interface design create the phenomenology of reciprocity while structurally preventing reciprocal power?
MISSING: An account of which AI infrastructures are experientially available to users and which remain structurally absent from the apparent encounter.
TEST: Take one chatbot conversation and produce two analyses: dyadic encounter versus contact-zone analysis. Record which actors, constraints, histories, and power relations become visible only in the second.

## ENCOUNTER-008 — Barad makes encounter potentially too late: interaction already assumes the entities it claims to relate.
QUESTION: Which boundaries in a human–AI episode pre-exist the episode, and which are enacted by interfaces, prompts, logging systems, institutional categories, evaluation procedures, and discourse?
DEEPER QUESTION: Is “human-AI encounter” already the output of a cut?
MISSING: A non-metaphorical specification of what counts as an apparatus and agential cut in actual HCI experiments.
TEST: Choose one human-AI study and list every operation required before its data can contain separate columns/categories called USER and SYSTEM. Treat those operations, rather than the categories, as the initial object of inquiry.

## ENCOUNTER-009 — For Deleuze, encounter is diagnosed by failed recognition, not by reciprocity.
QUESTION: Should the test for human–AI encounter concern what the AI is, or whether the episode reorganizes the user’s space of thought?
DEEPER QUESTION: Can engineered “surprise” force thought, or does genuine encounter require an event that escapes the designer’s and user’s available recognitional schema?
MISSING: A measure of conceptual reorganization that is not merely self-reported surprise or output novelty.
TEST: Compare AI outputs rated equally surprising. Test whether any produce durable changes in the user’s subsequent questions, categories, representations, or problem formulation. Treat those transformations, not surprise ratings, as the candidate encounter variable.

## ENCOUNTER-010 — Althusser’s encounter is successful only when contingency “takes hold” long enough to make a world.
QUESTION: When does a human–AI exchange “take hold”?
DEEPER QUESTION: Could persistent workflows, vocabularies, prompts, artifacts, or habits be the empirical residue by which an encounter becomes detectable only afterward?
MISSING: A temporal criterion for “taking hold” that does not simply equate persistence with importance.
TEST: Take a corpus of memorable human–AI episodes and inspect them weeks later. Did any create persistent vocabulary, workflows, artifacts, relationships, or research directions? Compare momentary surprise against durable structural change.

## ENCOUNTER-011 — “Interface” converts meeting into a boundary that can be engineered.
QUESTION: Did computing’s interface concept make heterogeneity manageable by relocating relation into a standardized boundary?
DEEPER QUESTION: What must be hidden on both sides for an interface to appear as a clean surface?
MISSING: The actual historical pathway by which the physical-science term “interface” entered specific computer engineering communities in the 1960s.
TEST: Corpus-search ACM, IEEE, ARPA, and manufacturer documentation from 1955–1975 for “interface.” Classify what sits on each side of the interface: device/device, program/program, human/machine, organization/organization. Identify when “human” becomes one of the bounded systems.

## ENCOUNTER-AI-012 — Floridi’s Encounters is an experiment that contains encounters, not yet a theory of encounter.
QUESTION: What exactly is the referent of “encounter” in Floridi’s project: human-machine collaboration, fictional character meeting, confrontation between authorship regimes, or all three?
DEEPER QUESTION: Does the literary experiment accidentally furnish a stronger model of AI encounter than Floridi explicitly theorizes?
MISSING: The forthcoming book’s full theoretical introduction is not yet publicly available as of September 28, 2026. The publisher announces publication for February 2027. ([cup.columbia.edu](https://cup.columbia.edu/book/encounters-with-ai/9780231225014/))
TEST: When the book introduction becomes available, search every occurrence of encounter/encounters and classify its referent: fictional meeting, human-AI process, philosophical category, or ordinary-language usage. Compare that distribution with distant writing, agency, design, and responsibility.

## ENCOUNTER-AI-013 — Distant writing moves authorship from inscription into control of a generative possibility space.
QUESTION: Is the distinctive AI encounter located in dialogue with a machine, or in gaining operative access to a previously inaccessible distribution of possible artifacts?
DEEPER QUESTION: Does generative AI change authorship because it behaves like another author, or because description can now operate directly upon a possibility space?
MISSING: A precise account of which creative decisions originate in model generation rather than being anticipated in the initial constraint set.
TEST: Reconstruct one Encounters story revision-by-revision from prompt history if available. Mark each narratively consequential feature as:
human-specified,
model-introduced,
human-selected,
human-corrected,
or emergent through iterative interaction.

## ENCOUNTER-AI-014 — Floridi permits artificial agency while refusing artificial intelligence.
QUESTION: Could encounter be defined around agency rather than intelligence or subjecthood?
DEEPER QUESTION: What minimum form of artificial agency is sufficient for something to become an encountered other rather than merely part of the environment?
MISSING: A mapping between Floridi’s abstract criteria for agency and the interaction-level conditions under which users experience an AI as an other.
TEST: Construct a matrix crossing:
agency,
intelligence,
sentience,
reciprocity,
mutual vulnerability,
linguistic responsiveness,
and consequentiality.
Place Floridi, Buber/Machidon, Demuro-Gurney, Goffman, and encounter ontology into the matrix and determine which variables actually discriminate their encounter concepts.

## ENCOUNTER-AI-015 — The AI ethnographic encounter is valuable when it deforms the observer’s categories.
QUESTION: Should an AI encounter be detected by changes in the participants, or by changes in the categories through which the researcher can describe what happened?
DEEPER QUESTION: Can category deformation serve as an empirical trace of encounter?
MISSING: Evidence showing that actual users, rather than researchers, revise their concepts of language through repeated AI interaction.
TEST: Before and after sustained LLM use, ask participants to operationally define language, understanding, authorship, interlocutor, and agency. Compare conceptual changes against a non-generative software condition.

## ENCOUNTER-AI-016 — Encounter Ontology gives human–AI encounter structural admission criteria.
QUESTION: Can its four structural criteria discriminate encounters empirically, or do they merely select longer, more open-ended conversations?
DEEPER QUESTION: What additional evidence is required to demonstrate emergence rather than iteration?
MISSING: Operational variables for “relational intelligence,” “epistemic emergence,” and “altered cognition,” plus evidence that outputs cannot be explained as human interpretation of stochastic model responses.
TEST: Construct four conditions:
single-turn/fixed;
multi-turn/fixed;
single-turn/generative;
multi-turn/generative/open-ended.
Measure persistent changes in user concepts and subsequent problem representations. If only duration and generativity change subjective involvement but not durable cognition, the stronger emergence claim weakens.

## ENCOUNTER-AI-017 — Buberian opposition makes refusal and vulnerability conditions of encounter, not defects to optimize away.
QUESTION: Is the possibility of genuine refusal a necessary variable for encounter?
DEEPER QUESTION: Could an AI that is designed to resist, disengage, maintain boundaries, or decline user demands produce a structurally different kind of human-machine relation even without consciousness?
MISSING: Longitudinal causal evidence that frictionless AI companionship diminishes users’ human relational capacities. The theoretical mechanism is stronger than the present empirical receipt.
TEST: Build otherwise identical conversational agents varying:
agreement,
refusal,
boundary maintenance,
availability,
and repair after conflict.
Measure whether users perceive greater otherness, relational reality, frustration, trust, and transfer to later human disagreement.

## ENCOUNTER-AI-018 — Artificial otherness may require a new category rather than a verdict of humanlike or nonhuman.
QUESTION: Which of the six characteristics actually become perceptible during an encounter, and which are concealed by interface design?
DEEPER QUESTION: Is “AI otherness” produced less by model architecture than by the contradictory stacking of interlocutor and commodity roles?
MISSING: Empirical evidence that users actually organize AI into a stable new ontological category rather than shifting opportunistically among person/tool/service metaphors.
TEST: Present the same LLM through interfaces emphasizing respectively:
person,
tool,
corporate service,
surveillance infrastructure,
and model.
Ask users to classify what kind of entity they encountered and inspect whether classifications remain stable across tasks.

## ENCOUNTER-AI-019 — Human–robot research proposed a new ontological category fifteen years before LLM encounter ontology.
QUESTION: Did LLMs create a new human-machine ontology, or did they relocate an older HRI category problem from embodied robots into language?
DEEPER QUESTION: What changed when ontological ambiguity moved from visible artificial bodies to fluent text?
MISSING: A direct historical citation path from HRI’s new ontological category literature into current LLM encounter scholarship.
TEST: Replicate new-ontological-category measures across:
industrial robot,
social robot,
voice assistant,
text LLM,
avatar LLM,
and agentic LLM.
Test whether embodiment, language, autonomy, or persistence most strongly destabilizes animate/inanimate categories.

## ENCOUNTER-AI-020 — HCI operationalized long-term human–computer relationship before generative AI made relation appear spontaneous.
QUESTION: Which properties of present LLM encounters are genuinely new relative to relational-agent HCI?
DEEPER QUESTION: Is “encounter” new because the machine changed, because interaction became linguistically open-ended, or because users can no longer easily perceive the relationship script?
MISSING: A direct experimental comparison between scripted relational agents and generative LLM agents holding memory, embodiment, and interaction duration constant.
TEST: Compare scripted and generative agents across one month while matching relational cues and memory. Test whether generativity predicts conceptual change, perceived autonomy, surprise, attachment, or only conversational breadth.

## ENCOUNTER-AI-021 — Before theorizing AI encounter, philosophy must decide whether communication occurred at all.
QUESTION: What does “encounter” allow us to analyze without first settling whether the LLM understands or means anything?
DEEPER QUESTION: Could encounter be deliberately thinner than communication at the semantic level while thicker than interaction at the consequential level?
MISSING: A theory specifying which properties encounter requires while remaining neutral on contested claims about AI semantics and consciousness.
TEST: Take candidate definitions of encounter and remove, one by one:
machine meaning,
machine intention,
machine sentience,
machine belief,
shared language.
Identify the weakest definition that still distinguishes encounter from arbitrary causal interaction.

## ENCOUNTER-AI-022 — The strongest counterexample to AI-as-other is AI-as-instrument.
QUESTION: How could evidence discriminate AI-as-interlocutor from AI-as-instrument when both predict responsive text?
DEEPER QUESTION: Is “encounter” useful precisely because it permits the encountered entity to remain ontologically undecided?
MISSING: Cases where interlocutor and instrument models make different empirical predictions rather than merely supporting different vocabularies.
TEST: Run identical LLM tasks under two framings:
“you are conversing with an AI partner”
versus
“you are probing a stochastic linguistic instrument.”
Measure interaction strategy, epistemic trust, anthropomorphic attribution, repair behavior, and conceptual change. Determine which encounter phenomena depend upon the interlocutor framing.

## ENCOUNTER-AI-023 — Language may construct the artificial Other before the machine earns that status ontologically.
QUESTION: Does prompting create an artificial second person through grammar before any philosophical determination of agency or personhood occurs?
DEEPER QUESTION: Could second-person address be one of the smallest operative mechanisms by which interaction becomes encounter-like?
MISSING: Controlled evidence isolating grammatical address from embodiment, social cues, model behavior, and interface personification.
TEST: Hold model outputs constant while varying interface and prompt grammar:
IT-form,
YOU-form,
named-persona form,
tool form.
Measure perceived agency, reciprocity, obligation, trust, and remembered relation.

## ENCOUNTER-OP-024 — An encounter needs observable entry, maintenance, and exit conditions before it needs a score.
QUESTION: What observable events mark the transition from mere availability to an active human–AI encounter?
DEEPER QUESTION: Can an encounter fail to begin even though prompt-response traffic is occurring?
MISSING: Textual or multimodal equivalents of the engagement signals that allow beginning, maintenance, and ending to be inferred reliably.
TEST: Create a corpus containing complete human–LLM sessions rather than isolated turns. Have independent annotators mark:
NO ENCOUNTER,
INITIATION,
ESTABLISHED,
MAINTENANCE,
DISENGAGEMENT,
END.
Then test whether turn count, elapsed time, adjacency structure, topic persistence, acknowledgment, and explicit closing predict those boundaries.

## ENCOUNTER-OP-025 — Connection events make relational participation computable without claiming access to inner states.
QUESTION: What are the LLM equivalents of HRI connection events?
DEEPER QUESTION: Which connection events require actual reciprocal contingency rather than merely an output that resembles reciprocity?
MISSING: Connection events whose occurrence depends upon previous relational state rather than generic dialogue competence.
TEST: Build a turn-level annotation scheme for the proposed textual connection events. Compare:
single-shot QA,
multi-turn factual assistance,
iterative co-creation,
and sustained open conversation.
Test which event types discriminate these conditions after controlling for raw turn count.

## ENCOUNTER-OP-026 — An encounter classifier can be accurate while operationalizing no stable encounter construct.
QUESTION: What construct-validity test should an “encounter” feature pass before entering a detector?
DEEPER QUESTION: Should encounter even be represented by a single ground-truth label?
MISSING: Discriminant validity: variables that separate encounter from engagement, enjoyment, anthropomorphism, task success, conversation length, and mere linguistic fluency.
TEST: Give the same interaction corpus to annotators using four definitions:
ENGAGEMENT,
COLLABORATION,
CONVERSATION QUALITY,
ENCOUNTER.
Measure label overlap. Features that predict all four equally well are poor encounter-specific indicators.

## ENCOUNTER-OP-027 — Breakdown exposes reciprocity more clearly than smooth conversation does.
QUESTION: Who carries the burden of keeping a human–AI encounter intelligible when shared progress breaks?
DEEPER QUESTION: Does a stronger encounter require both participants to detect trouble, or only the coupled system to recover from it?
MISSING: A criterion distinguishing substantive trouble detection from generic clarification behavior.
TEST: Seed ambiguous reference, contradictory assumptions, under-specification, and mistaken premises into otherwise identical conversations. Code who first detects each trouble and who performs the work required to recover. Compare human-human and human-LLM baselines.

## ENCOUNTER-OP-028 — Successful repair can conceal that the human is carrying the encounter.
QUESTION: Can repair-burden asymmetry distinguish fluent interaction from stronger encounter-like coupling?
DEEPER QUESTION: How much human compensatory work can occur before “bidirectional coupling” becomes a misleading description?
MISSING: Large-scale controlled comparisons across current models rather than illustrative GPT-3.5 dialogues.
TEST: Generate a benchmark of minimally different ambiguous turns with known alternative interpretations. Measure whether the model:
detects ambiguity,
asks a discriminating question,
maintains both alternatives,
updates after correction,
and avoids reverting.
Compare those scores with ordinary “repair success.”

## ENCOUNTER-OP-029 — The encounter may have to be measured as a trajectory of coordination rather than as properties of either participant.
QUESTION: What properties exist in a human–AI trajectory that cannot be assigned to either participant independently?
DEEPER QUESTION: Could relational intelligence be measured without attributing cognition to the AI at all?
MISSING: A principled translation from human team interaction to human–AI systems whose participants have radically different internal architectures.
TEST: For the same corpus, compare prediction of independent expert-rated task development using:
A. individual user/model features,
B. final output features,
C. temporal relational features.
If C adds explanatory power beyond A+B, the interaction contains measurable structure not captured by participant properties alone.

## ENCOUNTER-OP-030 — Joint attention to a shared object can be more diagnostic than attention to the artificial partner.
QUESTION: What is the textual/generative equivalent of joint attention to a shared object?
DEEPER QUESTION: Are the strongest AI encounters actually encounters around a third thing rather than encounters with AI itself?
MISSING: A reliable way to distinguish shared-object attention from mere lexical repetition of object descriptions.
TEST: Create matched AI tasks with:
A. ephemeral chat only,
B. persistent visible shared artifact.
Measure reference accuracy, repair, artifact improvement, turn efficiency, and post-task conceptual change. Test whether encounter signatures concentrate around the shared artifact rather than social-language intensity.

DEEPER QUESTION: What distinguishes revising an existing idea together from merely asking the model to regenerate alternatives?

TEST: Annotate each human and AI turn by whether it:
introduces,
evaluates,
preserves,
rejects,
transforms,

## ENCOUNTER-OP-032 — Recursion must be separated from being stuck in a loop.
QUESTION: What observable state change distinguishes co-development from frustrated repetition?
DEEPER QUESTION: Can an encounter be recursively coupled yet epistemically stagnant?
MISSING: Domain-independent criteria for meaningful state change. A visual-design improvement and a conceptual reframing cannot be measured with the same raw outcome variable.
TEST: Give blinded coders pairs of iteration sequences with turn count matched. Ask them to classify each transition as:
REPEAT,
PARAMETER TWEAK,
CORRECTION,
CONSTRAINT DISCOVERY,
PROBLEM REFRAME,
ARTIFACT DEVELOPMENT.
Test which transition mixtures distinguish expert-rated development from loops.

## ENCOUNTER-OP-033 — Linguistic convergence is measurable but does not prove encounter.
QUESTION: When linguistic alignment increases in an AI conversation, who is adapting to whom?
DEEPER QUESTION: Could high surface synchrony coexist with low bidirectional coupling?

TEST: Randomly vary AI lexical and syntactic style across otherwise equivalent interactions while separately manipulating how strongly the model mirrors user language. Estimate H→AI and AI→H adaptation independently and relate each to repair success and artifact development.

## ENCOUNTER-OP-037 — Operational encounter needs admission conditions, process signatures, and after-effects kept separate.
QUESTION: Which observable variables belong to encounter admission, which characterize encounter dynamics, and which are evidence of encounter consequences?
DEEPER QUESTION: Are any post-encounter transformations necessary for encounter, or are they merely possible traces of an encounter that already satisfied structural admission criteria?
MISSING: Necessary/sufficient relationships among A, P, and R.
It remains possible that:
A without R is still an encounter,
R can occur without A,
or some dimensions currently treated as process signatures are merely correlates.
TEST: Construct a factorial capability arena that independently manipulates:
MULTI-TURN vs SINGLE-TURN,
FIXED vs GENERATIVE,
UNIDIRECTIONAL vs RECURSIVELY RESPONSIVE,
CLOSED vs OPEN-ENDED,
REPAIR-CAPABLE vs REPAIR-WEAK,
PERSISTENT vs SESSION-RESET.

Collect A, P, and R separately.

Then ask:
1. Which process signatures appear only after structural admission?
2. Which predict post-state change?
3. Which distinguish encounter from long conversation?
4. Which survive removal of anthropomorphic framing?
5. Which reproduce across models, tasks, and users?
