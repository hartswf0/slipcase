TITLE:
Distant writing moves authorship from inscription into control of a generative possibility space.

SOURCE:
Luciano Floridi — Distant Writing: Literary Production in the Age of Artificial Intelligence — 2025 — Minds and Machines 35(3), 1–26. ([doi.org](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11023-025-09732-1?urlappend=%3Futm_source%3Dresearchgate.net%26utm_medium%3Darticle&utm_source=chatgpt.com))

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE] Floridi describes distant writing as literary creation in which the human functions as designer while LLMs perform the actual writing; prompting and iterative refinement retain human creative control. He describes the result as expanding the feasible narrative possibilities available to authors. ([doi.org](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11023-025-09732-1?urlappend=%3Futm_source%3Dresearchgate.net%26utm_medium%3Darticle&utm_source=chatgpt.com))

RESEARCH OBJECT:
The encounter with generative AI changes the operative locus of authorship from making tokens to constraining and selecting possibilities.

LOCAL MOVE:
Floridi distinguishes the act of producing prose from the higher-level operations of conceiving, specifying, prompting, revising, validating, and curating.

SOURCE TERMS:
distant writing
wrAIting

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
If the human does not write any sentences, the prompt becomes neither merely instruction nor text fragment. It becomes an operation over a modal field of candidate texts.

QUESTION:
Is the distinctive AI encounter located in dialogue with a machine, or in gaining operative access to a previously inaccessible distribution of possible artifacts?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Does generative AI change authorship because it behaves like another author, or because description can now operate directly upon a possibility space?

SOURCE FORMALISM:
A seven-stage methodology has been reported for Floridi’s distant-writing model:
conception and development;
formulation of requirements;
prompt engineering;
wrAIting;
progressive refinement;
validation and verification;
curation and assembly. ([philpapers.org](https://philpapers.org/archive/MOLALD-2.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

TENSION:
An encounter ontology emphasizes co-emergence. Floridi’s design account preserves a strongly asymmetric locus of conception, evaluation, termination, and responsibility.

MISSING:
A precise account of which creative decisions originate in model generation rather than being anticipated in the initial constraint set.

BOUNDARY:
Distant writing demonstrates delegated textual production. It does not by itself establish co-authorship, mutual intentionality, or shared understanding.
