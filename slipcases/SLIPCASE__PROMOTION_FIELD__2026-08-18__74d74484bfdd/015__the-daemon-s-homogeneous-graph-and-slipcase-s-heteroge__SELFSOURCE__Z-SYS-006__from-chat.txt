TITLE:
The daemon's homogeneous graph and Slipcase's heterogeneous graph are different data models

SOURCE:
User-provided prompt system — AUTONOMOUS GRAPH INQUIRY v4.0 and SLIPCASE — ANCIENT MASTER v15.0 — supplied 2026-08-18.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE] The daemon declares that its graph consists entirely of ZETTELS. Ancient Master defines graph nodes as ZETTEL, PLATFORM, CONCEPT, GHOST, SOURCE, RESOURCE, and PROMPT.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
The research daemon and the archival compiler use the word “graph” for structurally different objects.

LOCAL MOVE:
Exposes an interface mismatch hidden by shared vocabulary.

SOURCE TERMS:
graph

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
“Reconsider the entire graph” becomes ambiguous once the daemon is embedded inside Slipcase. Does it inspect only zettels or also source nodes, ghosts, prompts, and platforms?

QUESTION:
Which graph is the daemon actually steering when Ancient Master is the containing system?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Can a source hub, ghost, or unresolved address become the highest-value research frontier even though the daemon's declared graph does not admit that object as a node?

MECHANISM:
Daemon:
ZETTEL ↔ ZETTEL relations create research frontier.

Slipcase:
multiple object types and typed relations create the preserved field.

OUR FORMALIZATION:
[OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX]

G_field = heterogeneous preserved graph

G_inquiry = projection(G_field, node_type = ZETTEL)
            + selected derived research edges

The unresolved design choice is whether steering operates on G_inquiry or G_field.

TENSION:
The daemon says a repeatedly appearing source is a SOURCE_HUB while simultaneously refusing SOURCE as a graph node. Ancient Master makes SOURCE an explicit node.

MISSING:
A projection contract between the archival graph and the inquiry graph.

BOUNDARY:
Different graphs are not inherently inconsistent if they are explicitly layered. The supplied system does not yet state that layering.
