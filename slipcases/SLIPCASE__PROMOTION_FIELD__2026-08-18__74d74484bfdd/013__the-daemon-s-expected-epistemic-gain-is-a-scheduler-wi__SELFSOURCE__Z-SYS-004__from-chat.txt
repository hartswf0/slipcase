TITLE:
The daemon's “expected epistemic gain” is a scheduler without a utility function

SOURCE:
User-provided prompt system — PRIME ZETTEL FORAGE — AUTONOMOUS GRAPH INQUIRY v4.0 — supplied 2026-08-18.

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE] Before every round the daemon surveys the active frontier and chooses the unresolved edge with the greatest expected epistemic gain, favoring edges likely to change several nodes, discriminate explanations, expose mechanisms, or connect branches.

RESEARCH OBJECT:
The daemon identifies the correct optimization problem but does not operationalize its objective.

LOCAL MOVE:
It converts research direction from “continue the latest child” into an explicit global selection decision.

SOURCE TERMS:
ACTIVE FRONTIER

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
The most consequential decision in the autonomous system is expressed entirely as judgment language rather than executable machinery.

QUESTION:
How should expected epistemic gain be estimated before the evidence that would create that gain has been retrieved?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Can an autonomous research scheduler distinguish “high expected gain” from whatever currently sounds intellectually exciting?

MECHANISM:
Candidate edges are generated.
The controller ranks them.
One edge receives research resources.
New evidence changes the graph.
The frontier is ranked again.

MISSING:

BOUNDARY:
