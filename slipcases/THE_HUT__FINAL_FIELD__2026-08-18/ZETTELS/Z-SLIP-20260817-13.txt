    TITLE:
    A PROMPT MAY BE AN ITINERARY: GUIDANCE THAT WORKS BY REFUSING COMPLETE SPECIFICATION

    SOURCE:
    Tim Ingold — Making — 2013 — Chapter 8, “Telling by hand,” p. 110.

    SOURCE URL:
    file:MAKING_INGOLD(1).pdf

    PASSAGE:
    [QUOTE] “In place of specification without guidance, the story offers guidance without specification.”

    RESEARCH OBJECT:
    Natural-language programming may be misdescribed when every useful utterance is treated as a defective specification. Some language works by orienting attention and enabling continuation rather than by exhaustively defining an end state.

    LOCAL MOVE:
    Ingold distinguishes specifications, which define a project, from stories, which lay down an itinerary and support guided rediscovery.

    SOURCE TERMS:
    telling; education of attention; guided rediscovery; complete specification; guidance; anticipatory foresight; itinerary

    WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
    The endpoint of deferred formalization need not be total formalization. A capable system may continue through a hybrid of invariants, examples, metaphors, and attention-directing cues.

    QUESTION:
    Are prompts specifications, programs, or itineraries?

    DEEPER QUESTION:
    Could “programming by itinerary” name a distinct computational regime in which guidance and evaluation remain distributed across time?

    MECHANISM:
    telling → attention → situated encounter → judgment → action → further cue.

    FORMAL SHIFT:
    <GUIDANCE> → <ENCOUNTER> → [NOTICE + JUDGE] → <NEXT ACTION>

    SOURCE FORMALISM:
    NONE

    OUR FORMALIZATION:
    [OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX] PROGRAMMING_BY_ITINERARY = directional guidance + executable candidate generation + situated failure oracles + preserved constraints.

    TENSION:
    An LLM is not a human apprentice and may not possess the perceptual education Ingold describes; the analogy must be tested rather than assumed.

    MISSING:
    A computational criterion distinguishing useful underspecification from mere ambiguity.

    BOUNDARY:
    Ingold does not claim stories execute on computers.

    CITATION TRAIL:
    Gibson; apprenticeship; natural-language programming; human-in-the-loop systems.

    TEST:
    Compare exhaustive specification, minimal imperative, and itinerary-style guidance on novel cases not explicitly covered by the input.
