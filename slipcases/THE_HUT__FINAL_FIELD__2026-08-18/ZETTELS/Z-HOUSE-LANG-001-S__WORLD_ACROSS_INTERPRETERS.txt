TITLE:
A WORLD CAN BE BUILT ACROSS INTERPRETERS WITHOUT ANY SINGLE REPRESENTATION CONTAINING THE WHOLE WORLD

SOURCE:
Walter Benjamin — “On the Concept of History,” Thesis IX — 1940 — reading of Paul Klee’s Angelus Novus, with Scholem’s “Gruß vom Angelus” as epigraph.

SOURCE URL:
https://www.sfu.ca/~andrewf/CONCEPT2.html

PASSAGE:
[PARAPHRASE] Benjamin begins from Klee’s depicted angel and transforms visible features of the image into the temporality, agency, obstruction, and orientation of an “angel of history.”

RESEARCH OBJECT:
The supplied worldtext exposes an unusually clean COMPILATION CHAIN:

PAINTING
→ PHILOSOPHICAL DESCRIPTION
→ HISTORICAL ALLEGORY
→ QUOTED / TRANSLATED TEXT
→ ONTOLOGICAL ENCODING.

No stage merely copies the previous one. Each makes something newly operable.

LOCAL MOVE:
Benjamin does not merely describe Klee’s painting. He takes eyes, mouth, wings, orientation and makes them participate in another world containing past, future, dead, wreckage, reparation, storm, and progress.

The image becomes infrastructure for a historical ontology.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
“How many interpreters away from the wall is a word?” has a representational analogue:

HOW MANY INTERPRETERS AWAY FROM THE PAINTING IS THE ANGEL OF HISTORY?

The answer is not a defect. The interpretive layers are what make the new world possible.

QUESTION:
At what point in a chain of representations should we say a new world has been created rather than the old one translated?

DEEPER QUESTION:
Can provenance preserve transformation without pretending that later representations are faithful copies of earlier ones?

TENSION:
The structured ontology in the uploaded file is not Benjamin’s ontology. Terms such as EVENT, ROLE, MODALITY, and STATE TRANSITION belong to the later encoding layer.

Flattening these layers would falsely attribute our formalism to Benjamin.

MISSING:
A transformation ledger recording for every representational step what survived, what disappeared, what was invented, what became explicit, what became executable, and what remained ambiguous.

BOUNDARY:
Benjamin supplies the philosophical transformation from painting to historical allegory. The full pipeline through “ontological encoding” belongs to the supplied worldtext and our reconstruction, not to Benjamin.

TEST:
Give Klee’s Angelus Novus independently to a poet, historian, ontology engineer, programmer, and generative model.

Require each output to become input to the next. At every transformation record Δ. Then reverse the chain.

Determine which distinctions are recoverable and which transformations irreversibly built a new world.
