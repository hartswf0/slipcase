    TITLE:
    A FAILURE IS LEGIBLE ONLY IN A WORLD CAPABLE OF MAKING IT APPEAR

    SOURCE:
    Tim Ingold — Making — 2013 — Chapter 4, “On building a house,” pp. 48–49.

    SOURCE URL:
    file:MAKING_INGOLD(1).pdf

    PASSAGE:
    [QUOTE] “Completion is, at best, a legal fiction.” [PARAPHRASE] Ingold contrasts architectural conception with rain, leakage, rot, infestation, and continual maintenance.

    RESEARCH OBJECT:
    The boundary of deferred formalization is not merely outcome legibility. The artifact must be placed in an evaluation world rich enough to expose the relevant failure mode.

    LOCAL MOVE:
    Ingold uses rain and maintenance to show how formal architectural worlds omit forces that later become decisive to residents and builders.

    SOURCE TERMS:
    plan; improvise; rain; leak; resident-builder; formal world; fluid reality

    WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
    A rendered roof cannot leak. Absence of visible failure can therefore mean the evaluation world is too thin, not that the artifact is correct.

    QUESTION:
    Is failure legibility a property of the artifact, or of artifact-plus-evaluation-world?

    DEEPER QUESTION:
    How much world coverage is enough before iterative inspection becomes trustworthy?

    MECHANISM:
    formal design → omitted environmental variable → exposure → failure → new constraint.

    FORMAL SHIFT:
    <ARTIFACT> + <EVALUATION WORLD> → [EXPOSE] → <FAILURE OR PASS>

    SOURCE FORMALISM:
    NONE

    OUR FORMALIZATION:
    [OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX] LEGIBLE_FAILURE(A,E); no observed failure implies little unless E covers the relevant failure modes.

    TENSION:
    This limits visual prompt iteration in domains such as structural safety, security, numerical stability, and privacy.

    MISSING:
    A theory of world coverage: scenarios, simulations, adversaries, users, climates, and timescales.

    BOUNDARY:
    The building example does not establish a universal testing metric.

    CITATION TRAIL:
    Simulation testing; adversarial evaluation; property-based testing.

    TEST:
    Evaluate one generated artifact in increasingly rich worlds and record the first environment in which each latent defect appears.
