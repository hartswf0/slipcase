TITLE:
A TRANSLATION CAN REBUILD THE TEMPORAL HOUSE OF A POEM

SOURCE:
Gershom Scholem — “Gruß vom Angelus” — German poem; source fragment and accompanying English rendering reproduced with Walter Benjamin’s Thesis IX.

SOURCE URL:
https://www.sfu.ca/~andrewf/CONCEPT2.html

PASSAGE:
[QUOTE] German: “lebendige Zeit”

[QUOTE] Supplied English rendering: “timeless time”

RESEARCH OBJECT:
The two expressions do not merely decorate the same world differently. They construct different temporal conditions.

LIVING TIME is not TIMELESS TIME.

The translation variance produces two possible temporal ontologies rather than a transparent copy.

LOCAL MOVE:
Scholem’s German fragment places the speaker at a threshold among flight, return, and remaining. The supplied English rendering changes a key temporal phrase, producing a second possible world rather than a transparent copy of the first.

WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
Translation may change more than semantic nuance. It can change the RULES OF THE WORLD.

QUESTION:
When two language versions support different possible actions, states, or temporal structures, in what sense are they implementations of the same world?

DEEPER QUESTION:
What must translation preserve for HOUSE_A(Language₁) and HOUSE_B(Language₂) to count as the same house?

WORLD(Language) = entities + relations + modalities + temporal rules + possible transitions.

TENSION:
The translator of the supplied English rendering is unidentified. The discrepancy must therefore be preserved rather than silently “corrected.”

MISSING:
A method for determining which linguistic distinctions are WORLD-CRITICAL. Not every lexical difference changes ontology. Some do.

BOUNDARY:
The evidence licenses the claim that these two supplied phrases differ materially. It does not license a claim about the translator’s intention or about one definitive philosophical meaning of Scholem’s poem.

CITATION TRAIL:

TEST:
Take five translations of the same world-defining passage.

For each, independently extract entities, relations, permissions, temporal states, possible transitions, and impossibilities.

Compare the resulting world models. A translation difference is WORLD-ALTERING when it changes the reachable state space rather than merely its verbal presentation.
