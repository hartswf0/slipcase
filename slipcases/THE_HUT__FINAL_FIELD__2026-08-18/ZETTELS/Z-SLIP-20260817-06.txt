    TITLE:
    A CONSTRAINT HISTORY CAN PRESERVE DECISIONS AND STILL LOSE THE THEORY

    SOURCE:
    Peter Naur — “Programming as Theory Building” — 1985 — Microprocessing and Microprogramming 15(5), pp. 253–261.

    SOURCE URL:
    https://doi.org/10.1016/0165-6074(85)90032-8

    PASSAGE:
    [PARAPHRASE] Naur argues that the primary achievement of programming is a theory held by programmers about how the problem is solved, and that program text and documentation can fail to transfer that theory adequately.

    RESEARCH OBJECT:
    Replacing chat history with constraint history is not enough if the system preserves what survived but not why those distinctions mattered.

    LOCAL MOVE:
    Naur shifts the unit of programming away from textual product toward the programmer’s acquired understanding needed for explanation and modification.

    SOURCE TERMS:
    theory building; program text; explanation; modification; programmer knowledge

    WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
    A perfect archive of constraints may remain theoretically dead if it cannot support principled response to a novel case.

    QUESTION:
    What must a constraint genealogy preserve in order to carry a theory rather than only a history?

    DEEPER QUESTION:
    Can an AI inherit theory from rationale, counterexamples, and modification histories, or only imitate their sediment?

    MECHANISM:
    problem engagement → theory acquired → program produced/modified; text may survive while theory fails to transfer.

    FORMAL SHIFT:
    <PROGRAM HISTORY> → <TEXT + DOCUMENTATION> → [TRANSFER] → <POSSIBLE THEORY LOSS>

    SOURCE FORMALISM:
    NONE

    OUR FORMALIZATION:
    [OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX] THEORY = constraints + reasons + analogies + causal expectations + rules for novel cases.

    TENSION:
    Structured constraint memory is better than raw chat but may still fall short of the understanding Naur describes.

    MISSING:
    Representations for rationale, causal model, analogy, and conditions for relaxing invariants.

    BOUNDARY:
    Naur does not discuss LLMs or prompt histories; this application is an inference.

    CITATION TRAIL:
    Design rationale; truth-maintenance systems; reflective practice.

    TEST:
    Compare agents receiving final prompt, constraint history, and constraint history plus rationale on an unforeseen modification.
