    TITLE:
    THE MACHINE CAN BE A HOUSE WITHOUT ITS MAKER AUTHORING EVERYTHING THAT INHABITS IT

    SOURCE:
    A. M. Turing — “Computing Machinery and Intelligence” — 1950 — Mind 59(236), pp. 433–460, theological objection.

    SOURCE URL:
    https://doi.org/10.1093/mind/LIX.236.433

    PASSAGE:
    [QUOTE] “instruments of His will providing mansions for the souls that He creates.”

    RESEARCH OBJECT:
    Turing’s hypothetical theological metaphor separates constructing a material mansion from creating its possible inhabitant. The structural distinction matters even if the theology is bracketed.

    LOCAL MOVE:
    He answers an objection to machine intelligence by comparing machine construction with procreation and limiting the maker’s claim to ultimate authorship.

    SOURCE TERMS:
    construct; machines; procreation; instruments; mansions; souls; creates

    WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
    A program can be understood as an architecture of possibilities rather than a complete script of everything that later happens inside it.

    QUESTION:
    When does program authorship end and inhabitant/environmental history begin?

    DEEPER QUESTION:
    What parts of emergent behavior should be attributed to architecture, agent, interaction history, or evaluator?

    MECHANISM:
    maker → constructed substrate → inhabitation/operation → behavior exceeding the construction act.

    FORMAL SHIFT:
    <MAKER> → <MANSION> → [INHABIT/OPERATE] → <TRAJECTORY>

    SOURCE FORMALISM:
    NONE

    OUR FORMALIZATION:
    [OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX] PROGRAM_AS_HOUSE = possibility space whose trajectories depend on more than the initial builder.

    TENSION:
    The theological argument is explicitly speculative; it cannot be used as evidence of machine souls.

    MISSING:
    An account of computational inhabitation: state space, memory, rules, interfaces, agents.

    BOUNDARY:
    The source supports a metaphorical separation of substrate construction and soul creation, not a theory of emergent software agency.

    CITATION TRAIL:
    Artificial life; open-ended systems; Brautigan.

    TEST:
    Hold a computational environment constant while varying agents and histories; partition variance by architecture, inhabitant, and interaction.
