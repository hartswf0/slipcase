    TITLE:
    A GENEALOGY FAILS WHEN IT RECORDS ONLY THE CONSTRAINTS THAT WON

    SOURCE:
    Walter Benjamin — “On the Concept of History,” Thesis IX — 1940.

    SOURCE URL:
    https://www.sfu.ca/~andrewf/CONCEPT2.html

    PASSAGE:
    [QUOTE] “Where we perceive a chain of events, he sees one single catastrophe”

    RESEARCH OBJECT:
    A clean version history can falsely narrate iterative work as progress if rejected artifacts, failed assumptions, unresolved damage, and reasons for rejection disappear.

    LOCAL MOVE:
    Benjamin opposes the ordinary chain of events to the angel’s view of accumulating catastrophe and wreckage.

    SOURCE TERMS:
    chain of events; catastrophe; wreckage; dead; make whole; storm; progress

    WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
    A genealogy of constraints that stores only what survived can erase the causal residue that makes later decisions intelligible.

    QUESTION:
    What should be retained from failed branches so that the final program does not rewrite its development as inevitable?

    DEEPER QUESTION:
    How can wreckage be compressed without turning it into triumphal progress?

    MECHANISM:
    candidate → failure → either deletion or preserved counterexample/rationale/nogood.

    FORMAL SHIFT:
    <FAILED BRANCHES> → [PRESERVE CAUSAL RESIDUE] → <COUNTER-ARCHIVE OF THE PROGRAM>

    SOURCE FORMALISM:
    NONE

    OUR FORMALIZATION:
    [OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX] PROGRAM_MEMORY = surviving constraints + failed candidates + rejection reasons + superseded assumptions + unresolved tensions.

    TENSION:
    Preserving everything produces an unusable dump; the engineering problem is selective retention without historical erasure.

    MISSING:
    A principled retention rule for counterexamples, obsolete detail, rationale, and unresolved debt.

    BOUNDARY:
    Benjamin is not writing about software; the application is a pressure-test, not technical evidence.

    CITATION TRAIL:
    ATMS; Naur; design rationale; version control.

    TEST:
    Compare final-prompt, surviving-constraint, and full-rationale memories on a late requirement that reopens an old decision.
