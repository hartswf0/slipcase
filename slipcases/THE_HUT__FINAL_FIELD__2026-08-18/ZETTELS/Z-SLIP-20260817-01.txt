    TITLE:
    A GRAMMAR CAN GENERATE A PLAN WITHOUT GENERATING THE PRACTICE THAT BUILDS IT

    SOURCE:
    George Stiny and William J. Mitchell — “The Palladian Grammar” — 1978 — Environment and Planning B 5(1), pp. 5–18.

    SOURCE URL:
    https://doi.org/10.1068/b050005

    PASSAGE:
    [PARAPHRASE] The paper develops a parametric shape grammar that generates Palladian villa ground plans and applies it to the Villa Malcontenta.

    RESEARCH OBJECT:
    Architectural “language” becomes technically generative when rules transform a representational state into a new plan, but this still stops before material construction.

    LOCAL MOVE:
    Stiny and Mitchell turn architectural canons into a formal generative system rather than merely classifying existing buildings.

    SOURCE TERMS:
    parametric shape grammar; ground plans; Palladian style; generation; rules

    WHAT BECAME STRANGE:
    The phrase “a language builds a house” becomes testable only after specifying what layer is generated: plan, geometry, fabrication command, or inhabited building.

    QUESTION:
    Where does architectural generation stop being metaphor and become operation?

    DEEPER QUESTION:
    How many interpreters remain between a generative grammar and a wall?

    MECHANISM:
    Formal rules transform shapes through ordered derivations to produce admissible ground plans.

    FORMAL SHIFT:
    <ARCHITECTURAL CANONS> → <PARAMETRIC SHAPE RULES> → [APPLY] → <GENERATED PLAN>

    SOURCE FORMALISM:
    A parametric shape grammar for Palladian villa ground plans.

    OUR FORMALIZATION:
    [OUR FORMALIZATION — NOT SOURCE SYNTAX] PLAN_GENERATION != MATERIAL_CONSTRUCTION; each additional interpreter must be named.

    TENSION:
    Ingold’s account of medieval building argues that real workmanship cannot be factored into rules without remainder.

    MISSING:
    A theory of interpreter layers between plan and building.

    BOUNDARY:
    The source licenses generation of plans, not a claim that formal grammar exhausts architectural practice.

    CITATION TRAIL:
    Tim Ingold, Making; program synthesis; robotic fabrication.

    TEST:
    Trace one generated plan through documentation, fabrication, assembly, occupancy, and maintenance; record every non-entailed judgment.
